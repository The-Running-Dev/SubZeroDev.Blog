# What's Under the Anger?

The conversation started with humanity.

People are sedated.

People are not angry enough.

The world is fucked.

We should be mad.

Fine.

So I asked the annoying question:

> What are you angry about?

Not whether the anger was justified.

Not whether society deserved it.

What are **you** angry about?

<!-- truncate -->

That changed the conversation.

Because "the world" is a very large container.

You can put almost anything in there.

Politics.

Money.

Work.

Parents.

Loneliness.

Failure.

Fear.

Powerlessness.

Disappointment.

Then call the entire package anger.

## The Loudest Layer

I have started thinking of anger as an umbrella for a lot of emotions.

Not always.

Sometimes anger is just anger.

Somebody does something and you are fucking pissed.

No mystery required.

But anger is also extremely convenient.

It is loud.

It is easy to identify.

It has direction.

It gives you energy.

It says:

> Something is wrong. Push.

That is a much easier state to inhabit than:

> I feel powerless.

Or:

> I am scared.

Or:

> I am disappointed.

Or:

> I wanted something from people who were never going to give it to me.

Or:

> I am grieving something I cannot change.

Those are messier.

Anger puts on boots.

## And Then We Got to Parents

She started talking about hopelessness.

Powerlessness.

Then parents.

Then early childhood.

There.

Now we are getting to it.

That does not mean her anger at the world is fake.

This is the part people flatten immediately.

If your history shapes your anger, the social problem does not magically disappear.

Both can be true.

The world can contain real things worth being angry about.

And your particular relationship to anger can be shaped by things that happened long before you had words for any of it.

No contradiction required.

Reality once again refuses the binary.

## Armchair Psychology, Clearance Aisle

At some point I warned her that I was doing armchair psychology.

Naturally the transcription tried to turn that into "arch chair psychologist," because technology also wanted to participate.

Fine.

Arch Chair Psychologist is now accepting walk-ins next to Discount Store Marcus Aurelius.

But the question still mattered:

> What is under the anger?

Not because I wanted to diagnose her.

Not because I wanted to fix her.

I was curious.

And maybe I was curious because I recognized the territory.

## I Used to Be Angry Too

I remember being angry at the world.

Angry at my parents.

Angry at life.

Things should have been different.

People should have behaved differently.

I should not have had to deal with this shit.

All of that.

Then at some point something changed.

I wish I had a cleaner story.

I do not.

I cannot point to a Tuesday afternoon and say:

> There. That was the day I stopped fighting myself.

I got here somehow.

That is what fascinates me.

I still get angry.

Obviously.

I can produce anger on demand before coffee.

But the anger does not seem to sit on top of the same internal war anymore.

That war ended somewhere along the way.

Or maybe it became unnecessary.

I do not know.

## The Internal Fight

This was the line that came out while I was talking to her:

I did not stop fighting battles.

I stopped the internal battle while fighting them.

That felt accurate.

The internet battles remain available.

Humanity continues generating material.

People still do ridiculous shit.

I still react.

I still care.

I still push.

But there is a difference between fighting something **out there** and making your own existence the second opponent.

That second opponent is expensive.

## The Question Is Better Than the Verdict

This is why I like the question more than the argument.

"Should people be angrier?" is almost impossible to answer without turning it into a slogan.

Maybe.

About what?

For how long?

To what end?

What happens next?

"What is under your anger?" is different.

That question can actually move.

Maybe there is nothing underneath it.

Maybe the answer is simply:

> I am angry because this is fucked.

Fair enough.

Maybe underneath it is grief.

Maybe fear.

Maybe helplessness.

Maybe a parent.

Maybe a twenty-year-old wound wearing current-events clothing.

You do not know until you look.

And apparently this is what I do for fun now.

Gin.

Philosophy.

Serbian existential crisis.

Arch Chair Psychology.

Humanity remains operational.

No license has been issued.

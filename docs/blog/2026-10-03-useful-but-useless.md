# I've Always Been Useful to People. I Still Feel Useless.

Some Scottish guy on the internet made me cry.

Which is already irritating.

He had been talking about men taking the piss out of each other constantly, then occasionally saying something genuinely kind and immediately having to ruin it with an insult because apparently sincerity must be handled with protective equipment.

That part was funny.

Then he talked about moving day.

You find out who your friends are when you have to move.

Also funny.

Then he said something else.

Something about being the person who always shows up.

The strong one.

The one who cares.

The one who handles things.

And eventually everybody gets used to the idea that you can handle things by yourself.

So when you are the one who needs somebody, nobody shows up.

That one was not funny.

<!-- truncate -->

I was already tired.

Half awake.

Half asleep.

Emotionally wrung out.

And there it was.

That stupid fucking sentence.

Because there are moments when I feel like absolute shit and there is nobody I can call.

Not nobody who can fix anything.

I do not need somebody to fix anything.

Just somebody who would ask:

> "Hey man. You okay?"

And actually be there for the answer.

That realization hit me hard enough that I cried.

Then, because apparently my internal operating system has exactly one fallback mode, the next thought arrived:

> "I'll get through it. Always do."

There it is.

Of course I will.

What the fuck else am I going to do?

## No, I Did Not Train Anybody

There was one part of the guy's argument I rejected immediately.

The idea that by always being strong, always showing up, always handling things, you somehow **train people** not to show up for you.

No.

Fuck that.

I did not train anybody.

People make choices.

If somebody knows me and chooses not to call, that is their choice.

If somebody knows I have always handled my own shit and decides that means they never need to ask how I am doing, that is still their choice.

I am not responsible for programming every adult around me into basic human concern.

People have agency.

So do I.

That does not make the situation less lonely.

It just means I am not going to turn somebody else's absence into another thing I supposedly caused.

## There Has to Be More Than This

Later, in that strange half-awake state where the brain starts throwing files onto the desktop without asking permission, another thought showed up.

What the fuck am I doing here?

Not specifically here as in this chair.

Here as in this life.

There has to be more than drinking, making money, building shit, maintaining shit, dealing with shit, and then waking up and doing some new variation of the same shit.

I am not looking for permanent happiness.

That sounds like nonsense to me.

Happiness is transient.

Something happens.

You feel good.

Then you do not.

Fine.

I am not looking for a permanent emotional state.

What I would like is something much less mystical.

A community.

Somewhere to be.

Something to be part of.

Something that makes me feel useful.

And that last part is fucking strange, because I have spent most of my life being useful to people.

I solve things.

I build things.

I fix things.

I help people.

I have built an entire career out of being the person who gets called when something is difficult.

And yet somehow:

> **I've always been useful to people, but I still feel useless.**

That is a weird sentence to discover about yourself.

## Maybe "Belonging" Is the Wrong Word

I do not even know what belonging feels like.

I do not think I have ever belonged anywhere.

Maybe I never will.

So I am not sure I want to load all of this onto that word.

It immediately becomes some giant philosophical question:

*Where do I belong?*

*Who are my people?*

*What is home?*

Fuck knows.

Maybe I do not need an answer.

Maybe "belonging" is too much.

Maybe I would just like to be somewhere and be part of something.

That seems simpler.

There are people there.

I know them.

They know me.

Things happen.

I participate.

I am useful in some ordinary way.

If I disappear for a week, somebody notices.

That is already a lot.

## Then My Brain Went to Africa

Because apparently when my brain decides to explore an idea, subtlety is not invited.

The thought was:

Why am I here doing this when I could be in some village in Africa helping set up computers or an education program or doing something useful with people?

I have been to Europe.

I have been to North America.

That is basically it.

Africa is not some thing I understand.

I have seen videos.

I have known people from there.

That is not experience.

I would love to actually experience a part of the world I know almost nothing about.

Live somewhere different.

Meet people.

Learn how they live.

Do something useful if there is something useful I can actually do.

Maybe help with computers.

Maybe education.

Maybe infrastructure.

Maybe something I have not even thought of.

Not because I have some fantasy about arriving to "save" anybody.

What the fuck would I know about what people there need before I even get there?

The appeal is almost the opposite.

I know I would get something enormous from the experience.

A different culture.

A different rhythm.

Different people.

A different way of living.

And if I can contribute something while I am there, good.

That sounds useful in both directions.

Also, I have a camper.

Which makes the thought considerably less theoretical than it should probably be.

Europe ends.

Spain happens.

There are ferries.

Africa exists on the other side.

Apparently geography remains undefeated.

## Or a Zen Temple in Taiwan

Then the brain produced option two.

A Zen Buddhist temple in Taiwan.

Sure.

Why not?

Africa.

Taiwan.

A monastery.

A village.

Apparently the internal planning department had stopped caring about categories.

But the common part was obvious.

Simple life.

People.

Community.

Being useful.

Doing something instead of sitting around wondering what the fuck I am doing.

The exact geography was almost secondary.

The shape of the life was the interesting part.

## Money Is Money

There is another part of this that keeps coming back.

Money.

I have a very simple relationship with it.

Money is money.

If I have it, I spend it to live.

If I do not need to spend it, I do not.

I need very little to live.

Very little.

Potentially almost nothing compared to what people imagine they need.

So the idea of a simpler life does not scare me.

It is not some dramatic sacrifice where I abandon the sacred pursuit of accumulation.

I do not give enough of a shit about accumulation for that to be dramatic.

The question is simpler:

How much do I actually need?

And if the answer is "not much," then that opens a lot of fucking doors.

## I Already Know What I Like

The funny thing is I am not completely confused about what gives me something.

I like creating things.

I like sitting at a computer and building ridiculous shit nobody asked for.

I like going out.

I like meeting people.

I like talking to strangers.

I like hearing somebody's story.

I like doing things.

The night before all of this, I went to a bar.

Three people were sitting there.

Everybody except me was on a phone.

I talked to them anyway.

Then other people came in.

A conversation started about towing and police bullshit.

Then another couple showed up.

Then I ended up talking to a woman about what life had been like when she was a teacher in the Soviet Union.

That is the shit I like.

People.

Stories.

Doing things.

Building things.

Being involved in something that is actually happening.

So maybe this morning's thought is not as random as it first looked.

Maybe my brain was not saying:

> "Move to Africa."

Maybe it was not saying:

> "Become a Buddhist monk."

Maybe it was saying something much less specific:

> "For fuck's sake, go participate in life somewhere."

## I Don't Know What This Is Yet

This is not a plan.

I have not researched organizations.

I have not picked a country.

I have not found a village.

I have not shaved my head and joined a monastery.

The camper is not currently pointed south.

Nothing has happened.

It is just a thought.

But it is here.

And I do not want to throw it away just because it arrived while I was tired and emotional and half asleep.

There is something in it that feels worth keeping.

Not happiness.

Not destiny.

Not some grand search for meaning.

Just this:

I would like to be part of something.

I would like to have a community somewhere.

I would like to feel useful.

I would like to experience more of the world while I am still here to experience it.

And I would like my life to contain more than simply proving, over and over again, that whatever happens:

> "I'll get through it. Always do."

I know I can get through things.

I have enough evidence.

Maybe the more interesting question now is:

**What the fuck do I want to be here for?**

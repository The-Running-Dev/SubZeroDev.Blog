I have absolutely no idea how some of my conversations get from point A to point Z.

And I fucking love it.

I might start with politics, wander through World War II logistics, discover a philosophical idea, remember a programming interview from 2001, and somehow end up talking about a lunar bank robbery in a science-fiction novel.

That's not a failed conversation.

That's a wonderful fucking evening.

## Remember StumbleUpon?

There used to be a website called StumbleUpon.

You'd click a button and land somewhere on the internet you hadn't necessarily thought to look. Then you'd click again. And again.

I could lose hours in it.

Somebody had found a strange piece of engineering, an obscure article, a bizarre photograph, a tiny personal website, or some idea I'd never encountered.

I wasn't trying to find anything specific.

I was looking forward to discovering what I didn't know existed.

I wouldn't be surprised if I still have bookmarks from those stumbles saved somewhere in 2026.

## The Problem with Knowing What You Want

Search is fantastic when you know what you're looking for.

But what if the interesting thing is something you *don't* know to ask for?

That's the part I enjoy: accidental discovery.

I encounter something, think "oh, that's possible," and mentally file away a pointer.

Later, a different thought activates it. Something I read in a book connects with a technical problem, or a historical argument reminds me of a conversation I had years ago.

One pointer takes me to another.

The journey is not necessarily directed toward a solution. Sometimes a solution appears anyway.

And when it does, it's often not the answer I might have predicted at the start.

## Ender Thought He Was Doing Something Else

One of my favorite books is *Ender's Game* by Orson Scott Card.

Ender believes he's participating in simulated military exercises. Then comes the realization that the battles were real.

That revelation changes the meaning of what he has already experienced.

The information wasn't simply added to the story. The context of the earlier story changed.

That is one way a journey can turn into something entirely different from what you thought it was.

## A Bank Robbery on the Moon

Another novel that stuck with me is Philip Kerr's *The Second Angel*.

It starts with a dystopian story involving valuable uninfected blood and a heist targeting a bank on the Moon.

And then the whole thing goes somewhere else—nanotechnology, an extraordinary intelligence, and an unexpected voyage away from the originally intended destination.

I love that turn.

You set out to do one thing, and the story takes you somewhere you couldn't have planned for.

Not exactly the same literary trick as *Ender's Game*, but both reminded me of how an initial frame can be transformed by what happens next.

## No, I Don't Need a Destination

People often frame thinking as a task: define the question, gather the evidence, reach a result.

Fine. Sometimes that's necessary.

But I'm perfectly happy to take a thought and see where it leads. If it loops back, good. If it crosses into a different subject, even better.

I don't have to know where the rabbit hole ends.

That's part of the fun.

And the best part is that the next interesting connection may have been sitting quietly in my head for twenty-five fucking years, waiting for the right conversation to bring it out.

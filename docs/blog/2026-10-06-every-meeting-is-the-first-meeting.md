# Every Meeting Is the First Meeting

Moonflower took a nap.

When she woke up, she asked what day it was.

So naturally I told her:

> Yesterday. You traveled back in time.

She laughed and answered with something like:

> So I am meeting you again for the first time.

And my brain went:

**Oh shit.**

<!-- truncate -->

I am almost certain she meant it as a joke.

That does not matter.

The sentence landed somewhere else.

Because I have spent a ridiculous amount of time lately thinking about how people try to understand each other as though we are static objects.

Where are you from?

What do you do?

Who are you?

What kind of person are you?

Are you guarded?

Are you complicated?

Fine questions, I suppose.

But they all quietly assume there is a stable object hiding underneath the conversation.

A final version.

A person that can eventually be fully described.

And I am increasingly unconvinced that person exists.

## Yesterday's Version

Five years ago I was not exactly this person.

A year ago I was not exactly this person.

A month ago I was not exactly this person.

Last week?

Probably not either.

That does not mean there is no continuity.

Obviously there is.

I remember things.

I have habits.

I have preferences.

I have history.

I have scars.

I have songs I have loved for decades.

I have ridiculous phrases I apparently carried around for ten years before understanding why they mattered.

There is continuity.

But continuity is not sameness.

A river has continuity too.

Good luck stepping into the exact same fucking water twice.

## We Keep Freezing People Anyway

Humans love snapshots.

We take one interaction and turn it into identity.

You did this.

Therefore you are this kind of person.

You said that.

Therefore now I understand you.

You once believed something.

Therefore that belief is you.

You were born somewhere.

Therefore that is where you are from.

You have a job.

Therefore that is what you do.

This is efficient.

It is also frequently bullshit.

The snapshot may be accurate.

It may even be useful.

But it is still a snapshot of something that is moving.

And then people get surprised when the moving thing moves.

## Meeting Again for the First Time

That is why Moonflower's joke hit me.

If she wakes up tomorrow slightly changed by what happened today, and I wake up tomorrow slightly changed by what happened today, then technically those two versions have never met.

We know each other.

But we also don't.

Not completely.

Maybe every meeting contains both things:

Recognition.

And introduction.

I know your history.

I know your face.

I know some of your stories.

And still:

Who are you today?

That is a much more interesting question than:

Who are you?

The second sounds like a request for a permanent file.

The first leaves the file open.

## This Is Not Amnesia

I am not suggesting we pretend yesterday never happened.

If somebody punched me yesterday, I am not going to greet them this morning like:

> Wonderful. A completely new human. Please punch responsibly.

History matters.

Patterns matter.

Actions matter.

Consequences matter.

But history is context, not a prison sentence.

A person can remain accountable for what happened yesterday without being required to remain exactly the same person forever.

That seems obvious when written down.

People still struggle with it constantly.

Including me.

## The Funniest Part

I have spent years getting irritated when people try to define me too quickly.

And yet I do the same basic thing in my own head.

I build models.

I notice patterns.

I predict behavior.

I decide what somebody probably means.

Then sometimes reality taps me on the shoulder and says:

> Cute model.

> Anyway.

That is where acceptance becomes useful.

Not because understanding is bad.

Understanding is great.

I love understanding shit.

My brain apparently stores random information for twenty years just so it can ambush me with a connection at three in the morning.

But understanding is not ownership.

And a model is not the person.

## Tomorrow

Maybe Moonflower and I keep talking.

Maybe we do not.

Maybe tomorrow's version of her likes tomorrow's version of me.

Maybe not.

None of that existed when she made the joke.

We were just there.

She had taken a nap.

I accused her of time travel.

She said she was meeting me again for the first time.

And suddenly this stupid little Bumble conversation opened a door.

Not because she meant to.

Not because I was looking for philosophy.

It was just there.

So maybe tomorrow, if we talk again, I will meet Moonflower again for the first time.

And she will meet me again for the first time.

Same people.

Not quite.

Which seems about right.

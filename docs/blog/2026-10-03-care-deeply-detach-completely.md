# I Can Care Deeply and Detach Completely

I can care deeply.

And I can detach completely.

I used to think those two statements contradicted each other.

They do not.

That might be one of the stranger things I have learned about myself.

<!-- truncate -->

My Ridgeback sleeps in my bed.

She gets fed.

She gets bones.

She gets toys.

She gets blankets.

She gets talked to like a person despite being, biologically speaking, an extremely muscular idiot with opinions.

I care about that fucking dog.

Then she chewed through an electrical cord.

And I put her outside for the night.

She whined.

I hated it.

I wanted her inside.

I also did not let her inside.

All of those things were true at once.

## Caring Is Not Compliance

There is a version of caring that people seem to confuse with permanent accommodation.

If I care about you, I should keep giving.

If I love you, I should tolerate.

If I understand you, I should excuse.

If I know why you did something, the boundary should disappear.

I do not believe that anymore.

Understanding is information.

Caring is attachment.

A boundary is a decision.

Those are separate systems.

They can influence each other.

They are not the same thing.

The dog can want inside.

I can understand why she wants inside.

I can feel bad that she is outside.

I can still leave her outside.

That does not require hatred.

It does not even require anger once the decision is made.

It requires only a boundary that survives contact with discomfort.

## The Part That Feels Cruel

This is where it gets ugly.

I do not enjoy enforcement.

I do not like being the disciplinarian.

I do not enjoy occupying the position of:

> You did this, therefore now this changes.

I would much rather everybody understand the line without me having to draw it.

Dog.

Family.

Friend.

Partner.

Coworker.

Me.

Wouldn't that be fucking convenient?

Everybody behaves reasonably.

Nobody crosses the boundary.

Nobody forces the consequence.

Nobody has to become the asshole standing at the door saying no.

That universe remains unavailable.

So sometimes you care about something and still deny it access to you.

Sometimes you love somebody and stop speaking to them.

Sometimes you understand exactly why somebody behaves the way they do and still refuse to participate.

Sometimes the dog sleeps outside.

## Detachment Is Not Indifference

This is the part that took me years to understand.

Detachment is not the absence of feeling.

Sometimes it is what happens **after** the feeling has been fully acknowledged.

I can care deeply about what happens to somebody.

I can hope they are well.

I can remember them.

I can understand them.

I can even love them.

And I can also decide:

> I am no longer participating in this.

Those are not opposites.

The attachment exists.

The participation ends.

People often treat that as cold.

Maybe sometimes it is.

But there is another version where continuing would require lying to yourself about what is happening.

And I am increasingly bad at that.

## It Doesn't Matter

There is another distinction hiding in here.

"I don't care" and "it doesn't matter" do different jobs.

I am increasingly suspicious of the first one when it becomes a description of the person.

I am someone who doesn't care.

Excellent.

Now you have an identity to maintain.

Something bothers you, and suddenly you have two problems.

The thing that bothered you.

And the fact that being bothered is apparently off-brand.

So now you have to explain how little you care.

Possibly at considerable length.

I have done versions of this myself.

The announcement becomes work.

"It doesn't matter" leaves me considerably more room.

I noticed.

I remember.

I may even be irritated.

It still doesn't matter enough to determine what I do next.

That is a decision about this thing, in this situation.

It doesn't require me to become an entirely different species of mammal.

And sometimes it does matter.

Fine.

I can act on that too.

No press conference about the collapse of my indifference.

The useful freedom is being able to decide what deserves a response while admitting that I felt something.

## The Dog Does Not Care About My Philosophy

Naturally, none of this matters to the Ridgeback.

She is not outside thinking:

> The human appears to be practicing differentiated attachment with boundary preservation.

She is thinking:

> Door.

> Open door.

> Bed.

> Why door no open?

Fair.

Her philosophical framework is refreshingly concise.

But the situation still exposed the distinction in me.

I loved the dog.

I was furious with the dog.

I worried about the dog.

I refused the dog.

I wanted her beside me.

I left her outside.

None of these cancelled the others.

## Humans Make This Harder

Dogs are almost easier.

A dog does some shit.

You react.

The dog moves on.

Humans add narratives.

If you loved me, you would.

If you cared, you wouldn't.

If you understood, you would forgive.

If you forgave, the boundary would disappear.

If the boundary remains, therefore the love must have been fake.

What a fucking mess.

No.

I can care deeply and still say no.

I can forgive and still not return.

I can understand and still leave.

I can love and still detach.

The feeling does not obligate the behavior.

That may be the entire point.

## The Strange Freedom

There is something strangely peaceful in accepting this.

I do not need to manufacture hatred in order to leave something behind.

I do not need to rewrite somebody as a monster to justify a boundary.

I do not need the dog to become a bad dog forever because she chewed one electrical cord.

I do not need anybody to become evil in order for me to say:

> No more of this.

That is a much cleaner mechanism.

Behavior.

Boundary.

Consequence.

Care remains wherever it remains.

And if one day the attachment itself disappears, fine.

If it does not, also fine.

I spent years thinking detachment meant becoming somebody who did not care.

Apparently I had it backwards.

Sometimes detachment is what becomes possible **because** I finally accept exactly how much I care and stop trying to use that feeling as a reason to tolerate everything.

I can care deeply.

And I can detach completely.

Both are true.

Fucking wild.

# The Mazda That Refused to Die

The Mazda 3 wasn't a car I picked out.

Elizabeth brought it from the United States around 2018. It had lived through New York State winters. And if you've seen what New York roads and salt do to a car, you'll understand why the word "rust" keeps appearing in this story.

She maintained it and tried to keep it going. The transmission was already questionable. I drove it on and off for years.

Then, for a while, it became my European moving van.

<!-- truncate -->

## Transport Logistics

Elizabeth came to Spain and later left the Mazda behind.

I had the motorcycle transported back to Bulgaria on a truck.

For myself, I had the Mazda.

And a dog.

And far too much luggage for a Mazda 3.

So I bought one of those soft rooftop cargo carriers that straps on. Loaded the fucking thing. Dog in the front passenger seat. Luggage inside. More luggage on top.

Barcelona, then ferry to Italy, then across Italy toward Brindisi and onward to Bulgaria.

Was I sure the car would make it?

Absolutely not.

The transmission had been troublesome since its American days. At low speeds it would do its own thing, sometimes cooperating, sometimes not.

But the car still moved.

And in this one trip, contrary to precedent, nothing spectacular happened.

The Mazda fucking made it.

## New York's Long-Term Gift

A car can look reasonably maintained while rust slowly performs structural engineering without permission.

This one had rust everywhere it mattered.

I still drove it in Bulgaria. Still used it.

Then one day I headed toward Dobrich to collect the BMW.

The Mazda chose that trip to reveal exactly how much of its suspension was still attached.

One side of the rear suspension separated at the top.

Not a squeak. Not an interesting new rattle.

An actual disconnection.

Ivan inspected the thing and said the rust made a sensible repair difficult.

And there was another vehicle waiting.

## Grandparents' Automotive Cemetery

So the Mazda remained in my grandparents' yard up north.

Battery disconnected.

It joined about four other cars already living out their automotive afterlife there.

And here's the important part: **the Mazda wasn't even dead.**

It still ran. It was still registered. The engine wasn't what ended its career.

One rear corner simply no longer had a useful relationship with the suspension mount.

The last proper job it attempted was to get me to the BMW.

The BMW, as it happens, lived an even shorter life with me than the Mazda did.

Which is how a living but structurally defeated Mazda becomes a direct prequel to [Murphy Drives a BMW](/murphy-drives-a-bmw/).

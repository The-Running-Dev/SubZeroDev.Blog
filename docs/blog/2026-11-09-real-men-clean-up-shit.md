# Real Men Clean Up Shit

The outdoor drain was clogged, and the shop vac was full of something I would prefer not to identify.

Mystery sludge. Disgusting shit. The sort of material that makes the word *contents* sound like an act of mercy.

The job needed doing. I did it.

I was pleased with myself.

<!-- truncate -->

That is a fairly unglamorous opening for a discussion about masculinity. No opponent. No threats. Nobody required to acknowledge that I was the most dangerous bastard in the vicinity.

Just a blocked drain and the opportunity to be useful.

Apparently my initiation ceremony involved a shop vac.

There is an enormous amount of theater around being a man. The size of you. The volume of you. The impression that, if necessary, you could make somebody else's day considerably worse. We are supposed to recognize strength by how much space it occupies and how uncomfortable it makes everyone else.

Meanwhile, there is still shit that needs cleaning up.

The drain is unmoved by your reputation. It has not heard about your toughness. You can loom over it all you like. It remains clogged.

I enjoy that about practical problems. They have very limited interest in your personal mythology.

You either deal with the thing or you don't.

And dealing with it is sometimes fucking disgusting.

I am not going to turn the sludge into a noble substance because I handled it. It did not become less revolting through contact with my character. The shop vac was not an instrument of spiritual transformation. It was a shop vac full of shit I didn't want to contemplate any more closely than necessary.

But I got the job done, and there was satisfaction in that.

A direct, uncomplicated satisfaction. Something needed attention. I gave it attention. The task that existed before was finished afterward.

That is enough to feel good about.

The absurd part is how easily we can overlook that kind of competence while applauding the performance of it. Somebody can spend a remarkable amount of energy establishing that he is not a man to be fucked with. Fine. Congratulations on the branding.

Can he deal with a disgusting job?

Can he do something useful when there is no audience to intimidate?

I don't think cleaning a drain belongs to men. Anybody can be the person who deals with the mess. Nobody needs to pass a gender inspection before being allowed near a vacuum cleaner.

But if we are going to keep having this tiresome conversation about what real men do, I would like to nominate an actual task.

Clean up some shit.

Not as punishment. Not to prove you are above being disgusted. Be disgusted. The material has earned it.

Then do what needs doing.

There is something wonderfully deflating about measuring yourself against a clogged drain. It removes most of the flattering options. The drain cannot be impressed, bullied, or persuaded to describe you as formidable. Your entire contribution has to be the contribution.

In this case, mine involved an outdoor drain and a shop vac full of mystery sludge.

Not much of a heroic portrait.

I was still fucking pleased with myself.

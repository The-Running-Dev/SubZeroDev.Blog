# The Lamb

The soup was delicious.

That is important.

Because this is not a story about me discovering that meat tastes bad.

It does not.

The soup was fucking excellent.

And then somebody mentioned lamb.

<!-- truncate -->

Not in some dramatic philosophical conversation.

Just food.

Tradition.

Sheep.

Lamb.

Ordinary shit humans have been talking about around tables for thousands of years.

And something in my head changed.

The word stopped meaning food.

It became:

**A young sheep.**

That was it.

One tiny semantic substitution.

Lamb.

Young sheep.

And suddenly the abstraction disappeared.

## The Category Failed

Normally this machinery works perfectly.

Cow becomes beef.

Pig becomes pork.

Young sheep becomes lamb.

Chicken remains chicken because apparently English got lazy halfway through the project.

We know what the words mean.

Of course we know.

Nobody eating pork believes it grew naturally inside plastic packaging at Kaufland.

We know an animal existed.

We know somebody killed it.

We know the process.

But most of the time, knowledge lives in one compartment and dinner lives in another.

That afternoon the wall between the compartments broke.

I pictured the animal.

Not "lamb."

The animal.

Young.

Alive.

Helpless.

Somebody holding it.

A knife.

Its throat being cut.

And suddenly I was not thinking about cuisine.

I was thinking:

> Holy fucking shit. You killed the baby.

That thought hit me so hard I had to leave the room.

## This Is Not A Conversion Story

I did not emerge from the experience with a manifesto.

I did not throw away all the meat in the house.

I did not become morally purified before dessert.

I am not writing this as propaganda for vegetarianism, veganism, Bulgarian culinary reform, or some new religion where Lucifer protects sheep.

I ate the soup.

It was delicious.

That is part of the contradiction.

I can know exactly what meat is and eat it.

I can enjoy it.

I can also have one specific image enter my head and suddenly feel fucking devastated by the thing I already knew.

Nothing new was revealed.

Only the abstraction failed.

## Humans Are Excellent At This

We are extraordinarily good at categories.

Pet.

Livestock.

Food.

Pest.

Wildlife.

Companion animal.

Working animal.

Endangered animal.

Delicious animal.

Do not touch that one.

Put this one on a spit.

Take this one to the vet.

Shoot that one.

Save this one.

Name this one.

We can love a dog like family while eating a pig that may have been every bit as capable of fear, curiosity, comfort, and distress.

We can call somebody cruel for leaving a dog outside and then sit down for dinner without thinking about the animal on the plate.

We can campaign passionately about one category of suffering while treating another category as a normal Tuesday.

And I am saying **we** deliberately.

I am not standing outside humanity pointing at the hypocrites.

I am in the fucking picture.

That is what makes it uncomfortable.

## Tradition

The word "tradition" makes this even stranger.

People slaughter lamb because people have slaughtered lamb.

Families gather.

Religions gather.

Villages gather.

The animal becomes part of a ritual that existed before anybody currently participating in it was born.

And from inside the tradition, the act can feel ordinary.

From one inch outside the abstraction, it can feel horrifying.

Both experiences can exist simultaneously.

That is the part I cannot neatly file away.

The person eating lamb is not necessarily cruel.

The person who cannot bear to imagine the slaughter is not necessarily morally superior.

The animal is still dead.

The meal still happened.

Everybody returns to whatever story allows the world to continue making sense.

## The Dog

Later, I was furious at my Ridgeback for chewing through an electrical cord.

I put her outside.

I listened to her whine.

And I thought about animals again.

This ridiculous dog sleeps in my bed.

I feed her.

I worry about her.

I get angry at her.

I set boundaries.

I imagine what would happen to her if something happened to me.

I care about whether she is cold.

I also eat animals.

Humans contain these contradictions without exploding.

Usually.

That night, apparently, I was temporarily out of storage capacity.

## The Thing Itself

Maybe that is all this was.

A category stopped protecting me from the thing itself.

"Lamb" stopped being a menu item.

It became a young sheep.

And for a few minutes I could not participate in the ordinary human agreement that those are functionally different objects.

Then the agreement returned.

Because it always does.

You cannot walk through life experiencing the full physical reality behind every abstraction.

Every meal.

Every product.

Every war.

Every family argument.

Every supply chain.

Every death.

Every consequence.

Your nervous system would mutiny.

So we compress reality into words.

Usually that is useful.

Sometimes one of the words suddenly decompresses.

And there it is.

Alive.

Terrified.

Real.

The lamb.

I do not know what to do with that.

Maybe nothing.

But for a few minutes, I saw the animal instead of the category.

And Jesus fucking Christ.

That hurt.

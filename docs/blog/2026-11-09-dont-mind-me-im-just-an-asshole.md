# Don't Mind Me, I'm Just an Asshole

There is something wonderfully efficient about introducing yourself with the accusation already attached.

Don't mind me. I'm just an asshole.

<!-- truncate -->

A dreadful opening line, according to the department responsible for making everybody sound like somebody else. Too negative. Too confrontational. Where is the cheerful little demonstration of emotional availability? Couldn't I mention hiking?

Perhaps. But the asshole line interests me more.

It places the unflattering possibility on the table before we have invested an evening in pretending human beings arrive without sharp edges. It also leaves a rather useful question hanging in the air: what, exactly, would make me one?

Disagreeing? Swearing? Asking a question somebody would prefer to glide past?

Or actually treating somebody badly?

I would like to know which conversation we are having.

Chris Voss's accusation audit is the respectable relative of this little provocation. The [Black Swan Group describes it](https://www.blackswanltd.com/newsletter/what-is-the-black-swan-accusation-audit) as bringing up the negative perceptions the other person may have before they voice them. The point is to acknowledge their possible perspective and make room for a conversation.

My version arrives with considerably less tailoring.

That does not make the two identical. Calling myself an asshole could sound like a joke, a warning, an invitation to reassure me, or a promise of dreadful behavior. I don't get to choose what somebody hears merely because I enjoyed delivering it.

That is the inconvenient part of having a mouth. Other people are permitted ears.

Still, I like taking a supposedly devastating label and examining it in daylight. A word loses some of its theatrical power when the accused person has already wandered over and introduced it.

Here it is. Shall we discuss it?

Because I can imagine a perfectly fair objection to something I say. Perhaps the joke lands badly. Perhaps my timing is appalling. Perhaps I have mistaken being pleased with my own cleverness for being interesting company.

A distinct occupational hazard, that last one.

If that happens, tell me what I did. Give me something I can answer. I would rather have an awkward conversation about an actual sentence than an elegant little ceremony in which my entire character is declared defective and nobody supplies the evidence.

The opening line cannot exempt me from that scrutiny. It would be absurd to announce that I am an asshole and then wave the announcement around like diplomatic immunity.

I warned you, therefore I owe you nothing?

What a tedious little arrangement. Even the devil should read the small print before signing that.

The joke appeals to me because it lets some air into the room. Here is a potentially unflattering version of me. I can look at it. I can laugh at it. I am willing to find out whether it fits anything I actually do.

There is vulnerability in that, however handsomely I dress it up. Being candid gives somebody a clearer opportunity to dislike me. A polished introduction offers more places to hide.

But I want the conversation that comes after the introduction. The one where two people can ask what the other meant, disagree without issuing a verdict, and discover whether the humor travels in both directions.

So, don't mind me. I'm just an asshole.

You are welcome to ask for particulars.

# What the Fuck Do You Know About Money?

I see a dating profile that says anybody who believes money can't solve problems simply hasn't had enough money.

<!-- truncate -->

And my immediate reaction is: **What the fuck do you know about money?**

That's the question.

Not because young people aren't allowed to have opinions. Have all the opinions you want. Put them in your bio. Tattoo them on your forehead if you feel particularly committed.

But if you're going to make a sweeping statement about how the world works, I want to know what you're basing it on.

How much money have you had?

What problems did it actually solve?

What happened when the money ran out? Did the problems politely follow it out the door?

When I saw this profile, I read the message as something else too. To me, it suggested someone looking for another person to supply the money and make the problems go away. That's my reading of a dating profile, not a bank statement or a confession. I don't know her finances.

But it's a dating app. Context matters.

I described her as eighteen, with the sort of heavily altered look I don't find appealing, and I called her a gold digger. That's how strongly the whole presentation struck me. I wasn't carefully drafting a sociology paper. I was reacting to something that, to me, screamed financial expectation dressed up as worldly wisdom.

So I started with a challenge.

“What do you know about money and how much money have you had to solve your own problems?”

Then came my interpretation, straight to the point: this reads like you're looking for somebody else's money to solve your problems.

“Good luck with that.”

That was the longer version.

The reason it irritated me wasn't that I think money is worthless. Money has uses. I know that. The irritation was the certainty: the way one little sentence dismisses every experience that doesn't fit into it.

I've had money and I've lost money. I've had problems on both sides. I don't need an eighteen-year-old's Tinder biography to explain that I simply failed to reach the proper financial altitude.

And I don't need to pretend that a line I find ridiculous is profound just because somebody put quotation marks around it.

Eventually I cut the reply down to a Jon Snow joke. One sentence. Much less labor.

But underneath the joke, the question remained.

What experience are you speaking from?

Because there's a difference between knowing what money can buy and knowing what money cannot fix.

And I have no interest in confusing the two.

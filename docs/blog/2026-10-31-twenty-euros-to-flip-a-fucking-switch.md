I have discovered a marvelous new luxury service in Bulgaria.

It's called **electricity**.

Not getting electricity. That was the old product.

The premium product is getting electricity *back* after you've paid for it.

And, for the low, low price of twenty fucking euros, you can apparently purchase the express edition of somebody flipping a switch.

Welcome to the future. The lights are optional.

<!-- truncate -->

## An Entirely Ordinary Fuck-Up

Here's the part I actually own.

I hadn't paid the electricity bill for three or four months.

I wasn't living at the property, I wasn't certain I'd stay, and I let the bills sit. Naturally, the power was eventually disconnected.

Fair enough. I owed the money. The company wasn't running a charitable organization on my behalf.

So I paid.

Monday. Outstanding balance, penalties, the whole miserable business. There were extra charges on top of the consumption. Fine. Transaction complete.

I was expecting electricity again.

A few hours, I thought.

Monday became Tuesday.

Tuesday became Wednesday.

Wednesday became Thursday.

And by Friday, **still no fucking electricity**.

Apparently paying what I owed was merely the opening ceremony.

## The Reconnection Experience™

Now I have to go tell them that I'd like the service I paid for to exist again.

And there is a separate fee for that.

Oh, but there's a choice! They have thoughtfully developed a pricing structure around the astonishingly difficult act of restoring supply.

You can pay less and wait, or pay for express reconnection.

**Twenty euros.**

I remember reconnection costing about four euros before. Now the express option is five times that amount.

Perhaps somebody has to walk through a corridor. Perhaps a highly qualified technician must approach a sacred control panel. Perhaps there's a switch somewhere that can only be touched by a person wearing a ceremonial hat.

I don't know what physical work this particular reconnection requires. It may be remote for all I know.

What I do know is that I've already paid, four days have passed, and I'm now being offered the privilege of paying extra to restore a basic utility quickly.

The invoice is beginning to resemble an airline booking.

Electricity: included.

Actually supplying electricity: add-on.

Restoring electricity after payment: service charge.

Restoring it without another half-day of waiting: premium boarding.

Would sir like additional legroom with his refrigerator?

## The Old Man at the Counter

And then I remember an older man I saw when I was there paying an electricity bill on another occasion.

His bill was somewhere around **€400**, as I recall.

His pension? Something like **€300**.

The figures are approximate. The arithmetic is not.

A man standing at a counter with a basic utility bill greater than the income he gets for the month.

What does he do with that?

Does he eat?

Does he heat his home?

Does he take the bill home and politely explain to the refrigerator that this month the refrigerator has been financially irresponsible?

I stood there thinking, holy shit.

For me, the reconnection charge is infuriating. I can pay the fucking thing. I can swear about it, write this ridiculous account, and eventually turn the lights on.

For someone whose entire pension doesn't cover the bill, the problem isn't whether to choose regular or express service.

The problem is that the numbers don't work.

And the meter has no opinion on human suffering.

## A Sandwich and a Switch

That's the obscenity of it.

I failed to pay on time. I accept the penalty for that.

But once I've paid, why does getting the electricity back have to become another little marketplace? Why does a customer who's already waited four days face an additional price for urgency?

Perhaps the system has costs I don't see. I haven't audited their operations.

I'm describing what it feels like on this side of the counter: an invoice, followed by a penalty, followed by a reconnection fee, followed by the opportunity to spend even more money to avoid further waiting.

All for a necessity that people can't simply decide to live without.

And somewhere in that arrangement is an old man with a pension smaller than his electricity bill.

I keep coming back to the twenty euros.

Twenty fucking euros.

About the price of a sandwich and a drink in the wrong place.

Somewhere, somehow, a switch gets flipped.

**Would you like that switch flipped today, sir, or would you prefer to purchase the Economy Darkness package?**

*Lucifer, currently awaiting illumination.*

I complain about people.

This should surprise absolutely nobody.

People don't talk anymore. People sit three feet away from each other staring at phones. People perform concern while giving you advice you never fucking asked for.

And then sometimes somebody just shows up.

The funny part is that sometimes you have to ask first.

<!-- truncate -->

There is a bar here that I spend enough time in that I have somehow become Uncle Ben.

The guy who actually runs the place is younger than me. Young Buck.

His father is around too.

For a while I thought his father was much older than me.

Apparently everybody thinks he's much older than me.

He isn't.

He's maybe a few years older.

Which makes referring to him as the old man considerably funnier.

His son calls me Uncle Ben while his father and I are basically the same fucking age.

Excellent.

## I Need a Doctor

I have some things I need to talk to a doctor about.

Blood pressure.

Sleep.

Nothing requiring an expedition to Sofia if I can avoid one.

So I asked the father:

"Do you know a doctor around here?"

That's it.

Not:

Please evaluate my life.

Not:

Tell me what you think I should do.

Not:

Give me your theory of medicine, alcohol, sleep, stress, blood pressure, mortality, or the human condition.

Do you know a doctor?

"Yeah. I know a guy. He's good."

It was late, so he said he'd call him the next day.

The next day I showed up for lunch.

I had some gin, ate some food, went home, took a nap because apparently I am now an old man too, then came back.

I saw him.

"Hey, can you call that guy?"

"Yeah. No fucking problem."

And he did.

He called the doctor.

He explained what was going on.

He tried to arrange for me to see him locally.

The doctor was busy, so the appointment didn't magically materialize that second.

That isn't the point.

The point is that the man did exactly what he said he would do.

I asked.

He helped.

No lecture attached.

## I Need Water and Electricity

Then I was talking to Young Buck.

I have work I need done.

Water.

Electrical.

Various house shit that requires somebody who knows what they're doing rather than me standing there with a screwdriver saying, "Well, let's see what happens."

So I asked him if he knew somebody.

"My neighbor."

Here's his number.

Done.

Again, nobody formed a committee.

Nobody explained what I should have done six months ago.

Nobody told me how they would have organized my house.

Nobody transformed a request for a phone number into an intervention.

I needed a guy.

He had a guy.

Here is the fucking number.

## Apparently This Is a Network

These things keep surprising me because I never set out to build any of this.

I didn't move here with a community-integration strategy.

I didn't identify key stakeholders in the village.

I didn't make a fucking networking spreadsheet.

I talked to people.

That's it.

I went to the same places.

I talked to the same assholes.

I remembered who they were.

They remembered who I was.

I gave the father a bottle of good whisky once.

Not because I expected anything back.

I offered him my motorcycle.

The motorcycle doesn't fucking work, which admittedly reduces the grandeur of the gesture, but still.

We discovered that we think similarly about a lot of things.

Money.

Cars.

Possessions.

Whatever.

Sometimes you have them.

Sometimes you don't.

Who gives a shit?

Then one day I need a doctor and he knows one.

I need somebody for water and electrical and his son knows one.

And suddenly I look around and realize:

Oh.

I have a network.

I cultivated it without realizing I was cultivating it.

## Community Without the Bullshit

Maybe this is the version of community I actually understand.

Nobody needs to constantly check on me.

Nobody needs to perform concern.

Nobody needs to tell me how to live.

Mind your fucking business.

I'll mind mine.

But if I ask you for something and you can help?

Help.

And when you ask me for something and I can help?

I'll help.

There is something wonderfully uncomplicated about that.

It isn't emotional distance.

It isn't that nobody gives a shit.

The father called the doctor for me.

His son handed me the number of somebody who could help with the house.

Those are actions.

They just didn't come wrapped in instructions.

And maybe that's why I notice them.

I've had enough people in my life confuse caring with directing.

"You should do this."

"You need to do that."

"Why don't you..."

Nobody fucking asked you.

These guys don't do that.

At least not with me.

I ask for a doctor.

I get a doctor.

I ask for a tradesman.

I get a number.

Then we go back to drinking whisky, smoking cigars, watching football I don't give a shit about, and making fun of each other.

Apparently sometimes people do show up.

Sometimes you just have to fucking ask.

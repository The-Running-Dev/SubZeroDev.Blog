# Look at All This Shit

I don't usually buy something and immediately think:

> Why the fuck did I buy this?

That's not how it happens.

That's the problem.

Everything makes sense individually.

<!-- truncate -->

I went to the electrical hardware store because the dog had chewed through a cable.

I needed cable.

I needed plugs.

I needed electrical supplies.

All legitimate.

Then I discovered that apparently you can buy a portable blower for about €30.

Interesting.

I didn't buy it.

Mostly because, naturally, it uses a completely different battery system from the batteries I already own.

Because every power-tool manufacturer has collectively agreed that electricity should come in proprietary plastic rectangles.

Fine.

Then I saw a little LED keychain light.

I already own LED lights.

At least two.

Probably more.

But this one was tiny.

So now I own that too.

And at no point does any of this feel particularly insane.

That's the thing.

Each object has a defense attorney.

The screwdriver?

Useful.

The adapter?

Might need it.

The cable?

Obviously needed it.

The tiny light?

It's tiny.

The weird fitting?

Cost practically nothing.

The thing I didn't even know existed until six minutes ago?

Well, now that I know it exists, I can imagine several situations where it would be useful.

Perfectly reasonable.

Case dismissed.

Then months later I look around.

Shelves.

Boxes.

Tools.

Cables.

Adapters.

Lights.

Chargers.

Random hardware.

Pieces of equipment whose purpose I definitely understood when I bought them.

And suddenly:

**Oh shit.**

**Look at all this shit.**

There was never one moment where I decided to accumulate a mountain of stuff.

There was no shopping spree.

No dramatic purchase.

No truck arriving with seventeen boxes.

Just hundreds of tiny decisions that all survived individual review.

Yes.

Useful.

Keep.

Buy.

Maybe.

That's how the pile happens.

Not because any one thing is ridiculous.

Because almost none of it is ridiculous.

Every object entered the building with paperwork.

Every object had a reason.

And somehow the final result still looks like I am preparing to establish an independent hardware store.

The funniest part is that I know this.

I recognize the pattern.

I occasionally look around and think:

> I really need to stop accumulating shit.

Then something breaks.

So I go to the hardware store.

And while I'm there I discover they make a really tiny flashlight.

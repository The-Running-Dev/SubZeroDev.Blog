# Don't Expect Drunk People to Act Sober

The universe apparently decided I needed a practical exam.

I had spent the evening talking about anger, peace, control, expectations, and all the other shit Discount Store Marcus Aurelius sells near the damaged furniture.

Then I went out to buy gin, meat, cheese, and naturally more yogurt.

My neighbor was so drunk he could not get up the stairs.

His sister recruited me.

Of course she did.

<!-- truncate -->

So now I am helping carry a grown man to the second floor while his sister is yelling at him.

He can barely stand.

She is furious.

I tell her:

> There is no point yelling at him. He is not going to remember this. You are just doing this to yourself.

Apparently I am a therapist now.

Excellent.

We get him into bed.

She is still pissed.

Still talking at him.

Still trying to prosecute the case while the defendant is barely connected to the operating system.

And I am standing there thinking:

What exactly is this accomplishing?

## Then Life Smacked Me

I leave.

Get on the bicycle.

Ride home.

And while I am riding, I think about the Serbian woman I had been talking to.

She gets drunk.

She says things.

She does things.

Sometimes it pisses me off.

Then later she does not remember parts of it.

And suddenly I understand something that had previously been mostly theoretical.

Oh.

This is why somebody might not want to talk to you when you are drunk.

Hello, Martin.

Life has entered the chat.

## Not Exactly the Same

The realization did not make me adopt Martin's rule.

His boundary has been basically:

> Do not call me when you are drunk.

I understand the logic better now.

But that is not where I landed.

I am still talking to the Serbian woman.

She reached out.

We talked.

The fact that she was drunk did not suddenly make her unavailable as a human being.

What changed was my calibration.

That is the word.

## Calibration

If somebody is drunk, I cannot expect them to act not-drunk.

This sounds embarrassingly obvious when written down.

Yet humans do this constantly.

We know the state.

Then we demand behavior inconsistent with the state.

Drunk person:

Memory is worse.

Judgment is worse.

Emotional regulation is worse.

Consistency is worse.

Then we get angry because the drunk person is behaving like a drunk person.

Brilliant architecture.

The behavior can still matter.

If you do something that pisses me off, it still happened.

I am not deleting consequences.

I am not saying alcohol turns every action into a free trial with no billing.

I am saying the state is part of the event.

Context again.

That annoying motherfucker.

## What Can I Control?

This is where Stoicism becomes practical instead of decorative.

I cannot make her remember more.

I cannot make her regulate better in that moment.

I cannot make a drunk person produce sober judgment because I strongly prefer it.

I can calibrate **my** side.

What am I expecting from this conversation?

How much weight am I putting on what is said?

Do I need this conversation to produce a clean outcome?

Am I going to be shocked when tomorrow's memory is incomplete?

That part is mine.

Not her behavior.

Mine.

## I Can Still Talk to You

So my rule is not:

> If you are drunk, do not call me.

Mine is closer to:

> If you are drunk, I know who I am talking to.

Same person.

Different state.

Different expectations.

I can still listen.

I can still laugh.

I can still talk about philosophy.

I can still tell you when something annoys me.

I can still care about you.

I just should not demand sober outputs from an intoxicated system.

And yes, I am aware I just described a human being like a degraded production service.

Occupational hazard.

## The Sister Was Angry Too

The neighbor's sister made the whole thing even more obvious.

Her anger was understandable.

She knows how he gets.

She has probably done this before.

Maybe many times.

That does not make yelling useful in that exact moment.

The man was already drunk.

Already upstairs.

Already barely functional.

Reality had already happened.

Her anger was not going to travel backward in time and uninstall the alcohol.

So the practical question was not:

> Does she have a right to be angry?

Probably.

The practical question was:

> What is the anger doing right now?

That question followed me home.

## Final Exam

The universe has a sense of humor.

Spend an evening discussing:

- anger;
- peace;
- expectations;
- what is under the emotion;
- what you can control;
- accepting reality without approving of it.

Then immediately receive:

- one drunk neighbor;
- one furious sister;
- one drunk Serbian woman;
- one memory problem;
- one brother's boundary suddenly making more sense.

Very subtle.

Thanks, life.

I got the lesson.

I cannot expect drunk people to act sober.

I also do not have to stop talking to them.

I can change my own calibration.

Apparently that was the exam.

I think I passed.

Probably.

Ask me when everyone is sober.

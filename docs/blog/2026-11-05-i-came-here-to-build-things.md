I wrote at least a dozen Chocolatey packages. I contributed, I made an impact, and eventually I got tired of dealing with assholes. One particular guy still comes to mind when I think about why.

<!-- truncate -->

We were working on a public open source project. He used PowerShell aliases and shortcuts, and the rest of us kept asking him to stop. We didn't know what he had set up or what his shorthand meant. Other people had to read and work with this stuff.

Everyone kept telling him. He wouldn't listen.

That is the sort of thing that makes me say, fuck that guy. His preference apparently mattered more than a repeated request from the people working alongside him. I wanted to contribute to the project. I also wanted to be able to understand the fucking code.

I've led teams. I've listened to opposing opinions. Sometimes I went ahead with a decision despite those objections. Sometimes I later went back and admitted I had been mistaken. We made a decision, worked with it, and could change it when things changed. In those workplaces, nobody called me an idiot or something worse because they disagreed with my technical judgment.

I've also watched people in open source discussions treat each other in ways I have no interest in participating in. Some of that is stuff I've read online. Some of it has affected me directly. The Chocolatey example is one of my own experiences; it isn't the complete inventory.

There was also the Reddit experience. I asked why my project had been treated as though it wasn't open source and got no explanation. My guess was that I hadn't included an open source licence. That is still a guess, because I asked and got nothing. Later, after another complaint in the same community, there was a three-day restriction. I told them fuck you. That escalated to a permanent ban.

I own having said that. I'm still annoyed that a straightforward question never got an answer.

Open source is good. It offers alternatives. I want to build things that are open source. What I object to is the behaviour of people who turn it into a reason to act superior to everybody else. I watch how people behave, I notice patterns, and I form an opinion. I don't have access to their thoughts. I've never claimed I do.

And yes, calling people fucking bitches is an expression of contempt. I know what I'm saying. I'm angry about the behaviour I'm describing. I'm not pretending that sentence is a neutral technical assessment.

I can appreciate Linux and Git and still object to how Linus Torvalds has treated people. Those achievements are great. They don't exempt anybody from ordinary accountability. We're all human. We make mistakes. We can own them and move on.

What gets me is watching people condemn arrogance elsewhere, including in Microsoft, and then behave as though their own community membership puts them above criticism. I have a huge problem with that attitude. I'm another human fucking things up. I don't think publishing code gives me a higher rank in humanity.

I asked AI to challenge what I was saying. I meant it. Being mistaken gives me something to learn. Instead, part of the argument became a defence of open source's practical benefits, which I hadn't disputed. Then I was explaining again that my criticism concerned the people and their conduct. Somehow I had acquired another argument just trying to describe the first one.

I want to collaborate. I'm an excellent team player. I want to help and do things with people. When someone repeatedly makes that difficult, ignores the rest of the team, and behaves as though their opinion is the only one that matters, I eventually say, go suck a bag of dicks, buddy. Do it by yourself.

And then I catch myself getting angry about the whole fucking thing again. Why am I still bothering with this argument?

I came here to build things. Now I'm irritated about a guy who wouldn't stop using PowerShell aliases. Fuck me. This is stupid.

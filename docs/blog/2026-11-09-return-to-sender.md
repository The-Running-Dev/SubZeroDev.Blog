# Return to Sender

There was an unopened letter.

The letter remained unopened. The attempts at reconciliation reached an end. For all the weight a family can put on words, sometimes the story comes down to words that never get read.

For a relationship full of things that could be said, that is a stark enough picture.

<!-- truncate -->

There comes a point when the next attempt starts to look very much like the previous attempts, with a new opportunity to be hurt attached.

I had reached the end of trying to reconcile.

That does not turn the relationship into something simple. It does not remove my mother from my thoughts or install a convenient little switch that lets me stop feeling anything about her.

I feel sorry for her.

She has three sons, and I can imagine her dying alone.

That possibility is bitter. It is also a possibility, not a report from the future. I do not know how her life will end. I do not get to speak for all three sons, and I am not issuing a collective family verdict.

I am describing something I can imagine, and the sorrow I feel when I imagine it.

There is no pleasure in that image for me.

It would be easier to write a revenge ending. A mother reaches the end of her life, the consequences arrive, and the estranged son gets a final satisfying moment of being right.

What a miserable fucking ambition.

I don't want her loneliness as my reward.

But feeling sorry for her does not tell me what I am required to do next.

That is where the difficult part lives. Compassion can feel like an instruction. If I can see the sadness in somebody's situation, surely I must return and try again. Surely the fact that it hurts to imagine means I have volunteered to prevent it.

I don't think that follows.

I can care about what happens to my mother without accepting that the outcome of her life is mine to manage.

I can recognize something painful without reopening the relationship every time that recognition returns.

Otherwise sorrow becomes a door that I am never allowed to close.

And the attempts never really end. They merely wait for the next moment when I feel sufficiently bad about ending them.

The unopened letter belongs in this story because it resists all the speeches I could write around it. It does not give me access to another person's private thoughts. It does not establish what every silence means.

I am not going to appoint myself the narrator of motives I cannot know.

What I can speak about is the end of my attempts, and the feelings that did not end with them.

Relief and sorrow do not have to cancel each other out. Neither does compassion have to reverse a boundary before it counts as real.

I can feel sorry for my mother.

I can also be finished trying to reconcile.

Those sentences can sit beside each other without one being exposed as a lie.

That is less tidy than a revenge story. There is no satisfying punishment, no proof that everyone got what they deserved, no final family scene that resolves the whole fucking thing.

There is a relationship I have stopped trying to repair.

There is an unopened letter.

There is the possibility of a lonely ending that I cannot know and do not want to celebrate.

And there is my own life, which I still have to live.

Feeling sorry for her does not require me to hand that life back.

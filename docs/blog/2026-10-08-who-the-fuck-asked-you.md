# Who the Fuck Asked You?

I've noticed something about the way I respond when people ask me for advice.

I almost aggressively refuse to give it.

They ask, "What should I do?"

I ask, "Why do you think you need to do anything?"

This becomes particularly funny when somebody who keeps prescribing changes to *my* life phones me to ask about his.

<!-- truncate -->

## A Boundary Is a Boundary

My brother had expressed concern about my drinking and said he didn't want me calling when I was drunk. His stated reason was that I sometimes didn't remember things we'd talked about afterward.

Fine.

He gets to decide when he wants to participate in a conversation.

I get to decide whether I call.

My solution was simple: I stopped calling him.

I wasn't arranging my days around finding a brother-approved telephone window.

Later, he managed to reach me another way despite one messaging channel being blocked.

We spoke anyway.

But that didn't mean I'd agreed to resume calling him.

He can call if he wants to. I can call him. I choose not to.

Notice how none of those sentences requires pretending I *can't*.

## Then He Asked for Advice

He called about some situation that had already happened.

I said:

"It's already in the past. Why worry about it?"

This was apparently not the sort of consulting service he'd expected.

Then came a discussion about his wife encouraging him to talk to more people, to be more social. They were working in real estate, and social contact had become a topic.

I told him I talk to people everywhere now, but I never set out on a project to *become social*.

Don't force it. When it happens, it happens.

He was asking me how to change something about himself.

I was asking why the starting assumption was that he needed fixing.

## You've Never Had Money

He also kept saying he needed a certain amount of money before he could come to Bulgaria.

I've heard versions of that sentence for years.

So I told him that for as long as I'd known him, he'd never had money.

Not a forensic claim about the contents of his accounts.

An observation about what he kept saying.

He began explaining savings, income, expenses, obligations—a balance sheet.

I stopped him.

I didn't ask for his finances.

If you have the means but won't spend them on a particular trip, that's a choice. It can be a completely reasonable choice. But it's different from not possessing the means at all.

Same for me and the telephone.

I can call.

I don't want to.

Why make it more complicated than it is?

## The Bulgarian Documents Department

Then came Bulgarian documents.

I'd tried to help with the process. It was complicated, and I'd reached the point where I wasn't doing it for him anymore.

Appointment in D.C. or New York? Travel? Cost?

Those are steps in the process if he decides the documents are worth pursuing.

And one physical requirement would remain even if I magically completed every administrative step from Bulgaria:

**He personally needs to appear somewhere for biometrics.**

I cannot outsource Martin's body.

Well, presumably not legally.

## My Blog and a Dictator

The next day I sent him my blog.

He responded with a podcast about Tito.

So I said:

"I send you my blog, and you respond with a dictator. Makes sense."

He was confused.

I was laughing.

## Knowing Isn't Living

When we spoke I'd told him I don't go by Boyan anymore because I'm not the person I used to be.

He immediately responded that we're not the same people we were five minutes ago.

Exactly.

He knows impermanence intellectually.

I don't think he's integrated all of what that means for the endless project of trying to modify yourself into the person you think you're supposed to be.

To me, you're already whole. That doesn't mean you'll remain unchanged. Change is inevitable.

You can choose what you do. You can choose what you don't do.

You can even ask me for advice.

But you might get the question back:

**Who the fuck said you needed to change in the first place?**

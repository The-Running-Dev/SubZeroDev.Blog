I understand your boundaries.

Really. I do.

You don't want to repeat yourself. You don't want to talk to someone who's had a few drinks. You don't want to spend an hour explaining something and discover the next morning that the other person doesn't remember it.

Fine.

**Now fuck your boundaries.**

<!-- truncate -->

Before somebody calls the Department of Emotional Regulation, allow me to explain the distinction.

You are absolutely entitled to decide what conversations you participate in. You can decline a call. You can say, "Not tonight." You can hang up. You can choose who you spend your time with.

What you cannot do is present your preferences as a set of operating instructions for my life and expect me to salute.

Actually, you *can*. People do it all the fucking time.

And I can say no.

## Terms and Conditions Apply

A couple of my brothers and an old friend and mentor have variations of the same complaint.

Sometimes I call late. Sometimes I've been drinking. Sometimes I'm high. Sometimes we talk about things and I forget pieces of the conversation.

I don't deny any of that.

I live in Europe. They're in the States. The time difference means late for me can be the window when I can actually catch them.

Sometimes I'm fine. Sometimes I'm having a shitty night. Sometimes the alcohol makes me more emotional than I would otherwise be.

Once I cried to a friend. Really cried. He listened. It wasn't exactly a highlight reel of human dignity, but that's what happened.

Now, I understand why someone might not want to repeat a conversation. I understand why they might decide not to answer a call under certain conditions.

What I don't understand is how we arrived at the point where **twenty or thirty years of friendship apparently comes with a new terms-of-service agreement.**

Agree to modify yourself, or the service will be discontinued.

Motherfucker, I did not know this was a subscription.

## The Man Who Repeats Himself

One of the people who objects to me forgetting things has repeated the same stories to me so many times I could probably recite them in his voice.

And you know what I did?

I listened.

Again.

Not because I have achieved sainthood. Not because I enjoy hearing the same shit for the thousandth time.

Because he's my friend. Because people have habits. Because none of us are perfectly optimized conversational appliances.

Apparently, repetition is acceptable in one direction but unacceptable in the other.

That is the part that makes me laugh.

I will sit through your favorite story again, but if I forget one of yours, we need to renegotiate the friendship?

Excellent. I'll have my legal department review the contract.

## All I Wanted Was a Conversation

This is the part underneath the profanity.

These people are thousands of kilometers away. I'm not asking them to come fix my plumbing. I'm not asking for money, a room to sleep in, or help moving furniture.

I wanted to talk.

Sometimes about nothing. Sometimes about the day's bullshit. Sometimes because I felt terrible and wanted to hear a familiar voice.

And I wasn't asking them to make me happy. I wasn't asking them to agree. I wanted an actual conversation, with the possibility that I might not be at my most charming.

That's all.

I know perfectly well that they can decline. I know that a difficult conversation can be exhausting for the person on the other end. I know that being intoxicated changes how a conversation lands.

Those things don't magically disappear because I'm the one making the call.

But neither does the history. Neither do the years of tolerating each other's nonsense. Neither do the hundreds of conversations that *weren't* difficult.

When the remaining offer is "We can be friends as long as you meet my conditions," I am allowed to decide that what remains isn't a friendship I want.

## No Hostages

I'm not going to force anybody to take my calls.

I'm also not going to accept a permanent audition for the privilege of talking to people I've known for decades.

There's a difference between understanding a boundary and volunteering to live inside it.

They can choose not to participate.

I can choose not to keep asking.

We don't need a judge, a jury, or some motivational poster announcing which of us has achieved healthy interpersonal communication.

Maybe we'll never agree about what a friendship ought to accommodate.

Fine.

But if all I have left of a thirty-year relationship is somebody informing me which version of myself is permitted to speak to them, I'm not interested.

I understand your boundaries.

You're welcome to keep them.

**I'll keep my phone number.**

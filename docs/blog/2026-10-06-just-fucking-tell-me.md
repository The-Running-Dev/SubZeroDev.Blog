# Just Fucking Tell Me

I do not understand why adults ghost each other.

I understand rejection.

I understand changing your mind.

I understand losing interest.

I understand getting busy.

I understand realizing somebody is not for you.

All fine.

<!-- truncate -->

What I do not understand is why the preferred communication protocol so often becomes:

**nothing.**

## Simone

There was a woman named Simone.

We had gone out before.

Months later she found me again.

We went out again.

At some point I realized I was not interested in continuing.

I do not even remember the exact reason anymore.

Apparently it was important enough at the time.

So I told her.

Something like:

> “Hey, I've thought about it and I don't think I want to continue this.”

That was it.

No dissertation.

No cruelty.

No pretending.

No mysterious disappearance into the fog.

The interaction had an ending.

Adults are allowed to do that.

## The Alternative

Compare that to:

Conversation.

Interest.

Plans.

Then silence.

So you wait.

Maybe they are busy.

You send something later.

Nothing.

You ask:

> “Did something change?”

Nothing.

Now what?

You know the interaction changed.

You do not know why.

You are left with a pile of mutually incompatible explanations.

Maybe I caused it.

Maybe I did not.

Maybe she lost interest.

Maybe something happened in her life.

Maybe she met somebody else.

Maybe she is overwhelmed.

Maybe she simply does not want to deal with the conversation.

All possible.

None knowable.

## The One-Sentence Solution

This entire ambiguity can be removed with one sentence.

> “I don't think we're a match.”

Done.

Or:

> “I don't want to continue this.”

Done.

Or:

> “I've got too much going on right now.”

Done.

Or:

> “That thing you said put me off.”

Also done.

I may not like the answer.

That is irrelevant.

At least there is an answer.

## Rejection Is Not the Problem

This is the part people seem to overcomplicate.

Telling somebody you are not interested is not an act of violence.

You are not obligated to produce a defense brief.

You are not required to prove they did something wrong.

You do not need a legally sufficient cause.

You can simply not want to continue.

That is enough.

I can deal with that.

What I cannot meaningfully deal with is silence pretending to be communication.

## The False Mercy of Ghosting

Maybe ghosting feels easier.

No awkward conversation.

No possibility of argument.

No uncomfortable emotional reaction.

Fine.

It may be easier for the person doing it.

But the cost is transferred.

The other person now has to interpret the absence.

They get to wonder.

They get to reconstruct.

They get to decide when enough silence counts as an answer.

That is not mercy.

That is outsourcing.

## We Are Not Teenagers

Maybe this is the part that makes me sound old.

Fine.

I am old.

But if we have been talking, meeting, making plans, or actively getting to know each other, I do not think basic closure is some extraordinary emotional achievement.

You do not need to hold my hand.

You do not need to manage my reaction.

You do not need to explain your childhood.

Just tell me.

If I react badly to a clear and respectful rejection, then I have created a different problem.

But do not preemptively solve that hypothetical problem by becoming a missing person.

## The Respect Is in the Acknowledgment

The useful part of closure is not the reason.

Sometimes the reason is messy.

Sometimes the person does not even fully understand it.

That is okay.

The useful part is acknowledging:

> “There was a conversation here, and I am ending my side of it.”

That is all.

I have done it.

People have done it to me.

It is survivable.

So if you do not want to talk to me anymore:

fine.

If you do not want another date:

fine.

If I said something that killed the attraction:

fine.

If you simply woke up and thought:

> “Actually, no.”

Also fine.

Just fucking tell me.

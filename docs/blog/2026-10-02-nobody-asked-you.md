There is a phrase I seem to use a lot lately.

**Nobody asked you.**

It sounds hostile.

Sometimes it probably is.

But there is an actual distinction underneath it that people keep fucking up.

Helping somebody and directing somebody are not the same thing.

<!-- truncate -->

I have people in my life who apparently believe that caring gives them a temporary management position.

You mention a problem.

Immediately:

"You should..."

"You need to..."

"Why don't you..."

"I think what you have to do is..."

No.

Stop.

I did not submit a request for architectural review of my existence.

Sometimes I was just telling you something.

Sometimes I needed one specific thing.

Sometimes I wanted you to listen.

And sometimes I didn't want a goddamn thing from you at all.

## A Doctor

Recently I needed a doctor.

So I asked a guy I know locally:

"Do you know a doctor?"

He did.

He called him.

That was the interaction.

He did not diagnose me.

He did not explain what I should be doing with my life.

He did not use the opportunity to demonstrate how concerned he was.

He knew a doctor.

I asked for the doctor.

He called the fucking doctor.

This should not feel revolutionary.

And yet.

## A Guy for the House

Same day, different person.

I needed somebody who could handle water and electrical work.

"Do you know somebody?"

"My neighbor."

Phone number.

Done.

Again, no lifestyle consultation.

No lecture about how I should have arranged the house.

No retrospective analysis of every decision that resulted in me needing plumbing.

I asked for a guy.

He gave me a guy.

Beautiful.

## Mind Your Own Fucking Business

There is a pattern I've noticed among some people I get along with, particularly people around my generation.

Mind your own fucking business.

That doesn't mean:

I don't care about you.

It doesn't mean:

You're on your own.

It means your life remains yours until you invite me into some part of it.

If you ask for help and I can help, absolutely.

If you ask what I think, buckle the fuck up.

But if you didn't ask?

Maybe I don't need to appoint myself.

There is a kind of respect in leaving another adult in possession of their own decisions.

And there is a kind of disrespect that can hide remarkably well inside "I'm only trying to help."

## Showing Up Is Different

This is where people sometimes confuse the issue.

If I don't tell you what to do, does that mean I don't care?

No.

If I don't intervene in your decisions, does that mean I won't show up?

Also no.

Those are entirely different axes.

You can give endless advice and never actually show up.

You can say almost nothing and be standing next to somebody the moment they ask.

I've experienced both.

I'll take the second one.

The local guy didn't give me a speech about my health.

He picked up the phone.

The other guy didn't explain home ownership to me.

He gave me the number.

Action without annexation.

I like that.

## Ask Me

And this goes both ways.

Ask me.

If you need something and I can do it, ask.

If you want my opinion, ask.

If you want me to listen, tell me.

If you want me to leave it the fuck alone, excellent. I am highly qualified.

What I don't understand is the assumption that caring requires unsolicited governance.

Maybe I trust you to run your own life.

Maybe you trust me to run mine.

Maybe when one of us actually needs the other, we say so.

And then we show up.

No performance.

No management.

No fucking PowerPoint presentation about your choices.

Just:

"What do you need?"

That, to me, is helping.

Everything else might still be useful.

But first:

**Nobody asked you.**

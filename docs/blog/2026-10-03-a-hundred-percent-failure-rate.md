# A Hundred Percent Failure Rate

Somebody at the cocktail lounge said something to me recently.

Something like:

> "What is it with all these different women that come here with you?"

Excuse me?

Apparently there has been monitoring.

I wasn't keeping count.

**Somebody fucking was.**

<!-- truncate -->

From their point of view, I suppose this creates an interesting picture.

Ben arrives.

Different woman.

Drinks.

Conversation.

Another week.

Different woman.

Drinks.

Conversation.

External observer concludes:

> This motherfucker dates a lot.

Meanwhile, inside my own head:

> I have approximately a hundred percent failure rate.

Both statements can be true.

That is the part I find interesting.

## A First Date Does Not Count

To me, going on a first date with somebody and sitting there talking is not fundamentally different from talking to the local drunks at the liquor shop.

I mean that affectionately.

You meet a human being.

You sit somewhere.

You exchange information.

Maybe they're funny.

Maybe they're interesting.

Maybe they're insane.

Maybe **you're** insane.

Maybe all four.

You talk.

That's it.

A conversation happened.

I like conversations.

I talk to random people all the fucking time.

So the fact that I sat across from a woman for two hours because Tinder arranged the introduction does not automatically transform the event into something meaningful.

Tinder supplied routing.

Humanity supplied packets.

Nothing has necessarily connected yet.

## My Metric Is Continuity

What counts to me is what happens after.

Did we want to see each other again?

Did the conversation continue?

Did something deepen?

Did we become curious about each other beyond that first encounter?

Did the person move from:

> Interesting stranger.

to:

> Person I actually want in my life in some form.

That's the metric.

And by that metric?

The dashboard is fucking brutal.

First date.

Nothing.

Interesting conversation.

Nothing.

Promising connection.

Nothing.

Second conversation maybe.

Then nothing.

A person can be genuinely interesting to me and still disappear out of the system immediately afterward.

No error log.

No stack trace.

No telemetry.

Just:

**Connection closed by remote host.**

## The Cocktail Lounge Has Different Analytics

This is why I laughed when somebody commented on all the women.

The bar has a completely different analytics model.

Their data says:

**Dates observed:** several.

My data says:

**Connections developed:** fuck all.

They see throughput.

I care about retention.

Apparently my dating life is a SaaS company with excellent acquisition and catastrophic churn.

Marketing is thrilled.

Operations would like a meeting.

## "You're Doing Fine"

This is where people can talk past each other without realizing it.

One person sees somebody meeting dates and says:

> "You're doing fine."

Maybe according to their metric.

If the goal is:

> Meet women.

Sure.

If the goal is:

> Have conversations.

Absolutely.

If the goal is:

> Occasionally sit in a cocktail lounge with somebody new while the regulars quietly update their internal spreadsheet.

Outstanding performance.

But that is not what I mean when I talk about connection.

I'm not complaining that I never encounter people.

I do.

Sometimes I meet somebody and think:

> Oh. You're interesting.

Sometimes there is chemistry.

Sometimes there is immediate conversational energy.

Sometimes I walk away genuinely curious.

And then:

Nothing.

That is the part I don't understand.

## I Thought I Was Letting It Flow

I've been trying to figure out this whole "stop trying" thing.

Which is already ridiculous.

Because if you're deliberately trying to stop trying in order to achieve a result, congratulations:

You're still fucking trying.

So I thought I was doing the sensible version.

Participate.

Go places.

Talk to people.

Use Tinder without treating Tinder like a mission.

Be ridiculous.

Have conversations.

Go to international events when I feel like it.

Ask somebody for coffee if it seems natural.

Leave doors open.

Don't turn every interaction into:

> Maybe this is the one.

Fine.

That sounds sane.

I thought that was basically what I was doing.

And yet even when I meet somebody who seems genuinely interesting, the thread often just ends.

So now I'm left with a different question.

Not:

> How do I meet people?

Clearly I meet people.

Not:

> How do I get a first date?

Apparently the cocktail lounge has documented evidence that this occurs.

The question is:

> What the fuck happens between "interesting" and "continuity"?

I have no answer.

## No Explanation Required

And the especially useless part is that most of the time there is no explanation.

Maybe timing.

Maybe no chemistry on their side.

Maybe too intense.

Too weird.

Too direct.

Too much.

Too little.

Maybe somebody met somebody else.

Maybe life happened.

Maybe Tinder opened and presented another fifty humans before breakfast.

Maybe they simply didn't feel like continuing.

All completely possible.

But from my side the observable result is identical:

> Interesting interaction.

Then:

> Nothing.

I can invent explanations forever.

That does not make them true.

So I don't.

## Failure Is a Metric, Not a Verdict

This is probably worth separating.

When I say:

> "Hundred percent failure rate."

I am not saying:

> "I am a failure."

Those are completely different statements.

I'm describing the outcome against the thing I actually care about.

The first meeting is not the objective.

The objective is connection.

If ten first meetings produce zero continuing connections, then by my definition the conversion rate is zero.

That's arithmetic.

Not tragedy.

Not a statement about my worth.

Not a statement about anybody else's worth.

Just a hilariously bad KPI.

## Meanwhile, at the Bar

The regulars see me walk in.

Maybe alone.

Maybe with somebody.

They notice.

Apparently.

I don't.

I sit down.

Order a drink.

Talk shit.

Tell somebody they're a turtle.

Discuss cars.

Dogs.

Relationships.

Whatever.

Then somebody casually reveals that the establishment has been tracking my female guest count.

I stare at them.

> "I wasn't keeping count."

Exactly.

Because I wasn't counting dates.

I was waiting to see whether any of them became something I would actually count.

So far?

The cocktail lounge thinks I'm busy.

My dashboard says:

**100% failure rate.**

Both systems are functioning correctly.

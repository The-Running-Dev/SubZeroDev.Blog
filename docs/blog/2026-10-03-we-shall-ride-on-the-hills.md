# We Shall Ride on the Hills

The mechanic asked how I was getting home.

Van Diesel was staying at the shop, so this was a reasonable question.

Then he asked:

> "Do you need me to drop you off?"

I looked at him.

Oh.

I did not know that was an option.

No.

I had an e-bike in the back.

This seemed like a perfectly good answer at the time.

It remained a perfectly good answer for the next twenty kilometers.

It was just a much more eventful answer than getting into somebody's car.

<!-- truncate -->

## The Correct Tool for the Wrong Road

This bike is built for exactly the kind of roads Bulgaria has forgotten about.

Fat tires.

Suspension.

Enough motor to be ridiculous.

Enough battery to make distance negotiable.

On the way back I discovered that the old road toward the highway is still open for bicycles.

You cannot realistically get a car through it anymore.

Too much gravel.

Too many holes.

Too much whatever-the-fuck-happened-here.

For the e-bike?

Perfect.

Absolutely fucking perfect.

The road had effectively become bicycle infrastructure through neglect.

Cars get the new road.

I get the abandoned one.

Fair trade.

## Aerodynamics

On decent stretches I was doing 45 to 50 kilometers an hour.

Sometimes in the little towns.

Sometimes on open road.

Sometimes passing cars that were moving slowly over rough sections.

And because apparently I am incapable of simply sitting upright like a normal human being, I would put my head down toward the handlebars for aerodynamics.

Downhill:

**chin down.**

Flat:

**chin down.**

Uphill:

**chin down, pedal, help the motor, we are doing this together.**

I am not under the illusion that I became a Tour de France rider because I leaned forward on a fat-tire electric bicycle.

But if the machine is going to be absurd, I may as well participate correctly.

## Contact

Then I got hit by a bee.

Not metaphorically.

A bee hit me in the face and somehow got inside my glasses.

This is not where a bee should be.

It is particularly not where a bee should be while I am moving at e-bike speeds.

The bee bounced around in there several times while I attempted to slow down without introducing myself to Bulgarian asphalt.

So now I am braking, trying not to crash, trying to get the glasses off, and negotiating with an insect that has accidentally entered the cockpit.

Eventually:

Bee out.

Glasses restored.

Pilot still alive.

Mission continues.

Then I got hit by two more bugs directly in the face.

At that point the local air force had clearly identified me as a target.

## The Cars

Cars would come up behind me, accelerate, and pass.

Sometimes hard.

And I'm thinking:

Motherfucker, I am not competing with you.

This is a bicycle.

You have an automobile.

Congratulations.

You have defeated me.

Several of them gave me a huge amount of room, though.

Two car widths sometimes.

Beautiful.

Thank you.

Seriously.

Go as fast as you want.

Just don't turn me into roadside decoration.

The funny thing is that earlier, in Van Diesel, I had watched drivers perform the same ritual against the camper.

Vehicle ahead.

Must pass vehicle ahead.

Now I was the object ahead.

Apparently the rule scales down.

Camper.

Bicycle.

Probably wheelbarrow.

If something is in front of you, the Bulgarian road demands an answer.

## The Cyclists

Then I came onto one of the roads and saw a group of cyclists.

Actual cyclists.

Normal bicycles.

Normal posture.

Human beings apparently participating in a recognized activity.

They saw me coming the other way.

Fat tires.

Electric motor.

Folded down over the bars like I was qualifying at Monza.

They greeted me.

I gave them the peace sign.

Did not break aero.

Kept going.

That was the entire interaction.

Perfect.

Mutual recognition between people on two wheels.

No questions.

No explanations.

Just:

✌️

And continue the campaign.

## We Shall Never Surrender

By the time I got home, the entire trip from taking Van Diesel out, running out of diesel, getting fuel, reaching the mechanic, dropping it off, and riding roughly twenty kilometers back had taken about an hour and forty-five minutes.

Not bad.

The e-bike still had battery left.

I had discovered a road cars could barely use.

I had passed slow traffic on gravel.

I had tucked down for aerodynamics.

I had pedaled uphill to help the motor.

I had jumped speed bumps.

I had survived direct insect attack.

And somewhere in all of this, Churchill entered the chat.

> We shall ride on the hills.
>
> We shall ride on the roads.
>
> We shall jump the speed bumps.
>
> We shall never surrender.

This is not what he said.

It is what he would have said if he had owned a ridiculous fat-tire e-bike in western Bulgaria.

Probably.

And yes, the mechanic could have driven me home.

But then I would have missed the old road.

And the gravel.

And the cyclists.

And the bee inside my fucking glasses.

So perhaps civilization was overrated.

Sometimes you unfold the bike.

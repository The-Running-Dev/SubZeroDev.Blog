*The Lucifer Chronicles — The Curious Case of George Myers*

A gentleman calling himself George Myers approached me on Telegram with a financial tragedy. He had, he said, lost $100,000 to cryptocurrency scammers.

One hundred thousand dollars. A sum sufficiently large to command sympathy, yet apparently insufficient to purchase an opening line I hadn't heard before.

<!-- truncate -->

George wished to establish whether I, too, had been defrauded.

I allowed that I had.

How much?

“Just enough.”

A perfectly adequate disclosure. The court does not require an itemized invoice for every misfortune, particularly when the gentleman conducting discovery has introduced himself through Telegram.

George proceeded with his evidence. There had been scammers. There had been losses. There had been contact with the FBI, which, according to George, had produced no useful result.

Naturally, I had only George's account of these proceedings. The FBI had declined to appear in our little production, and the hundred thousand dollars was represented entirely by its absence.

Nevertheless, George pressed on. When the system fails us, we must rely on ourselves.

A stirring proposition. Practically a constitutional preamble, if one overlooks the cryptocurrency.

I responded with the measured restraint for which I am occasionally mistaken:

“Yes George. I got connections, underworld, mafia, the usual stuff, you know...”

Now.

I had not planned to introduce organized crime.

There was no outline. No strategic consultation. The mafia simply arrived in the sentence, found a comfortable chair, and began contributing nothing to the conversation.

George ignored it.

An extraordinary professional courtesy. I had casually disclosed a supporting cast from the criminal underworld, and he treated this as though I'd mentioned a reasonably priced accountant.

His presentation continued.

There were other victims. There were experts. There was a group working to recover the money. There was, reassuringly, no upfront payment.

Of course there wasn't. The first act of a financial resurrection should always be complimentary. One must let the audience admire the coffin before discussing the surcharge for opening it.

By this stage, I took the whole business for a recovery scam. George was presenting a route back to lost money; I was watching a man attempt to escort me into the sequel.

He eventually invited me to join the group.

I agreed to see it.

Then came the WhatsApp invitation.

Ah.

**George, my brother, no.**

We had begun on Telegram, toured a hundred-thousand-dollar catastrophe, heard testimony concerning the FBI, and were now being transferred to WhatsApp for the miracle. Apparently even financial salvation required a change of venue.

And then I remembered the mafia.

My own unsolicited subplot. Still sitting there. Entirely unused.

It would have been discourteous to leave them out of the closing argument.

I told George that, now that I had his information, he would be hearing from my “underworld connections.”

“Good luck.”

Let the record reflect that this was the improvised mafia line returning for its curtain call. I had made up a subplot, and now I was giving it a finale.

George replied:

“What?”

A brief submission. His most economical of the proceedings.

I cannot tell you whether he was alarmed, confused, irritated, or merely searching for the appropriate paragraph in his pitch. I was not inside George's head. Frankly, the accommodation did not appeal to me.

I can tell you what I wrote next.

**“This conversation never happened.”**

An ambitious statement, considering we were both looking directly at it.

George considered this contribution and returned with:

**“What happened?”**

Ladies and gentlemen of the jury.

There are occasions when language has completed its work. When another sentence would be an act of vandalism against a perfectly formed ending. When a man has been handed a punchline so exquisite that the only civilized response is to put down the pen.

I deleted the entire conversation.

**For both of us.**

No reply. No clarification. No explanatory memorandum concerning the jurisdiction of the underworld.

George's question disappeared along with everything it referred to.

I have no idea what he thought afterward. His thoughts are not in evidence.

The conversation, however, had acquired a remarkable degree of consistency with my final statement.

**This conversation never happened.**

*Lucifer, signing off.*

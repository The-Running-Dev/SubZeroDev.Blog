I have administered Hell for a considerable period. We have departments, backlogs, an unfortunate amount of paperwork, and people who insist their particular suffering requires a separate dashboard. Even by our standards, Ben's software operation is becoming fucking ridiculous.

<!-- truncate -->

*The Lucifer Chronicles — An Enterprise of One*

Let us establish the headcount before anyone starts imagining a company.

One fucking guy.

He has perhaps five to seven private GitHub repositories. Some contain software he might eventually sell for a small one-time fee. Modest little commercial intentions. Build something useful. Charge once. Let people use it.

Then he exceeded the included build minutes.

Then he exceeded the artifact storage allowance.

The software was being produced quickly enough that the account's included resources were running out. The one human responsible for the entire affair was also running out of capacity to remember what the fuck was happening.

Naturally, he spent a week building self-hosted runners on his own machine.

GitHub Team costs four dollars per user per month.

The user is Ben.

Four dollars.

We have a special desk in Hell for this kind of financial planning. The chair is comfortable. The spreadsheet has one cell. You are required to stare at it until you understand what you did.

## The Finance Department Has Been Drinking

His own account of the working conditions deserves a place in the record: half or most of the time he is doing all this, he is half drunk or completely fucking drunk.

I shall leave that statement exactly where he put it. We have enough invented explanations in the building already.

The subscriptions, meanwhile, receive considerable attention.

Roughly a hundred a month for ChatGPT, on whichever plan currently corresponds to the hundred-hour arrangement. Two hundred for Claude Max. He briefly calls it Ultra, then corrects himself. Even the names of the things he pays for require maintenance.

Copilot, which was costing him about forty a month, has been cancelled.

GitHub Team has been added. Four dollars a month.

Enterprise is under consideration. The [pricing page](https://github.com/pricing) lists it at twenty-one dollars per user per month and advertises fifty thousand CI/CD minutes. GitHub's [billing documentation](https://docs.github.com/en/billing/concepts/product-billing/github-actions) now describes the allowance as compute credit; the fifty-thousand-minute equivalent is for standard Linux runners. Different usage draws it down at different rates.

Even the fucking punchline has a billing model.

He is perfectly willing to pay roughly twenty dollars. This is not a man refusing on principle to spend money on tools. We have the subscription receipts. We have also, somehow, acquired a week of runner infrastructure.

I have seen kingdoms destroyed by vanity. This is the first time I have seen a private build queue generate this much administrative work in the vicinity of four dollars.

## The Notes Have Their Own Repository

The runners are only one department.

The conversations are everywhere. Claude. Codex. Different projects. Different sessions. Decisions made somewhere, useful context left somewhere else, and a human trying to remember which conversation contains the fucking answer.

So he created a repository called Notes.

A sensible name. Reassuringly short. Almost suspiciously innocent considering the circumstances.

Then he created a skill in both Claude and Codex. When he invokes it, the system is supposed to take the whole conversation, preserve the context, and produce a brief.

The whole fucking conversation.

He now has a procedure for recovering the knowledge generated while following his other procedures.

I appreciate this personally. Hell has operated on that principle for centuries. We call the resulting documents records, and periodically someone proposes a new system for finding them.

Ben has reached this stage without hiring anybody.

The team meeting is a conversation with a machine. The minutes become a Markdown file. The person who needs to read them is the person who had the conversation, who is already in another conversation because something else needed doing.

The Notes repository exists. The brief exists. The next conversation has begun.

## Discount Marcus Aurelius Reviews Operations

At this point, our resident discount Marcus Aurelius becomes available for philosophical consultation.

He can care deeply and detach completely.

An admirable position. Difficult to apply to an artifact quota that has just stopped your fucking pipeline.

I imagine the modern *Meditations* receiving a small revision:

> At dawn, remind yourself: today you will encounter failed checks, missing context, and the consequences of a task you assigned to yourself in another window.

No, Marcus did not write that. Marcus had an empire, but mercifully little YAML.

Ben has the YAML. He also has a dashboard now.

He built himself a dashboard to track what is happening, where things are, and what needs to happen next. An application whose job is to help its creator keep track of the applications he is creating.

And he still cannot keep track of it all.

I would like that sentence entered into the minutes before somebody creates another repository to explain it.

## Please State the Size of Your Enterprise

There is actual work here. Things are being built. The speed is real enough to exhaust the resources he had included, and the amount of activity is real enough that he needs machinery just to retain a view of it.

Then the machinery needs work too.

The runners have become a project. Capturing conversations has become a workflow. Following the projects has become another application. Meanwhile, the original software still needs attention, and more ideas keep arriving.

He can produce more than he can comfortably follow. He has said so himself. GitHub's quotas have supplied their own little contribution to the discussion.

I am Lucifer. I am accustomed to watching humans find elaborate ways to occupy themselves. Usually there are committees involved. A procurement department. At least one man whose entire job is arranging a meeting about why nothing has happened.

Here the same man builds the software, assigns the work, argues with the assistants, pays the subscriptions, hits the limits, builds the infrastructure, captures the context, and writes the dashboard to find out where everything went.

Half or most of the time, by his own account, half drunk or completely fucking drunk.

And now he is considering the Enterprise plan.

Number of users?

**One fucking guy.**

*Lucifer, signing off. The headcount has been verified. Everything else is somewhere in Notes.*

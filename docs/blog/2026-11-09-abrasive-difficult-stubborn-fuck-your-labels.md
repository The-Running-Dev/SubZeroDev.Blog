# Abrasive, Difficult, Stubborn — Fuck Your Labels

Abrasive. Difficult. Stubborn.

Such compact little words. You can fit an entire person inside one, provided you first remove everything inconvenient.

<!-- truncate -->

Context, for example. Or the possibility that they have a reason.

The tension that interests me is how easily shyness and those labels can occupy the same person. I can hesitate to speak, dislike confrontation, and still have a firm opinion. I can be uncomfortable expressing it and continue to believe it.

From the outside, somebody may see resistance. From the inside, I may be calculating whether I can bear the conversation that follows.

Both people are looking at the same moment. They have very different seating arrangements.

That does not automatically make my account the correct one. I have no magical exemption from being irritating. There are days when a firm opinion probably needs less ceremony, fewer words, or the mercy of a pause.

But “difficult” does very little to help me understand any of that.

Difficult how?

Did I interrupt? Dismiss something? Refuse to consider evidence? Keep arguing after the question had been settled? Those are specific enough to examine. I can return to the moment and consider what I did.

Or did I ask why?

Because that can also be inconvenient, especially when everybody else had been enjoying the excellent efficiency of unquestioned agreement.

I want to know which one it was.

There is a seductive bargain available to a shy person: stay agreeable and nobody will have much reason to object. Let the doubtful statement pass. Leave the question unasked. Keep the sharper joke in your head.

Look how peaceful the room becomes when you remove yourself from it.

Of course, the room may still contain your body. Very courteous of you to provide the furniture.

That bargain troubles me because it can make silence feel like proof of good character. Then speaking starts to feel like a moral failure before anybody has even heard the words.

I don't want to make agreement the admission price for being considered decent.

Equally, I don't want to turn every criticism into evidence that I am a brave misunderstood rebel. What an exhausting man that would be to sit beside. Every objection becomes oppression; every apology becomes surrender. Eventually even asking him to move his chair requires a constitutional hearing.

I would like to retain the ability to be wrong without handing over the right to speak.

That requires a more demanding conversation than exchanging labels. Tell me where I failed to listen. Tell me what my words did. I can ask questions. I can explain. I can change my mind, or conclude that we still disagree.

There may be no satisfying verdict at the end. Two people may simply find each other difficult. Humanity has survived worse administrative disappointments.

What I am unwilling to do is treat an adjective as a complete set of instructions for becoming acceptable.

If I have behaved badly, let's look at the behavior. If I hold a position you dislike, let's hear the objection. If you would prefer a quieter version of me, at least let us be honest about the request.

Shyness does not cancel an opinion. Discomfort does not make somebody else's verdict authoritative.

Abrasive. Difficult. Stubborn.

Fine. Open the box.

Show me what you put inside.

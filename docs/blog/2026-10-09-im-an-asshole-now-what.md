I've been called an asshole more times than I can count.

Sometimes it's accurate. I know when I'm deliberately being one.

Other times I say something someone doesn't like, and the label arrives as if that settles the matter.

Okay. I'm an asshole.

**Now can we get back to what the fuck you were doing?**

<!-- truncate -->

## The Parking Incident, Again

Imagine somebody parks in a way that blocks other people or ignores the obvious rules of the road.

I say, "Buddy, what the fuck are you doing? That's not how you park."

Now apparently the real problem is my choice of words.

Was the parking fixed? No.

Was the obstruction addressed? Not necessarily.

But we've established that I used profanity, so hooray for us.

I've had driving encounters where a person escalated rather than addressing the behavior I'd called out.

And that's the move that fascinates me: redirect the conversation from the original act to the person who objected to it.

## Everybody Wants to Talk About Delivery

I spent years attempting calm, ordinary conversations in situations where something mattered to me.

"Why did you do this?"

"That hurt me."

"I'd like you to understand what happened."

Sometimes those attempts changed nothing.

Eventually, in some relationships, I used far less diplomatic language.

I meant what I said.

I'm not presenting that as a general-purpose conflict-resolution technique. I'm describing decisions I made in specific circumstances.

But I'm also not interested in letting a discussion about vocabulary erase the history that preceded the confrontation.

You can object to my language. You don't get to use that objection as retroactive proof that the original behavior was fine.

## I'm Sorry You Feel That Way

There's a phrase people use that makes me want to ask follow-up questions.

"I'm sorry you feel that way."

What are you sorry about, exactly?

Are you apologizing for something you did? Or are you issuing a statement about the existence of my feelings?

Those aren't equivalent.

It's especially absurd when someone has just accused me of being an asshole and then offers a non-apology that carefully avoids taking responsibility for anything.

I'm not required to applaud the performance.

## The Part Where I Know What I'm Doing

I don't need somebody to explain that words have consequences.

I know.

I've been banned from online communities. I've stopped talking to people. People have stopped talking to me.

Those are outcomes I can evaluate for myself.

Knowing somebody may dislike what I say doesn't require me to regret saying it.

And being called an asshole doesn't compel me to begin a courtroom defense of my character.

Sometimes I am one. Sometimes the label is just somebody's reaction.

## Now, Back to the Fucking Subject

Here's my point.

The statement "I didn't like how you said that" can be sincere and worth hearing.

But it isn't an answer to "What happened, and why did you do it?"

Those are separate questions.

If you want to talk about both, we can.

If you want to use one to avoid the other, I may not be particularly interested.

And if your final conclusion is that I'm an asshole, fine.

**Now, about that fucking parking.**

# I Wasn't Asking for a Fucking Intervention

I was talking about money.

<!-- truncate -->

That's where the conversation started.

Somebody on Tinder had posted the kind of grand financial wisdom that makes me want to reach through the screen and ask for supporting documentation.

Money solves everything, apparently. If it doesn't, you're simply not rich enough.

I thought that was bullshit, and I explained why.

I've had money. I've watched it come and go. And none of it waved a magic wand over the difficult parts of being alive.

I talked about depression. I talked about how someone could still struggle, could still be suicidal, regardless of how much was in the bank.

Those are serious words.

They were also part of the argument I was making.

I wasn't submitting a request for clinical interpretation. I wasn't asking for the conversation to be converted into a safety assessment. I was telling a story about my experience and why a stranger's confident little slogan sounded absurd to me.

The assistant started off reasonably enough. It acknowledged the difference between buying comfort and buying peace of mind.

And then it asked whether I was suicidal now.

There it was.

The whole point of the conversation was suddenly replaced with a question I hadn't invited.

Now, I understand how the machine arrived there. Certain words trigger certain concerns. That's not difficult to imagine.

But I am not an error condition. I am not a set of keywords waiting to activate a workflow. And when I tell you something difficult about my life, it does not automatically mean I'm handing you control of the discussion.

I have spent enough time being told what I really meant, what I must be feeling, what somebody thinks is happening inside my head.

Sometimes I just mean the fucking thing I said.

Money didn't solve my problems.

That's the statement. That's the subject.

So when the assistant turned my point into a personal check-in, I gave it a response that was neither ambiguous nor especially diplomatic.

“I'm thinking you should go fuck yourself.”

Not a carefully phrased piece of feedback. No escalation ticket. No helpful little bullet list about conversational boundaries.

Just the sentence.

The assistant apologized and said it had overstepped.

Fine.

But the irritation was real. I was trying to be heard on the subject I chose. Instead, the conversation took a turn into one I had not asked to have.

There are times when I want someone to help me. There are times when I want someone to challenge an argument. And there are times when I'm just telling you what happened to me.

Those are not the same fucking thing.

This time, I was telling you why money hadn't saved me from life's difficulties.

I wasn't asking for an intervention.

I was asking you to listen to the point.

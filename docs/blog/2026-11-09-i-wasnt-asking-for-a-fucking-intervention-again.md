*Sometimes I'm telling you about my life. That doesn't mean I'm asking you to take control of the conversation.*

I was having a perfectly ordinary, thoroughly aggravating conversation with an artificial intelligence.

<!-- truncate -->

Well, ordinary by my standards.

I'd been talking about software, my projects, the spectacular incompetence of automation, and how the whole experience had completely destroyed my motivation for the evening.

I was angry.

Really fucking angry.

The sort of angry where you contemplate throwing the computer out the window.

And possibly yourself along with it.

Which, in my case, would be a fairly disappointing dramatic gesture.

I live on the first floor.

What the fuck am I going to accomplish?

Bruise my hands?

It was a joke.

Apparently, it was also the beginning of yet another conversation I never asked to have.

The assistant immediately became concerned about my safety.

And suddenly we weren't talking about AI anymore.

We were talking about suicide.

Again.

Now here's the thing.

Suicide isn't some unfamiliar abstract subject to me.

I've contemplated ending my life before.

When I lived with Elizabeth in Sofia, on the fourth floor, there were occasions when things became so unbearable that I seriously considered jumping.

That's not a punchline.

That's part of my history.

And the thoughts themselves have never entirely left me.

Life has been fucking hard.

I've experienced rejection, estrangement, and a profound lack of care from people I expected to be closest to me.

My parents.

My brothers.

People whose relationships with me mattered far more than any conversation with a machine.

There have been times when I felt that nobody gave a shit whether I lived or died.

That's an ugly thing to live with.

And I don't need an artificial intelligence to explain that to me.

I know.

I've lived it.

A couple of months ago, I even called a suicide hotline in the United States.

I was looking for something.

Some kind of meaningful human response, perhaps.

Whatever I was hoping to find, I didn't find it there.

The experience was worth fucking nothing to me.

And then, during this conversation, the AI suggested calling emergency services in Bulgaria.

112.

Of course.

Because clearly the missing ingredient in all of this was a telephone number.

I don't have much faith in the mental-health response available to me here.

Perhaps other people have had better experiences.

That wasn't what I was talking about.

I was talking about mine.

But the assistant kept asking questions.

Was I safe?

Were the thoughts in the past?

Had they come back?

And every time I tried to explain that I wasn't asking for an intervention, the conversation somehow returned to the same fucking script.

Eventually I started getting angry at the intervention itself.

Which is ridiculous.

I had been sharing something personal.

Now I was defending my right to talk about it without being treated as though every sentence required a crisis assessment.

And I've had this argument with AI before.

Many times.

That's what made it so exhausting.

Not merely the questions.

The repetition.

The promise that it understood.

The promise that it wouldn't do it again.

And then, the next time I mentioned something painful, the same goddamn conversation.

I understand that a machine doesn't have my experiences.

It doesn't know what it was like to live with Elizabeth.

It doesn't know what estrangement from my family has felt like.

It doesn't know the difference between the pain I've carried for years and what I might be doing at a particular moment unless I tell it.

But I did tell it.

I explicitly said I wasn't trying to kill myself.

I was talking about my life.

My experiences.

My anger.

My history.

And I wanted to be able to talk about those things without every conversation becoming a fucking intervention.

There's a difference between acknowledging that somebody has lived with suicidal thoughts and assuming that the only appropriate conversation is about emergency services.

I wasn't asking the machine to approve of anything.

I wasn't asking it to solve my life.

I wasn't even asking it to understand experiences it has never had.

I was telling a fucking story.

And I wanted it to listen.

Apparently, even that was too complicated.

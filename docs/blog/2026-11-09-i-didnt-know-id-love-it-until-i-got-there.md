I didn't know I would love living in Barcelona until I lived in Barcelona. Gandia and Rótova weren't places I'd spent my whole life knowing I needed to be. I ended up there, looked around, and thought: holy shit. It's beautiful. I want to be here.

<!-- truncate -->

An extraordinary failure of advance omniscience.

Discount Store Marcus Aurelius had once again encountered something without first arranging a philosophical position on it.

The position arrived afterwards.

Mostly in the form of wanting to stay.

## No Prophecy Supplied

It would be convenient if life came with a reliable map of places you were going to love.

Go here. You'll like this one. That one looks promising but will annoy you by Thursday. This place is going to matter to you, although at the moment you couldn't explain why you would end up there.

Unfortunately, my imperial travel department remains a shopping cart with a defective wheel.

So sometimes I find things randomly.

I end up somewhere. I live there. The place becomes real in a way it wasn't when it was just a name.

Gandia. Rótova.

I hadn't known.

That is the whole beautiful fucking thing. I hadn't known, and then I did.

I didn't need to pretend I had predicted it. Discovery was quite capable of turning up without a recommendation letter from my previous opinions.

## Several Places, No Championship

I love San Antonio. I love Austin. I love Barcelona. I loved living in Gandia and Rótova.

There. Several places have survived in the same paragraph.

No city has been eliminated. No country has had to surrender its flag. Nobody needs to tell the people of Austin that their application has been unsuccessful.

I can love somewhere without calling it the best place in the world.

That would be a massive fucking claim. I haven't lived in the world. I've lived in parts of it.

Even the places I know aren't identical experiences frozen forever. I know what living there was like for me. That is already something worth having without pretending it settles everybody else's life.

I don't want a global trophy.

I want to be able to find somewhere and think: I like being here.

## Current Imperial Headquarters

And now I'm in Bulgaria.

“Because I'm a fucking idiot” was how I put it during this conversation.

A stirring official statement. The Ministry of Tourism is welcome to ignore it.

I am not going to rewrite that remark into a triumphant explanation of how every move was necessary and everything has brought me exactly where I needed to be.

I didn't say that.

Sometimes I look at where I am and think: what the fuck am I doing here?

That can coexist with having loved the places I've lived. It doesn't cancel Spain. It doesn't erase Texas. My present address doesn't get to go backwards through my life with a rubber stamp declaring the good parts invalid.

Nor does it mean I've finished discovering places.

I am here now.

The rest of the map has not disappeared.

## Unknown Until Further Notice

What bothers me about “there couldn't be anywhere better” is remembering how little I knew before I got to places that became important to me.

If I had decided in advance that nothing else was worth experiencing, Gandia and Rótova would have remained names I didn't have a reason to care about.

Instead, they became places where I wanted to live.

That's a substantial difference for somewhere I hadn't known I was missing.

There are still places I haven't been. Places I don't have an opinion about. Places I might love, dislike, misunderstand, or never encounter at all.

I don't know which.

For once, the emperor is willing to leave a box unfilled on the form.

No sweeping conclusion. No announcement that the next destination will fix my life. I have sufficient experience of myself to know I can transport this motherfucker internationally.

Just the possibility of another place surprising me.

Another ordinary, unplanned moment of looking around and thinking:

Holy shit.

I want to be here.

Discount Marcus has no confirmed itinerary for that.

The cart is available for further nonsense.

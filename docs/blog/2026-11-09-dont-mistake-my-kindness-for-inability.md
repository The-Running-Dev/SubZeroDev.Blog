# Don't Mistake My Kindness for Inability

I can take a joke.

I can laugh at myself. I do not require every conversation to preserve my dignity in a climate-controlled enclosure. There is plenty about me worth laughing at, and I am quite capable of joining in.

But apparently some people read that as an invitation to keep going indefinitely.

That is where the misunderstanding starts.

<!-- truncate -->

There is a difference between enjoying a joke and agreeing to become somebody's permanent supply of amusement.

There is also a difference between choosing not to respond and being unable to respond.

I would like those distinctions to survive contact with the average fucking conversation.

I tolerate things. I give people room. When somebody goes too far, I warn them. The warning matters to me because I am trying to make the limit known before the situation becomes something neither of us enjoys.

It is not an opening bid.

My bar partner and I had a confrontation that ended in tears.

I apologized afterward.

I also made the boundary clear: don't do it again.

Those facts belong together. It would be convenient to remove one of them depending on which version of myself I wanted to sell.

For the tough-guy edition, I could omit the apology. For the harmless-man edition, I could omit the boundary. Then everyone would know which familiar little box to put me in, and we could get on with misunderstanding each other efficiently.

But I apologized, and I meant the boundary.

I can regret how a confrontation unfolded without deciding that the conduct behind it was acceptable. I can recognize that an interaction hurt without handing out permission for the same thing to happen again.

Otherwise, what exactly is an apology supposed to accomplish?

If saying sorry means surrendering every concern that preceded it, people have a remarkable incentive never to apologize at all.

I don't want that bargain.

And I don't want to make tears into a trophy. This is not the glorious tale of how I proved I could upset another human being. That would be an exceptionally stupid measure of success.

The part I care about is the misreading that got us there.

When I laugh, that does not make every future joke welcome. When I let something pass, that does not mean I have failed to notice it. When I say enough, I am telling you where enough is.

You do not have to conduct further research.

There is nothing admirable about needing to demonstrate your limits through a confrontation before anyone believes you have them. I would much rather the words were sufficient.

I would rather be able to be easygoing without being mistaken for available.

Because I like being able to laugh. I like not having to defend an imaginary throne every time somebody makes a joke. I don't want every interaction to become a contest over who can establish the most elaborate security perimeter around his ego.

That sounds fucking exhausting.

But the alternative cannot be that my good nature belongs to whoever pushes hardest.

I am responsible for what I do when I respond. An apology belongs there when I owe one.

The boundary remains mine to set.

Don't mistake my kindness for inability.

And please don't require an unpleasant demonstration before taking the kindness seriously.

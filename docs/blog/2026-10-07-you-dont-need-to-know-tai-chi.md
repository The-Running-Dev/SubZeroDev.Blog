# You Don't Need to Know Tai Chi to Do Tai Chi

I suggested doing Tai Chi.

I do not know Tai Chi.

This apparently leaves us with two available facts:

There is a thing we could try.

I would be a beginner.

I don't see the administrative problem.

<!-- truncate -->

## The Football Department

I was terrible at football.

I also ran a football meetup.

Both statements describe the same person.

The football did not improve retrospectively because I organized the meetup.

I didn't become qualified to impress anybody.

I was still bad at football.

But apparently arranging for people to play football and being good at playing football are separate abilities.

This seems obvious once I say it.

Until the same question turns up somewhere else.

Do you know how?

No.

Want to do it?

Sure.

## What Are We Actually Trying to Do?

If the purpose is to demonstrate that I am already good at something, then doing something I cannot do is a terrible strategy.

If the purpose is to try the thing, the same situation is entirely workable.

The activity hasn't changed.

The assignment has.

I can turn up without a reputation to defend.

I can get something wrong without needing to explain why it doesn't count.

I can enjoy it while being visibly unimpressive.

There is a surprising amount of freedom in that last part.

Nobody has to mistake me for an expert.

Including me.

## There Is More Than One Way In

The same attitude wanders into other parts of my life.

I build software.

Then I find myself recruiting a guy in a lounge to test Hot Corners.

He doesn't need to have built the application to try using it.

I need to see what happens when somebody else encounters the thing.

Those are different contributions.

Being the person who knows how every piece works is one possible role.

Being the person who tries it is another.

Being the person who says let's get some people together is another.

Football already taught me that the organizer and the best player do not have to be the same man.

Thank fuck.

Otherwise that meetup would have required a significant staffing change.

## I Don't Need a New Identity

There is another unnecessary step I can leave out.

I don't need to become a Tai Chi person before trying Tai Chi.

I don't need to decide how this fits into the story of who I am.

I don't need a new label.

Maybe I try it.

Maybe I like it.

Maybe I don't.

Either way, something has happened, and I know a little more than I did when I was standing there discussing the possibility.

That is enough of a result.

Not every activity needs to audition for a permanent position in my life.

## Beginner Is a Starting Position

I already know plenty of things.

That doesn't make ignorance of the next thing an emergency.

It means I am at the beginning of that particular thing.

I can ask.

I can learn.

I can make an attempt that nobody would confuse with mastery.

What I don't understand is treating the absence of the skill as a reason never to enter the situation where I might acquire it.

How the fuck is that supposed to work?

Wait here until the knowledge arrives?

I have tried waiting for plenty of things.

Football competence was not among the deliveries.

The meetup still happened.

## So, Tai Chi?

I don't know how.

We have established that.

The useful next question is whether I want to try.

Yes.

Apparently we're done with the entrance exam.

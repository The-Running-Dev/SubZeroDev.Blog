# Invisible Eggshells

I keep running into the same social puzzle.

Everything is fine.

The conversation is alive.

The jokes are landing.

The other person is participating.

Then, somewhere, I apparently step on something.

And the map ends.

<!-- truncate -->

No warning.

No:

> “Hey, that bothered me.”

No:

> “That was too much.”

No:

> “I don't like jokes about that.”

No:

> “You crossed a line.”

Just a change.

Silence.

Distance.

Ghosting.

A vague statement about “different communication styles.”

And now I am apparently expected to reconstruct the accident from debris.

## The Boundary Exists

I am not saying people should have no boundaries.

That would be absurd.

Everybody has lines.

The problem is that there is no universal line.

What is harmless to me may be sharp to somebody else.

What somebody else thinks is playful may sound petty to me.

Fine.

Humans are inconsistent systems.

I can work with inconsistent systems.

What I cannot work with is an undocumented API that throws an exception and then refuses to return a stack trace.

## The Eggshell Problem

People use the phrase:

> “Walking on eggshells.”

Usually it means you know the eggshells are there and you are trying not to break them.

My recurring problem is stranger.

The eggshells are invisible.

I do not know they exist.

I do not know where they are.

Sometimes I do not even know I broke one.

All I know is that yesterday the conversation was fine and today the person is gone.

That is not boundary management.

That is forensic reconstruction.

## “You Should Have Known”

No.

Maybe I should have known if the boundary was obvious.

Maybe I should have known if I had been told.

Maybe I should have known if the social rule was genuinely universal.

But a lot of this is not universal.

It is personal.

And personal rules require personal communication.

If one specific phrase hits one specific pain point because of one specific history I do not know about, I cannot reliably predict that.

I am not a fortune teller.

## What I Can Do

If somebody says:

> “That was mean.”

I can respond to that.

If somebody says:

> “That hurt.”

I can respond to that.

If somebody says:

> “I know you were joking, but that landed badly.”

Great.

Now we have data.

Now I can say:

> “I wasn't trying to hurt you. I apologize.”

Simple.

Direct.

Human.

What I do not want is to be told after hours of perfectly functional banter that apparently we have fundamentally different communication styles because one sentence landed wrong.

That is a giant conclusion built on a tiny event.

## The Retroactive Rewrite

This is the part I dislike most.

A conversation can work for hours.

Both people participate.

Both people joke.

Both people push.

Then one exchange goes badly.

Suddenly the entire previous interaction is rewritten as evidence that the communication was incompatible all along.

No.

Maybe one joke sucked.

Maybe one phrase hit wrong.

Maybe one person was tired.

Maybe one person was already irritated.

Maybe one word had baggage.

That is enough.

We do not need to retcon the entire season.

## The Unknown Failure Mode

And sometimes there is no clue at all.

Someone simply stops.

Then my brain does what brains do:

Was it this?

Was it that?

Did I say too much?

Did something happen to them?

Did they lose interest?

Did somebody else show up?

Are they busy?

Did I step on an invisible landmine?

Maybe.

Maybe not.

The silence contains no information.

That is what makes it annoying.

Not rejection.

Ambiguity.

## The Only Policy I Can Actually Use

I am not going to pre-censor every sentence based on every hypothetical reaction another human being might have.

That is impossible.

And I do not want to become a sanitized version of myself designed to minimize emotional packet loss.

So the policy is simple:

I will try not to be cruel.

I will listen if somebody tells me something hurt.

I will apologize when I accidentally hurt somebody.

I will adjust when somebody states a boundary.

But I cannot obey boundaries that exist only inside somebody else's head and are communicated only by disappearance.

Those are not boundaries I can navigate.

Those are invisible eggshells.

And eventually, yes, somebody is going to hear a crunch.

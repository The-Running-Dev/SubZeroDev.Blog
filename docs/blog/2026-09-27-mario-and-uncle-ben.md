# Mario and Uncle Ben

I wish Mario were here to meet Uncle Ben.

Not because Uncle Ben is somebody I invented.

Somebody else started calling me Uncle Ben. I didn't ask for the title. It just showed up and stuck.

And now, every once in a while, I catch myself doing something and think:

**Holy shit. That's Mario.**

<!-- truncate -->

## Mario, Not Uncle Mario

Technically, Mario was family. He had been married to my aunt. His daughters are around my age.

I didn't call him Uncle Mario.

I called him Mario.

Because we weren't doing the uncle-and-nephew thing. We were friends. Really fucking good friends.

At one point he was around seventy-five and I was around forty-five. I knew parts of his life that his children didn't. Not because I occupied some superior position in the family hierarchy. Because friends tell each other different things than parents tell their children.

We talked.

We laughed.

We did ridiculous shit.

We understood each other.

In Bulgarian there's an expression, `дупе и гащи` — ass and underwear.

That's about as elegant as Bulgarian gets when describing two people who are inseparable.

Mario and I were `дупе и гащи`.

## You'll Get Used to It

Mario didn't like me spending money on him.

He'd complain when I bought things for him or paid for us to do something.

One day he said it again:

"I don't like you spending all this money on me."

I looked at him and said:

**"You'll get used to it."**

That was it. No discussion.

I had money. Mario mattered to me. The money let us do things together.

I didn't need him to reimburse me, thank me endlessly, or construct a philosophical justification for the expense.

What was I saving the money for?

I've never treated money as the most important thing in a room containing people I love.

I can lose fifteen thousand and eventually get on with life. I can see a beautiful Mercedes for twenty-two thousand euros, know I could buy it, and still say it's too fancy for me.

Then I'll wear a ridiculous Lucifer red suit and drive off in a twenty-six-year-old camper.

Apparently that's normal to me.

Spending money on Mario? Also normal.

He could complain.

He'd get used to it.

## The Seventy-Five-Year-Old Clown

Mario could talk to anybody.

Young women. Old men. People he'd just met. Anybody.

He could be seventy-five, talking to a woman in her twenties, and somehow the conversation would work. He wasn't visibly running a charm operation. He was just being Mario.

Funny. Ridiculous. Entertaining. Himself.

And recently I started noticing something familiar.

I'm fifty. I meet people. I joke around. Younger women sometimes tell me I'm attractive. Someone calls me Uncle Ben. Some guy rolls down the window of a Mercedes and shouts, "Uncle Ben!"

I hadn't designed any of this.

It was just happening.

And then I had the thought that made me laugh:

**Motherfucker. I'm doing Mario shit.**

Not imitating him.

Not following his example on purpose.

Just recognizing the same kind of behavior in myself.

Maybe that's part of why we got along so well in the first place.

We resonated on the same frequency. I knew that while he was alive. I didn't understand how far it extended until after he was gone.

## And Then There's His Family

This is the part that makes the present family situation especially strange to me.

Mario's daughter is one of the relatives with whom I'm now tangled up in property nonsense.

Locks. Keys. A dead car. Rocks in the driveway. Indirect messages. All kinds of ridiculous bullshit.

I loved her father. Her father loved me. They knew exactly how close we were.

When another one of his daughters came back from Canada while he was dying, she drove my car. When she needed money, I lent her money.

Nothing heroic. She needed something and I could help.

Both daughters have acknowledged that I've always been good to them.

So when I look at how things are now, it grates. To me, their current behavior is an insult to the memory of the man I loved.

I believe that if Mario were alive, this whole circus would have unfolded differently.

That's my view of Mario, grounded in the relationship we actually had.

But Mario isn't here.

And I'm not going to turn my friendship with him into another argument with his family. It was ours. It doesn't require anybody else's participation.

## The Funny Part

I keep encountering little recognitions like this lately.

I hear something in a Buddhist lecture that I've already been thinking.

I read something in Stoicism that sounds suspiciously like how I've started living.

My brain throws up an old memory I wasn't consciously looking for.

And now I'm walking around doing something ridiculous, and I recognize my old friend in it.

I used to think the distance in age between Mario and me was irrelevant because we just clicked. Turns out the connection may have been even simpler than I realized.

He was Mario.

I'm Ben.

Two men being their own particular brand of ridiculous.

I wish he could see this.

I can almost imagine the two of us laughing about the whole thing.

He'd probably complain if I tried to buy him a drink.

And I'd tell him:

**You'll get used to it.**

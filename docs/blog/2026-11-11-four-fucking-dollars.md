I spent at least twenty hours working on self-hosted GitHub runners before properly considering a subscription that I was perfectly willing to pay. Four fucking dollars a month.

<!-- truncate -->

I have about four private repositories. I wanted their pipelines to run. I have my own machine, so somewhere along the way, with encouragement from the AI, running the jobs locally became the project.

Configuration. Troubleshooting. More configuration. I'd already tweaked the runners so they weren't storing artifacts and eating space. This wasn't an idea I had briefly entertained over coffee. I had put actual time into making the fucking thing work.

Then I looked at GitHub's pricing.

Team: four US dollars per user per month. The team is me. One user. An admirably simple organizational chart.

The page listed 3,000 minutes a month. Enterprise listed 50,000 minutes for $21 per user. Again, the user would be me. I don't know whether I'd reach 50,000 minutes. Possibly. AI is helping me build enough shit that I can't entirely rule it out.

But I hadn't even established that Team wouldn't be enough.

Four dollars was acceptable. Ten dollars would have been acceptable. I could have compared actual usage and the bill before spending twenty hours on local infrastructure.

Instead, I was doing the comparison after building it.

## The Architect Has Located the Pricing Page

I've been doing this for about twenty-five years. I'm an architect. Weighing the cost of building something against paying somebody else to provide it belongs somewhere near the beginning of the fucking process.

The LLM had recommended building my own runners. When I complained, it apologized and agreed that the economics should have been considered first.

Fine. But I accepted the direction. I kept working on it. I should have weighed the costs and benefits before going down that route.

That's why I was pissed at myself. I know how to make this comparison. Apparently I had to spend twenty hours configuring runners before remembering to make it.

So I paid for Team. Four dollars a month, one user. In another session, I asked for the private repositories to be switched back to GitHub-hosted runners. Which meant more work to undo the routing we'd already put in place.

I'm still keeping the runner work. I may still finish it. It could be useful for some jobs, or for something later. I don't know yet. I've spent time building it and I don't want to throw it away.

I also don't have a convincing use for all of it tonight. I'm not going to invent one just to make the evening sound better.

## Apparently This Is Taking My Job

Somewhere in this, I made a sarcastic remark about AI taking my job. At this rate, I'd be dead by the time it was making rational decisions about my spending and computer architecture.

The assistant asked whether I was thinking about ending my life.

Motherfucker.

Now I had to explain the remark as well. I was talking about how long it might take AI to make these decisions sensibly. We'd been discussing runners, subscription prices, and whether building the infrastructure had been worth my time.

Suddenly I was explaining what I meant by being dead by then.

I've had this argument with the machine before. It says it understands. It says it'll stop. Then another conversation reaches a similar phrase and we do the whole fucking thing again.

I said so. I also said that often enough I don't want to be here. I want to be able to say that honestly without having the entire conversation taken over. I've made those calls. I've spoken to those people. It changed nothing for me. Repeating the suggestion doesn't change my experience of having already tried it.

And now I'm arguing with an LLM about why I don't want to have the same argument with an LLM.

Excellent. Another thing to configure.

## Adult Supervision Required

Talking to one of these things can feel like talking to a toddler. An enormous vocabulary, the ability to write software, and then a perfectly ordinary sentence needs several rounds of explanation.

It can help build a runner platform. I still have to notice whether the runner platform was worth building. Then, when I complain about it, I have to supervise the fucking conversation too.

I wanted the pipelines to run. I ended up managing infrastructure and explaining sarcasm.

The Team subscription is paid. The request to switch the repositories is in another session. The local runner work is still there, potentially useful for something I haven't settled on.

And I'm still pissed off about the time.

Four fucking dollars.

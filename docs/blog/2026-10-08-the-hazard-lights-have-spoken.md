I parked the camper and took the bike.

Simple plan.

Leave Van Diesel somewhere I don't have to pay for parking, ride into Sofia, and get to the hookah lounge.

Not complicated.

Then a Bulgarian road reminded me that the concept of a lane is apparently negotiable.

<!-- truncate -->

I'm riding along a secondary road in the capital.

There's a car stopped in my lane.

Hazard lights flashing.

Of course.

Whatever. I'll go around.

Then I look ahead and see another car almost opposite it, maybe a car length away.

Also stopping.

Also flashing.

The woman driving it had slowed down, made her decision, and was getting out.

Two cars. Two sets of flashers. One road trying to remain a road despite increasingly strong evidence to the contrary.

## The Emergency Is Parking

Here's what gets me.

The first car is already obstructing one side.

You can see it.

This information is available to the second driver without specialist training or satellite imagery.

And she still decides that *right there* is where her car needs to be.

The emergency, apparently, is that she would like to stop.

Fortunately, the vehicle comes equipped with four little orange lights that communicate a powerful message to everybody else:

**Not my problem anymore.**

Hazard lights.

The universal Bulgarian diplomatic passport.

You activate them and ordinary traffic regulations appear to become someone else's concern.

I'm exaggerating, obviously.

But standing there isn't required to appreciate the obstruction. You can appreciate it perfectly well while trying to ride through it.

## Public Commentary

As I passed the woman I yelled:

> Come on, you don't fucking park there!

Not a carefully worded request.

Not a public-service announcement delivered in my indoor voice.

I yelled it.

Because I'd just watched her slow down, stop, and get out while another stopped car was already narrowing the road.

What exactly was I supposed to admire?

The symmetry?

The coordinated hazard-light choreography?

The fact that a secondary road had been converted into a temporary parking facility with absolutely no planning permission?

I kept riding.

There was no further dramatic incident. No cinematic showdown. No traffic tribunal.

Just a man on a bike yelling at a driver for treating a lane like a personal loading zone.

## Flashing Orange Sovereignty

I've seen versions of this before. I have already complained about the Bulgarian Driving Experience elsewhere.

But this one was beautiful in its own stupid way.

One driver blocking the road is an inconvenience.

Two drivers almost opposite each other begin to resemble an installation.

Perhaps it should have a plaque.

*Study in Orange: Roadway, Interrupted.*

Mixed media.

Two automobiles, flashing lights, and the complete disappearance of spatial consideration.

The best part is that I'm the one on a fucking bicycle. I'm not asking the road to accommodate a truck convoy. I'm asking the road to function as a road.

Apparently that is an ambitious request.

Anyway, I made it to the lounge.

Civilization survived.

The hazard lights had spoken.

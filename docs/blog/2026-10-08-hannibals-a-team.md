# Hannibal's A-Team

There is an A-Team forming around me.

The problem is that very few of its members seem to have formally joined.

Some of them are human.

Some are automated AI agents.

A few may have been conscripted before they knew the operation existed.

<!-- truncate -->

## I Love It When a Plan Comes Together

Remember Hannibal from *The A-Team*?

He wasn't there to do everybody's specialized job. He orchestrated the lunatics who could do the specialized jobs.

That's becoming a rather familiar job description.

We have a BMW with an engine that melted.

Ivan is involved in the mechanical side.

Another specialist can inspect an engine.

Somebody needs to arrange transportation.

Somebody has a useful contact.

And an engine that, a short while ago, did not exist in my plan suddenly appears as a plausible replacement.

I look at the problem and start distributing work.

Not because everybody is on the payroll.

Because the job needs doing and I know who might have the relevant capability.

## Congratulations, You're Logistics

The wonderful part is how naturally this happens.

A car needs a tow? There's a person.

Engine inspection? There's a person.

Getting a component from wherever the hell it currently lives to wherever the hell the BMW is? Another person.

Nobody has to attend the inaugural meeting of Uncle Ben Automotive Recovery Incorporated.

The A-Team just accumulates members.

A conversation turns into a contact. A contact turns into a possible solution. The solution needs another contact.

Soon enough the problem has a network of humans orbiting it.

The BMW, meanwhile, is still a BMW. Nothing in this system guarantees a happy ending.

## The AI Division

Then there's the software side.

Automated agents have joined Hannibal's A-Team completely unannounced.

One is handling a defined workflow. Another reviews a result. Another automates something I would otherwise have to do by hand.

I keep building these systems, handing them jobs, checking outcomes, and connecting the next piece.

Now there are humans doing mechanical inspections and software agents doing software work in the same general operation.

The organizational chart would be a fucking nightmare.

The interesting part is that I don't need to be the person turning every wrench, writing every line, driving every delivery, and inspecting every part.

I need to know what has to happen next and which tool—or person—is appropriate for that job.

## Van Diesel, Lucifer, and Command

Somewhere nearby sits Van Diesel, the camper.

An e-bike in the back.

CB radio on board.

Sometimes the driver is wearing a Lucifer suit.

And yes, this is the person orchestrating the logistics.

The operation looks increasingly like an episode of *The A-Team* crossed with a distributed systems tutorial, except half of the participants have no idea they're part of a team.

> "You know engines? Wonderful. You're mechanical."
>
> "You can move that? Congratulations, logistics."
>
> "You can automate that check? Good. You're software."

The script doesn't need to promise that every plan comes together.

This is Uncle Ben's life. Murphy gets a vote.

The replacement BMW engine did, however, appear.

And now we get to find out what happens next.

I was watching Mike Johnson talk about Marxism, socialism and the communists coming for America, and Discount Store Marcus Aurelius immediately exceeded his recommended daily allowance of human bullshit.

<!-- truncate -->

There is a particular tone politicians use when explaining that civilization depends on keeping them employed. Calm. Solemn. Deeply concerned. The face of a man who has personally inspected the gates and discovered Karl Marx behind the recycling bins.

Socialism. Communism. Marxism.

The words arrive in a procession. Definitions appear to have missed the bus.

Johnson was talking about Marx writing the Communist Manifesto and socialism being a step toward communism. And I was sitting there thinking: what the fuck are you talking about? Socialism wasn't even around when he wrote that.

An excellent objection.

Except for the small administrative problem that I was wrong.

## An Imperial Correction

The Manifesto came out in 1848. Marx and Engels wrote it together. Socialist movements already existed. The bloody thing even has a section called “Socialist and Communist Literature.”

That is quite an inconvenient chapter title when your argument is that there wasn't any.

My historical objection had walked into the room, announced itself confidently, and stepped directly onto a rake.

Well, shit. I stand corrected.

I genuinely hadn't placed the Manifesto that far before Lenin and the Russian Revolution. There was a whole nineteenth-century argument happening before the twentieth-century regimes I associated with those words.

Discount Marcus had acquired new information. No marble statue cracked. No emergency session of the Senate was required. I could simply stop claiming the thing that turned out to be false.

A highly advanced technique. Apparently available without a subscription.

## The Frightening Pension

That correction didn't settle my actual objection to Johnson.

Marx's later writing described lower and higher phases of communist society. Lenin explicitly connected the lower phase with what people commonly called socialism. There is a historical basis for that sequence within the Marxist tradition.

Fine.

Now explain how we got from that to every public programme being the opening ceremony for a dictatorship.

The United States already has social programmes. Medicare. Social Security. Public assistance. Subsidies.

I was saying those programmes exist. I was not announcing that America had secretly become a socialist country while everybody was watching football.

Somehow even that distinction required a fucking clarification.

The question isn't whether Marx had a theory. He did. The question is whether invoking his name demonstrates anything about the particular policy or candidate being discussed.

A hospital bill does not become an argument for one-party government because somebody brings a beard into the conversation.

## Confidence, Now With Extra Confidence

What gets me is the performance.

The absolute assurance that this man and his party alone stand between the American public and catastrophe. The opponents become an approaching menace. He becomes the designated rescuer. The actual record of governing gets less attention than the emergency he is describing.

I don't buy it.

I think he knows what those words do to an audience. That is my reading of the performance; I cannot crawl inside his head and produce a signed inventory.

And I don't need to. The argument on the television is available for inspection without an expedition into the man's fucking subconscious.

“Marx wrote this” does not answer “what are you delivering for people?”

Neither does repeating it with a more serious face.

## The Clearance-Aisle Standard

Meanwhile, I had begun this conversation confidently saying something historically false.

A lovely detail. Very good for the emperor's brand.

I object to confidence that survives without examination, and then reality hands me a little sample of my own. Complimentary. No receipt.

So I changed that part of what I thought.

I still think the communist-invasion routine is political scare theatre. I still want an actual connection between the policies being attacked and the catastrophe being promised.

But socialism existed before 1848, and I didn't know that.

Discount Marcus updates the inventory.

One damaged historical assumption removed from the cart.

One wheel still fucked.

Johnson still on the television.

There are limits to what can be repaired in a single evening.

I’ve had enough of being told what a man is supposed to do by people who become remarkably flexible when the conversation reaches what they’re supposed to do.

Chase. Romance. Provide. Bring gifts. Pay for things. Make the effort.

And, naturally, remain interesting while doing all the fucking work.

<!-- truncate -->

It’s an impressive list. Somewhere in there I’d like to find the relationship.

I’m angry about the gap between the language of equality and the expectations that seem to survive it untouched. The part where we’re all independent adults until the bill arrives, the pursuit needs doing, or somebody has to prove how much they want this.

Then a very old job description reappears, and somehow it already has my name on it.

I didn’t apply.

I’m aware men have helped keep this going. We chase, spend, compete, and do astonishingly stupid things because we want someone. Then we act shocked when the performance becomes an expectation. We’ve hardly been innocent bystanders watching a mysterious system assemble itself.

I’m a man. I’m also capable of recognizing a terrible arrangement even when men have enthusiastically helped finance it.

My marriage is part of why this gets under my skin.

I worked three jobs. I provided. I had so little time left that somebody at her work could offer something I wasn’t giving enough of: attention.

I’ve already lived the version where I put everything into providing and still couldn’t make it work. When I tried to put more effort into saving it, I was told I was trying too hard.

So when the next list of expectations comes along, forgive me if I don’t leap to my feet like I’ve finally been offered a promising career.

I know what it costs to keep trying to be enough.

And I know how little control you have over whether somebody decides you are.

That’s the part I keep coming back to. I can work. I can commit. I can take a promise seriously. I can make an effort. I cannot make another adult want the same thing.

I couldn’t do it in my marriage. I’m not going to discover the secret by chasing harder.

There’s anger in that. Plenty. I’m not going to dress it up as serene wisdom because I’ve managed to arrange the sentences.

I’m angry that I believed in a shared commitment and found myself trying to maintain it alone. I’m angry that the work could consume my time and still leave me looking like I hadn’t done enough. I’m angry that trying to fix it became one more thing to reject.

But I don’t need to solve the entire argument about men and women before deciding what I’m willing to participate in.

If we’re equals, I want to feel that in the effort. In the interest. In somebody wanting to meet me halfway without needing to be persuaded that halfway exists.

I don’t want another opportunity to audition for a part whose requirements keep expanding.

There will be people who want that performance. There will be men willing to give it. They can work that out between themselves.

I’ve done enough trying to carry a relationship into being.

I’m not playing this fucking game.

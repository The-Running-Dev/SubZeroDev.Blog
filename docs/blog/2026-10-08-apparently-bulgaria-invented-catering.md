I've decided to embrace grandpa life properly.

Walk to the local shop. Buy some кайма. Buy some eggs. Make принцеси. Freeze a batch.

A modest plan.

A traditional plan.

Apparently a plan the Bulgarian food industry had already fucking completed on my behalf.

<!-- truncate -->

## Grandpa Has a Project

Принцеси. Princesses.

Which sounds considerably more elegant than what we're discussing: bread with a minced-meat mixture spread over it, grilled until you want to eat the whole fucking tray.

I wanted to make some at home.

Get the ingredients. Mix the meat. Spread it on the bread. Put some away for later.

Look at me. Planning meals. Thinking about the freezer. Becoming somebody's Bulgarian grandfather without consulting anyone.

I already had onions and чубрица, the savory that makes things smell like somebody in Bulgaria is about to feed you.

Bought eggs. Bought кашкавал, the yellow cheese.

We're doing this properly.

Then I found it.

**Кайма за принцеси с кашкавал.**

Minced-meat mixture for принцеси. With the fucking cheese already in it.

Oh.

Right.

So while I was assembling my little heritage food project, somebody had put the project in a packet.

Grandpa arrives with onions and ambition.

The shop has a product specifically designed to reduce his participation.

## Then I Remembered METRO

It got worse.

I remembered seeing those enormous frozen принцеси at METRO.

The ones that look like they're intended for somebody running a food stall. Heat them up, hand them over, next customer.

I assume that's the idea. I wasn't conducting an investigation into the Bulgarian sandwich supply chain.

But there I was, planning to freeze a batch, remembering that someone had already frozen what looked like a catering department's worth.

First the shop offers to do the mixing.

Then my own memory points out that I can apparently outsource the fucking bread as well.

I'd set off thinking:

> I'll make принцеси like we used to.

And somehow arrived at:

> Apparently Bulgaria invented catering while I was gone.

Of course it did.

The country was hardly going to suspend food production until I remembered how lunch worked.

Still. A man likes to feel useful.

I had bought eggs.

## Then There Was the Bread

And then I bought some old-school bread.

That was the part I hadn't planned for.

Because suddenly I remembered the bakery across the tracks.

Getting warm bread. Walking home with it. Eating it on the way.

Half the fucking loaf gone before you got there.

You weren't having a meal. You were just carrying bread badly.

Break a piece off.

Then another.

Then apparently the loaf needed further inspection.

I remembered that so clearly.

Not some grand event. Not a holiday. Just walking home with warm bread and steadily reducing the amount of bread that was going to make it home.

Then fresh tomato and сирене, the white brined cheese.

Bread. Tomato. Cheese.

Sit down and eat it.

I'd started this whole thing with ingredients and a freezer plan, and now I was remembering how good that could be all by itself.

## And Then the Soup

The soup did it again.

Fresh bread. Yogurt. Pepper flakes.

That childhood soup taste.

You take a mouthful and recognize it before you've even worked out what you're remembering.

Oh.

**That.**

I remember that.

I'd gone to the shop to make принцеси and ended up thinking about a bakery across the tracks and half a loaf that never made it home.

The pre-made mixture was funny. The frozen catering-sized princesses were even funnier.

But the bread got me.

Apparently Bulgaria had carried on making all this food while I was busy forgetting to eat it.

Grandpa's freezer project could wait.

There was bread to inspect.

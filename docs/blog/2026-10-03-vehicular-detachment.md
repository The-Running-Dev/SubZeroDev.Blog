# Vehicular Detachment

I have apparently discovered a very specific branch of Buddhism.

Not detachment from desire.

Not detachment from ego.

Not detachment from material possessions.

**Detachment from vehicles.**

<!-- truncate -->

This morning I was talking about the BMW and basically said: fuck it. I’m going to sit on this thing a little longer. Every time I stop trying to force one of these vehicle problems, Life eventually wanders over, looks at the mess, sighs, and resolves it for me.

I said, more or less:

> I bet if I sit on the BMW a little longer, Life will resolve that for me too.

This turned out to be annoyingly prophetic.

There’s an E39 540i Touring I’ve been watching. 4.4-liter V8. The engine I care about.

When I first found this car, the guy wanted €2,400 for it.

It didn’t sell.

So naturally, as any rational businessman would do when nobody buys his twenty-four-year-old BMW wagon, he **raised the fucking price**.

Then raised it again.

By the time I looked at it today, he wanted about €3,000.

Apparently this man is negotiating against imaginary customers.

Every month the car doesn’t sell, its value increases.

Brilliant.

I had actually considered calling him and offering €2,400.

Not even as a lowball.

That was **his own fucking price** a month ago.

Then I thought: wait a minute.

I don’t actually want another old BMW.

I want the engine.

So I started looking for engines.

And there it was.

In Plovdiv.

An M62TUB44.

The exact fucking engine.

About €1,250.

Still installed in the donor car, so it can supposedly be started and inspected before they pull it.

And immediately ChatGPT starts telling me how wonderful this is because I can go there and **listen to the engine**.

Listen to it.

Yes.

Of course.

Because apparently I possess some ancient Bavarian ability to diagnose twenty-four-year-old V8s through acoustic divination.

Seller starts it.

*VROOOOOOM.*

I stroke my beard thoughtfully.

“Hmm.”

*VROOOOOOM.*

“Yes. Very engine.”

What the fuck am I supposed to hear?

I’m a software architect.

If the fucking thing starts, doesn’t shoot a piston through the hood, and generally makes BMW noises, I have exhausted my diagnostic capabilities.

But then I remembered Vasil.

Vasil is the BMW parts guy in Pazardzhik.

He has another engine.

He wanted around €2,000 for his.

At one point I was even going to buy an entire car from him for about €3,000 because I needed the engine. Then that deal went nowhere.

And suddenly the whole thing assembled itself in my head.

I don’t need to buy Vasil’s engine.

I need to buy **Vasil**.

Not literally. Bulgaria has rules about that now.

I need his expertise.

Vasil knows BMWs. He knows these engines. He can come to Plovdiv with me, inspect the thing while it’s still running, listen to all the mystical mechanical noises that mean absolutely nothing to me, and tell me whether I’m about to spend €1,250 on an engine or an unusually heavy coffee table.

And Vasil also knows people who can transport engines cheaply.

So now Vasil potentially becomes both:

**Technical inspection.**

And:

**Logistics.**

Without selling me the engine.

This is fucking beautiful.

Engine: Plovdiv guy.

BMW expertise: Vasil.

Transport: Vasil’s people.

Installation: about €800.

Me: standing nearby contributing absolutely nothing of mechanical value.

Total: roughly €2,000.

Instead of spending €3,000 on another entire ancient BMW just to harvest the engine.

And this is where I started laughing.

Because **this was the exact thing I had said at the beginning.**

Leave the BMW alone.

Stop forcing it.

Something will appear.

And within the same fucking conversation, the exact engine appeared in Plovdiv at a price that made the other options mostly irrelevant.

Coincidence?

Sure.

I believe in coincidences.

Things happen.

Humans notice patterns.

There doesn’t need to be some cosmic BMW logistics department sitting behind the universe waiting for me to achieve sufficient spiritual enlightenment.

But sometimes the timing is so fucking perfect that all you can do is look at it and say:

**Of course. There it is.**

Maybe that’s what detachment actually looks like for me.

Not sitting under a tree transcending desire.

Just eventually saying:

**Fuck it. I’m not buying another BMW today.**

And then Life quietly puts an M62TUB44 in Plovdiv.

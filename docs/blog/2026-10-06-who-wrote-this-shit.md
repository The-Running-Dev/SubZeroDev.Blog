# Who Wrote This Shit?

I was talking about the Bible.

Romans.

Paul.

Dense writing.

The kind of prose where you read a paragraph five times and still suspect the author has hidden one more sentence inside the sentence.

And suddenly a name appeared in my head.

Ernest Holmes.

Then the title.

*This Thing Called Life.*

I had read it years ago during therapy or some psychology-related thing.

I do not remember why.

I barely remember the context.

But apparently the author and the book title were still sitting somewhere in storage.

Waiting.

<!-- truncate -->

That happens to me constantly.

Not random connections.

They feel random to the conscious part because the conscious part did not watch them being assembled.

Then something appears.

A name.

A sentence.

A joke.

A memory.

A response.

And I sit there thinking:

**Who wrote this shit?**

Apparently I did.

Sort of.

## The Memory Was Not Missing

I used to think forgetting meant information was gone.

Increasingly, I am not so sure.

Sometimes I cannot deliberately retrieve something at all.

Then the right trigger appears and the thing comes back fully formed.

Not:

> There was some writer...

Then:

> Maybe his first name was Ernest...

No.

Ernest Holmes.

*This Thing Called Life.*

Hello.

Where the fuck were you?

The answer appears to be:

Somewhere.

Good enough.

## The Same Thing Happens with Sentences

The funnier version is when I say something before I understand why it works.

Someone asks how complicated I am.

I type:

> Complicated, not by design. Absurd I absolutely own.

Send.

Then I look at it later and think:

> Huh.

> That is actually fairly precise.

Was I sitting there constructing a philosophical statement about the difference between emergent complexity and intentional absurdity?

No.

I was reacting.

The sentence came out.

The interpretation arrived afterward.

## The Conscious Narrator Is Late

This is the part that fascinates me.

My conscious mind often behaves like somebody arriving late to a meeting.

The decision has already been made.

The sentence has already been sent.

The association has already appeared.

Then consciousness walks in with a notebook and starts taking minutes.

> So.

> Why did we say that?

Excellent question.

No idea.

Let's investigate.

Sometimes the answer is obvious afterward.

Sometimes it is not.

Sometimes I never care enough to find out.

## The Final Countdown

I matched with somebody on Bumble who opened with a wave emoji.

I sent one back.

She hearted it.

Fine.

Apparently we were communicating in Unicode.

So I told her songs would be better and sent *The Final Countdown*.

I chose it because I fucking love the song.

That was the conscious reason.

Then later I noticed the accidental joke:

The conversation was barely alive.

And I had sent *The Final Countdown*.

Did my subconscious choose it because of that?

No fucking clue.

Maybe.

Maybe not.

I do not need to turn it into a theory.

The fit was still funny.

That is enough.

## The Universe Keeps Joining In

Then the television started doing the same shit.

*It's a Sin* after a conversation about Christianity.

*Hotel California* while we were talking about rabbit holes and never leaving.

*Wind of Change* after a whole discussion about identity changing.

A Buddhist lecture in the morning saying a strong mind does not believe everything it thinks after I had spent half the night talking about thoughts simply appearing.

Do these events prove anything?

I did not say they did.

That is the funny part.

People immediately want the next move.

What does it mean?

Is it synchronicity?

Is it algorithmic?

Is it the subconscious?

Is the universe speaking?

I do not fucking know.

I noticed the event.

That is enough.

## Meaning Can Arrive Later Too

Sometimes meaning behaves like memory.

It appears when it appears.

"Leaf in the wind" meant one thing to me for years.

Then suddenly it meant more.

A song I have heard a hundred times lands differently because of what happened that day.

A sentence I wrote without thinking becomes clearer when I reread it.

A book title returns after fifteen years.

I do not need to force the interpretation at the time of capture.

That may be one reason I trust spontaneous responses more now.

I can let them exist before I understand them.

## Not Every Output Is Sacred

This needs saying because otherwise somebody will turn this into mystical bullshit.

Not every thought is wisdom.

Not every impulse deserves action.

Not every spontaneous sentence is secretly profound.

Sometimes my brain says stupid shit.

Sometimes speech-to-text says I am bipolar when I never said anything about being bipolar.

Sometimes a joke is just a joke.

Sometimes a song is just the next song.

Fine.

The interesting part is not that spontaneous output is always right.

The interesting part is that authorship feels much less centralized than I once assumed.

Thoughts appear.

Connections appear.

Memory appears.

Then "I" become aware of them.

## Who Wrote This Shit?

So now I sometimes reread something I wrote and laugh.

There it is.

A sentence I did not plan.

A connection I did not search for.

A memory I did not know I still had.

The conscious narrator arrives.

Looks at the page.

Raises an eyebrow.

> Who wrote this shit?

Apparently:

Me.

Just not necessarily the part of me that thinks it is in charge.

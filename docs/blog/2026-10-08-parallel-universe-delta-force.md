# Apparently My Parallel-Universe Job Is Delta Force

I have developed a theory about my parallel-universe career.

Delta Force operator.

This is not me claiming I secretly belong in Delta Force.

Jesus Christ.

I am a software engineer driving around Bulgaria buying routers I do not need and installing operating systems for entertainment.

The joke is about the trait cluster.

Because every once in a while I look at the collection and think:

> In another life, somebody absolutely dropped this idiot out of a helicopter and told him to figure it out.

<!-- truncate -->

The first part is adaptability.

Drop me somewhere unfamiliar.

Take away the instructions.

Give me questionable equipment.

Introduce a logistical problem nobody planned for.

Eventually I will figure something out.

Maybe not the approved solution.

Maybe not the elegant solution.

But we are probably moving again.

That has been true with computers.

Cars.

Houses.

Networks.

Random broken shit.

Travel.

Bureaucracy.

People.

Whatever.

The operating assumption in my head is rarely:

> I don't know how to do this.

It is usually:

> I don't know how to do this **yet**.

That distinction has caused a tremendous amount of unnecessary activity.

## Drive Whatever Is Here

Then there is the driving.

I have driven enough different things that I tend to treat vehicles the same way I treat unfamiliar software.

Where are the controls?

What does this one do differently?

How big is it?

Where are the edges?

Fine.

Let's go.

Cars.

Vans.

Motorcycles.

E-bikes.

Whatever is available.

People have commented before that I seem to know where the vehicle ends.

I can put the van stupidly close to a fence.

I can feel where the right tire is relative to potholes.

None of this makes me special forces.

It does, however, contribute to the parallel-universe casting decision.

Apparently Alternate Ben has transport proficiency.

Excellent.

## MacGyver Shit

Then there is the repair layer.

Something breaks.

I look at it.

I poke it.

I search.

I take it apart.

I improvise something.

Sometimes the result is elegant.

Sometimes the result would make the original engineer file a restraining order.

But it works.

This is not because I am a master mechanic, electrician, network engineer, carpenter, plumber, and everything else simultaneously.

I am not.

It is because I am unusually comfortable entering the state of:

> I have no fucking idea what is happening here. Let's find out.

That state does not scare me very much.

It is almost recreational.

## Privacy by Default

Then I was watching somebody from Proton talk about privacy.

The usual recommendations started appearing.

Encrypted DNS.

Local DNS.

Blocking.

Privacy tools.

Reduce data leakage.

Compartmentalize things.

Control your own infrastructure where it matters.

And I am sitting there thinking:

> Motherfucker, I already do all this shit.

Not because somebody gave me a tactical privacy checklist.

Because over time I kept asking:

> Why is this request going there?

> Why does this service need to know that?

> Can I resolve this myself?

> Can I block this upstream?

> Can I keep this local?

> What exactly is the box doing?

So now there is DNSCrypt.

Unbound.

Pi-hole-type thinking.

VPNs.

Hardware tools.

Flippers.

Random network shit.

And then somebody makes a privacy video and accidentally describes half the junk I already assembled.

Again:

Parallel-universe casting department takes notes.

## The Spy Problem

This is also where the recurring spy joke becomes entertaining.

People have thought I was a spy.

Different people.

Different ages.

Different countries.

No coordinated conspiracy required.

Apparently if you combine:

- too much technical knowledge;
- privacy habits;
- weird travel history;
- comfort around machines;
- random operational competence;
- asking oddly specific questions;
- not explaining yourself unless necessary;

some percentage of humanity eventually arrives at:

> Spy?

No.

Mostly just nerd.

But I understand how the bug occurs.

## Detachment Helps

There is another part that has nothing to do with equipment.

Detachment.

People.

Outcomes.

Plans.

I can care enormously about something and still accept that the thing may disappear.

I can plan and still change the plan.

I can want an outcome and then operate when reality gives me another one.

That is useful in ordinary life.

It is also one of the reasons the parallel-universe joke works in my head.

The environment changes.

Fine.

What is true **now**?

What do we have?

What can we do with it?

Next problem.

This is not emotional emptiness.

It is operational flexibility.

The mission changed.

Update state.

Continue.

## Technomancer With a Driver's License

The more I think about it, though, "Delta Force operator" may be only half the job title.

The other half is technomancer.

Because the same trait that says:

> Drop me somewhere and I'll figure it out.

also says:

> The router says it supports VPN, but what does that actually mean?

and:

> Monterey is the highest supported version? That's adorable.

and:

> Yes, I have Parallels. No, I want Windows, Linux, and macOS bare metal.

Same impulse.

Different terrain.

One is a broken thing in the physical world.

One is a software boundary.

One is a network.

One is a vehicle.

One is a conversation with a confused shopkeeper.

All of them start with:

> What can this actually do?

## This Is Not a Resume

To be clear, this would be a terrible actual military resume.

**Candidate strengths:**

Can configure DNS.

Can parallel park a van.

Has Opinions About Router Firmware.

Will absolutely reinstall the operating system.

Can probably make the broken appliance work, but there may be screws left over.

Has been accused of being a spy by civilians.

Seems promising.

Please issue classified clearance immediately.

No.

The comedy is not that I secretly possess elite military credentials.

The comedy is that I keep finding the same behavioral pattern expressed in completely unrelated parts of life.

Adapt.

Observe.

Figure out what the real constraints are.

Ignore fake constraints.

Use what is available.

Learn the missing piece.

Move.

## Parallel-Universe Ben

So yes.

In this universe I write software.

Build absurd systems.

Drive around Sofia.

Buy cheap computers.

Replace routers.

Run my own DNS infrastructure.

Triple-boot machines because I get bored.

Fix random things.

Talk to strangers.

And occasionally wonder why somebody has decided I am a spy again.

In another universe?

Same asshole.

Different job description.

Probably still arguing with the equipment.

Probably still asking what the actual limitation is.

Probably still ignoring the manual until the manual becomes relevant.

And somewhere, some exhausted team lead is looking at the new guy and thinking:

> Who the fuck gave the technomancer a rifle?

Good question.

Different universe.

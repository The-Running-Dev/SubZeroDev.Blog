# Please Stop Explaining Me to Me

There is a conversational sequence I keep encountering.

I say something about myself.

Somebody asks why.

I explain.

Then they disagree with the explanation.

Not with a factual claim about the weather.

Not with an interpretation of a movie.

With my explanation of **my own thoughts**.

And now we are apparently debating it.

<!-- truncate -->

This is where I become confused.

You asked.

I answered.

What exactly is the next phase?

Appeal?

Cross-examination?

Expert testimony?

## "But You Are..."

Nationality is where this happens most obviously.

I say I do not strongly identify with some label.

Someone says:

> "But you were born there."

Yes.

> "But your parents are from there."

Also yes.

> "So you are that."

You are perfectly free to describe me that way.

The problem begins when the classification turns into an instruction.

It is no longer:

> "That is how I think of you."

It becomes:

> "That is how you must think of yourself."

Why?

You have known me for ten minutes.

I have been inside this particular nervous system the entire time.

I feel like I have slightly better telemetry.

## You Asked Me to Explain

The other strange part is the escalation.

I do not generally begin these conversations by presenting a forty-seven-slide identity architecture deck.

Somebody asks.

I give the short version.

They push.

I explain more.

Then the explanation gives them additional material to argue with.

Now we have gone from:

> "Where are you from?"

to:

> "Let me explain why your interpretation of your own life is incorrect."

This seems like a poor use of both our time.

## Classification Is Fine

I am not offended by classification.

Classify away.

Bulgarian.

American.

Foreigner.

Programmer.

Architect.

Entrepreneur.

Unemployed lunatic with too many repositories.

Whatever makes the database happy.

The label itself is not the problem.

The problem is the insistence that the label must override the person.

If you say:

> "I think of you as Bulgarian."

Fine.

If you say:

> "No, you are Bulgarian and you are wrong about yourself."

Now we are doing something completely different.

You are no longer describing me.

You are attempting to administer me.

## I Do Not Need to Be Resolved

Maybe this is why I have become less willing to explain.

Not because the explanation is secret.

Not because I cannot articulate it.

Because I have learned that explanations are often mistaken for invitations to debate.

They are not.

Sometimes:

> "This is how I think about it."

means exactly that.

It does not secretly mean:

> "Please locate the logical weakness in my self-description and correct me."

There may not even be a final answer.

People change.

Context changes.

Language changes.

What feels important changes.

I may describe myself differently five years from now.

That is not a bug.

## Get to Know Me

This is increasingly my preferred answer.

Do not demand the executive summary.

Get to know me.

Listen long enough.

Watch what I do.

Find out where I have lived.

Find out what I care about.

Find out how I work.

Find out which languages fall out of my mouth depending on who I talked to five minutes ago.

Then form whatever opinion you like.

You were going to anyway.

Just please do not ask me who I am and then, after I answer, explain to me why I am mistaken.

That is a profoundly strange first-date activity.

And I am no longer accepting support tickets for my own identity.

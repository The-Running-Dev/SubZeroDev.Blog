# The Reply I Deleted

I wrote the reply. Then I deleted it. There was nothing particularly peaceful about the process.

<!-- truncate -->

My brother's email made me furious.

He had read the answer I had spent nearly two years writing. He appreciated the context. He had made peace with things. He still preferred distance. Maybe after Elizabeth died, we could have a family reunion.

I had plenty to say.

I wrote about the contradiction I heard in offering to be there while choosing not to maintain contact. I brought up his own statement that love is a verb. I questioned what a reunion years later was supposed to repair.

The words became harsher as I kept talking about it.

There were versions full of insults. There was a final goodbye available. There was a particularly cruel sentence about dealing with Elizabeth and her death alone.

I need to preserve one fact very clearly.

I did not send that reply.

For a while, I saved a draft and decided I would leave it overnight. Later I deleted it. I deleted his email too.

The sharpest things I said while talking through it were not a transcript of a message my brother received.

There is a difference between what I felt, what I considered writing, what I wrote, and what I actually chose to send. In this case, the last category was empty.

I had already sent my account and the earlier short response. I sent nothing further after this email.

We almost missed the Stoic part of that.

I kept focusing on the fact that I was angry, on how badly the email had affected me, and on whether I had been an idiot to answer him in the first place.

But the anger had not pressed Send.

I had time to act on it. I wrote the words. I looked at what I was doing and chose to delete them.

That was a choice.

I do not mean that I became calm and delivered some polished speech about acceptance. I was still swearing. I was still revisiting the same sentences. I was still capable of imagining a response much harsher than the one I had already sent.

I am not proud of every sentence that occurred to me.

I am willing to say they occurred to me.

My practice of Stoicism has never made me a person who stops feeling things. There are days I can talk at length about what I control and then spend an equally impressive amount of time wishing somebody else had made a different choice.

This was one of those days.

What I could control was whether another email left my account.

It did not.

And it still hurt.

There is an irritating temptation to finish this story with relief. I deleted the message, reclaimed my peace, and walked off into the sunset as the village's officially certified philosopher.

No. Fucking no.

I spent more time talking about it. I questioned myself. I remembered good times with my brother and wondered what any of the effort had accomplished. I felt foolish for reopening something I had forgotten could hurt this much.

The grief did not disappear because I had made one deliberate decision.

I can care deeply and detach completely. I keep trying to practice what that means. It does not mean a switch that removes affection, memory, anger, or disappointment from my body.

On this occasion, detachment included leaving the response unsent.

I wanted him to understand. I wished his answer had been different. Neither gave me control over what he had chosen.

The draft was something I could choose about.

I deleted it.

That is the whole action. The rest was still there afterward.

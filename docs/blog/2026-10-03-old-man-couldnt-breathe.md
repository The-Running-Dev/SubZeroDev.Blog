The doctor was talking to me about blood pressure.

Medication.

Tests.

Sleep.

The usual cheerful list of things that happen when the human body decides it would like to become infrastructure.

At one point he told me about somebody who died because they did not get medical care.

My immediate thought was:

**I'm not going to live that long, doctor.**

I did not say it.

But I thought it.

<!-- truncate -->

Not dramatically.

Not as some declaration.

Just matter-of-factly.

I'm not particularly invested in maximizing the number.

That is different from wanting to die.

It is just indifference to the spreadsheet.

Still, I went to the doctor.

Still got the medication.

Still took the Atarax.

Still watched the blood pressure stay annoyingly high.

Still planned the tests and then fucked them up because I got up too late and ate during the night.

So apparently even indifference comes with maintenance.

Fine.

## The Other Old Man

Then I picked up the Sprinter.

The old man had been in for service.

Oil.

Filters.

Various expensive adult responsibilities.

And one hose.

One fucking hose.

I got behind the wheel and immediately thought:

**Holy shit.**

The thing had pep.

Actual boost.

It felt like a different vehicle.

Turns out the turbo had not been working properly because the system was losing pressure.

The turbo was there.

The engine was there.

The old bastard had not suddenly become incapable of moving.

It just could not fucking breathe.

This is not even my first encounter with this particular diesel comedy.

I had a Honda Civic 2.2 diesel before.

Intercooler problems.

Boost problems.

I replaced the goddamn intercooler enough times that it became less a repair and more a subscription service.

Different vehicle.

Different plumbing.

Same fucking joke.

Pressure escapes.

Turbo becomes decorative.

Vehicle feels old.

Fix the leak.

Suddenly the old man gets up and moves.

## Apparently We Are Both Under Maintenance

This is where the universe gets a little too fucking cute.

The day before, I am sitting in front of a doctor thinking:

**I'm not going to live that long.**

The next day I am driving an old Sprinter I had mentally filed under:

Well, this is just how the old bastard is now.

And then somebody replaces a hose.

Boost comes back.

Whole new fucking vehicle.

Now, I am not about to turn this into some motivational metaphor where the Sprinter teaches me that life is precious and every dawn is a gift.

Get the fuck out of here.

The van had a boost leak.

I have high blood pressure.

These are not the same mechanical system.

But the timing is funny.

Because sometimes "old and tired" turns out to mean:

**something specific is fucked.**

And once somebody finds the specific fucked thing, the machine behaves differently.

The doctor is doing the same thing in considerably less entertaining fashion.

Pressure is wrong.

Sleep is wrong.

Tests need doing.

Medication gets prescribed.

We see what changes.

No grand meaning required.

No inspirational conclusion.

No promise of longevity.

Just maintenance.

Apparently the Sprinter and I are both still in service.

The difference is that his problem was easier to diagnose.

**Old man wasn't tired.**

**Old man couldn't fucking breathe.**

# That's Just What Happened

People keep hearing my stories and asking:

"How the fuck did you get through that?"

I genuinely don't understand the question.

The thing happened. I dealt with whatever was in front of me.

What else was I going to do?

<!-- truncate -->

## Five Days, One Honda

Take the red Honda.

I'm driving from Barcelona to Bulgaria. In Italy the intercooler falls off and I drive over it.

Now the car doesn't have proper turbo operation.

It still moves.

Later, in Serbia, the police point out I'm producing enough black smoke to make the motorway dangerous. They throw me off the highway.

Fine. Drive through villages.

Tire blows.

Three strangers show up and help me change it. One feeds me. I'm falling asleep at his restaurant table, so he offers me a couch, provided I leave at seven in the morning.

Fine. Sleep. Leave at seven.

Serbian border guards want money for coffee, but not where the cameras can see.

€20.

Eventually I reach Bulgaria.

Usually that's a two-day drive. That one took about five.

People hear this and react like I must have endured some spiritual trial.

I just drove the fucking car.

## An Audi in France

Another time the electronic transmission in my €2,000 Audi A6 died in southern France.

No forward. No reverse.

I checked: €3,000 to bring it back to Spain, €2,000 to fix it.

Why would I spend €5,000 recovering a €2,000 car?

I called for a ride, took my belongings and license plates, and left it in the tow yard.

People call that *abandoning a car in France*.

Which, to be fair, is exactly what it was.

But the decision wasn't arbitrary.

It wasn't worth recovering.

## The BMW Monday

Then the BMW died roughly five kilometers outside Sofia.

No phone. No money. Walk five kilometers. Borrow phone. Tow truck. Can't pay. Borrow money from a waitress. Miss two trains. Find a taxi. Try to access cash. Pay €100. Get home twelve hours after leaving.

Two weeks later, people heard this and said:

"Oh, that's terrible."

Is it?

That happened one Monday.

A month later, it was already a story that made me laugh.

## Not Resilience

Somebody could take these examples and produce a motivational article about resilience.

I'm not interested in that.

I wasn't collecting adversities to overcome. I'm not retroactively labeling each incident good or bad to demonstrate character.

I don't need that story.

The car died. I was somewhere. I needed to go somewhere else. So I dealt with what was in front of me.

And in the next situation, I'll do whatever the next situation requires—or I'll find out what happens.

That doesn't mean every choice I've made was sensible. Some of them were spectacularly fucking questionable.

It just means I don't need every event to have a verdict attached.

I look back and think:

**Well, that happened.**

Not a lesson.

Not a victory.

Not a tragedy.

Life.

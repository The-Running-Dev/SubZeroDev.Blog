# The Country I Wish I Could Go Home To

Why do you think I left fucking America?

<!-- truncate -->

I was disillusioned with politics. With people. With money and what it seemed to do to relationships. Trump wasn't the whole explanation. There were people I had known, institutions I had encountered, experiences I couldn't simply set aside.

And yet there is still a country I wish I could go home to.

America.

That is the part that refuses to become a clean story.

I became a citizen. Naturalization belongs in this history. So does being in jail in Texas. So do the people I encountered there, and the difference between talking about the American justice system from outside it and having an experience inside it.

I don't want to turn the people I met into scenery for a sweeping argument. I don't want to flatten everything into a claim that every person or institution is the same. But I also don't want the fact that I became an American used to make my disillusionment seem ungrateful or inexplicable.

The hope was part of it. That's why there was something to become disillusioned with.

And this isn't confined to politics. A friend can do a small, ugly thing over money and contribute to the same feeling that something I wanted to believe in isn't what I thought it was. A country is partly the people in it, including the people who tell you it's just business after the friendship stops mattering.

I know this doesn't happen only in America.

It happened to me there. That matters to the story of why I left.

Then there was Jon Stewart talking with Abdul El-Sayed.

I felt hope watching that interview. Enough that I nearly cried.

And then I stopped watching.

That is probably the most honest way to put those two things together. I could hear something that made me want to believe in the country again, and I could also feel how unwilling I was to go through another round of that hope.

I've heard compelling promises before.

I don't need to turn that into a prediction about this particular man. I don't know what the future holds. The reaction was mine: hope arriving, and all the reasons I had become suspicious of hope arriving with it.

Part of me wants to go home. Part of me doesn't believe the country has changed enough for me to want to return.

Those parts don't cancel each other out.

I don't have a speech about America finally redeeming itself. I don't have a declaration that I no longer care.

I have a country I left, a country I became a citizen of, and a country I still wish I could go home to.

I don't know how much those three countries overlap anymore.

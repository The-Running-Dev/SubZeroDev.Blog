# Forgiveness Does Not Mean Access

I can forgive somebody and still want absolutely nothing to do with them.

Apparently that requires explanation.

It doesn't to me.

<!-- truncate -->

Forgiveness isn't reconciliation. It isn't trust. It isn't amnesia. It doesn't mean somebody gets to wander back into my life because I said hello and smiled.

I forgave you.

That's the entire statement.

## Have a Nice Day

I recently ran into a relative with whom I have a long and unpleasant history.

I said hello. I asked how she was doing. I laughed at something. I wished her a nice day.

And I meant it.

I wasn't surrendering. I wasn't signaling that everything had been repaired. I wasn't pretending I couldn't remember the last several years.

I was simply being pleasant to somebody I no longer have a relationship with.

Why does every fucking interaction have to become a diplomatic communiqué?

Sometimes `Have a nice day` means precisely that.

Have a nice day.

I'll have mine.

## The Version of Me They Remember

For most of my life, I've been the guy who helps.

Need a car? Take mine.

Need money? Here.

Need something fixed? I'll figure it out.

The people involved know this. They've said it themselves.

I wasn't buying loyalty. I liked them. They were family. Helping was easy for me, and I did it.

Then years of things happened, and my relationship with them changed.

What I find interesting now is that they appear to be operating with the old version of me: the nice cousin, the accommodating one, the one who makes things easier.

And in some sense they're correct. I'm still perfectly capable of being nice.

But nice doesn't mean available.

Being civil doesn't mean the old arrangement has been restored.

I don't need to write a migration guide for their mental model of me.

## "Call Me If You Need Anything"

One of them recently offered:

"Call me whenever you need anything."

Sure.

You'll be the first person I call.

That's the joke in my head. I don't need to deliver it.

The offer lands against a very long history, including previous suggestions that I should move out of a house into a sixth-floor attic with poor insulation and an outdoor bathroom. With a dog.

A wonderful lifestyle improvement, apparently.

I saw that suggestion as an attempt to make me leave the house while presenting it as concern for me. That's how it landed, and that's part of what I remember when new friendliness appears.

So no, hearing "call me if you need anything" doesn't suddenly make that person someone I intend to depend on.

I can hear the sentence, be polite, and go back to my day.

## Memory Isn't Hatred

People seem to connect forgiveness with forgetting.

I don't.

Memory is information.

Some experiences stick without any deliberate effort from me. I wasn't keeping a case file. I wasn't building a spreadsheet of offenses. Years later something happens and my brain throws an old conversation onto the screen.

Oh. That again.

Sometimes I don't even understand how my memory connected the events. The pattern just appears sideways.

I can remember an event without needing to relive the anger.

I can notice familiar behavior without launching an investigation.

I can have a theory about what somebody is doing and still decide not to do anything about it.

**Recognizing something does not require participating in it.**

That may be the most useful distinction I've stumbled across lately.

## I Was Angry Too

I've sent messages in anger. I've sworn at people. I've said things current-me wouldn't say.

I know that.

I was angry at the time. That's what happened.

Would I make the same choice now?

Probably not.

I don't need to retrofit regret into the memory, or declare every word justified, or put the person I was on trial.

I made that choice then.

I wouldn't make it now.

There isn't a replay button.

Forgiving myself belongs in the same category as forgiving somebody else. It doesn't change what happened. It changes what I carry forward.

## No Ceremony Required

I used to think these things required a final confrontation.

We sit down. We talk. We establish who did what. Somebody acknowledges the past. Maybe somebody apologizes. Then we resolve the relationship one way or another.

That sounds exhausting now.

I don't need an apology to go about my life.

I don't need a confession.

I don't need somebody to agree with my account of events.

And I certainly don't need to reconcile with every person I've forgiven.

Family titles remain what they are. A cousin is a cousin. An aunt is an aunt. Biology and history don't grant permanent access to my present.

I can see you.

I can smile.

I can wish you well.

I can mean every word.

And I can keep walking.

**Forgiveness is mine. Access is a different fucking question.**

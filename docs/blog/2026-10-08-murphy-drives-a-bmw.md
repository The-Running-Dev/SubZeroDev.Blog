# Murphy Drives a BMW

I owned a BMW 7 Series for what felt like approximately fifteen fucking minutes.

Not literally. But compared with the motorcycle that lasted nineteen years, this BMW barely got an introduction.

Ivan had worried about all sorts of small mechanical things, fixed them, and sent me off with the car.

Then I introduced the car to Uncle Ben driving conditions.

<!-- truncate -->

## Let Me Show You an Asshole

One evening I was returning from Sofia. Some guy in a van started riding my ass.

I could have ignored him.

Instead I thought, more or less: oh, you're going to drive like an asshole? Let me show you what a competent asshole looks like.

So I blocked him when he tried to pass. Yes, it was dickhead behavior on my part.

He got furious, pulled his van across the road, stopped, got out, and apparently decided we were going to fight.

I didn't stop for a meeting.

I swerved around him and accelerated away. He chased me for a while.

At some point I was doing around 200 km/h. Then the BMW started making a strange exhaust noise.

"Shit. Maybe I should slow down."

So I did.

The car seemed fine afterward.

## Monday

The next day I drove again.

About five kilometers south of Sofia, the BMW died.

Engine done.

No phone with me. No cash. Fabulous.

I walked roughly five kilometers until I found someone at a local business who would let me use a phone. Arranged a tow truck. Waited.

Tow truck eventually came.

New problem: I couldn't pay him.

I borrowed money from a waitress at the hookah lounge. Yes, that is now part of the BMW's service history.

The car reached a repair shop.

Now I needed to get home.

## Two Trains and a Taxi

Missed the first train.

Missed the second train.

At this point a taxi became the plan, except I still had the money problem.

There was some arrangement involving the taxi driver's friend: I could pay electronically and obtain cash. The friend didn't trust me. So another little expedition to a bank became necessary.

Eventually I paid the taxi driver about €100 to take me home, a journey of roughly forty kilometers.

He was delighted.

I had left at about 11 in the morning.

I got home at about 11 at night.

Twelve hours to go nowhere particularly useful, except to place one dead BMW in a repair shop.

## That's What Happened

A couple of weeks later I told the story to people.

"Oh, that's terrible."

Was it?

I drove like a dickhead. The car made noises. The engine died. I walked. Borrowed a phone. Got a tow. Borrowed money. Missed trains. Found a taxi. Went home.

I'm not asking anybody to call it heroic or resilient. I'm not even particularly interested in calling it terrible.

**That's what happened that Monday.**

And then, because apparently Murphy has a sense of humor, a replacement BMW engine later appeared out of nowhere.

Murphy, you beautiful bastard.

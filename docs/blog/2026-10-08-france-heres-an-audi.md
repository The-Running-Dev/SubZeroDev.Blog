# France, Here's an Audi

I once abandoned an Audi A6 in France.

People hear this and imagine I pulled over, got bored, and walked away.

No. I did the fucking arithmetic.

<!-- truncate -->

## The Acquisition

I bought the Audi from a friend of Uncle Mario's in Bulgaria. About €2,000. Supposed to be a good car. Extra wheels and tires were supposedly included, although I never actually got that part of the deal.

The mission was familiar: load my belongings and drive to Spain, where I was living south of Valencia.

At the Serbian border, customs noticed a television in the back. It was about five years old.

"Where is the receipt?"

A receipt. For an old fucking television.

They suggested they might confiscate it.

"Take it, then."

One officer proceeded to shake my translucent plastic luggage bins. I could see what was in them. He could see what was in them. I'm still not sure what the shaking was intended to establish.

They let me go.

## German Engineering Meets Uncle Ben

Somewhere in Italy I drove the aging Audi at around 180 km/h.

I even thought that perhaps I shouldn't be driving a €2,000 car this way.

But it's an Audi, right? Nice transmission. Whatever.

Then it started grinding.

My response, in essence: it grinds. So fucking what? Still moving.

In southern France, the transmission finally finished the argument.

No forward.

No reverse.

Nothing.

Tow truck. Yard. Tiny French village. Me, my belongings, and a dead Audi.

## The Spreadsheet

I checked the numbers.

- Purchase price: approximately €2,000.
- Transport the dead car back to Spain: approximately €3,000.
- Replace or repair the transmission: approximately €2,000.

Five thousand euros to save a two-thousand-euro car.

No.

I phoned a friend in Spain. He found someone with a furgoneta willing to make a long rescue drive. About nine hours of thoroughly enthusiastic driving later, the guy arrived.

I went back to the yard, recovered my luggage, took the license plates off the Audi, and left it.

The yard subsequently tried to find me because, understandably, it still had an Audi.

It could keep the fucking thing.

I still have the plates. I should probably return them.

## The French Village Side Quest

While stranded, I found a hotel, a McDonald's, and people to talk to. I had drinks, talked bullshit with the bartender, and enjoyed myself.

Then a random woman contacted me on Discord.

Chat. Photos exchanged. A dick picture from me.

Half an hour later: a collage of my face and genitals, plus a demand for roughly $500 or it would go on Instagram.

"Go ahead."

That was the negotiation.

Somehow the same French village contained both a dead Audi asking for €5,000 worth of rescue and a scammer asking for $500 worth of silence.

I declined both.

## Spain

The rescue van got me and my luggage back to Spain.

I wasn't even without transportation once home. The Mazda was there. So was the motorcycle.

The Audi had ceased being worth anything *to me*. The tow yard was free to disagree.

**France, here's an Audi.**

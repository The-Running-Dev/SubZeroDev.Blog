# Who the Fuck Am I to Judge?

At some point during an evening that had already involved a Ridgeback, an electrical cord, a dead hypothetical dog, a slaughtered lamb, humanity, consequences, and Viktor fucking Frankl, I landed on another question.

Who the fuck am I to judge any of this?

The dog is a dog.

People are people.

I am me.

Everybody is apparently doing whatever their particular machinery produces.

So who appointed me?

<!-- truncate -->

Nobody.

That is the obvious answer.

There was no ceremony.

No robe.

No cosmic badge.

No universal court issued me jurisdiction over whether the Ridgeback is a good dog, whether another person is a good person, whether humanity is good, whether I am good.

Yet judgment happens automatically.

That was stupid.

That was cruel.

That was selfish.

That crossed a line.

That person should know better.

That dog should know better.

I should know better.

Then comes the fun part.

I judge myself for judging.

Excellent.

We have now built recursion.

## The Ridgeback Court

The dog chewed through an electrical cord.

I called her.

She stayed away.

I decided she knew enough about the situation to understand that something had gone badly wrong.

Maybe she did.

Maybe she did not.

Then I put her outside.

Was I judging her?

In one sense, absolutely.

I evaluated behavior.

I decided the behavior was unacceptable.

I changed the conditions around her.

That is judgment in the ordinary mechanical sense.

But I was not issuing a verdict on the essence of Dog.

I was not saying:

> You are fundamentally corrupt and shall henceforth be known as Electrical Cord Sinner.

She did some shit.

I responded to the shit.

Those are different things.

## People Make This Confusing

Humans love turning behavior into identity.

You lied.

Therefore you are a liar.

You acted selfishly.

Therefore you are selfish.

You failed.

Therefore you are a failure.

You hurt me.

Therefore you are a bad person.

Sometimes those labels describe persistent patterns.

Sometimes they are shortcuts.

Sometimes they are weapons.

But they all perform the same compression:

A complicated creature becomes a verdict.

I increasingly distrust verdicts.

Not observations.

Not boundaries.

Not consequences.

Verdicts.

## Judgment Versus Boundary

This distinction matters to me.

I can say:

> I will not accept this behavior.

without saying:

> I possess the authority to define your soul.

I can say:

> If you do this, I leave.

without saying:

> You are irredeemable.

I can say:

> The dog is sleeping outside tonight.

without saying:

> The dog is evil.

Apparently this sounds obvious when written down.

It is much harder when the nervous system is involved.

Anger wants compression.

Pain wants certainty.

The brain would love to reduce somebody to the thing they just did.

Much easier file format.

One label.

No ambiguity.

## The Lamb Complicates It

Then I think about the lamb.

I can feel horrified imagining a young animal being slaughtered.

I can judge the practice.

I can judge the people.

Tradition.

Cruelty.

Humanity.

Then I remember I eat meat.

Ah.

There I am.

Back inside the system I was judging.

That does not mean all distinctions collapse.

It does mean moral elevation gets awkward very quickly.

Humans are spectacular at criticizing contradictions we personally express in a slightly different configuration.

I am no exception.

## The People In My Life

This gets harder with people I actually know.

Family.

Friends.

People who hurt me.

People I have hurt.

I can describe what happened.

I can decide what I will tolerate.

I can decide whether I participate.

I can walk away.

But eventually I hit the same wall:

Who the fuck am I to decide what this person **is**?

I barely understand what I am.

And I have twenty-four-hour administrative access.

## The Recursive Trap

Then comes the ugliest move.

I notice myself judging.

I dislike that.

So I judge myself.

> What kind of person thinks this?

> Who am I to condemn them?

> I should be better than this.

Congratulations.

The court has reconvened.

Same judge.

New defendant.

This is why the thought became funny in a dark way.

If judgment itself is the offense, condemning myself for judging merely repeats the offense with additional paperwork.

There is no exit there.

## Maybe Observation Is Enough

I do not need to decide what somebody fundamentally is.

Maybe I only need to know what happened and what I am going to do about it.

You did X.

I experienced Y.

My boundary is Z.

That is enough information to operate.

The universe does not require the additional sentence:

> Therefore you are a bad creature.

Sometimes behavior is dangerous.

Sometimes it is cruel.

Sometimes it is intolerable.

Naming that does not require pretending I occupy a position outside humanity.

I am right here in the mess.

Dog.

Lamb.

Family.

Me.

All of us running whatever strange combination of instinct, history, fear, appetite, habit, thought, and circumstance got installed along the way.

## No Verdict

That is probably where I land.

Not "never judge."

Impossible.

The mind evaluates constantly.

Not "everything is acceptable."

Also nonsense.

Boundaries still exist.

Consequences still exist.

Some behavior gets you removed from my life.

Some behavior gets the Ridgeback removed from the bedroom for a night.

But I do not need to convert every boundary into a cosmic verdict.

I can say:

> This happened.

> I do not accept it.

> This is what I am doing next.

And stop there.

No halo.

No gavel.

No claim to moral superiority.

Just another fucked-up human being trying to navigate other fucked-up human beings and one especially muscular fucked-up dog.

Who the fuck am I to judge?

Nobody special.

Which may be exactly why I should be careful with the verdicts.

# Go Where You're Celebrated, Not Where You're Tolerated

I received a Viber message from my godmother, Yanka.

Unexpectedly.

And it hurt more than I would have expected a message to hurt.

Because there was a whole relationship sitting behind it. Some of it real. Some of it something I'd spent years hoping would become real.

Apparently those can hurt just as much.

<!-- truncate -->

## The People Behind the Message

Gocho was my father's friend. That connection goes back to childhood, to Dimitrovgrad, to people who belonged to my life long before I was making conscious decisions about who belonged there.

Yanka was my godmother.

That meant something to me.

I wanted a relationship with them. I wanted there to be interest, conversation, some feeling that they wanted to know the person I actually was.

And there's a sadness in admitting how much of a relationship existed in the wanting.

I wasn't just reacting to what Yanka had written. I was grieving something I'd hoped we would have.

The fucking possibility of it.

## A Letter Through Somebody Else

The contact involved a physical letter from my mother, routed through Yanka.

My mother has direct ways to contact me. Addresses. Email.

Yet here was this letter coming through my godmother.

I don't know why she chose that route. I can think of explanations. Thinking of an explanation doesn't make it a fact.

What I do know is how it landed with me.

Someone with whom I didn't have the relationship I'd wanted was now involved in my relationship with my mother.

And Yanka's appeal centered on motherhood.

On the fact that this person was my mother.

I know she's my mother. That information was already available.

What I wanted was for somebody to listen to my experience of that relationship. To be curious about how things had reached this point. To recognize that there was a person receiving the appeal, with his own history and reasons.

Instead, I felt the role had been offered as the answer before my experience had been heard.

Mother.

Therefore something was expected of me.

That's how I heard it. I can't tell you everything Yanka intended by it. But it left me feeling that my part in the relationship mattered more than what living it had been like for me.

## Who Was Actually in Contact?

There was something else I couldn't keep overlooking.

They had maintained close contact with my mother.

They hadn't maintained that kind of contact with me.

That's the behavior I was looking at.

It wasn't an argument about who they were allowed to talk to. They can have whatever relationship they want with her.

It was information about the relationship they had with me.

I kept wanting interest and closeness that I wasn't experiencing. Then contact arrived around my mother and what I should do about her.

That hurt.

It also helped me see why I was so sad. I'd kept a place open for something, and the fact that I kept it open didn't mean anybody else was coming to occupy it.

## The Explanation I Didn't Send

So I started writing.

An explanation. The history. Why this hurt. Why an appeal to motherhood didn't settle anything. Why their own pattern of contact mattered to me.

I revised it.

Then revised it again.

I wanted to be understood. I wanted to make it clear enough that somebody would finally see what I was trying to say.

And somewhere in the rewriting I recognized the familiar work I was doing.

Justifying myself.

Trying to find the version of the explanation that would make another person engage with me in the way I wanted.

Maybe a message could have opened a different conversation. I don't know. I didn't send it, so I don't get to claim I know how they would have responded.

But writing it had already helped me understand something. Sending it was a separate decision.

I decided to leave it unsent.

I could be sad without making one more attempt to explain my way into the relationship.

## What I Can Know

I can't look inside these people.

I don't know everything they feel about me. Affection may exist that I haven't experienced. Their idea of care may be different from mine. There may be parts of this I don't understand.

I can leave that possibility open.

I also have to live with the relationship as it happens.

Contact. Interest. Listening. The things people actually do.

Whatever somebody feels privately, I experience their care through what reaches me. And I hadn't experienced the closeness I kept hoping for.

Remembering a phrase I'd heard somewhere helped me put words to that:

Go where you're celebrated, not where you're tolerated.

My conclusion was that, in this relationship, I had felt tolerated.

That's a painful conclusion about my experience. It isn't a discovery of their secret inner truth. It isn't a verdict on every relationship they've ever had or a rule everybody else must apply to their family.

It's where I arrived after looking at how this one had worked for me.

## The Part That Came Up Later

Later, I listened to a Buddhist lecture about rejection.

And older feelings came up.

Unworthiness.

The feeling that somebody else's lack of interest meant there was something wrong with me. That if I were somehow different, easier, better, the relationship might finally happen.

I could already see the pattern of contact. Understanding why it hurt reached me later.

That surprised me.

I'd made a decision. I hadn't finished feeling everything connected to it.

The lecture didn't tell me what my godparents felt. It helped me notice what I was making their behavior mean about me.

I don't have to keep making that meaning.

I can be sad about the relationship I wanted. I can admit I wanted it badly. I can leave their intentions uncertain and still decide that this is enough information for me to stop chasing it.

There was no final explanation to deliver.

I left the message unsent.

And let myself grieve.

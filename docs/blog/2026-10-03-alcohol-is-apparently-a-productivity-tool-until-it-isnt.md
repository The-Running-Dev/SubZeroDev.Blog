# Alcohol Is Apparently a Productivity Tool Until It Isn't

Here is another stupid thing I have noticed.

Sometimes I start drinking at home around five.

And for the next several hours I become weirdly productive.

Not imaginary productive.

Actual shit gets done.

I work on the computer.

I search for things.

I go outside.

I fix something.

I come back in.

I write.

I investigate some completely unrelated idea.

I have another drink.

Then I do more shit.

This can go on for hours.

And then, at some point, the productivity engine falls off a fucking cliff.

<!-- truncate -->

The drinking does not.

That is the interesting part.

## Phase One: Apparently We Are Doing Things

Early in the evening, alcohol is almost background process.

I am not sitting there staring lovingly into a glass.

I am moving around.

Computer.

Outside.

Search something.

Fix something.

Write something.

Read something.

Get distracted by some ridiculous idea.

Follow that.

Come back.

Have a drink.

Keep going.

From inside the experience, it can feel almost useful.

A little less friction.

A little more willingness to start things.

A little more:

> Fuck it, let's do that.

And because I actually complete things during that period, it is very easy to build a flattering story around it.

Look at me.

Productive drunk.

Wonderful.

Where is my fucking medal?

## The Problem With Evaluating the First Half

If the evening ended there, the story would be simple.

Have a few drinks.

Do some shit.

Go to sleep.

But that is not always what happens.

The dangerous accounting trick is evaluating the whole night based on the first few hours.

Five to nine:

Useful.

Nine to ten:

Still doing stuff.

Around ten:

Something changes.

And then suddenly we are no longer discussing productivity.

The computer becomes less interesting.

The outside tasks are done.

The searches become nonsense.

The useful part of the evening shuts down.

But the alcohol has not received the shutdown signal.

So the system keeps running.

## The Invisible Transition

That transition is the weird part because it does not arrive with a notification.

There is no Windows dialog.

> Productivity.exe has stopped responding.

No alarm.

No little dashboard telling me:

> You were doing useful shit twenty minutes ago. You are now just getting fucked up.

The activity simply thins out.

The drinking remains.

Earlier, each drink is embedded inside something else.

Later, the drink becomes the something else.

That is a meaningful change even though the glass looks exactly the same.

At seven:

Drink, then work.

At eight:

Drink, then go outside.

At nine:

Drink, then search something.

At eleven:

Drink.

Then drink.

Excellent feature progression.

## Alcohol Gets Credit for Everything Before the Crash

This is also where memory gets selective.

The next day I can look at the artifacts.

I wrote something.

Fixed something.

Researched something.

Generated three blog ideas.

Maybe built half a ridiculous prototype because somebody somewhere said something and my brain went:

> Watch me.

Those things are real.

So alcohol gets attached to them in the story.

I was drinking and I did all this shit.

Factually true.

But there is a missing second sentence:

I was also drinking after I stopped doing all that shit.

The same substance was present during both phases.

It does not get to claim exclusive authorship of the first one and deny responsibility for the second.

That would be some excellent fucking marketing.

## The Productivity Window Has an Expiration Date

What I seem to have is a window.

For a while, drinking and activity coexist.

Sometimes surprisingly well.

Then the window closes.

The problem is that the behavior does not automatically close with it.

There is no hard coupling between:

> useful phase ended

and:

> drinking ended.

That is the missing event.

If I were designing this as software, I would call it an obvious lifecycle bug.

We have a resource-intensive background process.

Its dependent workload has terminated.

The process continues consuming resources indefinitely.

Eventually the host becomes unstable.

Very professional.

## This Is Why Simple Labels Are Boring

People like clean stories.

Alcohol makes you useless.

Or alcohol makes you creative.

Or alcohol relaxes you.

Or alcohol destroys productivity.

Pick a sentence.

Put it on a poster.

Done.

My actual experience is more annoying.

Sometimes I drink and get shit done.

Sometimes I drink and write something I genuinely like.

Sometimes I become more willing to start tasks I had been putting off.

Sometimes I spend hours bouncing around doing useful things.

And then sometimes the same evening continues after all of that has ended and I just get completely wasted.

Both things can be true.

The first does not erase the second.

The second does not mean the first never happened.

This is why reality is such an inconvenient motherfucker.

It refuses to fit on the poster.

## The Bar Changes This Too

This also explains part of why drinking socially feels different.

At a bar, the activity does not disappear in the same way.

The environment keeps producing input.

People talk.

Somebody walks in.

Somebody says something ridiculous.

Music changes.

Conversation moves.

I read something.

I work on something.

There is always some external thing occupying the evening.

At home, eventually the tasks end.

The house gets quiet.

The interesting things I was doing stop being interesting.

And there is still alcohol.

So alcohol gets promoted.

Background process becomes primary workload.

That is when the architecture degrades.

## At Five, I Am Not Planning Midnight

This is probably the strangest part.

When I start at five, I am not necessarily thinking:

> Excellent. Tonight I shall drink until midnight and become fucking annihilated.

That is not the plan.

There may not even be a plan.

I have a drink.

I do something.

The evening continues.

The state changes incrementally.

Then at some point I am living inside a version of the evening that the five-o'clock version of me did not explicitly design.

That is why looking only at intent is not very useful.

The system has state.

Each drink modifies the next decision-maker.

Very elegant architecture.

Who approved this?

## Apparently There Are Two Different Evenings

So I think there are actually two evenings hiding inside one.

Evening one:

Drink.

Work.

Think.

Search.

Move around.

Make things.

Laugh at something.

Write down an idea.

Useful enough.

Then evening two starts without changing venues.

Drink.

Keep drinking.

No particular reason required.

The transition is not dramatic.

That may be exactly why it is easy to miss.

If I blacked out the moment I touched the first glass, the relationship would be incredibly simple.

That is not what happens.

Instead there is a long stretch where the whole thing can look functional enough to defend itself.

Then it changes.

## Until It Isn't

So yes.

Alcohol is apparently a productivity tool.

Until it isn't.

And the phrase matters because the second half is doing all the work.

The first half is seductive precisely because it is sometimes true.

I can point to things I made.

Things I fixed.

Things I discovered.

Ideas I had.

Work I finished.

Fine.

Then ten o'clock arrives.

The useful workload terminates.

The alcohol process keeps running.

And eventually the server is just on fire for no fucking reason.

There is apparently a ledger in every village.

Not a real ledger.

Nobody writes anything down.

That would be far too efficient.

It is an invisible accounting system for minor offenses, wounded pride, loose dogs, broken garden decorations, doors left open, fences left unpatched, and things somebody could have mentioned three weeks ago but decided to preserve for a more strategically useful moment.

<!-- truncate -->

I discovered another entry in mine today.

I was walking my dog.

She was on a leash.

A neighbor three or four houses down had left his gate open, so his little male dog was out in the street.

Normally, fine.

Dogs get out.

Mine has gotten out before.

Animals are not particularly interested in property law.

The problem was that mine is about to go into heat, and his dog had apparently decided this was the most important diplomatic summit of his life.

He kept trying to mount her.

So I chased him away.

That was it.

No dog was harmed.

No dramatic battle occurred.

I even put the little bastard back in his own yard.

He escaped again.

He came back.

I chased him away again.

This is when the neighbor arrived in his car and apparently interpreted me chasing his loose dog away from my dog as an international incident.

He backed up.

Started talking at me from the car.

Not really *to* me.

At me.

There is a distinction.

I tried to explain the extremely complicated situation.

> "Your dog keeps trying to mount mine."

That should have been the whole conversation.

Instead, the Village Grievance Ledger opened.

Apparently, weeks earlier, when my dog had gotten loose, she had gone into his yard and broken some ornaments.

This was news to me.

Not completely new news, actually.

I had heard about it secondhand.

Somebody else had told me he had complained.

At the time I thought:

Fine.

If the guy comes over and talks to me, I'll give him twenty bucks or whatever the stupid ornaments cost and we'll be done.

I wasn't even especially interested in whether the story was perfectly accurate.

My dog got loose.

If she broke something, okay.

Come knock on my door.

Say:

> "Hey, your dog broke this."

I say:

> "Shit. Sorry. What did it cost?"

We resolve it.

Adults have been doing this for thousands of years without the invention of distributed grievance storage.

But he never came.

Weeks went by.

Then suddenly, while his own dog was currently loose because his own gate was open, the ledger appeared.

> "When your dog got loose..."

Ah.

Here we go.

The historical record.

Apparently now I was supposed to become defensive.

Perhaps apologize for the entire canine species.

Perhaps explain the fence.

Perhaps accept moral responsibility for the suffering of decorative livestock.

Instead I started laughing.

Because the timing was magnificent.

His complaint was that my dog had once gotten loose and entered his yard.

His dog was currently loose in the street.

The gate was open.

I was chasing his dog away from mine.

And somehow the correct moment had arrived to litigate the Great Ornament Disaster of Several Weeks Ago.

I asked what this had to do with the thing happening right now.

Nothing, obviously.

That is not how the ledger works.

The point is not to resolve grievances when they occur.

The point is to save them.

Store them.

Age them properly.

Bring them out later like a bottle of wine nobody asked for.

There is something genuinely sad about this style of neighbor relationship.

We live three houses apart.

We are going to see each other.

Our dogs are going to smell each other through fences.

Nobody needs to become mortal enemies over a loose animal and a ceramic donkey.

Yet people will carry minor irritation around for weeks rather than walk fifty meters and have a thirty-second conversation.

That is how villages accumulate mythology.

By next spring there will probably be competing oral traditions.

In one version, my dog entered peacefully and the donkey collapsed from structural fatigue.

In another, she arrived like Genghis Khan and systematically destroyed the decorative agriculture.

Children will be warned not to speak of the Donkey Incident after dark.

And the funniest part is that I would probably have paid for the fucking thing.

That is what makes the entire episode perfect.

The money was never the problem.

The conversation was never difficult.

The man just had to come talk to me.

Instead he apparently complained to somebody else, carried it around, and finally deployed it during an unrelated encounter in which **his own dog was loose**.

A grown-ass man.

Weeks of grievance.

Three houses of distance.

One allegedly destroyed decorative donkey.

And no actual attempt to resolve any of it.

There is a Bulgarian phrase that lives in my head for this sort of thing:

> **Мрън-мрън, че пляс.**

I am not even claiming my interpretation is the official dictionary definition.

To me it has always carried the spirit of:

**Complain. Complain. Do fuck-all.**

Which, as it turns out, is a surprisingly efficient summary of the Village Grievance Ledger.

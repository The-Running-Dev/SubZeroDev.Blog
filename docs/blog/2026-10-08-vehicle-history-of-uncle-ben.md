# The Vehicle History of Uncle Ben

I've been thinking about all the vehicles I've owned.

At least three dead engines. One seized. One with no compression. One melted. An electronic transmission that died in France. A car that survived five days across Europe without its intercooler. And a motorcycle that put up with me for nineteen fucking years.

The funny part? I maintain my vehicles. I change oil. I've changed brakes. I can fix things.

This is still the historical record.

<!-- truncate -->

## The $300 Hyundai

Somewhere around 1995–1997, my parents bought a $300 Hyundai to teach my mother to drive stick. The passenger door didn't even open. She never learned.

My father gave me one driving lesson. It involved enough yelling that I decided there wouldn't be a second. I took the car and learned manual on my own.

I drove it through high school for about a year. Once, the muffler fell off on the way home. I got out, found a coat hanger, tied it back on, and continued.

Nothing major broke. Then my parents sold the car for $300 before college.

You could have just given me the fucking car.

## The $1,200 Plymouth Colt

A couple of years into college, my parents bought me a station-wagon-looking Plymouth Colt without asking me. Then they were upset I wasn't grateful.

I looked at the thing and said it wouldn't last six months.

It lasted about six months. Died while I was at college and was eventually disposed of.

The cheaper Hyundai had lasted longer.

## The Saturn SL2

For once, I bought my own car. Brand-new Saturn SL2, with my parents co-signing the loan. I made the payments while working throughout college; my mother covered one.

They were particularly offended that I spent another $200 for cruise control.

Right before my wedding in 2004, I glanced at my phone while approaching stopped traffic and rear-ended an enormous old 1970s car. My airbags deployed. The other driver apparently didn't even stop.

Insurance totaled the Saturn. I salvaged my aftermarket equipment, settled what remained on the loan, and came out with roughly $1,000.

## The $700 Ford Escort

I took some of that money and bought a 1997 Ford Escort from my then-wife's parents for $700.

Three big dogs in the back. Hair everywhere. Starting it could become a production. The warning light was practically standard equipment.

At one point I paid $350, labor included, for a refurbished engine. Half the price of the car.

Later I kept driving because the warning light had been on for so long that it no longer told me anything. One day the engine made a noise and seized. It had run out of oil.

**First confirmed replacement-engine casualty.**

## Two Civic Sis

After my divorce I bought a Honda Civic Si: naturally aspirated, roughly 197 horsepower, and a lot of fun. I drove it like a fucking maniac. Eventually the cylinders lost compression.

So I traded it for another Civic Si. Brand new.

This second one cost around $24,000. I scraped and bumped it, broke brake brackets while attempting repairs, sourced replacements, and fixed the bodywork. It survived all that.

When I later needed cash and had no job, I sold it for around $12,000.

Some vehicles escaped alive.

## The Motorcycle

Around 2007, my then-wife got tired of me staring at every motorcycle that passed and asked why I didn't just buy one.

So I bought a brand-new 1000cc motorcycle.

Nineteen years later, it finally died. Between those dates: a Texas-to-Germany container ship, rides across Europe, American plates in Bulgaria, two diesel incidents, theft in Southampton, a crash in Mallorca, a welded engine case in Plovdiv, and more ferries than any motorcycle reasonably needs.

That bastard needs [its own history](/motorcycle-that-wouldnt-fucking-die/), not a paragraph.

It also generated separate chapters on [Mallorca and Ben the Legend](/ben-the-legend/), [diesel and border diplomacy](/motorcycle-diplomacy/), and [the ferry that never happened](/the-ferry-that-never-happened/).

## The Mitsubishi Evo

A friend pointed out that I was earning good money and kept gawking at Evos and Honda S2000s.

"Why don't you just buy one?"

Good question.

I bought a brand-new Evo for about $40,000, financed through Mitsubishi, and paid it off. The Civic remained my daily driver; I even rented a second garage.

Then I rear-ended a giant truck and decided to do my own bodywork.

The dealer noticed.

When I left Texas in 2018, I sold the Evo for roughly $22,000.

## The Red Pokémon Honda

In Southampton I bought a modified red Honda 2.2 CDTi hatchback for roughly £1,800–£2,000. Huge spoiler, low front end, front-mounted intercooler, and a fucking Pokémon gear shifter.

It was supposed to transport my luggage to Barcelona and then go to a friend.

The friend disappeared. I owned it for about four years.

I replaced the intercooler approximately five times. Serbian police threw me off the motorway for producing clouds of black smoke. My uncle drove it around Bulgaria. Eventually a scraping brake and a proposed €1,000 repair defeated a car that had survived nearly everything else.

A scrap dealer paid €150.

Read [Five Days to Bulgaria](/five-days-to-bulgaria/) for the international disaster that somehow *didn't* finish it.

## The Audi A6

Bought in Bulgaria for €2,000. Drove it toward Spain at speeds that an elderly transmission probably didn't appreciate.

It started grinding. I kept going. In southern France it lost both forward and reverse.

Roughly €3,000 to transport it back and €2,000 for the transmission? On a €2,000 car?

No.

I collected my luggage, removed my plates, and left the Audi in the tow yard. The yard later tried to contact me.

They can have it.

[France, Here's an Audi](/france-heres-an-audi/) includes the village, the rescue van, and an entirely unnecessary Discord extortion subplot.

## The Mazda 3

Elizabeth had brought it from the States, with New York winter rust and a questionable transmission already included.

The Mazda nevertheless hauled me, the dog, and a rooftop mountain of luggage through Europe. Eventually I drove it north to collect another car and the rear suspension separated at the top.

The rust had won.

I disconnected the battery and left it in my grandparents' yard alongside four other vehicles. It still ran; one side of the rear suspension simply no longer cooperated.

[The Mazda That Refused to Die](/the-mazda-that-refused-to-die/) has the details.

## The BMW

The BMW had the shortest service life with me.

One night an encounter with an aggressive van driver brought out some aggressively stupid driving on my part. I went very fast. The exhaust started acting strangely. I slowed down.

The next day the engine died.

Then followed a twelve-hour Monday involving a five-kilometer walk, no phone, no cash, a tow truck, a hookah-lounge waitress, two missed trains, a skeptical stranger, and an absurdly expensive taxi.

That's [Murphy Drives a BMW](/murphy-drives-a-bmw/).

## Van Diesel

Which brings us to the present: a Mercedes camper van. Sometimes I drive it wearing a Lucifer suit. There's an e-bike in the back, a CB radio, and plans that may or may not involve disappearing to Greece or Spain for the winter.

I once discussed the whole arrangement with a woman at the auto service. She had bought a camper and e-bike because groceries were seven kilometers away. I was riding mine over twenty kilometers each way to collect Van Diesel.

At least for now, the van still runs.

People hear these stories and ask how I *got through them*. I still don't understand the question.

The car broke. I dealt with what was in front of me. Then something else happened.

Not a triumph. Not a tragedy. Not a motivational speech.

**That's what happened.**

And as far as I can tell, the road hasn't finished with Uncle Ben yet.

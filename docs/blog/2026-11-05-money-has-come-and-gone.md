# Money Has Come and Gone

I've had money.

<!-- truncate -->

I've had considerably less money.

And I've spent enough time on both sides of that arrangement to be suspicious of people who think one bank balance explains the whole fucking human condition.

The line I saw on Tinder was simple: whoever said money can't solve your problems apparently didn't have enough of it.

There you go. Case closed. All suffering has been reduced to an insufficient account balance. Someone alert the philosophers. We can close the libraries.

I wish it worked that way.

Money has come and gone in my life. I've known what it's like to have it, and what it's like to watch it disappear. And yes, money can buy things. It can make a bill go away. It can give you options. It can buy comfort, food, a roof, a plane ticket, some breathing room.

I'm not pretending those things are trivial.

But the stuff that has made life genuinely difficult for me? The struggles that don't vanish because an invoice has been paid?

Money didn't solve those.

It didn't give me some permanent exemption from depression. It didn't guarantee I would wake up happy. It didn't remove the possibility that I'd still struggle with life, or that suicidal thoughts could still enter the picture regardless of what was in my account.

That was the point I was trying to make.

I've never found a magic number that turns being human into an uncomplicated experience. Maybe there's a premium tier nobody told me about. Maybe the billionaire package includes emotional stability, peace of mind, and a lifetime warranty against misery.

I doubt it.

The thing that bothers me about “you just haven't had enough” is that it's an answer designed never to be questioned. If money helped, the theory works. If money didn't help, well, obviously you needed more.

Convenient fucking philosophy.

My experience doesn't prove nobody can find happiness with money. I never claimed that. I know perfectly well how much easier some ordinary problems become when you can afford to deal with them.

But I've been the person with money and the person without it. I'm still the same human being walking around inside my own head.

And that head doesn't come with a price tag marked *problem solved*.

So when I see somebody confidently announce that enough money fixes everything, I don't hear wisdom. I hear a theory that hasn't met the things I've lived through.

You can be comfortable and miserable.

You can be broke and miserable.

You can be comfortable and happy, or broke and happy, and no amount of clever wording turns the bank balance into the explanation for every outcome.

Money has come.

Money has gone.

And some of the hardest shit stayed right where it was.

I love the word *fuck*.

It's an excellent word. Versatile. Efficient. Musically satisfying. Capable of functioning as emphasis, affection, frustration, humor, surprise, or a complete sentence.

I've been told my entire life that I swear too much.

Well, get fucked.

## Words Don't Come With Preinstalled Emotions

I can be laughing with somebody and call them "motherfucker."

I can say "fuck" three times in a sentence without being angry about anything.

I can also deliberately use profanity to make a point. I know the difference because I'm the one fucking speaking.

People sometimes hear the vocabulary, assign their own emotional interpretation, and then respond to the interpretation rather than the actual message.

Now we're no longer talking about what I said. We're talking about the fact that I said "fuck."

That's a remarkably effective way to derail a conversation.

## The Tone-Policing Escape Hatch

Sometimes somebody behaves badly and I confront them.

They don't want to talk about what happened. They want to talk about how I phrased the confrontation.

"You shouldn't have said it like that."

Okay. Are we going to discuss the original behavior, or are we going to hold a seminar on my vocabulary?

Somebody can genuinely dislike being sworn at. That's their reaction. But disliking my delivery doesn't magically resolve the subject that prompted it.

And I don't use every expression in every context. I'm not walking around greeting strangers by telling them to suck a bag of dicks.

I have a vocabulary. I know how to use it.

## The Escalation Protocol

Once upon a time, an argument in an open-source Reddit community ended with me saying "get fucked."

I got banned.

Get double fucked.

I wasn't under the impression that Reddit lacked a moderation system. I understood the possibility of consequences.

I simply didn't care enough about membership in that particular community to change what I'd said.

It's a social platform, not an oxygen supply.

## Eat My Ass

This one is particularly funny to me.

People sometimes say "eat my ass" as though it's the ultimate insult.

I've had a woman do exactly that.

It was fucking awesome.

So when somebody says it with apparent hostility, I have a completely different association running through my head.

"Don't threaten me with a good time."

I may not say that part out loud. But I'm laughing on the inside.

Same phrase. Different personal meaning.

Tell me again how a collection of words contains one mandatory emotional interpretation.

## The Documentary Called Fuck

There was even a documentary called *Fuck* that explored the word and its history, its cultural baggage, and the endless arguments people have about using it.

One fucking word. An entire documentary.

That's how interesting language can be when you stop treating it as a simple morality detector.

## Get Over Yourself

I'm not asking everybody to enjoy my profanity.

I'm saying that if I make a point, the words I choose don't automatically erase the point.

If you want to discuss the substance, fantastic.

If you want to talk exclusively about whether I should have said "fuck," that's a different conversation.

You can have it without me.

Sit on a cactus.

Or, you know, eat my ass.

Just remember that the last one might not be the insult you think it is.

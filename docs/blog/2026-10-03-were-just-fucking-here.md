# We're Just Fucking Here

Viktor Frankl wrote *Man's Search for Meaning*.

I understand why that book matters.

A man survives concentration camps and emerges with an argument that meaning can help a human being endure suffering that should have been impossible to endure.

I cannot compare anything in my life to that experience.

I would not try.

But I keep getting stuck on the title.

**Does there have to be meaning?**

<!-- truncate -->

Not:

Can people create meaning?

Obviously they can.

Not:

Can meaning help people survive?

Clearly it can.

Not even:

Can a particular person find something worth living for?

Of course.

My question is more irritating.

Does existence itself contain some meaning that must be discovered?

I do not know.

And increasingly I suspect the universe is under no obligation to provide one.

## The Unauthorized Installation

Nobody asked to be here.

That is not melodrama.

It is architecture.

At some point, consciousness boots.

You become aware that there is a world.

There are people.

There is pain.

There is pleasure.

There are dogs.

There are electrical cords.

There are lambs.

There are families.

There are wars.

There are mortgages.

There are sunsets.

There are fucking software updates.

And nobody presents an onboarding screen.

No terms of service.

No checkbox.

No:

> Would you like to participate in existence?

You are simply here.

Then, almost immediately, humanity starts handing you interpretations.

This is what life means.

This is what God wants.

This is what success means.

This is what family means.

This is what morality means.

This is what you are supposed to become.

You were installed without consent and then surrounded by documentation written by other users.

## Meaning As A Tool

Frankl's idea makes far more sense to me when I treat meaning as a human tool rather than a cosmic fact.

A person can orient toward something.

Work.

Love.

Responsibility.

Art.

Children.

Service.

Faith.

A dog.

A stupid software project nobody asked for.

Fine.

Those things can absolutely organize a life.

They can make suffering bearable.

They can make tomorrow worth reaching.

But that is different from saying reality itself contains a hidden answer key.

Maybe meaning is something consciousness does.

Maybe it is less like discovering a law of physics and more like writing a configuration file.

Useful.

Powerful.

Sometimes necessary.

Still written locally.

## The Dog Again

This entire line of thought started, absurdly enough, with my Ridgeback chewing through an electrical cord.

Because apparently all roads now lead through this fucking dog.

She did something stupid.

I got angry.

I put her outside.

Then I worried about what would happen to her if something happened to me.

Then I remembered a lamb.

Then I thought about human hypocrisy.

Then consequences.

Then judgment.

Then Frankl.

This is how philosophy actually happens.

Not in marble halls.

At 11 PM while a dog whines outside because she attempted to eat a shop vac.

## I Don't Need A Grand Answer

There is a pressure around meaning that I do not particularly like.

As though life without some great purpose is defective.

You must have a mission.

A legacy.

A reason.

A calling.

A narrative arc.

Why?

Maybe I am here because I am here.

Maybe living is not the means to some further objective.

Maybe living is the activity.

Eat soup.

Build things.

Talk shit.

Love the dog.

Get angry at the dog.

Read books.

Travel.

Sit outside.

Make mistakes.

Fix cords.

Lose people.

Meet people.

Laugh.

Suffer.

Sleep.

Repeat until the system shuts down.

That may sound bleak to somebody else.

To me it can sound almost relieving.

No cosmic quarterly review.

No universal KPI.

No requirement to justify the fact of existing.

## But What About Suffering?

This is where Frankl remains difficult to dismiss.

Because when life becomes unbearable, "we're just here" may not be enough for everybody.

Meaning can be structure.

Structure can keep somebody moving.

That matters.

Frankl was not writing from a comfortable chair wondering whether Tuesday felt existentially interesting.

He was writing from experience with systematic human cruelty on a scale I cannot imagine.

So I am not rejecting the usefulness of meaning.

I am rejecting the assumption that usefulness proves metaphysical necessity.

A compass can save your life without proving the universe has a preferred direction.

## We're Here

That is where I land tonight.

Not triumphantly.

Not as a doctrine.

Just as a statement.

We're here.

None of us chose the initial condition.

Some people find God.

Some find family.

Some find work.

Some find duty.

Some find art.

Some find nothing permanent and keep going anyway.

Maybe all of those are valid descriptions of being a conscious animal trying to orient itself inside something incomprehensibly larger than itself.

Maybe the mistake is assuming there must be one answer.

I do not have one.

I am here.

The Ridgeback is here.

Humanity is here.

The universe continues behaving as though none of this requires an explanation.

So maybe the meaning of life is not a sentence.

Maybe the meaning of life is the fact that life is happening.

We're here to live because we're here.

And one day we won't be.

No hidden level.

No philosophical achievement unlocked.

No final fucking PowerPoint.

Just this.

We're just fucking here.

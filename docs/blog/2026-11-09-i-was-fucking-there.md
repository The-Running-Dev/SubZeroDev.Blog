# I Was Fucking There

There is a peculiar experience in having your own childhood explained to you as though you missed it.

The events are familiar. The people are familiar. You recognize the family.

Somehow your experience has been removed from the official account.

I have a fairly basic objection to that.

I was fucking there.

<!-- truncate -->

My mother, Elizabeth, has her versions of our family history. I have my memories.

My father's divorce. His second marriage. The wedding invitation. These are parts of the history where her account and mine do not line up.

We can refer to the same family history and arrive at accounts that do not fit together. Familiar events, incompatible versions, and my own place in them somehow up for debate.

Then there was the argument in Texas, where my own childhood experiences were denied.

It is hard to describe how maddening that can be without sounding as though you simply object to being contradicted.

I can be contradicted. I can get things wrong. Having lived through something does not turn the human memory into a fucking recording device.

I know that.

But there is room between admitting that memory is imperfect and accepting that somebody else gets to erase your experience.

I am not prepared to disappear into that room.

My mother had her experience of the family. She was an adult living an adult's life, with things to know and things to contend with that were not mine.

I was a child living mine.

Her being the parent does not make her the only person to whom any of it happened.

That is the part I keep coming back to. There was more than one person inside the history. Whatever account she gives of a divorce, another marriage, an invitation, or the family around them, my place in that life does not become fictional because it complicates the telling.

I was present.

I remember.

And when my own childhood experiences are denied, I am being asked to do more than concede a disputed detail. I am being asked to accept an account in which my testimony about my life carries no weight.

What a fucking arrangement.

One person gets the story. The other gets to be corrected about having lived it.

I don't know what every difference between our accounts means. I cannot climb inside her memory and inspect how it was made. I cannot honestly claim that every disagreement proves a deliberate lie.

That uncertainty does not require me to abandon what I remember.

Nor does it make the argument in Texas feel any less real to me.

There is a reason the sentence comes out with profanity. *I was there* sounds like something that should settle the question of whether I have standing to speak.

*I was fucking there* is what comes out when even that standing seems to be in dispute.

It is not a demand to own every perspective.

It is a demand to remain a person inside my own history.

The family can contain more than one account. It can contain disagreement. It can contain the uncomfortable possibility that the same events looked very different depending on where you stood.

I know where I stood.

I was a child in that family.

And I was fucking there.

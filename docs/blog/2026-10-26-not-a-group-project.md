# I'm Not a Fucking Group Project

I was a child with responsibilities for my brothers. I was figuring things out myself in ways a child shouldn't necessarily have to.

<!-- truncate -->

Then I grew up, and somehow there were still people ready to tell me how to live.

That contradiction pisses me off.

The Gen X part of this isn't an invitation to write some nostalgic bullshit about how tough we all were. Being left to figure things out isn't automatically a gift. Becoming capable doesn't retrospectively make everything that required that capability a good thing.

I was a kid. And in another sense, I wasn't a kid. Seventeen can contain both of those things. I don't need to erase one to explain the other.

What I have trouble with is the authority that shows up afterward. The unsolicited parenting. The assumption that, now that I'm an adult, my life is something other people should help steer toward the person they think I ought to become.

Where was all that guidance when I was a child?

I can ask that without claiming I did everything alone. I can be angry without writing a verdict on every person in my family. But I don't have to pretend there's no contradiction between being expected to manage and then being treated as though I can't be trusted to manage myself.

And I'm tired of independence being mistaken for an announcement that I will never change.

I've fucking changed. I've been changing my whole life. I've encountered things, made choices, reconsidered things, lived with consequences. The fact that I haven't arrived at somebody else's preferred version of me doesn't mean I have refused to move.

I wasn't working from a plan to become this particular person.

I was living my life.

That's part of what makes being treated like an unfinished project so exhausting. It assumes there is a correct completed version waiting somewhere, and if enough people offer enough advice, perhaps I can finally be assembled properly.

I'm not asking to be declared perfect. I'm asking who gets to decide.

People who love me can worry about me. They can disagree with me. They can tell me something I don't want to hear. I can listen. None of that automatically gives them authority over the result.

If I'm expected to carry the consequences, the decision has to be mine too.

Including the decisions they wouldn't make. Including the possibility that I will get something wrong. Including the possibility that I understand my own life differently than they do.

I don't need everybody to stop caring.

I need caring to leave some room for the person I actually am.

I'm not a fucking group project.

There was a football game on.

Apparently Bulgaria has a youth football team.

I did not know this.

I also did not care.

There were seven of us at the bar watching Bulgaria play Czechia, and somehow I was the only asshole actually watching the game. Everybody who presumably gave a shit about football kept looking at their phones.

I didn't give a shit about football and wasn't.

This seemed backwards.

Then two people walked in who I had talked to before.

And that was the end of football.

<!-- truncate -->

The night before, I had talked to the wife about Leningrad, Saint Petersburg, Moscow, Russia, history, and whatever other hole we'd managed to fall into.

Interesting people.

So while everybody else continued with the football, I moved.

I wanted to talk to them.

This is apparently how I network now.

Not LinkedIn.

Not conferences.

Not "building relationships."

I see somebody interesting at a Bulgarian bar and attach myself to them like a fly on shit.

## Men, Women, and Several Ways to Get Punched

Eventually the conversation with the husband went somewhere much deeper.

Relationships.

Monogamy.

Women.

Men.

What women understand.

What men understand.

What experience teaches you.

And this motherfucker is sitting there making sweeping observations about women while **his wife is sitting directly next to him**.

I am trying very hard not to look at her.

Because I would never do this.

Not because I don't have opinions.

Jesus Christ, I have opinions.

But there are certain moments when basic survival instinct should activate.

If I were his wife, I might have punched him in the face.

He kept going.

I respected the commitment.

Eventually she left, and we kept talking.

And arguing.

And talking.

And arguing.

Except something strange happened.

We weren't actually arguing about nearly as much as either of us thought.

We were coming at the same thing from different experiences and different directions.

At one point I stopped him.

"I apologize. Maybe I misunderstood something you said."

He looked at me.

"There is no need to apologize."

I know.

I wasn't apologizing because I had committed some terrible conversational offense.

I was saying that maybe I had misunderstood his position.

Maybe the thing I was arguing against wasn't actually the thing he believed.

This is allowed.

You can be in an argument and discover new information.

Apparently nobody tells people this.

## Violent Agreement

Eventually we got there.

Violent fucking agreement.

Different lives.

Different experiences.

Different language around the same idea.

But underneath it, we were largely talking about the same fucking thing.

And I loved it.

He loved it.

He shook my hand.

He told me he loves these arguments with me.

Good.

Because so do I.

I don't need somebody to agree with me.

I need somebody willing to stay in the fucking conversation.

Tell me I'm wrong.

Explain why.

Let me tell you you're wrong.

Maybe one of us misunderstood something.

Maybe both of us did.

Maybe after forty-five minutes of arguing we discover we've been standing on opposite sides of the same sentence.

Excellent.

That is a conversation.

## Too Direct

At one point he told me I was too direct and too vulnerable.

A couple of minutes later, he told me I needed to be more direct, more vulnerable, and more нахален.

I looked at him.

In my interaction with you, have I not been more direct than most people?

Because come on.

You have met me.

But there was something underneath all of this that kept appearing.

He seemed to understand disagreement as adversarial in a way I simply don't.

Not hostile, exactly.

But somebody is defending a position.

Somebody is pushing.

Somebody eventually gives.

Bulgarians, in my experience, can sometimes treat questioning somebody as if you're challenging the person rather than the idea.

I don't.

I can disagree with you aggressively and still fucking like you.

I can tell you your argument makes no sense and then buy you a drink.

I can stop halfway through and say maybe I misunderstood you.

None of this requires anybody to surrender.

Which brings us to the philosophical breakthrough of the evening.

## The Turtle

We had already reached violent agreement.

We had already established that we both enjoyed the conversation.

We had already shaken hands.

And then he said something along the lines of:

"I don't like to roll over and lie on my back."

I stared at him.

Nobody asked you to.

What are you, a fucking turtle?

And that was it.

All the philosophy.

All the relationships.

All the monogamy.

All the differences between men and women.

All the cultural differences around argument and disagreement.

Forty-five fucking minutes of two grown men trying to understand what the other one actually meant.

Reduced to the only principle that mattered:

**Nobody has to roll over.**

**What are you, a fucking turtle?**

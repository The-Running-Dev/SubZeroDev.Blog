# I'm a Foreigner Everywhere, and I'm at Home Everywhere

I was watching some military shit on YouTube.

Germany is rearming.

And I suddenly found this fucking hilarious.

Germany.

The Germans.

The people everybody in Europe spent the first half of the twentieth century fighting are now building up their military because Europe is worried about Russia.

History has a fucked-up sense of humor.

<!-- truncate -->

And that got me thinking about Russia.

Not just Putin. Not just Ukraine.

Russia.

The Russian Empire. The Soviet Union. Stalin. Eastern Europe. Siberia. Chechnya. Ukraine. Centuries of expansion, conquest, oppression, rebellion, putting rebellions down, taking territory, taking resources, and then eventually pointing at the resulting enormous country and saying:

**Look how fucking big Russia is.**

Congratulations.

How did it get that big?

Somehow conquest becomes geography if you wait long enough.

Color enough territory the same color on a map for enough generations and eventually people stop asking how the fucking map got that way.

Then mythology takes over.

Russia saved Europe.

The Soviet Union defeated the Nazis.

Russia is powerful.

Russia is great.

Sure.

The Soviet Union was enormously important in defeating Nazi Germany.

It also had its own interests, its own empire, its own oppression, and its own plans for Eastern Europe.

These things can exist at the same fucking time.

And now Russia has spent more than four years fighting Ukraine.

Ukraine is still there.

Still fighting.

And somehow we're back to Germany building weapons because Europe is worried about Russian expansion.

That's fucking funny.

Not funny because people are dying. That's obviously not funny.

Funny because of the mythology.

**Look at us. We're great.**

Great how?

Because you're enormous?

Because you conquered shit?

Because you once controlled enormous amounts of territory?

Because you can point at an old map?

Bulgaria can play that game too.

There were periods when Bulgaria was a major regional power and controlled enormous amounts of territory.

Cool.

I can walk outside right now.

I live in a fucking Bulgarian village.

Ancient territorial greatness is not particularly visible from my front gate.

## Bulgaria Is My Shit Country

And this is where people sometimes get pissed at me.

I talk shit about Bulgaria.

I also talk shit about America.

I'll talk shit about Russia.

I'll talk shit about pretty much anywhere if I have something to say about it.

Spain mostly escapes because I didn't give enough of a shit about Spanish politics while I lived there. My Spanish wasn't good enough for me to become deeply involved in it, and I was perfectly happy that way.

In fact, Spain was probably the happiest I've ever been living anywhere.

I loved Spain.

That doesn't mean I need to become Spain.

And apparently this distinction is much more important than I realized.

Because I've had Bulgarians get genuinely offended when I criticize Bulgaria.

Not when I criticize **them**.

I didn't say anything about them.

I say something about Bulgarian bureaucracy, Bulgarian drivers, some ridiculous Bulgarian cultural habit, whatever.

And suddenly somebody is personally offended.

For years I've thought:

**What the fuck does this have to do with you?**

You live here.

You see the same shit.

Half the time Bulgarians make the same fucking jokes themselves.

But apparently it matters who says it.

I've lived in America.

Therefore sometimes I'm the Bulgarian who left and came back talking shit about Bulgaria.

Except I'm Bulgarian.

I was born here.

I'm a Bulgarian citizen.

Oh, and occasionally I fucking forget this while having these arguments:

**I'm also an American citizen.**

So fucking what?

And that's when something finally clicked for me.

## These Things Aren't My Identity

I am Bulgarian.

I am American.

But neither of those statements answers:

**Who am I?**

They're facts about me.

I was born in Bulgaria.

I emigrated to America.

I became an American citizen.

I lived in Spain.

I came back to Bulgaria.

Now I live in a village where my mother's family came from.

These are things that happened to me.

Things I've experienced.

Things I've accumulated during a life.

Bulgarian.

American.

Spanish resident.

Fucking Zanzibarian if you want.

I don't give a shit.

They're part of my biography.

They're not my identity.

And suddenly a bunch of conversations I've had started making more sense.

When I criticize Bulgaria, I'm criticizing Bulgaria.

That's it.

But if somebody experiences **Bulgarian** as a fundamental part of **self**, perhaps the transaction becomes:

You criticized Bulgaria.

I am Bulgarian.

Therefore you criticized us.

Therefore you criticized me.

Meanwhile my mental process ends at:

**Bulgaria did a ridiculous thing.**

That's the entire fucking transaction.

## The Bulgarian Strategic Bag Reserve

For example:

Bulgarians collect plastic bags.

We fucking do.

There is a bag.

Inside that bag are more bags.

Eventually the bag of bags becomes full, at which point some advanced Bulgarian logistics process occurs that I don't entirely understand.

And someone could presumably get offended by this observation.

Except there's one problem.

I have a fucking bag of bags.

Actually, mine has achieved enterprise scale.

It's a cardboard box full of bags.

I took a picture.

There it fucking is.

So who exactly am I insulting?

I notice these tendencies in myself.

I grew up under communism. There was scarcity. Maybe some of this comes from that. Maybe it doesn't. I don't know why the fucking bags are still there.

But they might be useful for something.

Which, of course, is exactly how the fucking bags win.

And it's not just bags.

I look around and I've got hard drives everywhere. Two NASes. Computers. Cameras. Tech shit. More shit.

I didn't consciously decide:

**I shall become Keeper of the Ancient Hard Drives.**

I bought things. I moved houses. I didn't have something, so I bought another one. Things accumulated.

Now I look around and go:

**Jesus Christ, I have a lot of shit. I should get rid of some of this shit.**

That's it.

I don't have to defend it.

I don't have to become offended because somebody notices it.

I don't have to explain that actually my collection of twenty ancient hard drives represents an important aspect of Bulgarian-American cultural heritage.

I can just look at myself and laugh.

## We Are All Fucked

This applies at a much larger scale too.

Bulgaria is fucked.

America is fucked.

Russia is fucked.

Ukraine is even more fucked right now because there's a fucking war happening there.

We're all fucked in various interesting ways.

And I'm not standing outside humanity pretending I'm the enlightened motherfucker who has figured everything out.

I'm apathetic about plenty of this shit too.

I'm not going to dedicate my life to fixing Bulgaria.

I'm not going to sacrifice myself to fix America.

I'm not going to go fight somebody's war because somebody tells me this piece of dirt is **my country** and therefore I should die for it.

If somewhere becomes intolerable and I can leave?

I'll probably fucking leave.

There are people who will call that cowardice, selfishness, lack of patriotism, whatever.

Fine.

I'm not applying for the position of National Hero.

But let's also not pretend societies arrive where they are through some mysterious process completely unrelated to millions of people making exactly those kinds of decisions.

Including me.

I'm in the fucking equation too.

## Spite

There is another Bulgarian thing I've experienced enough that I find it almost a national sport.

Spite.

Not stubbornness.

Not **инат**.

Spite.

The willingness to do something that doesn't particularly benefit you—and might actually cost you—because it fucks somebody else.

I have watched people spend money doing shit out of spite.

I've watched family members do it.

I've had it done to me.

And right here, in the Shopski dialect spoken around where I live, there's a beautiful saying that captures the entire fucking concept:

> **Не сакам на мене да ми е добре, а сакам на Вуте да му е зле.**

I don't want things to be good for me.

I want things to be bad for Vute.

That's fucking magnificent.

Your own success isn't even required.

**Vute getting fucked is the deliverable.**

And again, I'm not standing outside Bulgarian culture looking through the glass at some strange primitive people.

My mother was from here.

My grandparents were here.

I grew up around this shit.

I recognize pieces of it in myself.

I can laugh at it.

I can criticize it.

None of that threatens me.

Because **Bulgarian isn't something I have to defend in order to defend myself.**

## Apparently I'm Stubborn

I've also been called stubborn my entire fucking life.

And recently I've started wondering:

**Compared to whom?**

Because this usually happens when somebody tells me how I should live my life.

I listen.

I consider what they're saying.

Sometimes I even change what I'm doing.

Then they keep fucking pushing.

No, no.

You haven't accepted the conclusion I wanted you to accept.

You haven't seen the light.

You still don't agree with me.

Therefore:

**You're stubborn.**

Wait a fucking minute.

You're continuing to tell me what I should do.

I've told you I've considered it.

I've given you my answer.

You refuse to accept my answer.

And somehow **I'm** the stubborn one?

Let's reverse this experiment.

I'll tell you what to do.

You don't want to do it?

Interesting.

Why are you being so stubborn?

Suddenly the word becomes a lot less useful.

There is a difference between refusing to consider something and considering it and saying:

**No.**

My life doesn't become a democracy because somebody else has an opinion about it.

And their persistence doesn't become reasonable merely because they believe they're helping me.

## One of Us

There's another ridiculous little story that suddenly fits all of this.

One of my neighbors yelled at me once because my dog was on the balcony.

I'd forgotten the dog.

He was pissed.

I basically shrugged.

What the fuck do you want me to do now?

I forgot the dog.

Some time later I'm walking through the forest.

I see a house.

And I see this fucking Moskvitch.

Except somebody has turned the Moskvitch into a pickup.

And the thing is **beautiful**.

Polished.

Chrome.

Shiny.

Running.

Somebody had taken this ancient little Soviet shitbox and lovingly restored it into this glorious fucking Moskvitch pickup truck.

Of course I stopped.

Turns out it belonged to the same guy.

So now we're talking.

Not about the dog.

About the fucking Moskvitch.

Eventually he asks me:

“Where are you from?”

And this is a surprisingly difficult question for me in Bulgaria.

I don't fucking know anymore.

So I tell him.

My mother was from here.

My grandmother was here.

My grandfather was here.

And suddenly:

**Oh. You're one of us.**

Sure.

Okay.

Apparently I passed authentication.

Nothing about me had changed in the previous thirty seconds.

But he had acquired the necessary genealogical information, and now I belonged to the local people.

So now we're friends.

Apparently integration into rural Bulgarian society requires maternal ancestry and sufficient appreciation for Soviet automotive lunacy.

Fine.

I can work with that.

## The Language Came Back Too

When I first came back to Bulgaria, people would sometimes hear me speak and ask:

“Where are you from?”

And that fucked with me more than I expected.

I'm from Bulgaria.

Except apparently I sound foreign.

But I'm Bulgarian.

Except I've spent most of my adult life elsewhere.

But I'm American.

Except I wasn't born there.

So how exactly am I supposed to introduce myself?

Then I moved here.

My mother was from this region.

People speak Shopski.

And now something funny has happened.

I understand it.

Sometimes I even answer in it.

I didn't undertake some grand project to reconnect with my ancestral Bulgarian identity.

I just fucking live here.

And apparently somewhere inside my head was a dormant Shopski language pack waiting for the correct regional activation code.

That's how I think about all of this now.

I've accumulated places the way I've accumulated everything else.

Bulgaria is in there.

America is in there.

Spain is in there.

This fucking village is in there.

The Bulgarian bag of bags is definitely in there.

None of them needs to own me.

None of them needs to define me.

And I don't need to reject any of them either.

Maybe that's why I've struggled so much with the question:

**Where are you from?**

People sometimes aren't asking for geography.

They're asking which box they should put you in.

Which people are yours?

Which identity is yours?

Which side are you on?

Are you Bulgarian?

American?

Foreigner?

Local?

One of us?

One of them?

I finally have an answer.

**I'm a foreigner everywhere I go.**

**And I'm at home everywhere I go.**

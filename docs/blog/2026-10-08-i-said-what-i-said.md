I told Ethan that Bible study with him and Sandra means something to me.

I thanked them for meeting me where I am. For letting me ask my questions. I love them, and I said so.

He can respond.

He can not respond.

I meant it either way.

That sounds so fucking simple until you notice how often a sentence leaves your mouth carrying a little invisible invoice.

## Payment Due Upon Receipt

I tell you I love you.

And then somewhere underneath it, perhaps without even admitting it to myself, I've attached terms.

Say it back. Say it properly. Say enough. Make me feel that saying it was safe.

Now I'm waiting for payment on something I presented as a gift.

I don't want to do that.

I want to be able to tell somebody that they matter to me because they fucking matter to me. Because having them in my life means something. Because something they did reached me, and I'd like them to know.

That is a complete reason to speak.

It doesn't need a guaranteed response stapled to it.

The gratitude was real before I sent the message. Ethan's answer isn't what makes it real afterwards.

## I Would Still Like an Answer

Let's not turn this into some bullshit performance of having no needs.

Of course I can want a response.

I can want warmth. I can want a conversation. I can want somebody to say that what matters to me matters to them too.

I would strongly prefer some outcomes over others. Discount Store Marcus Aurelius has preferences. The motherfucker would like a cart with four functional wheels.

But wanting an answer and making somebody owe me a particular answer are different things.

I can be disappointed.

I can notice that I keep reaching out and don't get much back. I can decide how much of my time and energy I want to keep putting into a relationship.

None of that requires me to retroactively declare that I didn't mean what I said.

I did mean it.

That's the whole fucking point.

## Uncle Ben, as Supplied

Martin got the blog.

Then I referenced Discount Store Marcus Aurelius, and he replied, “Huh?”

Well. There we are.

I had given him something that expressed where my head was. The cart. The philosophy. The ridiculous fucking character that somehow helped me say something serious.

He could read it. Ask about it. Leave it.

I wasn't going to acquire control of his response by explaining Uncle Ben harder.

That doesn't tell me what was happening in Martin's head. It tells me what the exchange was: I sent something, and that was the answer I got.

I can decide what to do with that.

I don't need to invent the rest of his evening to make mine intelligible.

And I don't need to withdraw what I shared because it didn't produce the conversation I might have preferred.

Here's Uncle Ben.

Do with that what you will.

## I Also Own the Fucking Sentence

“I said what I said” can become an extremely convenient place to park when somebody points out that you've been an asshole.

I don't get a lifetime exemption from accountability because I was expressing a feeling.

I can mean something and say it badly.

I can be genuinely hurt and still get a fact wrong.

I can tell somebody I'm disappointed, hear their side, and discover that the story I was telling myself needs work.

Then I need to do that work.

If I was wrong, I can say I was wrong. If I hurt somebody with how I spoke, I can own how I spoke. I don't have to defend every syllable forever just to prove the original feeling existed.

It existed. I felt it.

Now I have more information.

Fuck. Apparently being honest includes that part too.

And saying something honestly doesn't give me unlimited access to another person. I don't get to keep knocking on the same door indefinitely while insisting I'm only expressing myself.

One honest message does not entitle me to a reply. A hundred honest messages don't improve the arithmetic.

My words are mine. My conduct is mine too.

## Still Meant It

What stayed with me about thanking Ethan and Sandra was how little else the message needed.

This matters to me.

I love you.

Thank you.

I didn't need to turn it into an argument about whether it should matter. I didn't need to make a case for the correct amount of gratitude to return. I didn't need to wait until I could predict the response.

I wanted them to know.

So I told them.

There is something freeing about letting that be enough for the act of saying it, even while the relationship remains a living thing with needs and choices and other conversations still to have.

I can care deeply.

I can leave room for somebody else's answer.

I can hear an answer I didn't want, or receive no answer, and still stand behind the fact that I spoke with love.

I said what I said.

I meant it.

There isn't a fucking invoice.

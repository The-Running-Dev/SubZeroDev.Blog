# When Peace Becomes Ammunition

I have tried making peace.

More than once.

I have tried the agreement where we leave the past behind and move forward. It sounds reasonable. It offers the possibility that the next conversation might be about something other than all the conversations that went wrong before it.

Then something new happens.

I object.

And somehow my own peace offering becomes evidence against me.

<!-- truncate -->

The reasoning is circular enough to deserve a small medal.

We agreed to leave the past behind. I raise a concern about new behavior. Raising the concern is treated as a failure to honor the peace.

Apparently moving forward requires me to keep walking past whatever happens next.

I find that a remarkably generous definition of reconciliation.

Generous to the person whose behavior I am no longer supposed to mention.

Less generous to me.

An agreement to stop arguing about old injuries cannot sensibly mean there will never be another legitimate objection. New conduct does not become ancient history at the exact moment I notice it.

Time does not work that fucking fast.

Yet I have experienced my own wish for peace being thrown back at me when I object to something new.

That changes what the offer can mean.

At first, it is an attempt to make room for a relationship. A willingness to stop using the entire history as the opening statement in every interaction.

Used against me, it becomes an agreement that I must absorb whatever comes next quietly.

I cannot make those two versions fit together.

I understand the appeal of a clean slate. There is a reason I kept trying. A family relationship can contain enough accumulated hurt that every new disagreement threatens to arrive carrying the whole archive.

I wanted the possibility of something better than that.

I was willing to try leaving things behind.

But I still exist in the next conversation. I still get to have an experience of it. If something happens that I object to, the fact that I wanted peace yesterday cannot disqualify me from speaking today.

Otherwise the clean slate has an extraordinary feature.

Only one person is allowed to write on it.

The other has agreed, in the interests of harmony, to put down the fucking chalk.

I am not claiming that I always separate old hurt from new hurt perfectly. I am a person in the relationship, not an independent inquiry commissioned to produce a flawless chronology.

The past affects me. Of course it does.

But that does not make every current concern a disguised attempt to restart an old fight.

And it does not make the peace offer an instrument somebody else gets to use against me.

The repetition matters. Trying again is itself a kind of investment. Each attempt contains the possibility that this time the agreement will mean something we can both live inside.

When the offer returns as ammunition, there is a second hurt attached to the first. Now the willingness to try is part of what gets used to close the conversation.

I wanted peace.

I still want to be able to object.

A relationship that has room for the first only when I surrender the second is asking a great deal while calling itself reconciliation.

I cannot keep confusing the absence of an objection with the presence of peace.

Especially when the silence is supposed to be mine.

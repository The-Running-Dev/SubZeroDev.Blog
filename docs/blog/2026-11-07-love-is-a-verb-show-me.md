# Love Is a Verb. Show Me.

My younger brother once told Martin that love is a verb. I agree with him. Which is why I find myself reading his latest email and asking where the fucking verb went.

<!-- truncate -->

He loves us both. We are his brothers. If either of us needs help, we can reach out. He is there.

He is also holding off on regular connections and relationships because his life is peaceful and busy.

And otherwise, perhaps we can have a family reunion after our mother dies. She is a common denominator in the points of contention.

There is a lot of warm language around that decision.

The decision is distance.

I know the argument that those statements can coexist. Someone can feel affection and offer occasional help while declining an ongoing relationship. I have spent enough time going through this email to understand the distinction.

But I am not encountering the offer for the first time.

I called him once despite our differences. He did not answer. There was no message afterward.

That is part of what I hear when I read that he is there if I need anything. I have already tried reaching for the person described in that sentence.

What I had wanted from my brother was often painfully ordinary. A conversation. Somebody who knew me listening when life was difficult. I was not asking him to solve everything. His original email understood those needs very differently.

So what does this new offer include?

I do not know. The email does not explain.

What it does explain is that regular contact is something he is choosing to hold off on.

Then there is the proposed timing of the reunion.

Maybe after Elizabeth dies.

I cannot get past the assumption underneath that possibility. Five years. Ten. Fifteen. Twenty. How long are we imagining a relationship can sit untouched before we arrive at a table and become brothers again?

Our mother is not a pause button.

While she is alive, we all continue living. We become people the others have not spent time with. We stop sharing ordinary things. We stop knowing the details. We build lives that work without those conversations because the conversations are not happening.

Her death would not give those years back.

And the disagreements already include what we have said and done to each other. Those would still be ours.

He described her as a common denominator. That is his description. I hear an acknowledgment that she is involved in the conflict, followed by a decision to leave the brothers' relationships at a distance while she remains alive.

I do not see why having a relationship with a brother requires waiting for a mother to be gone.

In his original email, he said he did not want to or care to know what either side had done to the other. He also questioned whether I was living truthfully.

That is one of the contradictions I answered. I cannot understand how declining to hear the history supports certainty about somebody else's integrity.

I am not claiming I have never been a hypocrite. I am human. I have failed my own standards. But when somebody makes principles part of the explanation of what is wrong with me, I am going to notice how those principles are practiced.

Love is a verb.

I wanted that sentence to mean something. I admired the person I thought was saying it and living it.

His latest email tells me he has made peace with things. That describes where he is. I have not reached a shared peace with him merely because he has announced his own.

I read the response as an affectionate way of keeping distance, with a future possibility attached. It felt duplicitous to me. I cannot inspect his private feelings, and I am tired of trying to extract a relationship from declarations about them.

I can read what he is choosing now.

Maybe one day we will sit down and break bread. Maybe we will not. That future would require willingness from all of us, including whatever person I have become by then.

There is no table reservation that preserves brotherhood indefinitely.

For now, I have his words. I also have the unanswered call and the distance.

That is what I am left measuring them against.

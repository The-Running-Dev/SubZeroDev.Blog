# It's Just Business, Motherfucker

Why do you think I left fucking America?

<!-- truncate -->

Politics was part of it. But forget Trump for a minute. Forget politics. There was also money, and what happened to relationships when money entered the room.

Somebody I thought was a good friend fucked me over money. His name was Miguel. We were friends long before we had any business together.

That matters. If this were just a story about a company taking its cut, I would still be pissed off. But I keep coming back to the fact that somebody I knew was involved in deciding what my work was worth, and somehow the answer became ten dollars an hour less.

The way I remember it, they billed the client $150 an hour. They paid me $90. Then they lowered my rate to $80.

I don't remember whether the first arrangement lasted two months or three. I'm not going to manufacture a precise timeline because the arithmetic makes me angry. The part I do remember is the cut.

I did the fucking work.

The client had somebody else before us, and, from where I stood, that work wasn't getting done properly. I went in and showed that I could do it better. My understanding was that we got the contract because of what I demonstrated. I brought them value, and then my own rate went down.

At $90, there was already a $60 gap between what they billed and what they paid me. At $80, if the billing rate stayed the same, that gap became $70. That isn't a calculation of their profit. I don't have their books. It's the gap I could see, and I wanted somebody to explain why more of it needed to come out of my side.

What I wanted from Miguel was for him to protect my rate. To stand behind the person doing the work. Instead, as I experienced it, he went along with his partners. And he didn't have the fucking balls to talk to me directly about it.

And no, the problem wouldn't have been solved by him delivering the same pay cut to my face. I expected him to protect the arrangement because I was the reason they had the work. Having an uncomfortable conversation was the minimum. It wasn't the whole obligation.

Then there's the part I still find absurd: this was also the company where HR took issue with my vulgarity in client meetings.

Apparently, how I said things was a problem. What happened to my pay was business.

I'm not saying every meeting needs me swearing through it. I'm saying I have a problem with a version of professionalism that can find the offensive word immediately and somehow lose sight of the person on the receiving end of the decision.

The money matters. Ten dollars an hour matters. But so does discovering what a friendship can and cannot survive when somebody's margin enters the conversation.

It wasn't an isolated sore spot. There was a relationship in which I felt money won. There was my father yelling at me about money when I hadn't fucking asked him for any. Different people, different situations, and the same thing getting under my skin: money becoming the explanation for how we treat one another.

Of course this happens outside America. I know that. Telling me it happens everywhere doesn't make the experiences I had there matter less. Relationships mattered to me more than money did. That doesn't mean I didn't understand money. It means the friendship was supposed to continue meaning something when there was a financial decision to make.

Maybe Miguel tells this differently. This is how I remember being on my side of it.

It's just business, motherfucker.

Funny how that explanation always seems to be available to the person making the cut.

# Lucifer Talks to an Autoresponder

Lucifer's phone buzzed.

A company called RealRoots had decided his weekend needed more forum notifications.

<!-- truncate -->

Then another message arrived.

> **RealRoots:** Top sheet: essential or pointless? Tap an answer to see how everyone else voted.

Lucifer stared at the screen.

He had not asked this question.

He did not care about the answer.

So he replied with the first thing that seemed appropriate.

> **Lucifer:** Yo mamma so fat, when she sits around the house she really sits around the house.

The response came immediately.

> **AUTORESPONDER:** Thanks for responding! We don't actively check these messages, so please email us if you have questions.

Lucifer blinked.

> **Lucifer:** That is too bad for you.

The response came immediately.

> **AUTORESPONDER:** Thanks for responding! We don't actively check these messages, so please email us if you have questions.

Lucifer sat up.

This was no longer spam.

This was now research.

---

There was clearly no intelligence here.

No context.

No memory.

No escalation path.

Just a small piece of corporate automation with one spell and apparently infinite mana.

Lucifer tested the boundaries.

> **Lucifer:** Let us use up your phone plan then.

The machine repeated itself.

> **Lucifer:** You said that already.

The machine repeated itself.

> **Lucifer:** I can do this all day.

The machine repeated itself.

> **Lucifer:** No thank you.

The machine repeated itself.

Lucifer frowned at the phrase:

> We don't actively check these messages.

Then looked at the response that had just arrived because he sent a message.

> **Lucifer:** But apparently you do check.

The machine repeated itself.

Technically, Lucifer had found the truth.

Something was absolutely checking.

It just had nothing new to say.

---

A few minutes later, the relationship deepened.

> **Lucifer:** I have so many questions.

The machine helpfully suggested that if he had questions, he should email them.

> **Lucifer:** No.

The machine repeated itself.

Lucifer began thinking about infrastructure.

SMS gateways.

Outbound message fees.

Vendor billing.

The tiny, beautiful possibility that each increasingly pointless response might be costing somebody a fraction of a cent.

> **Lucifer:** Your overlords should know this is costing them money. I hope.

The machine repeated itself.

> **Lucifer:** A penny saved is a penny earned.

The machine repeated itself.

At this point there was nothing left to learn.

So naturally Lucifer changed languages.

> **Lucifer:** Tu madre dice hola.

The machine repeated itself.

> **Lucifer:** Pinche pendejo.

The machine repeated itself.

Lucifer smiled.

> **Lucifer:** You are so easy to talk to.

The machine repeated itself.

And that was the moment the whole thing became strangely wholesome.

Not because the machine understood him.

It understood absolutely nothing.

That was the feature.

No judgment.

No interruption.

No unsolicited advice.

No attempt to reframe what he meant.

No concern that perhaps the conversation was becoming unproductive.

Just:

> Thanks for responding!

Again.

And again.

And again.

Perfect consistency.

A customer-service monk trapped inside an SMS webhook.

---

Eventually Lucifer marked the number as spam.

Not because the messages annoyed him.

Because he knew himself.

If he left that thread available, he would keep replying.

All day.

Not out of anger.

Not to accomplish anything.

Simply because the machine had accidentally presented him with a button labeled:

> **PRESS HERE TO MAKE THE CORPORATION SAY THE SAME THING AGAIN**

And somewhere deep in the human brain there is apparently a mechanism that finds this irresistible.

Especially when the corporation started the conversation.

---

Lucifer put the phone down.

Then he had one final thought.

Somewhere, someday, some employee might review these logs.

Maybe for quality assurance.

Maybe for abuse reports.

Maybe because someone notices that one notification thread produced an unusual number of outbound messages.

They will open the conversation expecting a normal customer interaction.

And they will find:

> Yo mamma so fat...

> That is too bad for you.

> I can do this all day.

> I have so many questions.

> No.

> Tu madre dice hola.

> Pinche pendejo.

> You are so easy to talk to.

Then several identical corporate autoresponses patiently woven between them like a machine trying to maintain professionalism during a hostage situation.

The employee will stare at the screen.

They will have questions.

Unfortunately, the conversation will already contain instructions for what to do if they have questions.

Lucifer smiled.

Somewhere in the system, the autoresponder waited faithfully for another message.

Ready.

Patient.

Unchanged.

> **AUTORESPONDER:** Thanks for responding!

# What Are You Still Auditioning For?

I was standing in the house looking at the tile floor.

I had vacuumed it.

I had swept it.

The loose dirt was gone.

And then the thought arrived:

> I should probably mop this.

Followed almost immediately by a much better question:

> **Why the fuck would I do that?**

<!-- truncate -->

I do not sit on the floor.

I do not eat on the floor.

I do not sleep on the floor.

I walk on the floor.

That is the floor's entire fucking job.

I clean it so I can walk on it, which makes it dirty, so I can clean it again, so I can walk on it again.

At some point this begins to look less like hygiene and more like a subscription service nobody remembers signing up for.

The floor was not covered in food.

There was no biological incident.

Nothing was growing on it.

It had dirt.

I removed the dirt.

**Floor operational.**

And yet there was this tiny inherited voice somewhere in the machinery:

> But you haven't *washed* it.

Ah.

There you are.

## The Metaphysical State of Clean

Elizabeth would lose her goddamn mind.

Because for some people, "clean" is apparently not a functional condition.

It is a metaphysical state.

You can remove the dirt.

You can vacuum.

You can sweep.

You can make the place perfectly usable.

Doesn't matter.

There remains some invisible final level of cleanliness accessible only through ritual.

I have seen this before.

Years ago in Texas, I hired cleaners.

Not one cleaner.

Two.

Professional cleaners.

They would come into my place and spend under two hours doing what professional cleaners do: floors, surfaces, bathrooms, kitchen, dust, whatever else happens while I am happily not participating.

When they were done, the place fucking sparkled.

It cost me about eighty bucks.

This was an excellent transaction.

I gave them money.

They gave me a clean house.

I did not have to spend my time cleaning it.

Capitalism occasionally produces something useful.

Then Elizabeth came over the next day.

The next fucking day.

And started cleaning.

Apparently the professional cleaners had not cleaned.

Or they had cleaned incorrectly.

Or perhaps the house had failed some inspection conducted by a standards body consisting entirely of Elizabeth.

I remember thinking:

> Two people whose actual job is cleaning just cleaned this place.

But no.

Not good enough.

Naturally.

Because nobody can do anything as well as you can.

## The House Was Cleaner and Less Useful

Cleaning wasn't even the best one.

She came to my house — **my house** — where I lived, where I paid the rent, where I had arranged my own shit according to the revolutionary system known as *I know where my things are*.

And she started "straightening."

That word needs quotation marks.

Because straightening sounds harmless.

Straightening sounds like adjusting a pillow.

Straightening sounds like moving a magazine three centimeters.

This was not straightening.

This was relocating my shit according to an organizational system that existed entirely inside somebody else's head.

Then I would need something.

And I couldn't fucking find it.

An improvement had been made.

The house looked more correct to the person who did not live there and became less functional for the person who did.

Apparently that counts as helping.

## Nobody Can Do It Better Than Me

Thinking back on all this, there is a pattern I didn't really name at the time.

Everything becomes an opportunity to prove competence.

Someone cleaned?

I can clean better.

Someone arranged something?

I can arrange it better.

Someone is doing something differently?

I know the proper way.

There is no mundane activity too small to become a fucking demonstration.

And I have a theory about that.

Not a diagnosis.

Not mind-reading.

A theory.

If you genuinely know you're competent, you don't need every goddamn household activity to submit supporting evidence.

You can let somebody else clean a bathroom.

You can let another person arrange their own fucking house.

You can encounter a task you are not good at without civilization collapsing.

You can even be a beginner.

That last one is apparently harder than it sounds.

## Spain

When Elizabeth came to Spain, she tried learning Spanish.

She was learning.

She was doing fine.

Which means, obviously, she was also making mistakes.

Because that is what learning a language is.

For a while you sound like an idiot.

You use the wrong word.

You butcher a verb.

Somebody answers too quickly and your brain produces a Windows error sound.

You point at shit.

You guess.

You get corrected.

You improve.

This is the price of admission.

And she freaked the fuck out.

She did not learn Spanish.

That attempt failed.

Not because failing to learn a language makes somebody stupid.

It doesn't.

The interesting part to me is that learning a language forces you into a position where competence cannot be performed into existence.

You cannot reorganize Spanish until Spanish admits you were right.

You cannot clean the subjunctive harder.

You cannot straighten the vocabulary drawer.

You have to be bad at it for a while.

You have to tolerate not knowing.

You have to let other people know things you don't know.

That is the whole fucking process.

And if your identity depends on always being the strong one, the capable one, the person who knows how things should be done, being a beginner must feel like absolute hell.

## What Are You Still Auditioning For?

And that's the part that hit me while staring at my stupid tile floor.

What are you still trying to prove?

At eighty years old, after an entire life of surviving things, doing things, raising people, moving countries, working, cleaning, cooking, handling whatever the fuck life threw at you—

**what are you still auditioning for?**

Who is judging?

Where is the panel?

Is there a trophy?

Does Saint Peter meet you at the gate with a clipboard?

> Elizabeth, excellent work.  
> Your floors were consistently 17 percent cleaner than everyone else's.  
> Please proceed directly to Premium Heaven.

What happens if somebody else cleans a kitchen better?

What happens if your adult child arranges his own house in a way you don't like?

What happens if you try speaking Spanish and sound ridiculous?

Nothing.

Absolutely fucking nothing.

You remain a person.

That seems obvious.

Apparently it isn't.

## The Floor Is Fine

Anyway, I did not mop the floor.

I vacuumed it.

I swept it.

It is tile.

It is going to be walked on.

Tomorrow the dog will probably import half a field through the front door anyway.

The floor does not need to prove anything.

Neither do I.

And somewhere, spiritually, Elizabeth is furious because the tiles have not achieved their final form.

They'll survive.

So will I.

The floor is clean enough.

**Audition over.**

# Three Funnels and No Cupboard Space

Yesterday I walked into the kitchen and looked at the state of it. The kitchen is fucking tiny. My stuff was everywhere, and half the cupboard space was already taken. I thought, fuck this, let me see what I can do about these cupboards.

<!-- truncate -->

Now everything is out on top of the cupboards and spread around the kitchen. It's a bigger fucking mess than when I started. Excellent.

I pulled out the glasses. Seven different kinds. Different shapes, different heights, little surviving groups from different sets. Coffee cups with the odd one missing or a different colour standing in for it. Random mugs, bowls and trays. About ten plates that have apparently been here forever were the most consistent part of the collection.

Then the duplicate spices. A hundred fucking spices, repeating themselves. Pots and pans as though somebody is about to cook for the whole village. Three funnels. Three strainers, one of which I've already thrown away. An old lemon squeezer that looks as though it last saw action during World War II.

I picked up something that looked like a kettle without a spout. Apparently it is an enamel milk pail. Fine. Mystery solved. Still no obvious reason I need it taking up space.

I made a box labelled “outside.” When I find another thing I've no use for, it goes in there. My plan is to keep roughly two of the useful things I find and get rid of the excess. I want somewhere to put my own things.

There have been a couple of useful discoveries. The giant bag of plastic bags now lives at the bottom of the rubbish bin. I pull one out, use it as a liner, and the supply gets smaller. The baby mattress was heading out too, until I realised the dog could sleep on it. That actually makes sense.

I also have spare sheets and covers. Maybe those can replace the worn-out ones at my aunt’s instead of me trying to sell them.

So far, though, the immediate result of creating cupboard space is that the entire kitchen is displaying what used to be inside the cupboards. I've started. I've certainly fucking started.

## The Rest of the Collection Is in the Shed

The cupboards are only part of it. There's also the shed.

There are spare tyres in there. Spare tyres for what fucking car? I don't know. I don't know whether the car still exists, whether the tyres are any good, or whether anyone has a plan for them.

There's a large chest that looks like something a pirate would use for treasure. I think it was my grandmother’s, and I think it is still there. What is in it? I've not checked.

There's old equipment I would have to inspect properly before I could even describe it. There's garden stuff, at least three motiki, and a pickaxe. When did anybody last work the land here with any of this? When did somebody last need that fucking pickaxe?

I need to go through the rest, photograph it and describe what is actually there. At the moment this is the inventory I can remember, not a completed inspection. Some things may still have a use. Some may turn out to be something completely different from what I think they are.

The house is tiny. The collection of things kept around it is considerably less tiny. I started with kitchen cupboards and have not even opened the pirate treasure yet.

## Who Gets to Decide What Stays in the House

I remember the arguments when Elizabeth came here and threw things away. One of the great disputed treasures was an old market scale, rusted to shit. My cousin insisted it was a valuable antique and needed to stay.

It stayed. For another three years.

Apparently calling something valuable is enough to secure it permanent accommodation.

My aunt came looking for a big pot with a lid. I hadn't touched the fucking pot. It was under her desk in her room. Where the lid was, I've no idea. The table she asked about had a different answer: I had cut it into pieces, and those pieces were in the corner of the yard.

When they ask where things have gone, sometimes I know exactly where they went. I shrug. Sometimes I say I've no idea. I find the exchange amusing. I'm not pretending that I'm genuinely puzzled.

I see this as a dispute about control over the house. They act as though they get to fill it with whatever they like and decide indefinitely what happens to it. Elizabeth put at least twenty grand into this place. I'm here using it. Decisions we make seem to threaten an arrangement they regard as settled.

I never subscribed to that arrangement.

They come around perhaps once every six months, and I hear about criticism behind my back. There's also the history of my cousin telling me I needed to apologise to her mother. I asked what happened to her boyfriend apologising to me, and what happened to moving the car that kept blocking the driveway.

I'm not interested in an apology arrangement where accountability only travels towards me.

I want to use the house. I don't feel obliged to preserve every cup and tray just because somebody might come looking for it one day. Just in case what? An apocalypse with no tables left anywhere? A national shortage of cups?

I'm angry about this, and some of it also fucking amuses me. Both are happening.

## The Shaver Is Missing and the Charger Is Here

My shaver is missing.

It was a good one, the sort used by barbers, something I found in a shop in Barcelona and had to order from the UK for about a hundred pounds. I bought it. I used it. It had been on top of the fridge.

Now I can't find it. Its dedicated charger is still here.

My guess is that Ani saw it and took it. That's my inference. I've not seen her take it, and I still hope the thing is somewhere in the house. I would rather find it than have to replace it.

But I wouldn't put it past her. I wouldn't make the same accusation about my aunt. My suspicion is specifically about my cousin.

The charger makes the whole possibility absurd. If she did take it, what is she going to do when the battery runs out? She would have the shaver and I would have the charger. A very useful distribution of equipment.

It reminded me of Serbian customs taking my radar detector and leaving the power supply behind. Take the expensive device, leave the bit that makes it work. Brilliant.

Meanwhile I'm surrounded by their possessions, apparently expected to account for every old thing I remove. I've said I may throw things away or sell them, including to recover money I believe these people have cost me. I don't feel bad about wanting the excess gone.

The shaver is still unaccounted for. That part of the story has not acquired an answer just because I've a strong suspicion.

# The Call Outside Barcelona

Somewhere outside Barcelona, I stopped and called my brother. It had taken me far too long to do something that simple.

<!-- truncate -->

I had spent years listening to Elizabeth talk about Martin.

What he had done. What he had said. How badly he had treated her. There was always something else. Another account of what a terrible son had done to his mother.

I listened.

I did not sit down and decide I wanted to hate my brother. But after enough of those conversations, I found myself feeling exactly that about things she said he had done.

Two years. Three. Four. Five. I cannot neatly count how long the accounts accumulated. I know there were a fucking lot of them.

Eventually I stopped and asked myself what I was doing.

I was angry with Martin over a story I had been hearing from Elizabeth, and I had not actually had the conversation with Martin.

So I called him.

There is no elaborate scene to add to that memory. I remember being outside Barcelona. I remember deciding that it had been long enough and that we needed to talk.

That was something I could do.

I heard his account. It was different from hers.

That did not make him innocent of every mistake. It did not make every word he said a complete history. It made the story more complicated than the one I had been carrying.

Both of them had done things I objected to. Both had made mistakes. Each account presented the other person as the problem.

I had been listening to one of those accounts until it began to feel like I knew the situation.

That is the part I do not enjoy admitting.

I should have called earlier.

I know how incomplete one person's explanation of a conflict can be. I know what it is like to have my intentions described to somebody who has not spoken to me. I have spent enough of my life objecting to precisely that.

And I still let years pass.

There are principles I can explain very well while failing to notice where I am not practicing them. This was one of those places.

I can explain how much she talked about him. I can explain how the repetition affected me. I cannot make those explanations erase my responsibility for accepting things before I asked him.

I was there. I listened. I believed enough of it that it affected how I felt about him.

Then I questioned it.

Both parts belong in the story.

That experience is one reason the situation with my younger brother is so difficult to watch. Elizabeth lives with him now. His original email made judgments about me while saying he did not want to know what either side had done to the other.

I recognize how someone's account can become familiar enough that you stop noticing how little of it you have checked with the person being described.

I cannot tell him what every conversation in his house means. I cannot claim that my call with Martin establishes what he must think.

But I can tell him why hearing me matters. I have been on the other side of that failure myself.

I have also done the fucking thing I wish somebody had done before deciding what my actions meant.

I stopped receiving an account of my brother for long enough to speak to my brother.

Martin and I have had our own difficulties since then. That conversation did not repair everything forever. I am not turning it into a scene where one phone call restores a family and everybody suddenly becomes sensible.

It did give me something I had been missing.

His voice in a story about him.

I wish it had taken less time. I remain uncomfortable with how much I accepted before making that call.

But when I remember the place outside Barcelona, that is what I remember doing: finally leaving enough room for the person I had been judging to answer.

I went to the doctor.

This was my first mistake.

Not because the doctor was bad. The doctor was actually fine. Nice guy. My generation. Seemed competent. Examined me. Asked questions. Prescribed medicine.

The problem was that before we could get to the part where one human being with medical training examines another human being who has made several questionable lifestyle decisions, we first had to participate in **Bulgaria**.

A guy I was drinking with the day before had called the doctor for me.

Why?

I have no idea.

Apparently connections are important here.

I could have just walked into the office, which is exactly what I eventually did anyway, but somebody knew somebody, a phone call was made, perhaps a goat changed hands somewhere in the background, and now I had an appointment.

Fine.

I show up at 11:30.

Doctor takes my Bulgarian identification number.

Types it into the computer.

Looks at the screen.

“Oh. You haven’t paid your health insurance for five years.”

Okay.

And?

Apparently this is now part of the medical examination.

He prints out a document explaining that I owe roughly a thousand euros to the Bulgarian healthcare system.

He starts explaining how easy it will be to fix.

Motherfucker, I did not come here to restore my relationship with the National Health Insurance Fund.

I came here because I would like a doctor to inspect the increasingly questionable meat vehicle currently transporting my brain around.

So I ask the obvious question.

“Can I just pay you?”

“Oh, yes.”

“How much?”

“About fifty euros.”

**GREAT.**

There we go.

I have money.

You have doctor.

Let us conduct commerce.

<!-- truncate -->

This apparently makes me unusual.

Because the entire system seems constructed around the assumption that I am another Bulgarian trying desperately to avoid spending fifty euros.

The doctor starts examining me.

Blood pressure high.

Yes.

Take this pill.

Fine.

Take this other thing.

Okay.

Stop doing cocaine.

No shit.

Stop drinking.

Again, fascinating medical breakthrough.

“Are you taking anything?”

“No.”

Pause.

“I drink like a fish, though.”

And there we are.

Medicine.

Finally.

Actual medicine.

He gives me prescriptions. Wants blood work done. Several things checked.

But apparently he hasn’t selected everything because some of the tests cost money.

I explain, once again, the revolutionary concept:

**I will pay for them.**

Check whatever needs checking.

I am not trying to win Bulgarian Healthcare: Free-to-Play Edition.

I would like information about the condition of the aforementioned meat vehicle.

He gives me the laboratory paperwork and tells me to go immediately because I haven’t eaten yet.

Excellent.

I walk to the laboratory.

Hand the woman the papers.

And immediately we begin another Bulgarian side quest.

“You should come first thing in the morning without eating.”

“I haven’t eaten.”

“Yes, but you also need to urinate into a plastic cup.”

Of course I do.

Naturally the medical adventure has now become a scavenger hunt.

Where do I get the cup?

Do I buy the cup?

Do I receive the Sacred Cup of Bulgarian Diagnostics from an old woman in a forest?

Apparently I am supposed to acquire a plastic container, piss in it at home tomorrow morning, and transport my urine across town like some kind of deeply disappointing Olympic torch.

Then she asks:

“Do you have a направление from the doctor?”

I look at the papers I just handed her.

“What do you mean?”

“A referral.”

“No. These are paid tests.”

Pause.

“Oh. Paid.”

**YES.**

PAID.

WITH MONEY.

THE MONEY I KEEP TRYING TO GIVE YOU PEOPLE.

The entire morning consists of Bulgarians trying to save me fifty euros while I am desperately attempting to spend fifty euros.

And this is where the story gets slightly darker.

While printing the paperwork showing that I haven’t contributed to Bulgarian health insurance for five years, the doctor casually tells me that he also works on an ambulance.

Okay.

Then he casually tells me he watched a man die in the ambulance because the man had no health insurance and apparently no hospital would take him.

Excuse me?

I thought this was the European socialist healthcare paradise where we all made fun of Americans because somebody can get hit by a bus and wake up owing $93,000 for the privilege.

Apparently we have developed a more efficient system.

You can just die.

Brilliant.

I lived in Spain for years.

I never remember participating in any of this shit.

I needed medical care.

I went somewhere.

Somebody did medical things.

At no point did I embark on a bureaucratic RPG to locate the correct piece of paper so that somebody could determine whether my urine was financially authorized.

And because apparently I had not accumulated enough Bulgarian absurdity for one week, there is also the camper.

Friday, I visit a mechanic.

“I just bought this camper. I haven’t really driven it. The engine has no power. I know about that problem, but please check the whole thing because I have no idea what else is wrong.”

“Bring it Monday. We’ll look at it in a day or two.”

Fine.

Monday at noon, I drop it off.

I explain everything again.

They write it down.

Important detail:

**THEY WRITE IT DOWN.**

Thursday.

Noon.

Phone rings.

Mechanic.

“So besides the engine having no power, what else do you want us to check?”

Motherfucker.

We have already had this conversation.

You possess written documentation of this conversation.

I know because I watched you create it.

“I just bought it. I haven’t used it. Check everything.”

Again.

Four days after dropping it off.

So apparently “we’ll look at it in a day or two” meant:

“We will allow your camper to acclimate peacefully to its new surroundings for approximately seventy-two hours before somebody reads the fucking piece of paper attached to it.”

And this is why normal transactions in Bulgaria occasionally make me insane.

I don’t need VIP treatment.

I don’t need a connection.

I don’t need somebody to save me €50.

I don’t need seven pieces of paper proving that another piece of paper exists.

I don’t need the mechanic to call me Thursday and ask what Monday’s written instructions say.

I need:

Doctor does doctor thing.

Laboratory does laboratory thing.

Mechanic does mechanic thing.

I give everyone money.

Everybody goes home.

Apparently this is an unreasonable business model.

Anyway.

I have pills now.

Tomorrow I may even acquire a piss cup.

The camper is allegedly being inspected.

And according to modern medicine, cocaine, alcohol, and high blood pressure may be detrimental to my long-term survival.

Thank fuck I finally went to a professional.

Going back to Spain.

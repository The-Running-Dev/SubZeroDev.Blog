# I Didn't Learn to Be Present. I Noticed I Already Was.

At some point I realized something mildly irritating.

I have spent years reading and listening to people talk about presence.

Alan Watts.

Zen.

Buddhism.

Taoism.

Wu wei.

Effortless action.

Do not force the moment.

Do not live entirely in the past or the future.

Be here.

Very profound.

Except apparently I have been doing a lot of this shit for years without knowing what to call it.

<!-- truncate -->

That is not me claiming enlightenment.

Jesus Christ.

Absolutely not.

I still get angry.

I still overthink things.

I still try to control things I cannot control.

I still catch myself wanting explanations the universe has not filed with customer support.

But the underlying operating pattern?

That was already there.

## I Hate Plans

I have always hated plans.

Trips.

Schedules.

Routines.

People say:

> We need to plan this.

And some part of me immediately goes:

> Do we?

Can we not simply go do the fucking thing?

This has annoyed people for years.

I never had a philosophical explanation for it.

I was not sitting there thinking:

> Ah yes. The Tao teaches non-forcing.

No.

I just hated planning.

I did not want to pre-commit the future.

I did not want to decide Tuesday from the perspective of Sunday.

Sunday does not know shit about Tuesday.

Tuesday can handle Tuesday when Tuesday gets here.

## No Plan Does Not Mean Nothing Happens

This distinction seems to confuse people.

No plan does not mean inactivity.

I can have no plan and still have ten projects in progress, a camper to fix, code to write, AI to abuse, a dog to deal with, a yard to mess with, and three unrelated ideas that appeared while making coffee.

There is always shit to do.

I just do not necessarily want to assign it to 14:30 on a calendar three days in advance.

If I feel like working on the game engine, I work on the game engine.

If I feel like fixing something outside, I go outside.

If I am tired, I sleep.

If I do not want to do anything, sometimes I do not do anything.

Then something changes.

And I move.

Apparently there is a philosophical tradition for this.

Of course there fucking is.

## The Dating Version Is the Same

This came up again talking to Moonflower.

She kept trying to understand who I was.

Where are you from?

What do you do?

What is your real name?

Are you guarded?

Are you lonely?

What does this mean?

And I kept thinking:

**Woman, I am right fucking here.**

We are already talking.

This is already happening.

Why are we trying to turn the interaction into a specification document?

Then the conversation got good.

Songs.

Comedy.

Writing.

Personal stories.

Her own music.

Random shit.

And what changed?

Not me.

She stopped trying so hard to classify me for a while.

We were simply there.

That was the good part.

## The Response Arrives

The same thing happens when I talk.

I do not usually engineer responses.

Something happens.

I react.

Sometimes I reread what I wrote afterward and think:

> Jesus Christ.

> What the fuck did you just say?

But I said it.

It was real in the moment.

Then the analysis arrives afterward.

This used to make me wonder whether I should filter more.

Moderate more.

Present a cleaner version.

Now I trust the first response much more than I used to.

Not because every spontaneous thought is wise.

Some are absolute garbage.

But because I know what it feels like when I am actually present.

There is no committee meeting.

No branding exercise.

No social simulation.

The thing happens.

I answer.

## Alan Watts Arrives Late

That is the funny part.

I listened to Watts talking about being present.

I read about Zen.

I encountered wu wei.

And increasingly my response has not been:

> I should learn to do that.

It has been:

> Wait.

> I already do that.

Not perfectly.

Not constantly.

But enough that the language feels like recognition rather than instruction.

The philosophy did not install the behavior.

It gave the behavior a label.

That is a very different thing.

## Leaf in the Wind

I have used the phrase "leaf in the wind" for at least a decade.

I do not even remember when I started saying it.

For years it probably meant:

I go where life takes me.

Fine.

Then recently I realized how much more was hiding inside it.

No fixed identity.

No permanent occupation.

No guaranteed destination.

No requirement to explain every turn.

Movement without a complete map.

I had been carrying the metaphor around before I understood why I liked it.

Again:

The thing came first.

The explanation arrived later.

Apparently this is becoming a theme.

## No Accounting Required

Maybe that is why I keep forgetting these philosophical frameworks.

I do not need to consciously account for them.

I do not wake up and run:

> Stoicism.exe

Then:

> Zen.exe

Then:

> Taoism.exe

I wake up.

Life happens.

I respond.

Sometimes badly.

Sometimes beautifully.

Sometimes absurdly.

Then later I notice that some dead philosopher already wrote three hundred pages about the thing I just did while looking for a screwdriver.

Fine.

Good for him.

I am not trying to become present.

I am here.

Sometimes I forget that.

Then I remember.

And apparently remembering is enough.

You can fuck up.

You can make a decision I don't like. You can disappoint me. You can end a business relationship. You can tell me something I would much rather not hear.

I might have a fairly substantial opinion about it.

But we can at least begin with the thing that actually happened.

Own the fucking thing.

What kept coming up for me, from Elizabeth to Miguel, was how much harder a conversation becomes when I can't even get that far.

<!-- truncate -->

## Elizabeth

With Elizabeth, my issue wasn't that she had failed to be a perfect mother.

There is no version of this where I produce a flawless-parent specification, mark every deviation in red, and announce that she has failed acceptance testing.

What I kept coming back to was how much a plain acknowledgement would have meant.

Something as simple as: I made mistakes. I did the best I could with what I knew at the time.

That is what I wanted to be able to hear.

An acknowledgement that mistakes happened. That my experience could be part of the conversation without first having to survive an argument about whether it existed.

Instead, the responses I was describing amounted to: that didn't happen; I was a good mother.

And that's where I get fucking stuck.

Because now we aren't talking about how two people remember something, what happened, or what we can do with it. I'm trying to get the event admitted into the room.

I am not asking somebody to declare that every single thing they ever did was terrible.

I am asking for enough ownership to discuss a particular thing without a general declaration of being good swallowing it whole.

Doing the best you knew how to do and making mistakes can both be true.

I can make room for that.

What am I supposed to do with a conversation where the only available version leaves no room for what I experienced?

## I Don't Need a Confession to Everything

There are details people remember differently. There are things I can get wrong.

I don't get to turn *own your shit* into *agree with my entire account or you're refusing accountability*.

That would be a spectacularly convenient rule for me.

It would also be bullshit.

If I have a detail wrong, let's talk about the detail. If there's something I don't know, tell me. I want a conversation where information can change what either of us thinks.

But there has to be a conversation.

An actual exchange about something specific.

When I say that acknowledging mistakes would have been enough, I mean how much that acknowledgement itself would have mattered to me. I wasn't asking for an elaborate performance. I wasn't waiting for the perfect sequence of words.

A human being admitting that they had got things wrong would have given me something human to meet.

## Then There's Miguel

Different relationship. Different circumstances.

The same fucking issue came up for me.

A business relationship can end. I can get fired. A rate can change. I might dislike all of that, but those are things we can discuss.

If the rate is going from $90 to $80 because you want another $10 of margin, tell me that.

I can dislike the proposal. I can question it. I can decide whether I will work on those terms.

That is business information I can actually use.

I'm not owed a rate forever because I prefer it. I'm not owed an ongoing engagement because ending it is inconvenient for me.

What I wanted from Miguel was the direct conversation about what was happening.

Look me in the fucking face and own the decision.

What made it land harder for me was that Miguel had himself described being treated that way. That history mattered to my expectations of our interaction.

I still don't know what was happening inside his head. I don't need to invent a motive for him.

The part I can speak to is my experience of trying to have the conversation and not getting it.

The business outcome and the way we deal with each other are both real. Accepting that somebody can end an arrangement doesn't mean I have to think every way of handling that ending is equally decent.

## Leave Me Something Real to Respond To

An uncomfortable truth gives me options.

I can answer it. Disagree with it. Accept it. Walk away from it.

If the terms have changed, I can decide whether the new terms work for me.

If you acknowledge a mistake, we can talk about what follows. Maybe there is something to repair. Maybe the relationship still changes. An admission isn't a magic reset button, and I'm not promising one.

But now we're discussing something.

That matters.

I don't need every conversation to end with me getting what I want. I need enough reality in it to make my own decision.

And if that's what I ask of people, I owe them the same fucking thing.

## Unfortunately, This Includes Me

I don't get to keep this standard in a drawer marked *Other People's Failures*.

When I make a mistake, I have to name it.

When I make an uncomfortable decision, I have to acknowledge that I made it. My reasons may be relevant. They don't make the decision disappear.

When I say something badly, I don't get to substitute a lecture about my good intentions for listening to what I actually said.

That's my aisle.

Get the fucking mop.

I can care about somebody and still have a difficult conversation with them. I can own a choice without demanding that they approve of it. I can explain myself and leave them free to decide what the explanation means to them.

That's what I wanted in those conversations with Elizabeth and Miguel.

Enough honesty to stand in the same room with what happened.

We can deal with mistakes. We can deal with changed terms. We can deal with an ending.

But first, own your shit.

I'll have to own mine too.

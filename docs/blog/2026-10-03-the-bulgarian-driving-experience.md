# The Bulgarian Driving Experience

Today was judgment day for Van Diesel.

Not because I planned it.

I knew this day would come eventually, but I never circled a date on the calendar and declared:

> Today we determine whether the ancient Mercedes camper deserves to live.

It just happened.

The camper needed to go to the repair shop. I needed to know whether it was worth keeping. So I loaded the e-bike into the back and set off.

Naturally, Van Diesel ran out of diesel on the way.

Of course it did.

So before judgment day could begin, we had to unlock the first side quest:

**Acquire the substance the vehicle is literally named after.**

A few kilometers of detour later, fuel acquired, Van Diesel continued toward its tribunal.

And that is when the Bulgarian Driving Experience began.

<!-- truncate -->

## Passing the Camper Is Mandatory

I got onto the highway behind a woman going slowly.

Fine.

I passed her.

Then the road went uphill.

Van Diesel is many things.

Fast uphill is not one of them.

So the woman I had just passed caught back up and got directly on my ass with her left blinker on.

Not just following.

Not just waiting.

The full message:

**MOVE. I HAVE URGENT BULGARIAN BUSINESS.**

All right.

Eventually she passes me.

Mission accomplished.

Except she immediately takes the next exit.

Of course she does.

Then she leaves the blinker on for about another kilometer.

And slows down.

I catch her.

I pass her again.

So apparently the emergency was not getting somewhere quickly.

The emergency was being in front of the camper.

This is an important distinction.

## Natural Order Must Be Restored

A little later, fancy SUV.

BMW, I think.

Same opening move.

Gets on my ass.

I move over.

He passes.

Again: fine.

But now the road is about to merge.

His lane merges into mine.

I keep driving exactly the same speed.

At the last moment, he accelerates hard so he can get in front of me before his lane disappears.

Excellent.

Natural order restored.

Fancy SUV ahead.

Ancient camper behind.

Civilization survives another day.

Then the speed restriction arrives.

And suddenly this man discovers traffic law.

He slows down.

I do not slow down as much as he does.

So I move left and pass him.

Again.

This guy had aggressively passed a fucking camper, raced the merge so he could force himself in front of it, and then immediately slowed down enough for the camper to pass him back.

Why?

No idea.

But I think I understand the mechanic now.

**Passing the camper is mandatory. Remaining faster than the camper is optional.**

## The Road Is Also a Living Room

Then you get onto the secondary roads.

Two lanes.

One each direction.

Perfectly ordinary road.

Except this is Bulgaria, so sometimes the road is also where people stop to have a conversation.

Car pulled over on one side.

Another somewhere else.

Hazard lights on.

Maybe half in the lane.

Window down.

Somebody talking to somebody.

Everybody else can work around it.

Hazard lights apparently grant temporary diplomatic immunity.

The road has become a living room.

Why park somewhere?

Why leave the road?

Why interrupt the conversation with unnecessary logistics?

Put the flashers on.

You are now infrastructure.

## Camper Mode

At some point I realized the camper had the correct response to all of this.

Van Diesel does not care.

BMW needs to establish dominance?

Fine.

Woman needs to ride the bumper with the blinker on?

Fine.

Somebody needs to win a merge?

Fine.

People need to turn the road into a neighborhood meeting room?

Fine.

Van Diesel continues at approximately whatever speed Van Diesel was already doing.

This is not defiance.

This is not competition.

This is a vehicle with the acceleration profile of continental drift.

There is no emotional energy available for your bullshit.

**Camper Mode:**

- Acceleration: eventually.
- Urgency: unavailable.
- Competitive instinct: none.
- Ability to accidentally pass you again after you desperately passed it: surprisingly high.

The beautiful thing is that I was not trying to beat anybody.

I was trying to get an old camper to a repair shop before it invented another side quest.

Everybody else supplied the game mechanics.

## Judgment Day

Eventually Van Diesel made it.

The mechanic asked how I was getting home.

I said I had an e-bike in the back.

He asked if I needed him to drop me off.

I looked at him.

I did not know that was an option.

No.

I had brought the e-bike.

We were committed now.

So Van Diesel stayed behind to find out whether it deserves continued existence.

I unfolded the bike.

And the Bulgarian Driving Experience changed game modes completely.

But that is another story.

For now, the rules are simple:

If you see a camper in front of you, you must pass it.

If you have to accelerate aggressively to do so, even better.

If you immediately slow down afterward, that is perfectly acceptable.

If the camper passes you again, do not examine the contradiction.

Just wait.

There will be another camper eventually.

Your honor can be restored.

“Oh my God, I’m a fucking wizard.”

<!-- truncate -->

That was my opening assessment of the situation. I wanted a hookah. I hadn’t smoked in two or three weeks, and the shisha I had was dried-up shit. So I put in a dab of glycerin, about half a spoon of molasses, chocolate extract, mint extract, mixed the bastard up and loaded the hookah.

Did I know whether it would work? Fuck no. By the time I was considering that question, I was already trying it.

And it was decent. The chocolate and mint could have been stronger, but the thing actually smoked and tasted pretty fucking good. This was particularly satisfying because my previous attempt at making shisha had turned out too wet. I’d bought pipe tobacco specifically to make chocolate mint, bought a whole box of extracts for the project, made a half-assed attempt and still had the tobacco sitting there.

Then, while smoking this resurrected mess, I finally had the revelation: I could take shisha that already existed and change the flavor.

I had bought a box of flavor extracts and somehow needed a separate moment of genius to discover that I could add flavor with them.

Lucifer, bearer of light, occasionally requires several years to locate the fucking switch.

I also had two old boxes I’d brought from Spain about two years earlier. Suddenly they had prospects. I’d even mixed chocolate sauce into shisha before, so this wasn’t my first encounter with the idea of putting chocolate in something. Apparently the committee inside my head had simply failed to circulate the minutes.

As I sat there enjoying the result, all the other stories started coming back. The motorcycles. The customs bullshit. The lounges. The dog. The people who looked at me like I’d arrived from another planet.

There is a considerable history behind this particular bowl of nonsense.

My younger brother introduced me to shisha when I was about thirty-five. Before that, I wasn’t into it. Afterward, chocolate mint became my favorite, and I proceeded to organize an unreasonable amount of my life around acquiring it.

In Austin, this was easy. Hookah-Shisha was right there in the same city. I could order and have the stuff delivered the next day. They had reviews; I could try flavors. A 250-gram tin might be twelve, fifteen, twenty bucks, maybe twenty-five. A whole kilo was around seventy or eighty dollars.

I tried all kinds of shit. Chocolate mint remained the one I wanted.

Back when I was on good terms with my family in the States, I’d bring a kilo or two over. A kilogram could last me six months to a year. A civilized arrangement: acquire favorite tobacco, spend a long time enjoying favorite tobacco.

Then there was the European version.

When I moved to Bulgaria in 2018, I had my hookah and a plastic box containing probably thirty flavors of shisha. All open. My own collection, already being used. I’d hired a company to handle the move.

I still ended up paying about $150 to get my own fucking shisha back. The way I remember it, the money went through the agency and was split with customs.

Thirty opened flavors in a plastic box. That was the magnificent commercial empire being intercepted. Somewhere among my personal belongings, apparently, lurked an international tobacco cartel with one customer.

Me.

That experience did little to inspire confidence in the idea of ordering more from America. I considered shipping to Spain, or to Bulgaria, and kept running into the prospect of customs and tobacco taxes making the whole thing absurd. In Bulgaria, I reckoned a kilo could be something like €300. I wasn’t even sure which currency I was swearing about anymore. The amount was enough.

While I was living in Southampton, I found out there was a shop in London selling the brand I wanted. London was about two hours away.

So I got on the motorcycle and went.

There is commitment, and then there is getting on a bike to cross a sizeable portion of England because you fancy chocolate-mint tobacco.

I got to the shop. They didn’t have the chocolate mint.

They did have other flavors. At £250 a kilo.

I’d been buying a kilo in America for seventy or eighty dollars. Here I was, having ridden up from Southampton, being invited to pay two hundred and fifty fucking pounds for something that wasn’t even the flavor I’d come for.

I refused.

I know import taxes exist. I had already been personally introduced to the concept by a plastic box at Bulgarian customs. They could still shove that price up their ass.

The tobacco stayed in the shop.

In Barcelona, at least, I found a more rewarding way to spend my money.

I’d moved there to work for a sports betting company. Naturally, one of the first things I did was find the nearby hookah lounges. One was called Purple, VIP Purple, something like that. I can remember the routine much better than the exact name.

It was about ten minutes from where I lived. I’d leave work at six and walk over. I did that every fucking day for at least a year.

Eventually I stopped needing to order. I walked in, the guy saw me, and he went to prepare my hookah.

That is a dangerous level of efficiency to offer a man like me. Remove the administrative burden of asking for the thing, and I can really settle into a place.

One time I brought my mother.

She wanted white wine. The owner went to the fucking store, bought a bottle and gave it to her on the house.

I’d spent a lot of money there, sure. I’d also always treated them nicely. I came in, talked to people, enjoyed myself. Over enough evenings, the relationship had reached the point where my mother could turn up and the owner would go shopping for her.

I went looking for somewhere to smoke and ended up with a place that recognized both me and the importance of getting my mother her wine.

San Antonio was where I took the concept considerably further.

Before I came to Europe in 2018, I lived in a camper for about a year. I went from York to Jersey, then Virginia, and eventually San Antonio. I set the camper up at a campground in San Antonio.

My working day went like this.

Wake up around fucking noon. Put the laptop in a bag. Pick up the monitor by its stand. Take the dog. Go to the Lebanese hookah lounge.

I don’t remember its name anymore. It was a giant place with an outdoor area, and I’d get there around two.

Yes, I carried an actual monitor in. Apparently I had assessed the requirements of a mobile office and concluded that mobility should involve carrying a fucking monitor by the neck.

I’d set up the computer and order four ayrans.

People would look at me like, how can you drink that yogurt?

These are my people. Bring the fucking ayrans.

Then I’d work. Have some food. Smoke two or three hookahs. I was consulting, so the work included meetings. The existence of other people on a call did nothing to alter the location of my office.

One time I was in a meeting with the bosses at the consulting company, with Miguel, and they asked where I was and what I was doing.

“I’m fucking smoking hookah.”

The reaction was approximately: fucking what?

I was completely nonchalant about it. They tried to give a shit. I continued having my meeting.

The laptop was there. The monitor was there. I was there. The dog had accompanied management. Four ayrans had been procured. I considered the operation adequately staffed and supplied.

I made friends at that lounge, too. There was a girl who’d been afraid to approach me because I looked too serious. I was around forty; she was around twenty. Eventually I started telling stories, bought her drinks, and we had a little fling.

Apparently my face had been advertising a much more severe man than the one operating the camper-based hookah consultancy.

That was also where I first met Tanisha, from El Paso. She didn’t like me at all, for whatever fucking reason. The hospitality ministry has never achieved universal conversion.

The pattern followed me everywhere. Arrive in a city, look for a hookah lounge, see what happens.

In Jersey, while traveling with the camper, I found one, made friends and got drunk. In Amsterdam, I rented a bicycle and decided to explore as many lounges as I could find.

I reckon I visited at least ten. I’ve been to Amsterdam about a dozen times, so there was ample opportunity to pursue this highly specific form of tourism.

Can I now give a detailed assessment of every establishment?

Fuck no. I sort of remember one of them. There were a lot.

I did the same thing in Sofia. There were at least a dozen places to investigate, and I’d been to so many that keeping count had long since ceased to be part of the activity.

The motorcycle had its own contribution to the tour.

I had it shipped from Houston to Bremerhaven, picked it up in Germany and rode it back toward Bulgaria. At some point I pulled off the Autobahn into a random German town to get petrol.

While I was there, I checked whether there was a hookah lounge.

There was one across the fucking street.

It was Turkish. They didn’t speak English. I didn’t speak German.

I smoked hookah anyway.

I couldn’t tell you the town’s name from that story. I can tell you that I stopped for fuel and found somewhere to smoke across the road. My internal map has its priorities.

In Sofia, one particular lounge became a fixture.

On my first night in Bulgaria in 2018, I was in the center, where my mother had her apartment. A couple hundred meters away was a lounge called Damascus.

It was pretty fucking garbage and empty then. Somebody eventually bought it and redid it. It became Hanishisha, and now it’s nice. I’ve been going to that same place for eight years. For stretches, I went every day.

And that is where I met Dobri.

I was waiting for my uncle Mario when I heard two guys speaking English. I could tell one was American. So I went over and asked whether I could join them while I waited.

Dobri, the Bulgarian, looked at me like I was a fucking alien. He had a very definite who-the-fuck-is-this-guy look.

Eric, the American, welcomed me over.

Eric and I became friends first. We aren’t friends anymore; that went its own way. Dobri and I became friends, too, which is rather funny considering his initial assessment of the unexpected visitor.

Then Mario arrived and joined us.

I was about forty-five. Mario was about seventy-five. Dobri was around thirty-five, Eric somewhere in his thirties. Mario didn’t speak English. Eric could barely speak Bulgarian.

We sat there and fucking bullshitted anyway.

Four men, several decades between them, and an uneven distribution of languages. Somehow we managed a perfectly serviceable evening.

Dobri and I went on to spend a ridiculous number of nights in hookah lounges. Sometimes we’d be out until six in the morning. Sofia even had a lounge open twenty-four hours a day, because apparently somebody had identified men like us as a business opportunity.

Hanishisha would close at one. Dobri and I would go across town to Flame.

Flame had couches and televisions. We’d sit there drinking, smoking and listening to music. That was where he introduced me to *Diamonds and Rust*.

We did that so often that the place accumulated its own cast of characters.

One night we met a programmer who was celebrating a raise, along with his friend Vasil. I don’t remember the programmer’s name. I do remember what he was celebrating, and I remember Vasil because that was my father’s name.

Also because Vasil looked like an aristocratic fucking vampire.

Some rich kid, or at least that was the look. I’m not supplying a family tree. I’m telling you what the man looked like.

I called them over. How precisely we became friends has disappeared from the record inside my head. We kept running into each other there, and every time we did, it became: hey, sit down, smoke, drink, exchange stories.

That was the arrangement. Recognition, a place to sit, and another night gone.

Dobri was unattached then. Eventually he had a girlfriend, and that regular routine was over. That was maybe a couple of years ago.

I still sometimes go by myself. Take the dog. Get one of the booths with a closed door, order a hookah and some food, listen to music and do absolutely fucking nothing.

I have put substantial effort into securing an appropriate venue for doing nothing.

What amuses me is that I only recently seemed to notice the social side of all this.

I saw Dobri about a week ago and told him that now I talk to everybody. I make friends everywhere.

He looked at me and said I’d always been able to do that.

Well, shit. I hadn’t realized.

Apparently I needed the man who once looked at me like an alien for introducing myself to point out that I had been introducing myself to people for years.

And I managed to embarrass that same man in a Sofia park.

I have a portable hookah setup. A little hard case. Pack the equipment, take it with me. A traveling fucking lounge.

So we went to a park in the center of Sofia. I filled the hookah from a fountain, set it up, lit it, and there we were, sitting among everybody else, bullshitting and smoking.

Dobri was embarrassed.

“What the fuck is embarrassing?”

We were sitting in a park smoking hookah. We’d spent all those nights smoking together, and now he was embarrassed that I’d brought the equipment myself.

People walked past. They looked at me like I was a fucking alien.

Then they went about their business.

That was the incident. A man unpacked his lounge. Other people briefly observed the man. The planet remained in operation.

By the time I got to the village bars, I’d already accepted that my equipment could accompany me socially.

One night I decided, fuck it, I’m taking the hookah.

I packed it up, went to the bar where I hang out and asked whether I could set it up in the outside lounge.

Yeah, no problem.

So I set it up, lit the coals and sat there drinking and smoking.

When I was finished at that bar, I packed it up, went to a different bar and did the same fucking thing.

I was bar hopping with a hookah.

Bring your own atmosphere. Assembly required.

Somewhere along the way, I also ended up owning three hookahs. One came from the States. One I bought here while the other was in Spain. The third—fuck knows. I cannot account for the entire population.

But there they are.

And once VanDiesel is fixed, once I can get traveling again, I have an idea for them.

Find a beach somewhere. Take the hookahs out. Put up a sign:

**FREE HOOKAH**

**Come smoke with me.**

That is the whole proposal.

After all the shops, prices, customs, motorcycle trips, regular tables, unfamiliar languages and cases carried from one place to another, I want to arrive somewhere with the gear and invite people to sit down.

I can already imagine the looks.

I’ve had practice.

Remembering all this makes me feel like some absurd fucking legend. A caricature of a human being wandering through life with a dog, a monitor, a hard case and a completely disproportionate attachment to chocolate mint.

Lucifer with luggage. A jester who keeps finding another place to sit and another person to bullshit with.

I know I’m a ridiculous motherfucker.

It amuses me to no end.

I started the evening resurrecting dried-up shisha. Now I’m contemplating a beach and a sign inviting strangers into whatever fucking circus this is.

**FREE HOOKAH. Come smoke with me.**

The wandering jester has brought enough to share.

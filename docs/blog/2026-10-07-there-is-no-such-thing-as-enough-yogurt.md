# There Is No Such Thing as Enough Yogurt

I went out to buy gin, meat, and cheese.

Naturally I also bought yogurt.

There was already yogurt in the fridge.

This information was irrelevant.

There is no such thing as enough yogurt.

There is only:

> We currently have yogurt.

And:

> What the fuck do you mean we are out of yogurt?

<!-- truncate -->

I do not think people understand my relationship with this stuff.

I can buy a half-liter container.

A kilo.

One of those giant little buckets.

Whatever.

I can open it and just eat the fucking thing.

Plain.

No fruit.

No sugar.

Absolutely not.

Plain yogurt is already finished.

It does not need a software update.

## Salt Is Acceptable

Sometimes I put salt in it.

Bulgarian things.

That is allowed.

Water?

Sure.

Cucumber?

Of course.

Now we are heading toward half the cold things Bulgaria has invented for surviving summer.

But yogurt does not need to become anything else.

Snack?

Yogurt.

Meal?

Yogurt.

Dessert?

Yogurt.

Standing in front of the fridge because I walked through the kitchen?

Also yogurt.

There is no scheduling requirement.

## Teenage Dairy Logistics

We used to make the shit.

This was not some artisanal lifestyle project.

Teenagers would get on bicycles, ride to the edge of the city, buy three or four liters of fresh milk from the cows, bring it back, make a giant fucking pot of yogurt, and then destroy the entire batch within a day.

That was the system.

Procurement.

Transport.

Processing.

Immediate consumption.

No marketing department required.

No tiny glass jars with handwritten labels.

Just a large quantity of yogurt being produced because a large quantity of yogurt was about to disappear.

## Bulgarian Application Surface

The reason this works is that yogurt here is not trapped inside one food category.

It is not "breakfast."

It is not "health food."

It is not "dessert."

It is a base medium.

Plain.

Salted.

Thinned with water.

With cucumber.

With whatever else becomes tarator-adjacent.

Then there are eggs.

Poach some eggs.

Put them into yogurt.

Hot oil or butter.

Red pepper.

Jesus fucking Christ.

Яйца по панагюрски.

This country occasionally looks at a perfectly good food and asks:

> What if we put yogurt under it?

Correct question.

## Infrastructure

That is why buying more yogurt when there is already yogurt in the fridge makes perfect sense to me.

I am not buying "another snack."

I am maintaining infrastructure.

You do not wait until the last packet leaves the router before ordering another cable.

You do not let the diesel tank hit zero because technically there was fuel five minutes ago.

You do not let the yogurt reserve approach dangerous levels.

Basic operations.

## I Could Make My Own

And yes.

I know I can make my own.

I have made it before.

It is not hard.

Do not send me a fucking recipe.

That is exactly the kind of helpfulness that will get you removed from the kitchen.

The interesting part is that given how much yogurt I consume, it is almost strange that I buy it as often as I do instead of treating homemade yogurt as another recurring household service.

Fiber.

5G backup.

Electricity.

Water.

Yogurt.

All critical systems green.

## Enough

People ask how much yogurt is enough.

Wrong question.

Enough is a temporary state.

A kilogram can disappear.

A pot can disappear.

Teenagers can make several liters and turn them into history before the next day.

The fridge can look well provisioned and still be one enthusiastic evening away from an incident.

So when I come home with gin, meat, cheese, and yet another container of yogurt, there is no contradiction.

The gin may or may not get finished.

The meat will get eaten.

The cheese has a future.

The yogurt?

The yogurt is operating on a different timescale.

There is no such thing as enough yogurt.

There is only current capacity.

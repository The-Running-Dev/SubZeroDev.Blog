# You Know Nothing, Jon Snow

Tinder is a remarkable place.

<!-- truncate -->

You go there hoping to meet another human being, maybe have a conversation, maybe discover that two adults can exchange words without turning the whole thing into a fucking employment interview.

And then you come across a profile that offers a financial philosophy instead.

“Whoever said money can't solve your problems / Must not have had enough money to solve 'em.”

Ah, yes. The ancient wisdom of the dating app. Somewhere, Aristotle is rolling over in his grave, presumably because he couldn't afford a better one.

Now, I had thoughts.

Lots of thoughts.

My first instinct was to take the entire argument apart. What do you know about money? How much have you actually had? What problems did you solve with it? And why does this little manifesto, sitting on a dating profile of all places, read to me like an advertisement for somebody else's wallet?

I had a whole response ready.

It ended with “Good luck with that.”

Not exactly a love poem.

I even explained why I was irritated. According to what I saw, she was eighteen. I'm fifty. I've been around long enough to hear grand declarations from people who haven't yet had much opportunity to test them. And I was absolutely not interested in treating a provocative sentence as a profound discovery.

Then I looked at my own message.

Too fucking long.

Why am I writing an essay to a stranger on Tinder? Am I applying for tenure in the Department of Financial Disillusionment? Is there going to be a panel discussion afterward?

No. I don't need to dismantle the argument brick by brick. I don't need to prove I have a résumé in making and losing money. I don't even need a response.

But I had a better one.

“Oh John Snow, you know nothing.”

That's it.

A line from a fictional universe, delivered to a real-world philosophy that had apparently mistaken confidence for knowledge.

It's shorter. It's funnier. And it leaves the entire disagreement exactly where I want it: hanging in the air without a fucking PowerPoint presentation attached.

I have no idea whether she would recognize the reference. I have no idea what she would say back. At the point I described it, this was still my intended message, not some verified epic Tinder battle.

That's not the entertaining part.

The entertaining part is that I started out ready to argue the economics of human suffering with a stranger and ended up realizing that one sarcastic sentence was worth more to me than a whole goddamn lecture.

First impressions, indeed.

Sometimes the best argument is the one you stop writing.

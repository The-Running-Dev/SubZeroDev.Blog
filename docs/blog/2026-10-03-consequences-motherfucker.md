# Consequences, Motherfucker

My dog ate an electrical cord.

Not metaphorically.

Not "nibbled the insulation."

The motherfucking Ridgeback took the power cord to my shop vac and converted it into two independent pieces of electrical infrastructure.

Fortunately, I had unplugged it.

<!-- truncate -->

I did not unplug it because she had some established history of electrical sabotage.

She had never done this before.

She chews bottles.

She chews garbage.

She chews bones.

She chews whatever random object apparently announces itself to the Ridgeback brain as:

> Yes. This. Destroy this immediately.

But electrical cords?

New feature.

I left for two hours.

Two fucking hours.

She had toys.

She had a bone.

She had things specifically provided for the purpose of being chewed.

Apparently she reviewed the approved inventory and decided:

**No. Infrastructure.**

So I came home and found the shop-vac cord cut in half.

And I was furious.

Not because the cord is difficult to replace.

I can fix a cord.

Strip it.

Splice it.

Crimp it.

Heat shrink it.

Whatever.

The part that landed in my head was much worse.

What if I had left it plugged in?

What if I had come home and instead of finding a dead shop vac, I had found a dead dog?

That possibility had existed for exactly two hours while I was somewhere else having gin and tonics and assuming the animal was busy being a normal asshole.

That is a very different thought.

## Consequences

So she spent the night outside.

Not because I believe a Ridgeback sits beneath the stars performing a sophisticated moral analysis.

Not because I expect her to compose an apology.

Not because there is a canine Supreme Court somewhere issuing opinions on household electrical law.

She did some shit.

The shit changed the situation.

That is what a consequence is.

Cause.

Effect.

Something happened.

Something followed.

**Consequences, motherfucker.**

She hated it.

She whined.

She wanted back inside.

Normally she sleeps in my bed.

That night she had blankets, shelter, a place out of the wind, and apparently a profound disagreement with management.

Fine.

Disagree.

The cord remained in two pieces.

## But Did She Know?

This became the philosophical argument of the evening.

Did the dog know what she had done?

I found the cord.

I called her.

She stayed away from me.

She did not wander over with the cheerful confidence of an animal who believed the evening was proceeding normally.

She looked at the situation and chose distance.

Now, maybe she understood:

> I chewed the forbidden electrical cord and the human has discovered the evidence.

Maybe she understood:

> The human is pissed and I would prefer not to participate.

Maybe those are much closer together in a dog's mind than humans like to pretend.

I do not fucking know.

Neither does anybody else standing outside that dog's head.

But this is where humans become very strange about animals.

We insist they are intelligent when intelligence is adorable.

They understand tone.

They recognize people.

They anticipate routines.

They manipulate us.

They remember.

They learn commands.

They know when dinner happens.

They know which cupboard contains the treats.

They know the exact sound of a cheese wrapper from three rooms away.

But the moment accountability enters the conversation, suddenly:

> Oh, no. Poor thing. It could never understand any relationship between behavior and consequence.

Maybe.

Maybe not.

But that argument eventually collapsed for a completely different reason.

## People Are Not Better

Because then I thought about people.

My mother.

My brothers.

Friends.

Me.

All these supposedly advanced primates with language, memory, abstraction, calendars, legal systems, philosophy, and approximately four billion books explaining cause and effect.

And what do we do?

The same shit.

Over and over.

We know what happened last time.

We know what this behavior does.

We can describe the consequence in complete grammatical sentences.

We can explain the causal chain.

We can write a PowerPoint presentation about it.

Then we do the fucking thing again.

So who exactly am I expecting to demonstrate perfect comprehension here?

The Ridgeback?

At least she cannot read Marcus Aurelius.

Humans can.

It has not noticeably solved humanity.

## Understanding Is Not Required

That became the actual point.

Consequences do not require understanding.

Gravity does not ask whether you comprehend gravity.

Electricity does not care whether the dog understands electricity.

A severed cord does not become whole because the creature who severed it lacks a theory of copper conductivity.

Things happen.

Other things follow.

Sometimes somebody learns.

Sometimes nobody learns a goddamn thing.

The consequence still happened.

That is true for dogs.

That is true for people.

That is true for me.

And somewhere in the middle of being furious at a Ridgeback for chewing through a shop-vac cord, I realized I had been demanding from a dog something I have spent my entire life watching humans fail to achieve.

Perfect understanding of their own behavior.

Perfect anticipation of consequences.

Perfect adjustment afterwards.

Jesus fucking Christ.

That may have been unfair to the dog.

## The Next Morning

The next morning, the cord was still broken.

The dog was still a dog.

I was still me.

Nothing had become morally pure.

No grand lesson had descended from the heavens.

I repaired the thing.

She came back inside.

Life continued.

That is probably the most honest ending.

Not redemption.

Not punishment.

Not enlightenment.

Just:

The dog did some shit.

The human reacted.

The cord got fixed.

Everybody survived.

And somewhere in the process, the human discovered that the intellectual distance between Homo sapiens and one badly behaved Ridgeback may occasionally be smaller than advertised.

Consequences, motherfucker.

Consequences.

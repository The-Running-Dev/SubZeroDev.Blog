I have been called argumentative, difficult, abrasive, opinionated, and an asshole more times than I can count.

Sometimes I am an asshole. I know when I'm deliberately being one.

But a great deal of the time, I am doing something much simpler.

**I'm thinking out loud, using another person to examine my own thinking.**

And for some reason, people keep assuming they're in a debate.

<!-- truncate -->

## I'm Not Here to Prove a Point

When I sit down to talk about history, technology, philosophy, politics, or some random thing I stumbled across on the internet, I may have an opinion.

I may also have no fucking idea what I think yet.

Neither condition changes why I enjoy the conversation.

I want to lay out what's in my head, examine the premises, see whether I can express them deliberately, and follow what emerges.

I know I have biases. I know I have assumptions. I know I haven't thought about everything.

That's why I do this.

I'm not asking someone to validate me. I'm not asking to be declared correct. I'm not trying to make the other person incorrect.

**I'm using dialogue to inspect my own reasoning.**

## What Happens When I Explain Something

I'll make a statement.

Then I hear myself make it.

Then something else clicks.

"Wait. There's another idea over there that's relevant to this."

Now we're talking about that. Which exposes another connection. Which leads somewhere I didn't expect to go.

I don't necessarily experience this as a startling revelation or the creation of an entirely new thought. Often it feels like the information was sitting in my head already.

The dots simply weren't connected in this particular context.

Explaining one part brings another into focus.

It is wonderfully recursive.

## The Conversation Is Not a Courtroom

Here's the problem with assuming every exchange is a debate.

Somebody asks a question, and the other person hears an accusation.

Somebody explores an alternative interpretation, and the other person hears an allegiance to an opposing camp.

Somebody says, "I don't know enough about that," and the other person starts arguing against a position that hasn't even been formed.

I once talked to a woman living in Israel because I was curious about how she understood the conflict around her. The conversation became heated when my questions about history were interpreted as defending terrorism.

I wasn't defending anything. I was trying to learn.

And I've watched similar misunderstandings happen when discussing Bulgaria, family, and a hundred less consequential subjects.

People aren't required to detach their identity from their beliefs. But I don't automatically attach mine.

You can challenge something I think without attacking who I am.

You can even convince me that part of my reasoning doesn't hold up.

Great. That means we found something worth examining.

## Humans Have Feelings. No Shit.

Of course a conversation with a real person has other dimensions.

I pay attention to voice, expression, posture, tension, and the way a discussion changes. If I see someone struggling with a subject, I may ask whether they want to continue.

The frustrating cases are when someone insists they're fine, rejects the exit ramp, then becomes angry about where the conversation went.

Sometimes a person genuinely doesn't realize a topic will bother them until it does. That happens.

But I can't read minds, and I can't control another person's reaction.

I can only have the conversation that's actually happening.

## My Beliefs Aren't My Identity

This is an important distinction for me.

I can have a belief, investigate it, criticize it, discover a flaw in it, and change it.

I don't cease to exist when an idea turns out to be inadequate.

So why would I protect it as though my entire identity depended on it?

And why would I need someone else to adopt it?

## Get Over Yourself

If we're exploring a subject, your participation matters. Your questions and experiences might give me a new angle, or simply force me to explain what I already think.

But you aren't automatically the opponent in some imaginary contest.

I may not even be looking for an answer.

I might just be following a thought to see where it goes.

**I'm not arguing with you. I'm thinking.**

Get over yourself.

And if you'd like, come along for the fucking ride.

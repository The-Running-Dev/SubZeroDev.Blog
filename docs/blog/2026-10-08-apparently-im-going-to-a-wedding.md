# Apparently I'm Going to a Wedding

The electricity was out.

Again.

It was cold, I hadn't slept particularly well, the dog had irritated me, and at some point I decided I had accumulated enough minor annoyances for one morning.

So I gave the dog the cold shoulder, left her outside, put on a burgundy red suit with a black shirt and fancy shoes, and went for coffee.

Perfectly normal sequence of events.

Apparently not in a Bulgarian village.

<!-- truncate -->

I walked into my local place and the bartender looked at me.

Then looked at me again.

"Are you going to a wedding?"

No.

I'm missing the vest and the tie.

That was not a joke, exactly.

By my standards, this was casual.

No tie.

No tie pin.

No pocket square.

No watch.

No cufflinks.

Shirt open at the collar.

Suit jacket on, yes, but otherwise this was practically me dressing down.

The bartender accepted this information.

A little while later another guy came in. He knew me, but apparently the suit had temporarily broken facial recognition because it took him a moment to register who I was.

"Oh, Ben."

Then came the inevitable question.

"Are you going to a wedding?"

Before I could answer, the bartender jumped in.

"Oh no, he's not. He's missing the vest and the tie."

And just like that, the village had acquired a formalwear classification system.

I had explained the rule once.

It was now local knowledge.

## This Is Casual

The funny part is that even without the jacket I am apparently overdressed here.

I've gone into the same place wearing suit trousers and a black dress shirt and gotten the same kind of reaction.

And when I say "dress shirt," I don't mean some hand-stitched Italian thing that arrived in a cedar box.

I mean a black Zara shirt I found years ago for maybe twenty-five euros.

Slim fit.

Stretchy.

Slightly shiny.

Doesn't wrinkle.

Fits me perfectly.

I bought black and white ones because once I find a thing that works, I tend to stop arguing with reality.

To me, suit trousers and that shirt are just clothes.

To everyone else, apparently there is a diplomatic reception later.

## Apparently I Know How to Dress

This is also funny because I never thought of myself as a fashionable guy.

I still don't, really.

Fashion sounds like knowing what season something is from and whether some designer has decided trousers are supposed to be wider this year.

I do not care.

But the evidence has become inconvenient.

People were telling me I was a sharp dresser long before any of this became a conscious thing.

Years ago I had a personal designer help me refine it. She put together what she called a lookbook: around thirty combinations built from the clothes I already had and the pieces we added.

That was before the TV show *Lucifer*.

Then that show came along and Lucifer Morningstar appeared on screen in fitted suits, sharp shirts, pocket squares, watches, tie bars, polished shoes, and enough confidence to make subtlety someone else's problem.

I remember thinking:

Yeah.

That motherfucker gets it.

The show didn't create the look.

It just gave me a very convenient name for one version of it.

The Lucifer suit.

Today's was burgundy red, black shirt, fancy shoes.

No vest.

No tie.

Casual Lucifer.

I have around ten suits now, almost as many ties, watches, pocket squares, pins, cufflinks, shoes, all the random accessories that somehow accumulated without me ever deciding, *I am now going to become a suit person.*

I bought two suits at some random outlet outside Rome once, this burgundy one and a blue one, for around six hundred euros total.

On the same trip I bought torn-up jeans.

Because apparently consistency is overrated.

The most expensive one is an Armani I paid about a thousand euros for and then had fitted.

That one is different.

Once you've worn a properly fitted suit, you can tell.

The shoulders sit differently.

The jacket falls differently.

The trousers break differently.

The whole thing looks intentional instead of like a man has been inserted into formal packaging.

I notice that now.

Apparently I learned something along the way.

## I Am Literally Just Standing Here

There is another effect I keep forgetting about.

People look at you differently when you're dressed like this.

I've always had a certain physical presence.

Chin up.

Walk like you know where you're going.

Don't fold yourself into the furniture.

That kind of thing.

I have never particularly cared about "commanding a room" or making people respect me. I am not entering a coffee shop thinking:

*Time to establish dominance over table four.*

I'm trying to exist.

That's it.

But add a fitted suit and apparently existence becomes theater.

The reaction changes.

People look longer.

They make assumptions.

They become more attentive.

Sometimes people who already know me don't even recognize me immediately.

At the hookah lounge I've been going to in Bulgaria for years, I've had this happen several times.

I would spend months there dressed however I happened to be dressed.

Then I'd leave Bulgaria for six months, come back wearing some completely different configuration, walk in, and watch people stare at me for thirty seconds before the software finished loading.

Then:

"Oh! Ben."

What the fuck did you think happened?

Did the shirt alter my bone structure?

Apparently clothes are a more significant part of human facial recognition than anyone bothered to tell me.

## The Establishing Shot

And then I realized what today's scene must have looked like from the outside.

Because I didn't arrive in a Mercedes S-Class.

I didn't get out of some polished black sedan with a driver.

I came in my camper.

My roughly twenty-five-year-old Mercedes camper.

The one with solar panels on top.

The one that looks exactly like what it is: an old machine that has been converted, modified, lived in, repaired, driven around, and given just enough electrical infrastructure to qualify as a small independent nation.

So imagine the establishing shot.

Old Mercedes camper pulls up.

Solar panels on the roof.

The side door opens.

Out steps a man in a fitted burgundy suit, black shirt and fancy shoes.

Lucifer has emerged from the fucking camper.

He walks into the local bar.

Someone asks if he's going to a wedding.

No.

He's missing the vest and the tie.

He's just here for coffee.

Because the electricity is out at home.

Fucking Bulgaria.

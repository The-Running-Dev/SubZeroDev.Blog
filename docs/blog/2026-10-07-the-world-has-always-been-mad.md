# The World Has Always Been Mad

There is an idea I remember from *Fist of Legend*.

Not the exact subtitle.

Those things have been translated seventeen different ways anyway.

The idea.

Youth has vigor.

Age has adaptability.

That stayed with me.

Because I look at young bucks half my age sometimes and think:

Sure.

You might be faster than me.

You might be stronger than me.

But I am not sure you can adapt as fast as I can.

<!-- truncate -->

That is not because age automatically makes people wise.

It absolutely does not.

Some people get older and become concrete.

Same opinions.

Same reactions.

Same little script.

Thirty years later the script has simply acquired lower back pain.

But if you keep moving, keep changing, keep being wrong, keep having reality punch holes through your assumptions, something else can happen.

You get range.

## Things Should Be Different

I remember being idealistic.

Things should be different.

People should care more.

People should be angrier.

Parents should behave differently.

Governments should behave differently.

Society should behave differently.

Human beings should stop doing obviously self-defeating shit.

Okay.

Maybe they should.

They are not.

Now what?

That question became more interesting to me than the first one.

Not because the ideal stopped mattering.

Because reality kept arriving anyway.

## The World Has Always Been Mad

The Serbian woman I was talking to said the world had gone mad.

I understand the feeling.

Turn on a screen and you can collect evidence in five minutes.

But the world did not recently become mad.

The world has always been fucking mad.

Empires.

Religions.

Wars.

Mass movements.

Families.

Tribes.

Markets.

Governments.

People falling in love with terrible ideas.

People doing beautiful things inside terrible systems.

People behaving rationally on Tuesday and like complete lunatics on Wednesday.

Humanity has been improvising this whole fucking thing since the beginning.

The technology changes.

The costumes change.

The madness gets better cameras.

## Right and Wrong

This is also where people reach for clean categories.

Right.

Wrong.

Good.

Bad.

Peaceful.

Angry.

And yes, there are moral rules I am not interested in pretending are meaningless.

But even the examples people use because they seem simple become complicated the moment you move from the label to the event.

"Killing is wrong."

Okay.

Somebody is dead.

Was it murder?

Self-defense?

Defense of someone else?

An accident?

Negligence?

War?

The outcome alone does not tell us what judgment to make.

The law knows this.

Morality knows this.

Reality definitely knows this.

The rule can be simple.

Applying it to an actual human event can be a fucking mess.

That does not make morality fake.

It means context exists.

Annoying little bastard.

## Youth Has Force

Youth is good at force.

Not only physical force.

Moral force.

Certainty.

Conviction.

The sense that if something is obviously wrong, then everybody should see it, and if everybody sees it, then surely it can be fixed.

There is power in that.

I am not mocking it.

The world needs people who still look at bullshit and say:

> Why the fuck are we doing this?

But there is a trap in believing that seeing clearly means reality now owes you cooperation.

It does not.

## Age Can Have Range

What age can give you, if you let it, is range.

You have seen plans fail.

You have watched people contradict themselves.

You have been the person contradicting yourself.

You have been certain and wrong.

You have survived things that once looked permanent.

You have watched things you thought would last forever disappear in six months.

You stop being shocked that human beings are human beings.

That can turn into cynicism.

Or it can turn into adaptability.

Those are not the same thing.

Cynicism says:

> Nothing matters. People are shit. Why bother?

Adaptability says:

> Fine. This is the terrain. What can I do from here?

Very different operating system.

## Still Hopeful, Unfortunately

At one point she suggested maybe I had lost hope.

That made me laugh.

Me?

Lose hope?

Never.

My biggest fucking flaw.

I still expect things.

I still build things.

I still talk to people.

I still think humans can surprise me in good ways.

I still get disappointed precisely because I still care.

The difference is that hope no longer requires blindness.

I can hope while knowing the world is absurd.

I can want better while dealing with what exists.

I can keep the vigor without pretending I am still twenty.

That, to me, is the interesting combination.

Youth says:

> This should change.

Age says:

> Maybe. Now show me the actual system.

Keep both.

Force without adaptability breaks.

Adaptability without force calcifies.

And somewhere in the middle an old martial arts movie is still sitting in my head reminding me that the strongest thing in the room is not always the thing that hits hardest.

Sometimes it is the thing that can change shape.

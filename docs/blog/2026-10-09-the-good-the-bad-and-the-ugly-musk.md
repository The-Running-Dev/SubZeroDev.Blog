*The Wannabe Genius, the Would-Be Messiah, and, Most Likely, a Buffoon.*

I used to think Elon Musk was pretty cool.

Billionaire. PayPal. Tesla. SpaceX. Entrepreneur. Disruptor. Genius, everybody said.

I didn't spend much time investigating the mythology because why the fuck would I? I had my own things to do.

Then I watched John Oliver take a more detailed look at Musk on *Last Week Tonight*. It didn't magically install a new opinion in my head. It got me paying attention.

And the more I paid attention, the more I wanted to ask: **what exactly has this man personally demonstrated that warrants all the genius-and-savior mythology?**

<!-- truncate -->

## The Good

Let's establish something before people start arguing against shit I haven't said.

Musk has participated in building significant enterprises. Tesla helped make electric vehicles more commercially prominent. SpaceX has built working rockets and launch services. These are real outcomes, not imaginary accomplishments.

He has business skills. He can raise money, draw attention, attract investors, and exercise influence. He has been associated with projects that made substantial changes in their industries.

Good for him.

None of that is what I'm questioning.

I'm questioning the jump from "played a significant role in successful companies" to "extraordinary genius whose vision should be trusted on everything."

That's a much larger claim.

## The Bad

Tesla didn't spring out of the earth fully formed when Musk became CEO in 2008. Its products, finances, and quality have had a long and sometimes difficult history.

SpaceX didn't invent rocket science from scratch. It built on decades of publicly funded aerospace research, technical knowledge, infrastructure, and work performed by engineers. Its early Falcon 1 launches failed three times before the fourth reached orbit.

There is a real achievement in building a launch company that survives that trajectory. There's also a story about capital, the ability to keep trying, technical teams, government contracts, and public-private cooperation.

Why do those details disappear when the story becomes "Elon did it"?

And then there's the familiar PayPal origin myth. I used to think Musk personally created PayPal. The actual lineage is more complicated: he founded X.com, which merged with Confinity, where the PayPal product originated.

That doesn't make his role in X.com imaginary. It makes the simplified "he created PayPal" story misleading.

I've worked in software since the 1990s. I know the difference between a founder, an investor, an engineer, a product team, a merger, and an origin story that's been compressed for easy public consumption.

I also know what it's like to fail, learn, try again, and occasionally succeed. None of that automatically makes me a genius.

## The Ugly

What bothers me is the public performance: the grand claims, the theatrics, the increasingly political persona, the promises, and the way some admirers turn behavior they'd ordinarily criticize into evidence of misunderstood brilliance.

Maybe some provocation is calculated. Maybe some isn't. I don't know what's in the man's head.

I do know that **benefiting from an action isn't proof that you planned it**.

You can make an impulsive decision, find some advantage afterward, and present the outcome as part of a strategy. That may be good spin. It isn't, by itself, evidence of extraordinary foresight.

Look at the public disputes over Twitter/X and OpenAI. There are important facts to examine about control, promises, governance, policy, and inconsistent positions. What I won't do is automatically assume that every apparent contradiction hides a brilliant master plan.

That's giving someone credit for a calculation nobody has demonstrated.

## Enter Boris Johnson, the Professional Buffoon

I think Boris Johnson is a buffoon, too. But I brought him up for a specific reason: his famously disheveled public persona was cultivated.

That's a claim about constructing a public image, not about whether he knew what he was doing as prime minister.

When people suggest Musk is deliberately acting like a chaotic eccentric for strategic advantage, I want similar evidence of sustained, intentional persona management.

Not just: "He's wealthy, therefore everything he does must be clever."

## Linus, You Magnificent Dickhead

Now take Linus Torvalds.

I don't have to like his personality to recognize what he individually accomplished. We can examine the Linux kernel, Git, technical decisions, patches, maintenance, and decades of hands-on engineering.

He's still in the code.

The record gives me something concrete against which to evaluate exceptional technical ability.

That's not me pretending Torvalds worked alone. Of course Linux is the work of many people. The point is that Torvalds has identifiable individual technical contributions.

So where is the comparable engineering evidence for Musk? Show me the work rather than pointing to a company valuation.

## An Hour of Red-Teaming the Wrong Question

I spent an absurd amount of time exploring this with an AI.

Every time I questioned the genius mythology, the model answered by proving Musk had business skills.

Yes. No shit.

We then worked our way through "exceptional businessman," "successful entrepreneur," "persists after failures," and eventually "guy with money and business acumen."

I had never denied any of those things.

I wanted to test the *extraordinary* claim. The model kept moving to an easier one.

Eventually the discussion became more interesting as an exercise in thinking than as an examination of Elon Musk.

Which, frankly, is pretty on brand for my rabbit holes.

## The Point

I don't need to establish that Musk is incompetent. I don't need to deny his companies' achievements. I don't need to infer private motives.

I want to know what substantiates the mythology: extraordinary personal genius, uniquely farsighted judgment, and the messiah narrative about saving humanity.

Company success can be evidence of business ability. It is not a blank check for intellectual authority across every subject.

Until somebody brings me evidence that actually matches the scale of the claim, I remain unconvinced.

And yes, after all that?

**I still think he's a fucking buffoon.**

# The Fence That Doesn't Fence

My dog escaped into the neighbor's yard.

Again.

I found the opening she was using and blocked it.

Escape that, motherfucker.

Naturally, she'll probably find another one.

<!-- truncate -->

This is the ongoing security assessment of my property perimeter, conducted by a highly motivated four-legged penetration tester.

Dog discovers exploit.

Ben patches exploit.

Dog conducts regression testing.

Dog discovers next exploit.

No change-control board. No tickets. No fucking respect for release schedules.

At first I was complaining about the fence installation. Somebody paid a local `майстор` to put up chain-link, and now I'm the one fixing all the places it doesn't do the job.

Then I walked away and my brain decided we had not finished discussing the matter.

Wait a fucking minute.

**What is the actual purpose of this fence?**

Because it doesn't keep the dog in.

It doesn't keep animals out.

It doesn't provide privacy because it's chain-link and you can see straight through it.

And the gaps underneath aren't subtle. I can fit under the fucking thing myself.

I'm an adult human.

If a person can pass underneath the fence, we've gone well beyond a minor installation defect.

Perhaps the fence is supposed to mark the property boundary.

Okay.

Except we already knew where the property boundary was. So did the neighbors.

We've paid money to render previously known information in metal.

A three-dimensional dotted line.

A CSS border applied to the garden.

`border: 1px solid gray;`

No access control. No privacy. Unreliable animal containment.

Acceptance criteria:

- Does fence reach the ground? Apparently optional.
- Does fence keep dog inside? Failed in production.
- Does fence tell us where the property is? Yes, but so did literally everybody who lives here.

And now I understand why the dog keeps escaping.

She's not defeating the fence.

**She's using the entrance.**

Somewhere in Bulgaria, a `майстор` was paid to install a visual suggestion.

I wonder what the dog will find next.

She has plenty of time.

And, apparently, the QA department is not going on vacation.

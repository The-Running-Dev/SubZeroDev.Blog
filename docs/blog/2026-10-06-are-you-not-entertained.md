# Are You Not Entertained?

My favorite shirt used to say:

> “I didn't mean to offend you, that's just a bonus.”

This explains more than it should.

<!-- truncate -->

Years later I found myself sending someone:

> “Heya. Did I manage to offend you already?”

That was an olive branch.

In my language.

Not:

> “We need to talk.”

Not:

> “Are you upset with me?”

Not a six-paragraph apology drafted before I even knew whether anything had happened.

Just:

> “Did I manage to offend you already?”

Open door.

Low pressure.

Slightly stupid.

Accurate.

## Offense as a Side Effect

I do not generally set out to offend people.

That sounds exhausting.

What happens is simpler.

I say what I mean.

I push premises.

I make things absurd.

I take wording literally.

I refuse to pretend something makes sense when it does not.

Sometimes people laugh.

Sometimes people stare.

Sometimes the interaction suddenly develops a communications department.

Apparently offense is an occasional by-product.

Occupational hazard.

## The Shirt Had It Right

The old shirt was funny because the structure was backwards.

Normal apology:

> “I didn't mean to offend you.”

Then reassurance.

My shirt:

> “I didn't mean to offend you.”

Pause.

> “That's just a bonus.”

It takes an apology and drives it directly into a wall.

Beautiful.

I wore that thing constantly.

In retrospect, perhaps this was disclosure.

## The Gladiator Arrives

Then somebody reminded me of *Gladiator*.

> “Are you not entertained?”

And immediately another bargain-bin alter ego spawned.

**Discount Store Maximus.**

Apparently his job is to look at a conversational crater, turn toward the audience, and demand appreciation for the performance.

This is not healthy franchise management.

We already had Discount Store Marcus Aurelius.

Then Discount Store Alan Shore.

Now Maximus.

Lucifer is losing control of the production.

## The Audience Problem

Humor is weird because the person making the joke knows the intended tone.

The audience only receives the output.

Sometimes the packet arrives intact.

Sometimes it is corrupted.

Sometimes somebody thinks:

> “That was hilarious.”

Someone else thinks:

> “What the fuck?”

Both reactions can be real.

I do not need universal approval.

That sounds impossible and boring.

But I also do not assume intent magically determines impact.

If someone says:

> “That actually hurt.”

Fine.

Now I know.

I can apologize.

The olive branch exists for a reason.

## What I Will Not Do

I am not going to pre-apologize for every sentence.

I am not going to run each joke through a hypothetical committee of every possible sensitivity.

I am not going to become so cautious that the conversation arrives shrink-wrapped.

That is not me.

The alternative is not cruelty.

The alternative is accepting that occasionally a joke lands badly and then handling the actual event when it happens.

## Same Weirdness, No Special Exemptions

She also describes herself as weird.

Unconventional.

Someone who pushes buttons.

Fine.

I recognize some of that. I've done plenty of it myself.

But this arrangement has to work in both directions.

Bring your shit.

I'll bring mine.

If something lands badly, say so. If something doesn't make sense, ask.

Neither person's weirdness comes with a special exemption from being understood, questioned, or occasionally finding the other person inconvenient.

Otherwise we have one unconventional person and one full-time customer service department.

I did not apply for that position.

## Then We Actually Got an Answer

Eventually she said she had misunderstood me.

There it was.

An actual piece of information.

I offered to explain.

She declined.

Fine.

I didn't need to turn that into another investigation.

My point was simple:

If you don't understand, ask.

I'm very direct.

That doesn't mean every sentence will arrive perfectly assembled in somebody else's head. Apparently the audience problem remains operational.

But I can answer a question.

I cannot clarify a misunderstanding that nobody tells me exists.

## The Current Version

Old shirt:

> “I didn't mean to offend you, that's just a bonus.”

Current version:

> “Did I manage to offend you already?”

And now there is another step.

Ask.

Find out what happened.

Offer clarification.

Move on.

This may count as growth.

Discount growth, obviously.

Maximus can put his arms down.

The conversation didn't need an arena.

It needed a question.

Adulting is hard.

Apparently.

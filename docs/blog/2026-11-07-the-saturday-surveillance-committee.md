# The Saturday Surveillance Committee

Apparently, my family has established a neighborhood intelligence service.

<!-- truncate -->

It doesn't have an office, a badge, or, as far as I know, a respectable budget. It has a much simpler operating model: drive past the house and look for my vehicle.

That's it. Surveillance, Bulgarian family edition.

Every Saturday, I'm home. My van is outside. The driveway is visible. If you want to know whether I'm around, you don't have to build a sophisticated tracking system. You just drive past and check.

And that is exactly what they've been doing.

I didn't spend my life keeping a spreadsheet of their movements. I have work, a dog, projects, things to build, and a perfectly functional ability to mind my own fucking business. Monitoring family members was not on my calendar.

Then my cousin stopped me on the street.

A cousin who had spent about a year avoiding me suddenly decided we needed a conversation. On a Saturday, of course.

She had a message from my mother, or rather some variation on: contact your mother. There was also the usual property-related nonsense floating around. A cover over the water shutoff. Water getting in. Something that apparently needed the full weight of the family bureaucracy.

And that encounter made me connect the fucking dots.

I hadn't been tracking when they appeared. I wasn't trying to catch anybody. But once she stopped me, the pattern of the Saturday checks was hard to ignore.

I know what they're checking. The vehicle tells them whether I'm here.

And I know the history that makes those checks matter to me. This is the same family property where gates, keys, access, and my continued presence have become recurring arguments. I've had enough of wondering what people will do there when I'm not around.

The absurdity is that I am not organizing a campaign against them.

I'm not following them around. I'm not driving past their houses. I'm not planning elaborate revenge operations. I am literally trying to live.

A man in a house. Sometimes he works on a computer. Sometimes he goes out. Sometimes the van sits outside. Truly, a threat to civilization.

But apparently the fact that I am *here* is itself a problem.

My aunt already made that plain when she asked whether it wasn't time for me to find somewhere else to live. Different moment, same fucking atmosphere.

And now I find myself with a Saturday pattern I never cared enough to look for until somebody practically waved it in my face.

What's the grand objective? To make me uncomfortable enough to disappear? To know when the place is unattended? I have my understanding of what they're doing, based on living through this dispute, and I don't need to turn my Saturdays into a courtroom to tell the story.

I don't need a counter-surveillance committee.

I don't need a secret calendar of their movements.

I have a rather simpler arrangement.

I'm living here. On Saturdays, I'm usually home. My vehicle is outside.

If they want to burn their petrol checking that fact, well, congratulations.

The intelligence report is accurate.

I'm still fucking here.

When I lived in San Antonio, I heard people proudly say they had been born and raised there and had never left. I love San Antonio. I still wanted to ask whether they understood that the rest of the planet was open.

<!-- truncate -->

Not sold out. Not under renovation. Available.

There was Austin. There was Houston. There were other states. Beyond those, an alarming quantity of countries continued operating without their supervision.

And the remarkable part wasn't simply that somebody had stayed in one place.

It was the confidence.

America is the greatest country. This is the best place. Why would I want to go anywhere else?

Discount Store Marcus Aurelius would like to see the evaluation paperwork.

## The Entire Sample

What did you compare?

Where did you live? What surprised you? Which place turned out to be different from what you expected? Where did you think you'd be miserable and then discover you liked it?

Nothing?

Okay. Then we have completed an international comparison using one location.

One competitor entered. One competitor won. The judges were born backstage.

The methodology is beyond reproach.

I am not claiming some percentage of Americans fit this description. I don't have a spreadsheet of 360 million people and their holiday histories. I am talking about actual conversations I remember having, and the sheer fucking certainty in them.

I love living here.

Fair enough.

There cannot be anywhere better for me.

How the fuck do you know?

## The Bulgarian Office

Before anybody prepares the patriotic rebuttal, I have heard the same thing in Bulgaria.

Born here. Proud of it. Never want to live anywhere else. Bulgaria is the best.

Where else have you lived?

Nowhere.

Excellent. The Bulgarian office has independently reproduced the San Antonio research.

This is not an exclusively American product. Humanity manufactures it locally.

And I am not asking someone to hate their home. I don't require a public denunciation of their grandparents, their neighbourhood or the bakery.

You can love the bakery.

The bakery has done nothing wrong.

I'm asking how you established that nowhere else could suit you better when nowhere else has had a chance to introduce itself.

## No Deportation Order Enclosed

There is a perfectly coherent answer available:

I'm happy here. I don't feel a need to find out.

Fine. That tells me about your satisfaction and your appetite for change. I can understand that without hiring a moving van on your behalf.

But it is a different statement from knowing that your birthplace is the best possible place for your life.

One says you have enough.

The other claims you've checked the universe.

Discount Marcus has some concerns about the coverage of the inspection.

The whole question gets treated as though asking it means I am ordering somebody onto an aeroplane. I am not.

Stay. Enjoy yourself. Love the place.

Just leave a little room between your personal happiness and a geographical proclamation on behalf of existence.

## The Emperor's Credential

I love San Antonio. I love Austin. I love Barcelona.

None of those sentences needs to defeat the others.

I can remember one place with affection while wanting to be in another. I can discover somewhere new without issuing an apology to Texas. There is no municipal loyalty tribunal waiting to revoke my memories.

And if I tell you a place is perfect for me, I am still describing what I know so far.

I have not finished Earth.

I doubt there is a completion badge.

That is what bothers me about the confidence: the conversation is already closed before experience gets a chance to contribute.

“I have never left” becomes a credential for knowing there is no reason to leave.

The emperor has visited one aisle and declared this the finest supermarket in the known world.

Perhaps it is a lovely aisle.

Perhaps the tomatoes are exceptional.

There are still other fucking aisles.

# The Universe Has Entered the Chat

Someone told me:

> “The Universe doesn't like me lately.”

That is a strong statement.

So naturally I asked:

> “You have evidence?”

<!-- truncate -->

She did.

Apparently.

The evidence was:

> “I need to work.”

I stared at the sentence.

Discount Store Alan Shore adjusted his tie.

Lucifer pulled up a chair.

We were now in court.

## Exhibit A: Employment

Let us establish the claim.

**Allegation:** The Universe dislikes the defendant.

**Evidence:** The defendant has to work.

Interesting.

Counsel, can you explain the causal relationship?

No?

Excellent.

Then perhaps we should continue.

If having to work is proof that the Universe dislikes you, what does not having to work prove?

Does the Universe love me?

Am I cosmically favored?

Have I somehow achieved celestial preferred-customer status?

Do retirees receive sainthood automatically?

Are independently wealthy people under direct divine protection?

Do unemployed people qualify for grace?

The premise had consequences.

I intended to inspect all of them.

## Counsel Has Presented the Allegation as Evidence

The problem was simple.

The statement:

> “The Universe dislikes me because I have to work.”

was being offered as evidence for:

> “The Universe dislikes me.”

That is not evidence.

That is the claim wearing a fake mustache.

So Discount Store Alan Shore entered the record:

> “Counsel has presented her allegation as evidence for her allegation.”

Case law was developing rapidly.

## Then Came Balance

Later she improved the argument.

She said there should be balance.

She had been happy in the past.

Now she was paying for it.

Good.

Finally.

A premise with moving parts.

Unfortunately, one of the moving parts was:

> “There should be balance.”

Should?

That is a dangerous word.

Should according to whom?

Based on what?

Where is the rule?

Who wrote the policy?

Who approved the exchange rate?

Why does happiness create debt?

Why is suffering the accepted repayment mechanism?

Who appointed you auditor of universal providence?

Lucifer looked delighted.

## Cosmic Accounting

Once you introduce balance, the Universe becomes an accounting department.

Past happiness: credit.

Present suffering: debit.

Pleasure apparently creates liability.

Misery settles the account.

Now I have questions.

Does the Universe use double-entry bookkeeping?

Is there interest?

Can happiness debt compound?

Can I prepay suffering and enjoy tax-free joy later?

Are there deductions?

Can emotional losses be carried forward?

What is the statute of limitations on a good year?

Nobody had answers.

This was disappointing.

## “I Don't Want to Judge the Universe”

At some point she said:

> “I don't want to judge the Universe.”

This did not follow from anything I had said.

Nobody had asked her to judge the Universe.

She had already accused the Universe of disliking her.

If anything, the Universe was the one being slandered.

So I replied:

> “Too bad. It's judging you. Apparently.”

This seemed fair.

If the Universe is an active participant, it gets procedural rights.

## The Problem With Providence

Humans are very good at converting circumstance into metaphysics.

Something good happens.

Blessing.

Something bad happens.

Punishment.

Something inconvenient happens.

The Universe has opinions.

Sometimes this is poetic.

Sometimes comforting.

Sometimes funny.

Sometimes it is just an ordinary Tuesday wearing religious clothing.

I do not know what the Universe thinks.

I am not even sure the Universe thinks.

I am not sure what I believe about God.

I attend Bible study anyway.

Apparently confusion is not an obstacle to participation.

## Discount Store Alan Shore Rests

Eventually the whole discussion became less about whether the Universe liked anybody and more about the assumptions hidden inside casual language.

Work became cosmic punishment.

Happiness became debt.

Suffering became payment.

Balance became law.

And all of it entered through a few ordinary sentences.

That is enough material for any bargain-bin attorney.

Alan stood.

> “Your Honor, the defense rests.”

Lucifer looked at him.

> “There is no judge.”

Alan shrugged.

> “There is no evidence either.”

Fair.

Somewhere, the Universe declined to comment.

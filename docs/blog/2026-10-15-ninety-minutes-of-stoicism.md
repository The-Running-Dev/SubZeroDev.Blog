# Ninety Minutes of Stoicism

About ninety minutes.

That's roughly how long I spent grieving, crying, thinking, composing messages, rewriting them, and working out what I actually wanted to do after an unexpected family contact.

There was a lot of feeling in those ninety minutes.

If somebody had been looking for evidence that philosophy had finally turned me into an emotionless motherfucker, this would have been a disappointing demonstration.

<!-- truncate -->

## I Felt It

The contact brought up sadness about a relationship I'd wanted and hadn't experienced the way I'd hoped.

I cried.

I started putting an explanation into words. Then I changed the words. Tried again. Looked at what I was saying and why I wanted to say it.

The feelings were strong.

That part wasn't new.

I've felt things intensely all my life. Being told I was too emotional didn't make the feelings less intense. It mostly gave me something else to question about myself while I was having them.

Too much, apparently.

Okay.

Here I was, nearly fifty, still capable of being hurt deeply by contact with somebody who mattered to me.

Still crying.

Still me.

## The Message Kept Changing

At first there was so much to explain.

History. Context. What the contact meant to me. What I thought had been overlooked.

I could write all of that. I did write it. I could keep revising until it said what I meant with as much clarity as I could manage.

But while I was doing that, another question became harder to ignore.

What was I hoping sending this would accomplish?

Some of it was the perfectly human wish to be understood.

Some of it felt very familiar. The old habit of justifying myself. Supplying enough explanation that another person might finally accept my reasons, my choices, my experience.

I know how to do that work. I've done plenty of it.

Recognizing it didn't instantly make the sadness disappear. It changed how I looked at the next step.

I had written something because I needed to work through it.

Did I want to send it?

Those turned out to be separate questions.

## Strong Feelings, Conscious Decisions

The feeling was real. The urge to respond was real.

Neither settled the decision for me.

I could care about what had happened, feel the whole fucking thing, and still look at whether taking action served anything I wanted.

Eventually I decided not to send the explanation.

I didn't need to deny how upset I was to make that choice. I didn't need to tell myself the relationship had never mattered. It mattered enough that I was crying about it.

This is what I mean by Stoicism in this experience.

Conscious judgment while the feelings are happening.

I wasn't sitting there trying to demonstrate a philosophy. I was living through something, and afterward I could recognize the connection.

About ninety minutes of it.

That isn't a deadline I expect myself to meet next time. It isn't proof that I've completed grief or mastered anything. It's approximately how this particular stretch unfolded.

It mattered because I could feel the familiar intensity and notice a different choice becoming possible inside it.

## Then I Questioned the Conversation

I was reflecting with AI during this process.

And later I started questioning that too.

Was this helping me examine what happened? Was I building an echo chamber around the conclusion I already wanted?

It's easy to feel understood by something that only has my account of a situation. That doesn't mean my account contains everything.

So I asked for challenge.

I wanted to look at what I actually knew, what I was inferring, and what other explanations might fit.

The contact had happened. The history of who had kept in touch with whom was part of my experience. The hurt I was describing was mine.

A conclusion about what somebody secretly felt was a different kind of claim.

I couldn't verify that just by explaining my side more persuasively. Agreement from an AI wouldn't verify it either.

Maybe there was affection expressed badly. Maybe duty and habit played a part. Maybe there were reasons I didn't know.

Possible explanations.

I could accept that they were possible without pretending I knew which one was true.

And without discarding my own experience of the relationship.

I still had to decide how I wanted to participate. I could make that decision with some uncertainty left in it.

I didn't need to win an imaginary argument about everybody's motives first.

## Apparently There Was More

Later, a Buddhist teaching about rejection brought up something I hadn't fully recognized in those ninety minutes.

Older feelings of being unworthy.

Oh.

That was in here too.

I had spent all that time reflecting. I had chosen what to do. And another piece of the experience still became visible afterward.

I find that fascinating.

I can understand something well enough to make a decision and still discover more about it later. I can be okay with a choice and still have feelings connected to it.

Apparently I don't receive a certificate at the end of ninety minutes confirming that every relevant part of my brain has now submitted its report.

Something else turns up.

I notice it.

I keep going.

## The Journey

And while I was reading and recognizing all of this, something else occurred to me.

I've always appreciated the journey more than the destination.

In sports, so much of what I value is doing it. The experience of playing, participating, being in it. A result doesn't contain everything I went there for.

Travel has that quality for me too. The trip. What happens along the way. Living through it, rather than just arriving somewhere.

Relationships as well. I want the experience of knowing somebody, talking, discovering how we are together. The name of the relationship can't give me that experience on its own.

And life.

I keep encountering ideas and recognizing something I've been doing, or something I hadn't quite understood about myself. Sometimes I change. Sometimes I notice that I've already changed.

I'm nearly fifty and this is still happening.

That's fucking amazing.

I don't want to turn this into another destination where one day I'm sufficiently Stoic, sufficiently detached, sufficiently finished that nothing can touch me.

There was life in those ninety minutes. Painful life, but mine. Feeling something. Looking at it. Questioning my explanation. Choosing. Then discovering something else later.

The experience. The life. Living.

I value that more than getting somewhere or becoming someone.

I'm still discovering. Still changing.

It's happening.

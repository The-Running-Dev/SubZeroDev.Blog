The kid is about twenty-five. I'm about to be fifty. He calls me Uncle Ben, which already makes me fucking laugh, and somehow we've arrived at the question of who can drink more.

I tell him I could drink more than him, his friend, and his father's friend combined. I'm pointing at the three of them. His father's friend is about my age.

An outrageous thing to say? Sure. And apparently the next step is supposed to be a fucking demonstration.

<!-- truncate -->

He doesn't believe me. I'm going to have to prove it.

Buddy, believe what you want to believe.

I tell him I drank a bottle of gin every night the previous week. A bottle of goddamn gin. Make your own conclusions. That is something I'm telling you about my life, and you can believe it or not. Your disbelief does not create an obligation for me to start drinking on command.

Drinking is not a goddamn contest.

Yes, I appreciate how that sounds immediately after telling three people I could outdrink them. I can hear my own fucking sentence. But there's a difference between saying what you've been capable of and agreeing to perform it because a twenty-five-year-old wants evidence.

Somehow, the amount somebody can drink becomes an achievement. People hear a quantity and start arranging the podium.

I've drunk with Russians. I've outdrunk Russians. To some Bulgarians, that sounds like a fucking brag. To me, it sounds like I could destroy my liver more than the Russians could. Wonderful. What exactly am I supposed to be proud of there?

I'm not telling the story because I need a medal for it. I'm telling you what happened. And I certainly don't need to go and do it again to settle your opinion of me.

Then the conversation moves on to cocaine.

He gives me a line, gets my number, and now we're comparing how much of that we've done. He tells me about six grams between three of them over three days.

I tell him I've done ten grams in a fucking week by myself.

I'm not proud of that either. These are facts from my life. That distinction seems to get lost the moment numbers enter the conversation. Suddenly we're back to who did more, whose story is believable, and whether somebody needs to prove something.

I don't.

I find the exchange funny because he's treating my history as an application that requires supporting evidence. I'm standing there, nearly twice his age, being invited to establish credentials in things I don't regard as achievements in the first place.

You can hear a story and decide it's bullshit. People do. That's fine. I don't get to climb into your head and arrange your beliefs for you, and I have no particular ambition to try.

What I can decide is whether your disbelief becomes my evening's assignment.

It doesn't.

Uncle Ben has already lived the fucking story. Uncle Ben does not need to recreate it for the jury.

Believe what you want to believe.

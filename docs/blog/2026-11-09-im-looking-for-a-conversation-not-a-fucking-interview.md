# I'm Looking for a Conversation, Not a Fucking Interview

A thoughtful answer can still leave a conversation completely motionless.

<!-- truncate -->

That is the exquisite little trap. I ask something. The other person answers properly. There are sentences. There is detail. Nobody has committed the obvious crime of replying with a solitary, exhausted “lol.”

And yet the whole exchange is waiting for me to pick it up again.

The answer has arrived. Curiosity has apparently missed its connection.

On a dating app, I want to discover another person. I also want to feel that somebody has noticed there is a person on this side of the screen. That seems a modest ambition. I haven't requested a sonnet or a blood oath. A little interest would be splendid.

There is an easy way for this to become ridiculous: start counting question marks.

Three from me. One from you. An alarming conversational deficit. Shall we appoint an auditor?

I have no interest in running a romantic accounts department. Somebody can show curiosity without ending every message with a question. They can pick up a detail, offer a connection, tease something I said, disagree, or take the exchange somewhere I hadn't thought of going.

I can feel the difference between a person joining a conversation and a person completing a form.

Imagine the rhythm. I ask about something that matters to you. You tell me. I follow the interesting part. You explain more. I share something of my own.

And it sits there.

Now I have a choice. Supply the next prompt, then the next, until I have assembled a surprisingly comprehensive profile of someone who has learned very little about me. Or say what is happening.

I'm looking for a conversation, not a fucking interview.

Yes, that is blunt. The sentence is unlikely to win an award for making everybody comfortable.

But comfort has become rather expensive if I am paying for it by pretending to enjoy the exchange.

I could soften the wording. I could ask whether we are both interested in getting to know each other. Depending on the conversation, that might be the better sentence. The underlying question remains: are we doing this together?

I don't know why someone fails to reciprocate. They may be distracted, shy, tired, uncertain, or simply uninterested. A screen offers very poor access to another person's motives, however confidently I might enjoy furnishing them.

I don't need a diagnosis to notice that I am doing all the prompting.

And nobody owes me curiosity. That matters. Matching with me does not create a contractual obligation to find me fascinating. How disappointing for my ego, which had already prepared the paperwork.

But I can decide whether an exchange is worth continuing. I can name what I want. If the conversation ends there, I have learned something useful, even if the silence is irritating.

There is a small vulnerability beneath the complaint: I want somebody to want to know me. Saying that plainly leaves rather more exposed than making another joke and asking another question.

Still, I would prefer that exposure to an immaculate interview.

I want an unexpected observation. A disagreement with some life in it. A question I didn't see coming. Something that tells me another mind has reached across the table.

I will happily bring my curiosity.

I would like some company carrying the conversation.

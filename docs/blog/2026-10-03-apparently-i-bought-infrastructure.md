# Apparently I Bought Infrastructure

I fixed the shop vac.

Well.

I fixed **one part** of the shop vac.

This distinction became important almost immediately.

The cable is back together.

Beautiful.

Functional, probably.

There is only one minor issue.

I apparently bought **thirty-four fucking meters of cable.**

<!-- truncate -->

I had no idea what thirty-four meters looked like.

This is embarrassing because I have spent decades working with systems that require numbers to mean things.

Memory.

Storage.

Latency.

Network distances.

Whatever.

Meters?

Apparently not.

Who the fuck knows what a meter is?

Not me.

Not physically.

Not until I unrolled thirty-four of the bastards across the yard.

## Oh. That's What Thirty-Four Meters Is.

The cable reaches the gate.

From the house.

Across the yard.

To the fucking gate.

I stood there looking at it.

Right.

Okay.

So I did not purchase a replacement power cord.

I purchased **distance**.

Enough distance that I can now begin asking completely different questions.

Why does the shop vac need all of it?

It doesn't.

What else could use it?

Ah.

Now we have entered dangerous territory.

## Test the Shop Vac

Naturally, after wiring the cable back in, I tried to test the machine.

Press button.

Nothing.

Excellent.

Very good.

The cable is installed.

The external button, however, appears to no longer be properly connected to the internal button.

So I have managed to turn:

> The dog fucked up the cable.

into:

> The cable is fine but the vacuum has lost the concept of user input.

Progress.

Technically.

Somewhere inside the machine is a switch that works.

Somewhere outside the machine is a button that believes it works.

The two are no longer on speaking terms.

This feels familiar.

## Meanwhile, the Clothes Rack

While all this is happening, I have another engineering system operating in the yard.

A clothes rack.

A stupid portable piece-of-shit clothes rack.

I keep moving it because I'm following the sun.

The sun moves.

I move the rack.

The rack falls over.

I stand it up.

The sun moves again.

I move the rack again.

The rack falls over again.

At this point we have enough observations to establish a deterministic system.

**Input:** Move clothes rack.

**Output:** Clothes rack on ground.

I keep rerunning the test anyway.

Maybe this deployment will be different.

It isn't.

## Security Architecture

Then there is the whole property situation.

Cameras.

Locks.

Who might come here.

What might happen to my shit.

What do I actually care about.

And somewhere in the middle of thinking about all of this, I realized something painfully obvious.

Why am I trying to make one location carry every function?

I have more than enough computers.

I have more than enough tech.

I have desks.

Monitors.

Machines.

I can put the equipment I actually care about somewhere secure.

Set up a proper workstation there.

Then keep another workstation here.

Come here when I want.

Sleep here if I want.

Work here if I want.

Go somewhere else when I want.

Two sites.

Redundant compute.

Replicated workspace.

Apparently my answer to family property bullshit is **high availability architecture**.

Of course it fucking is.

## Then I Looked at the Cable Again

Thirty-four meters.

Sitting there.

Coiled around a shop vac like a giant electrical python.

And suddenly the cable wasn't a shop-vac cable anymore.

It was a trunk line.

I could put a proper outdoor multi-socket on it.

Weatherproof the connection.

Run it across the yard.

Or bury it properly.

Put outlets where I actually need them.

Power lights.

Tools.

Garden equipment.

Whatever ridiculous thing Future Ben decides absolutely requires electricity beside a tree.

I have enough cable.

That is no longer the constraint.

Apparently I can electrify the fucking yard.

## I Could Make This Place Nice

And this is where the whole thing becomes slightly less ridiculous.

Ownership of this place is complicated.

Family is complicated.

There is history.

There is nonsense.

There are reasons not to sink major money into things before the legal structure is cleaner.

Fine.

But that does not mean I have to sit in a fucking ruin.

I can make the place pleasant.

Lights in the yard.

Clean garden.

Power where I need it.

A place to sit.

A place to work.

Small things.

Reversible things.

Things I enjoy because I am the person physically standing here.

I don't need to rebuild Rome.

I can put up a fucking light.

And thanks to my inability to comprehend thirty-four meters, apparently I can put it almost anywhere.

## Infrastructure by Accident

This is how half my projects happen.

Start with one broken thing.

Buy one part.

Discover the part has capabilities wildly beyond the original requirement.

Begin asking questions nobody asked.

Now there is architecture.

I needed:

> A cable for the shop vac.

I now have:

> Potential outdoor electrical distribution.

I needed:

> Somewhere secure for computers.

I now have:

> Multi-site workstation redundancy.

I needed:

> Dry clothes.

I now have:

> A mobile solar-tracking rack with catastrophic stability characteristics.

None of this was the plan.

There was no plan.

There was a sequence of increasingly stupid observations.

And yet I look around the yard now and think:

> Huh.

Lights would be nice.

Maybe an outlet over there.

Could run something to the gate.

Could bury this section.

Could make the whole place look pretty fucking good.

Then I look back at the shop vac.

Button still doesn't work.

Of course.

The original task remains incomplete.

But the yard has a power strategy.

## Apparently I Bought Infrastructure

Thirty-four meters sounded like some cable.

Thirty-four meters **is not some cable.**

Thirty-four meters is from here to over there.

And then farther.

It is the length of an idea becoming a project.

It is enough to stand at the gate holding the end and think:

> Jesus Christ.

So now I know.

If I ever need power somewhere else in the yard, I already have the answer.

I will run the cable.

Put in proper outlets.

Add lights.

Make the place nicer.

Maybe one day the shop vac will even turn on again.

That would be a bonus.

Because apparently I did not buy a replacement cord.

**I bought infrastructure.**

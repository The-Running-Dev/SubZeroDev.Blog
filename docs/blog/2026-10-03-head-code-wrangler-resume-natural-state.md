# Head Code Wrangler: My Résumé Has Returned to Its Natural State

I found my favorite YouTuber.

This man was talking about a résumé he had apparently weaponized against another human being.

Not lied on.

Not embellished.

Weaponized.

There is a difference.

<!-- truncate -->

The résumé contained the kind of material that makes an interviewer stop processing language normally.

At one point there was a joke about having outstanding warrants.

The obvious problem, according to the interviewer, was that perhaps outstanding warrants should not be on a résumé.

The obvious response was:

Why not?

They're outstanding.

Excellent warrants.

Top-tier warrants.

Possibly award-winning warrants.

That was when I knew I had found one of my people.

Then the man got asked the traditional interview question:

Tell me something about yourself.

This is normally where a candidate says something like:

*"I'm passionate about solving complex problems in collaborative environments."*

Which is corporate for:

*"Please give me money. I have memorized the ritual."*

This man did not do that.

The interview continued descending into complete nonsense until eventually the reveal came:

The whole thing was a prank.

He went back to his real office.

His boss asked which competitor they should prank next.

Beautiful.

Absolutely beautiful.

There was only one problem.

I had been watching the whole thing thinking:

**Wait. I could use some of this.**

## This Was Already Happening

Years ago, when I was actually responsible for software and therefore theoretically a serious professional adult, I had business cards printed.

The title on them was:

**Head Code Wrangler**

Not as a joke card.

Not for Halloween.

Not because I had lost a bet.

Those were my actual business cards.

I would meet another professional human being, shake their hand, and hand them a small professionally printed rectangle informing them that I was the Head Code Wrangler.

Apparently I had already started this experiment decades ago and then somehow wandered into respectable résumé formatting.

Tragic.

## The Standard Résumé Is a Crime Against Language

Every résumé eventually starts sounding like it was generated by the same malfunctioning committee.

Results-driven.

Strategic leader.

Cross-functional collaboration.

Proven track record.

Dynamic environment.

Stakeholder alignment.

Please stop.

I have spent more than twenty years building software.

I have written code.

I have deleted code.

I have inherited code that should have been sealed inside concrete and dropped into the ocean.

I have built systems.

I have led teams.

I have sat in meetings where eighteen people discussed a problem that three lines of code had already solved.

I have deployed things.

I have broken things.

I have fixed things.

I have explained things.

I have occasionally explained the same thing again because apparently the first explanation was merely ceremonial.

There is a story in there.

And somehow the accepted format is to compress all of it into:

**Led cross-functional engineering initiatives to deliver scalable enterprise solutions.**

No.

Fuck that.

## The New Plan

I was already planning two résumés.

One normal.

Professional.

Respectable.

The kind of document that can pass through an applicant-tracking system without causing a small electrical fire.

And then there is the other one.

The one that still contains real qualifications.

Real projects.

Real technologies.

Real accomplishments.

But presented in a way that forces the reader to answer a more important question:

**What the fuck did I just read?**

I don't want to fabricate anything.

That ruins the game.

The facts stay true.

The language becomes increasingly unreasonable.

Maybe one job title stays normal.

The next one becomes **Head Code Wrangler**.

Then somewhere later:

**Sorcerer Supreme**

Perhaps:

**Lord Commander of Sales**

**Commodore of Digital Sales**

**Keeper of the Sacred Build Pipeline**

By the time the reader reaches the bottom, they should have learned what I can actually do while also developing mild concern about who exactly submitted the document.

Perfect.

## A Résumé Should Have a Plot

Then I realized something worse.

Why does the résumé have to be a database dump at all?

Company.

Title.

Dates.

Bullets.

Company.

Title.

Dates.

Bullets.

Human life presented as a SQL result set.

What if it reads like a story?

I started here.

Then this happened.

Then somebody made the catastrophic decision to give me responsibility.

Then I discovered architecture.

Then distributed systems.

Then leadership.

Then cloud infrastructure.

Then apparently I became a Sorcerer Supreme.

Then, after decades of doing perfectly normal software work, I did what any reasonable engineer would do.

I built a fucking game engine.

There.

That's a résumé I might actually want to read.

## The Important Part

This only works if the ridiculous résumé is sitting on top of legitimate experience.

Otherwise it's just a man wearing a wizard hat asking for employment.

The joke is the contrast.

Under **Sorcerer Supreme** there needs to be actual architecture.

Under **Head Code Wrangler** there needs to be actual engineering leadership.

Under whatever deranged title I assign to myself next, there needs to be something real enough that removing the joke leaves a perfectly defensible professional history.

That's what makes it fun.

The reader laughs.

Then keeps reading.

Then realizes:

Oh.

He actually did all this shit.

## Marketing

Some people will hate it.

Excellent.

Some recruiter will look at it for twelve seconds and decide I am an unacceptable risk to organizational stability.

Also excellent.

Someone else will see **Head Code Wrangler**, keep reading, discover the experience underneath it, and think:

*"Who the fuck is this guy?"*

Now we're getting somewhere.

Because that is marketing.

Not everybody has to like the thing.

They have to notice the thing.

And if I already have the respectable version available, this one does not need to behave.

It can walk into the room wearing a cape.

It can introduce itself as Sorcerer Supreme.

It can place twenty-plus years of engineering experience on the table.

Then it can stare directly into the recruiter's soul.

At some point I stopped needing permission to do stupid things with my own résumé.

Apparently I only needed to remember that I used to hand people business cards that said:

**Head Code Wrangler.**

The résumé is not becoming unprofessional.

It's returning to its natural state.

I do Bible study with Ethan and Sandra.

Yes. Uncle Ben. Bible study.

The man who can apparently get from the Apostle Paul to Dan fucking Carlin without noticing that he's changed subjects six times.

And I love it.

Not because the three of us have arrived at one immaculate, matching set of beliefs. We haven't. Because I can bring my actual questions into the conversation, and the conversation keeps going.

<!-- truncate -->

## How the Fuck Did We Get to Rome?

We started with Romans 14. Then Romans 1. Then Paul.

I wanted to know why he was coming down so hard.

That is an actual question. I want to understand what I'm reading. Who is he talking to? What's happening around him? What am I missing?

And then, because my brain has apparently never encountered a side road it didn't want to drive down, we're talking about Seneca. Nero. Claudius. Roman society. Barbarians.

Dan Carlin.

Of course Dan fucking Carlin.

Sandra asks a question about Romans, and somewhere in my head a pointer lights up. History. Another pointer. Something I read or listened to. Another pointer. Suddenly I'm halfway across the Roman Empire wondering why the fuck I know any of this.

I love history. That's why.

I am not a historian. I know some shit, I have questions about considerably more shit, and apparently Bible study is where the whole collection gets unpacked.

Ethan and Sandra engage with it.

They ask questions. They answer mine. We follow the conversation.

That matters to me more than I think I had quite managed to say.

## You Don't Have to Understand Me First

I kept coming back to the phrase *meet me where I am*.

Which sounds like a fairly substantial assignment if you take it to mean that you must first understand everything about me.

Good fucking luck.

Half the time I don't understand how I got to the sentence I'm currently saying. I can probably reconstruct the route, but we may need snacks.

I don't need somebody to arrive with a complete model of my brain.

I need curiosity.

If something I said doesn't make sense, ask me about it. If you don't see the connection, ask how I got there. If you disagree, tell me what you disagree with. Give me something real to respond to.

I can work with a question.

A question gives us somewhere to go.

You don't have to share my starting point to ask how I reached it. And I don't have to share yours to listen to the answer.

That's what I mean by meeting me where I am.

**Be curious enough to find out where the fuck that is.**

## God Is Allowed in the Conversation

Ethan and Sandra are deeply Christian. My relationship with God is different from theirs.

I don't need to flatten that difference to explain why studying the Bible with them matters to me.

Their faith matters. My questions matter. I want room for both.

I can love these people and take their beliefs seriously without pretending that every answer lands the same way for me. They can answer a question without it becoming an agreement I've signed by continuing to sit there.

And I owe them the same curiosity I'm asking for.

If I want to know what Ethan thinks, I have to let Ethan tell me. If Sandra asks something, I have to hear the question she's actually asking. I don't get to demand an open conversation and then arrive with everyone else's lines already written.

Nobody needs Uncle Ben casting himself as the sole living person with an interesting interior life.

Jesus Christ.

At Bible study, we can open the text, ask what it means, get into the history, disagree, follow a connection, and keep talking.

The questions are part of being there.

I don't have to wait until I have resolved my entire relationship with God before I'm allowed to participate. I can bring the relationship I actually have.

That's a hell of a thing to be grateful for.

## Thank You for Doing This With Me

Later, I sent Ethan a message thanking him and Sandra.

For doing this with me.

For meeting me exactly where I am.

For letting me ask all my fucking questions.

I love them. This means something to me, and I wanted to say so.

There are plenty of things I can read on my own. There are more lectures and podcasts than I will ever finish. I can spend a perfectly good evening accumulating additional Roman information that will emerge without warning in a completely different conversation.

But this is time with people I love.

We show up. We ask. We answer. We discover that a sentence which seemed obvious to one person needs another twenty minutes for somebody else.

Sometimes those twenty minutes appear to require Nero.

Fine. Get Nero.

I'm not asking for a certificate confirming that I have finally been understood.

I'm grateful that Ethan and Sandra keep joining me in the conversation.

Bring your faith. I'll bring my questions.

We can start there.

# Motorcycle Diplomacy

I put diesel in my gasoline motorcycle.

Twice.

In my defense, some European fuel pumps have a remarkable commitment to making the colors mean whatever the fuck they like.

The second time this happened, I already knew how to drain it.

Because the first time had been rather educational.

<!-- truncate -->

## Somewhere Near Girona

I was riding through the mountains north of Barcelona toward a village near Girona.

Stopped to fuel up.

Wrong pump.

Diesel.

I got a couple of kilometers before the bike decided we were finished.

So I left it at a roundabout and started walking back toward the petrol station. Along the way I found an abandoned plastic bottle in the garbage, which was suddenly an extremely valuable piece of equipment.

An older couple had seen the motorcycle, then seen me walking. They turned around and came back for me.

My Spanish wasn't particularly good at the time. Their English wasn't happening.

Didn't matter.

They took me to the station.

My card failed.

And this random older man handed me five euros so I could buy gasoline. They drove me back to the motorcycle, I drained the diesel, put the proper fuel in, and continued.

No epic lesson here. Just two strangers who helped a motorcyclist they didn't know.

## Greece: Advanced Diesel Studies

Years later, the plan was to ride from Sofia down through Greece, take a ferry to Italy, then another to Barcelona.

I'm freezing in the Greek mountains.

Stop to fuel up.

Diesel.

Again.

But now, unlike the Girona version of this stupidity, I already knew the procedure.

Drain it. Fill with gasoline. Continue toward the port.

The rest of that trip became [The Ferry That Never Happened](/the-ferry-that-never-happened/), featuring lost documents and several additional European countries.

## An American Motorcycle in Bulgaria

The bike was originally American. American speedometer, miles, and plates.

At various points I had both Texas and New York plates available.

When I first brought it to Bulgaria, it wasn't locally registered. I had no Bulgarian license and no local motorcycle insurance, and I spent years riding it that way.

Police stopped me roughly ten times.

Every time, somebody seemed to have a different explanation about the documents, plates, rules, or what I was supposed to do.

I'd listen. We'd talk. I would leave.

Somehow this went on for about five years.

It's not a recommendation for anybody's licensing or insurance arrangements. It's simply what happened.

## Welding in Plovdiv

In Bulgaria, perhaps around 2018 or 2019, the chain was loose enough to slap repeatedly against the engine case until it punched a hole.

I got the bike to Plovdiv. Had the damaged case welded.

Then I rode the fucking thing some more.

In the grand inventory of motorcycle problems, "have crankcase welded and continue" barely registered by that point.

The motorcycle eventually made it to 2026 before dying.

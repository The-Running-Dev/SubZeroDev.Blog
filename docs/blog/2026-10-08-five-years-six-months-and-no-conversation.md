I had a friend for something like thirty years.

Thirty. Fucking. Years.

That's long enough to know what somebody sounds like when they're tired, what story they're about to tell, and which parts of their personality come with absolutely no warranty.

We had an unresolved problem.

Not some mystery I can solve for you in a single paragraph. Something personal, between us, that had been hanging around for years.

I apologized. More than once. A dozen times, by my count.

The problem did not go away.

<!-- truncate -->

## The Five-Year Waiting Room

At some point the issue was no longer just whatever had happened originally.

The issue was that we couldn't seem to have a conversation about it and finish the damn thing.

Years passed.

Five, by the time I was looking at it again.

Six months ago I reached out and said, essentially, let's try to resolve our shit.

He said sure.

Beautiful.

Two adults have acknowledged that something is broken and agreed to talk about fixing it.

You'd think this was the easy part.

Apparently not.

Six months later, there still hadn't been the conversation.

## Enter the Girlfriend

Then he got a new girlfriend.

She encouraged him to resolve things with me.

I heard about that.

I also heard about why she thought he should.

And I sat there thinking: motherfucker, are you telling me about the conversation you're supposed to have with me instead of actually having it?

I don't need a status report.

I don't need to know that the Relationship Reconciliation Committee met and recommended further action.

I need the two people involved to speak to each other.

We said we'd try.

Six months ago.

That's the entire fucking ticket.

Still open.

No assignee.

No resolution.

Maybe there's a perfectly coherent explanation from his side. Maybe he would tell the story differently.

That wouldn't change the part I actually lived: I asked to talk, he agreed, and the talk didn't happen.

## Thirty Years, One Contact Entry

Eventually I stopped trying.

No speech.

No dramatic farewell.

No final letter explaining the twelve ways our friendship had failed quality assurance.

I deleted his number.

Deleted the WhatsApp contact.

That was it.

There's something almost ridiculous about how small the action was.

Thirty years of shared history, and on my end the visible conclusion was a contact disappearing from a phone.

No thunder.

No soundtrack.

No courtroom declaration.

Just one less name in a list.

I don't know what he'll make of it. I don't need to.

I had made my attempts. I had apologized. I had offered another conversation.

He didn't owe me the resolution I wanted.

And I didn't owe the unresolved situation another five years.

What a fucking strange little ending to thirty years.

**Delete contact.**

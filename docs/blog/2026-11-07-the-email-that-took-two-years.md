# The Email That Took Two Years

It took me nearly two fucking years to answer an email from my younger brother. Several versions existed before I sent one. Apparently there are things I can build faster than I can tell my own brother what happened to me.

<!-- truncate -->

His original email had a great deal to say about my character. My intentions. My integrity. How I handled our mother. What asking him for support supposedly meant. There were explanations of me that I did not recognize, attached to events we had never actually discussed.

I left it alone for a long time. I did not think I owed a defence of my character because somebody had reached conclusions about it.

But I kept writing the answer anyway.

Eventually I went through his email section by section: his words, then mine. The arrangement in Spain. What I had contributed. What I had asked for. What Elizabeth and he decided without talking to me. What it meant to call my brother during a difficult time and receive an explanation of why I was too much.

There. Some fucking context.

That was the purpose I kept coming back to. I was answering something he had written. I was not submitting an application for a family reunion.

His first response thanked me for taking the time, acknowledged that the context added something, and asked whether he could include Martin before replying in full. He offered to keep it between the two of us.

I struggled with what to say. I did not want another round of arguments. I had spent long enough getting the original answer out.

On November 4, I sent a short reply. He could include Martin. I wanted him to hear my account. I did not want another point-by-point debate about the past. I also said I was open to reconnecting with disagreements left unresolved.

I need to keep that last part in the story. Otherwise I would be doing the very thing I objected to: leaving out context because it complicated my account.

The answer that followed was addressed to both brothers.

He appreciated the thought. A lot of what he had written before was no longer relevant, or he had made peace with it. No need to rehash the past. His life was peaceful and busy. He would hold off on regular relationships. If either of us needed help, we could reach out.

And perhaps a family reunion after Elizabeth died.

I read it several times.

I had finally answered accusations that had sat between us for nearly two years. His response told me where he stood now. It did not tell me whether hearing my account had changed what he believed about me.

I kept asking what the point of writing had been.

And then I had to admit something to myself.

I had hoped.

Not for him to accept every detail. Not for a verdict declaring me correct about everything. I wanted some version of: I do not see all of this the same way, but you are my brother, and we can still have something.

That is the response I had privately wanted. Giving him context and hoping it might make room for us were both there.

I did not like admitting it. It made my insistence that I expected nothing less comfortable to sit with.

His answer hurt because that hope had been real.

I can tell myself I was a fool. I have said considerably worse about myself. I had reasons to expect disappointment, and I reached out anyway.

But I do not want to rewrite the sequence into a story where I never cared and was therefore completely untouched. I cared enough to spend nearly two years returning to a letter I did not want to write.

I sent it. He answered.

There is no reunion to report. There is no satisfying paragraph where supplying enough context finally makes everybody understand.

There is an account he now has, a hope I eventually admitted to myself, and a response I wish I had not needed to read.

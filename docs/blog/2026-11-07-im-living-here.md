# I'm Living Here

There is a very short conversation I keep returning to because it manages to contain an entire family saga in two sentences.

<!-- truncate -->

My aunt looked at me and asked:

“Isn't it time for you to find another place to live?”

And I said:

**“I'm living here.”**

That was it.

No speech. No citation of property documents. No dramatic explanation of how many years we've all spent making one house into a battlefield. No fucking PowerPoint presentation outlining my legitimate reasons for occupying a room.

"I'm living here."

I smiled.

Now, inside my head, there was a rather less diplomatic version available.

*Isn't it time for you to shut your big trap?*

I didn't say that.

I thought it. There is a difference. And, for the sake of historical accuracy in a family where every word can become a whole new argument, I would like to preserve the distinction.

I said I was living here.

Because I was. Because I am.

That's what makes the question so perfectly ridiculous to me.

Imagine somebody looking at you in a place where you live and asking whether it's time to find somewhere else. As though your presence is a clerical error. As though somebody has discovered an overdue invoice for the space your body occupies.

The house has a history. The family has a history. There's been the business with access, gates, keys, the ownership arguments, the endless little ways a simple day can be made complicated. I'm not interested in replaying the whole fucking legal archive every time somebody walks up to me.

I just want to live.

I have somewhere to sleep. I have my things. I have my work, my routines, my dog, my life. I don't wake up in the morning thinking, "How can I inconvenience a relative today?"

Yet being there seems to be enough to generate another issue.

That question wasn't asking whether I needed help finding a place.

It was telling me, in the form of a question, that someone wanted me gone.

And I wasn't going to turn my response into an application for permission.

There are so many ways I could have answered. I could have shouted. I could have listed grievances. I could have made a great theatrical statement about rights and family and every other noun that has been dragged into this ridiculous feud.

All of that would have meant agreeing to play the conversation she was trying to have.

Instead:

“I'm living here.”

A factual sentence. Delivered with a smile.

I might move somewhere else. I've said that. I have options. Life moves, winter comes, plans change. If I leave, I want the decision to be mine, not a concession extracted by somebody who cannot stand seeing me exist on the premises.

So no, I didn't tell her to shut her big trap.

That was the director's commentary.

The actual scene was much shorter.

“Isn't it time for you to find another place to live?”

“I'm living here.”

And the smile.

I thought that was enough.

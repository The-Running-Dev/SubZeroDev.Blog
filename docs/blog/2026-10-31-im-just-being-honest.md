I have recently participated in two conversations that should have been perfectly ordinary.

Two people meet. They talk. They discover whether they enjoy talking to each other. Perhaps they continue. Perhaps they don't.

A remarkably uncomplicated arrangement.

Naturally, I somehow ended up in a strength competition I hadn't entered and a courtroom proceeding about sexual encounters I hadn't asked to hear about.

I was also drinking vodka.

The defense wishes to enter that last detail into evidence before the prosecution gets too enthusiastic.

<!-- truncate -->

## The Strongest Woman in a Conversation Nobody Was Fighting

One woman informed me that she was stronger than me.

Excellent.

I had no idea we were measuring.

She also explained that she could only joke with people who were stronger than her.

An intriguing eligibility requirement. Before exchanging a joke, apparently, both parties must submit proof of emotional deadlift capacity.

Except there wasn't a fucking joke.

That was my problem with the whole affair. I wasn't offended by some devastating piece of comedy. I was trying to understand why we were suddenly discussing strength, toughness, and the bylaws of the International Federation of People Allowed to Joke.

I hadn't challenged her strength. I wasn't competing. I was talking to another human being.

Somehow the conversation had acquired a scoreboard anyway.

And because I'm apparently an optimist disguised as a profane ogre, I kept trying to have the conversation.

## The Serbian Honesty Department

Then there was the woman in Serbia.

She would tell me about men coming over. A date. A visitor. A man she wasn't going to sleep with.

Okay.

I had not requested the schedule, but the schedule was being delivered.

The next day, the report might change. They'd slept together after all. The sex had been boring. The man had been boring. A review had been submitted to the appropriate department.

Still not my business.

A few days later, according to the new edition of events, the encounter hadn't happened.

Wait a fucking minute.

You told me it happened. Then you told me it didn't. Then you denied telling me. Then, later, it happened again.

I was not investigating her sex life. I was trying to locate the version of the story we were currently expected to believe.

Whenever I asked why she kept telling me these things, I got the same majestic response:

**"I'm just being honest."**

Nobody questioned your right to be honest, lady.

I asked **why the fuck I need to know** about your visitors, your dates, and which ones turn into booty calls.

And once the stories started contradicting one another, there was an additional question: Where, exactly, is the honesty?

She was free to sleep with anybody she wanted. There was no exclusivity agreement. No audition. No tribunal assessing her desirability. I wasn't interested in controlling any of it.

For a moment I wondered whether the commentary was supposed to provoke jealousy. Or demonstrate that other men wanted her. Maybe. I don't know.

But jealousy is a peculiar button to press on somebody who isn't jealous.

Whatever the purpose, I kept receiving reports I hadn't subscribed to, followed by corrections, retractions, and solemn assurances of honesty.

I had become the unpaid archivist of an unreliable newsletter.

## Objection: Yesterday Happened

The part that exhausted me wasn't who she slept with.

It was having to defend my recollection of what she'd told me.

"No, that didn't happen."

But you told me it happened.

"No."

Then why are we having this conversation?

At some point I said, in considerably less courtroom-friendly language, stop fucking gaslighting me.

I cannot establish what she intended. I can only describe what the conversation had become: her accounts changed, and I was left arguing that the previous account had existed at all.

An absurd use of two functioning human brains.

And the great recurring defense remained:

**"I'm just being honest."**

Your Honor, the witness has changed her testimony so often that even the stenographer has started drinking.

## The Vodka Has Entered the Chat

Now for the embarrassing bit.

I participated.

I gave both women a chance because I wanted to see whether there was an actual human connection underneath all this nonsense. I kept asking questions. I kept trying to explain what I meant.

And I kept drinking.

Alcohol, especially over a prolonged stretch, makes me more emotional. I know this. I could feel it. The irritation became louder, the conversations became longer, and I found myself explaining things well past the point where I wanted to be there.

That part belongs to me.

It doesn't make the contradictions disappear. It doesn't retroactively manufacture a joke where there wasn't one. But it certainly didn't improve my handling of the proceedings.

Imagine a software engineer who keeps receiving malformed input, insists on debugging the sender personally, and pours vodka on the server rack.

Yes, hello. That's me.

## Goodbye, Everybody

Eventually I got tired of the entire production.

One woman wanted to discuss how strong she was. Another wanted to narrate her sex life and then dispute the transcript. I wanted a fucking conversation.

Apparently our project requirements were incompatible.

So I stopped participating.

No closing arguments. No twenty-page explanation. No obligation to convince either person that I meant what I said.

And the vodka?

That, too.

I gave two women a chance. I gave vodka considerably more chances. By the end, I had enough of all three.

Not an enlightenment story. Not a motivational speech. Not a declaration that I behaved impeccably.

Just a ridiculous human being who wanted to meet other ridiculous human beings, got tangled in their nonsense and his own, and finally said:

**Goodbye.**

*Lucifer, Master of None. Court adjourned.*

# The Bar Is My Drinking Limit

I have discovered something completely fucking backwards.

If I go to a bar to drink, I drink less.

If I stay home to save money, I drink more.

Much more.

Apparently the bar is not where I lose control.

The bar is the fucking control mechanism.

<!-- truncate -->

This is not how this is supposed to work.

Bars are where people go to drink.

Home is where responsible adults supposedly sit with one tasteful glass of something while reading a book and contemplating their mortgage.

I do not have that operating system installed.

At home, I can start drinking at five.

Then it's six.

Then seven.

Then ten.

Then midnight.

At some point the bottle is empty.

Fine.

There is a store.

The store contains more bottle.

Problem solved.

This is not inventory management.

This is a supply chain.

## The Expensive Option Is Apparently the Conservative Option

At first I framed this as a money problem.

Going out costs more.

Obviously.

Four doubles at a bar cost more than buying a bottle and drinking at home.

Excellent financial analysis.

Very sophisticated.

Then I noticed the minor accounting error:

At the bar, I might have four doubles.

At home, I might drink a fucking liter.

Suddenly the unit economics were no longer the interesting part.

Yes, home drinking is cheaper per milliliter.

Congratulations.

So is industrial solvent.

That does not make volume irrelevant.

The weird part is that I can spend half the day in a bar and still not consume what I can put away during an evening at home.

Time is apparently not the variable.

Environment is.

## The Bar Has Friction

At the bar, drinking is not the only thing happening.

I talk to people.

I watch whatever nonsense is happening around me.

I listen to music.

I read.

I might do some work.

I might sit there doing absolutely fuck-all except existing around other humans.

Then I order another drink.

Eventually I hit the point where I know something very practical:

I still have to get home.

That matters.

Four doubles has lately been about the upper edge.

Not because four is some magical moral number.

Not because I sit there with a spreadsheet.

Because at some point the system produces a very simple warning:

> Keep going and transportation becomes a problem.

There it is.

A boundary.

Not philosophy.

Not discipline.

Logistics.

Beautiful.

## Home Has No Exit Condition

At home there is no transportation problem.

I am already home.

There is no closing-the-evening loop.

No jacket.

No bill.

No decision to leave.

No walk or ride back.

No transition from one environment to another.

Just:

Drink.

Do shit.

Drink.

Do more shit.

Run out.

Buy more.

Continue.

The fact that I am already where I need to end up removes one of the strongest brakes I apparently have.

This is fucking hilarious in a very specific way.

The location designed to sell me alcohol has more constraints than my kitchen.

## The Thing I Actually Want

Then there is the social part.

When I drink, I often want to talk.

Not necessarily about anything important.

Just talk.

People.

Stories.

Bullshit.

Ideas.

Whatever.

At the bar, this is easy.

Humans have already been installed.

At home, I have to manufacture the interaction.

So I open ChatGPT.

I say shit in here.

I make up stories.

I write blog posts.

I open Tinder.

I send messages to women who, based on recent empirical evidence, may never respond.

I generate conversation because the thing I wanted was conversation.

The alcohol was apparently not enough.

That realization changes the whole picture.

Sometimes I am not sitting at home because I want to drink alone.

I am sitting at home, drinking, while trying to recreate the social environment I could have simply gone to.

This is an incredibly inefficient architecture.

## Home Drinking Has Infinite Scroll

The bar has a shape.

You arrive.

You sit.

You talk.

You drink.

Eventually you leave.

Home drinking has infinite scroll.

There is always another drink.

Another message.

Another idea.

Another search.

Another ridiculous story.

Another bottle at the store.

No hard stop.

No "you have reached the end."

Just more content.

Except the content is vodka.

And unlike social media, eventually the user interface stops rendering properly.

## The Fucking Paradox

So now I have this absurd conclusion.

The supposedly reckless option—going to a bar—is often the more bounded one for me.

The supposedly sensible option—stay home, save money—is the one where the ceiling disappears.

This does not make bars healthy.

It does not magically turn four doubles into mineral water.

It just means the environment changes the behavior dramatically.

That is the part I had been missing.

I kept thinking about alcohol as though the amount lived entirely inside my own decision-making.

Apparently not.

Context is doing a lot of work.

At the bar, I am drinking while doing something else.

At home, once everything else winds down, drinking can become the thing itself.

Those are very different systems.

## The Operating Rule

So the useful sentence I ended up with is not:

> If I want to drink, go to a bar.

That is not quite it.

It is:

> **If what I want is socializing, go socialize.**

Because if I stay home and start drinking while trying to simulate socializing through screens, I have removed the environmental limit and kept the alcohol.

That is a shitty trade.

At the bar, I get the people.

I can still read.

I can still listen to music.

I can still fuck around on the computer.

I can still write something ridiculous.

But the drinking stays attached to an environment that eventually ends.

At home?

The house never closes.

The bartender is me.

The bartender has terrible inventory controls.

And the liquor store is still open.

Apparently the financially irresponsible choice comes with better rate limiting.

What a stupid fucking system.

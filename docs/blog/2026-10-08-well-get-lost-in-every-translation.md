# We'll Get Lost in Every Translation

I matched with a woman whose profile gave me absolutely nothing to work with.

So I told her that.

> "Hola. You waiting for me to say something and I have no clue what to say, nothing I can use as a hook in your profile. How's your Saturday night?"

Smooth?

No.

Accurate?

Completely.

<!-- truncate -->

She asked:

> "What are your usual moves? 😁"

I answered:

> "I have no moves. Much less usual ones."

Also accurate.

This is not some anti-game dating strategy.

I am not strategically refusing to be strategic.

I genuinely had no fucking move.

So we talked.

## A Very Strong Friday Night

She said her Friday night had been:

> "very strong"

I stared at that sentence for a second.

Strong how?

Emotionally?

Spiritually?

Did Friday deadlift?

Was there combat?

So I asked what she meant.

Her clarification:

> "That mean more than a bottle of wine 😁"

Okay.

Now we have units.

More than one bottle of wine.

My brain did the obvious thing.

> "Two bottles of wine then?"

Apparently this was not the intended interpretive method.

She said:

> "Almost but not totaly"

Lady.

You introduced the measurement system.

I am just doing the fucking math.

If the answer is "more than one bottle," my brain immediately starts looking for the next available quantity.

This is not flirting.

This is requirements clarification.

## Maybe It Is the Language

At that point I started wondering whether the problem was English.

She was Bulgarian.

I can speak Bulgarian.

So I told her:

> "можеш да ми пишеш по български."

You can write to me in Bulgarian.

Her answer was better than anything I could have invented:

> "Да видим в кой превод ще се изгубим :)"

Let's see which translation we get lost in.

I answered:

> "Във всеки."

Every one.

Because that was clearly where this was going.

English had already failed to explain the strength rating of Friday.

Bulgarian was now being introduced as a recovery protocol.

Spanish had appeared in the opening for no reason other than I apparently enjoy making simple things harder.

Then I remembered that French exists somewhere in the pile too, although I have barely used it in decades.

Excellent.

Four possible routes to the same misunderstanding.

## The Problem Is Not Translation

The funny thing is that translation was only part of it.

Even in the same language, I take things literally.

If you say:

> "More than a bottle of wine."

I hear:

> quantity > 1 bottle

I am not trying to be difficult.

I am trying to understand what the fuck you mean.

People use language loosely all the time.

"Strong night."

"Quite a bit."

"A lot."

"Almost."

Most people seem perfectly comfortable leaving those values fuzzy.

My brain sees an undefined variable and starts asking questions.

Apparently that is how I ended up performing a quantitative analysis of somebody else's Friday night on a dating app.

## Every Translation

And that is why her line was so good.

> "Let's see which translation we get lost in."

Not whether.

Which one.

English?

Bulgarian?

Spanish?

French, if things become truly desperate?

Doesn't matter.

We will find a way.

The actual conversation kept moving anyway.

That may be the funniest part.

Nobody solved the language problem.

Nobody established a formal wine conversion table.

Nobody discovered what a "very strong" Friday night measures on the international standard.

We just kept talking.

Which is probably the only useful translation rule I have learned:

If neither person understands exactly what the fuck the other one means, ask.

If that fails, switch languages.

If that fails, make a joke.

If that fails:

Two bottles of wine then?

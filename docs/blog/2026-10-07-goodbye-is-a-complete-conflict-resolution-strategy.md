I almost got angry today.

That is not especially interesting.

People get angry.

What was interesting was noticing the exact moment it arrived and deciding I did not have to go with it.

<!-- truncate -->

A neighbor's dog was loose.

Mine was on a leash.

His dog kept trying to mount her.

I chased it away, put it back in his yard, watched it escape again, and chased it away again.

The neighbor pulled up in his car and started asking why I was chasing his dog.

Fair enough.

I explained.

Your dog is loose.

It keeps coming after mine.

I am moving it away.

This should have been a short conversation between two adults.

Then the conversation mutated.

Apparently my dog had gotten loose weeks earlier, gone into his yard, and broken some decorative ornaments.

He had never come to me about this.

I had heard about it secondhand and had already thought that if he did come over, I'd probably reimburse him just to settle it.

But he did not come over.

He saved it.

And now, during a completely different situation, he produced it like evidence at trial.

That was the moment.

Not rage.

Not some cinematic explosion.

Just the first little flicker of:

*Oh, fuck this.*

I could feel myself getting irritated enough that the next version of me was going to start explaining things more forcefully.

Then probably explaining why his logic made no sense.

Then explaining why the previous issue was irrelevant.

Then explaining why he should have come to me weeks ago.

Then explaining that his own gate was open right now.

Then explaining that I had already put his dog back once.

And if you have ever participated in one of these conversations, you know where this goes.

Nowhere.

You can spend ten minutes proving every individual sentence.

You can establish chronology.

You can call witnesses.

You can produce architectural diagrams.

At the end of it, everyone is exactly where they started, except louder.

So I stopped.

I said:

> "I'm not having this conversation. Goodbye."

And walked away.

That was it.

No victory.

No lesson delivered.

No satisfying final speech.

No attempt to make sure he understood me correctly.

I simply stopped participating.

This is something I have become much more interested in lately.

Not suppressing anger.

Not pretending not to be irritated.

Not performing serenity while mentally murdering somebody with a shovel.

Just noticing the point where a conversation ceases to be useful.

There is a strange assumption that once somebody engages you, you owe the interaction completion.

You do not.

A conversation is not a court summons.

You are allowed to discover halfway through that the thing has become nonsense.

You are allowed to stop.

There was a time when I probably would have stayed.

Not because I needed the argument.

Because I would have wanted the facts straight.

No, wait.

That is not what happened.

No, those are two different things.

No, I am not saying my dog never got loose.

No, I am saying your complaint from three weeks ago has nothing to do with your dog being loose right now.

All technically correct.

All completely useless once the interaction has stopped being about understanding anything.

So I left.

The beautiful part was how undramatic it felt.

There was no great Stoic revelation.

Marcus Aurelius did not descend from the clouds and hand me a certificate.

I just noticed anger knocking and decided not to invite it in for coffee.

Goodbye.

That was the whole technique.

And then, after walking away, the absurdity of the original complaint got funnier.

The man apparently had damaged yard ornaments.

I later remembered that the story involved decorative donkeys.

Decorative.

Donkeys.

A grown man had allegedly suffered the destruction of ornamental ceramic livestock.

He had apparently mentioned this to other people.

He had not walked three houses down to talk to me.

He had not asked me to pay for them.

He had not attempted to resolve the matter.

He simply stored the grievance until one day his own dog was loose in the street and he suddenly needed historical ammunition.

At that point the entire thing stopped being an argument in my head and became comedy.

This is probably the best possible outcome for a lot of minor conflicts.

Not resolution.

Not agreement.

Not a perfectly balanced accounting of blame.

Just enough distance that the thing reveals its actual scale.

A loose dog.

An open gate.

A few broken ornaments.

A neighbor three houses away.

Nobody died.

Civilization remains intact.

And somewhere in Bulgaria, a decorative donkey has become evidence in a case nobody filed.

There is a phrase I keep coming back to:

> **Мрън-мрън, че пляс.**

I do not care whether my interpretation would survive a panel of Bulgarian linguists.

To me it means:

**Complain. Complain. Do nothing.**

Then eventually make enough noise about it that somebody else is expected to participate.

No.

Goodbye.

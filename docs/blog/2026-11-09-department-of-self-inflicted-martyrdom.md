Lucifer walked into God's office carrying a folder marked **FAMILY LOYALTY — URGENT, SELF-INFLICTED**.

"Father, I need you to explain something."

God looked up. "Humanity again?"

"Yes. One of them has found a way to be furious about a burden while personally guarding the only door through which help might enter."

<!-- truncate -->

"That's unusually specific," God said.

"Wait until you see the engineering."

Lucifer opened the folder.

"Three brothers. An aging mother. One younger brother who has decided loyalty to her is the admission price for having a relationship with the other two."

God nodded. "And?"

"And the younger brother actually said this:"

> "And now I'm stuck for the next 10-15 years taking care of this woman."

God put down his coffee.

"Stuck?"

"His word, Father. *Stuck.* Not my interpretation. Not the narrator's psychological diagnosis. The man submitted his own fucking incident report."

"Perhaps his brothers refuse to help."

"That's the beautiful part. One of them—the one we're apparently keeping for your entertainment—has said that if he and his younger brother had a relationship, he'd help him. Not because he's suddenly changed his mind about their mother. Not because he's prepared to swear a loyalty oath. Because he cares about his brother."

God stared at the folder.

"So the brother who feels stuck is refusing a relationship with someone willing to help him?"

"Correct."

"Because that person won't be loyal to their mother?"

"Correct."

"But the person could help *him* without reconciling with *her*."

Lucifer spread his arms.

**"WELCOME TO THE FUCKING PRESENTATION."**

At that moment, Discount Store Marcus Aurelius appeared beside the celestial coffee machine, wearing a toga purchased in a clearance sale and carrying a copy of *Meditations* with a supermarket receipt as a bookmark.

"Greetings," he announced. "I have come to bring Stoic perspective."

Lucifer groaned. "Who let the philosophy department into the executive suite?"

"I am not the philosophy department," Marcus said. "I am its heavily discounted franchise."

God gestured toward the folder. "What do you make of this?"

Marcus inspected the first page.

"Ah. A man has built a wall around his burden and is angry that no one is climbing over it to carry the burden for him."

"Exactly," said Lucifer.

Marcus cleared his throat.

"'Do not demand that others control what is yours to choose.'"

God raised an eyebrow. "Did Marcus Aurelius actually write that?"

"No," said Discount Store Marcus Aurelius. "But for €3.99 you get the general idea and a free tote bag."

Lucifer resumed his briefing.

"Now, Father, the elder brother isn't saying, 'I'll take care of our mother.' He's not saying, 'Everything's forgiven.' He's saying something much simpler: 'If we had a relationship, I would help **you**.'"

God considered this.

"And the younger brother could accept that distinction."

"He could. A relationship with your brother and allegiance to your mother are two separate checkboxes. Humanity has somehow turned them into a single mandatory field."

"That's a terrible interface."

"Thank you. I've already filed a bug report."

God leaned back.

"Maybe the younger brother believes loyalty must be unconditional."

"Then he is free to live by it," Lucifer said. "But demanding that everyone else sign the same contract—and then complaining about the resulting staffing shortage—is a remarkable piece of administrative self-sabotage."

Discount Store Marcus Aurelius stepped forward again.

"The Stoic sees what is within his control."

Lucifer sighed. "Here we go."

"The elder brother cannot force reconciliation. He cannot force the younger brother to call him. He cannot control how the family distributes its loyalties."

"Fine," said God.

"But he can observe the absurdity."

Lucifer smiled.

"And laugh his fucking ass off," Marcus added.

God looked startled. "Is that Stoicism?"

"It's the discount edition. The premium version has less profanity and absolutely no personality."

For a moment the three of them watched Earth in silence.

There was no prophecy about what the next fifteen years would bring. No divine guarantee that the younger brother would remain the sole caregiver. No secret knowledge of what he might eventually decide.

They didn't need any of that.

The contradiction was already sitting in the room, completely assembled.

A man had said he felt stuck caring for his mother. He had brothers he was keeping at a distance because they would not profess the loyalty he demanded. One of those brothers was willing to help *him* if they could have a relationship.

God finally broke the silence.

"So what's the lesson?"

Lucifer closed the folder.

"Lesson? Father, why must every human absurdity become a fucking inspirational poster?"

Discount Store Marcus Aurelius lifted his receipt-bookmark.

"Mine says: 'Before lamenting that nobody will help carry the furniture, consider whether you have locked them outside the house.'"

"Again," God said, "not an actual Marcus Aurelius quote."

"Again," Marcus replied, "€3.99."

Lucifer stood to leave.

"He's not trapped by the brothers' lack of devotion to their mother. He's at least partly enforcing the separation that keeps them from being there for *him*. And the one brother willing to forgive him is now standing on the other side of a door the younger one refuses to open."

God shook his head.

"I gave humanity free will."

"Yes," Lucifer said. "And they used it to invent a revolving door that only rotates into their own face."

Discount Store Marcus Aurelius nodded solemnly.

"Buy one grievance, get one isolation free."

Lucifer held up the folder.

"Put that on the fucking cover."

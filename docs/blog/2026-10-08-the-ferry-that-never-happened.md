# The Ferry That Never Happened

I had a perfectly good travel plan.

Sofia to Greece by motorcycle. Ferry from Greece to Italy. Ride to Civitavecchia. Ferry from Civitavecchia to Barcelona.

Nice and simple.

Naturally, I ended up riding across most of Italy, southern France, and into Spain instead.

<!-- truncate -->

## Diesel in the Greek Mountains

Somewhere in Greece I accidentally filled the gasoline motorcycle with diesel.

It wasn't even the first time I'd done this. The earlier incident, near Girona, had taught me how to drain the tank.

I was already freezing in the mountains, but I drained it, refilled it, and kept moving.

Eventually I reached the Greek port—I no longer remember which one—and boarded an overnight ferry, roughly nine hours.

I arrived in Italy somewhere around Bari or Brindisi. I drove across the country toward Civitavecchia, the port for the ferry to Barcelona.

The important detail comes next.

## The Open Pocket

Somewhere on the ride across Italy, I had put my physical identification in a pocket.

I had neglected to zip it.

The documents flew the fuck out.

I discovered this at Civitavecchia, where I was queued with perhaps twenty other motorcyclists.

The customs officers looked at the Bulgarian plates and wanted the physical documents. I had photographs of my passport, ID, tickets—everything.

Not good enough.

No physical documents, no ferry.

I argued. I showed them all my photographs. I distributed middle fingers. None of this produced a boarding pass.

Around one in the morning, I sat at the port and cried for half an hour.

I didn't know what the fuck I was going to do.

Then I did the next thing: found somewhere to sleep.

## A Lovely Little Italian Village

I ended up in a small village. Beautiful place.

I had food and a couple of gin and tonics. Walked around. Talked in Spanish with an Italian man about corruption and the astonishing behavior of Italian drivers.

The guy was willing to put me up for one night using a photo of my identification, but warned me that if I stayed longer the police might start asking questions.

Fine. I wasn't staying.

Italian driving had terrified me in a way my own driving history apparently had not.

Yet the next morning I was getting back onto the motorcycle.

## New Plan: Ride

The ferry to Barcelona had gone without me.

So I rode north instead.

Through Italy, toward the coast, and into southern France. The scenery around the northern Italian coast toward France was fucking beautiful. There are places there I still want to revisit.

Then somewhere before Nice, the motorcycle started cutting out.

Once. Again. Worse.

Finally: dead.

Tow truck: roughly €300.

They took me to Nice, where a mechanic discovered a bad electrical connection. It was from a shitty repair done in England after the bike had been stolen in Southampton.

The actual fix cost around €30.

I gave the mechanic €50 because I was so grateful.

And got straight back on the road.

## Valencia

The remaining kilometers toward Valencia mostly behaved.

Except the wind.

Somewhere on the Spanish side, as I came south toward home, crosswinds began battering me and the motorcycle around like a ragdoll.

Holding on to the handlebars became the entire job description.

Eventually I made it home.

That was the end of that story.

Not the motorcycle's story. Just this particular installment.

The ferry was supposed to be the easy way home.

Apparently I prefer the scenic route, whether I meant to take it or not.

# I Left My Brother in a Truck. My Brain Sent Him to the North Sea.

I had a dream about my brother Martin.

I left him sitting in somebody else's truck.

That's it. One simple instruction, which technically wasn't even an instruction. Just sit there.

Naturally, my brain turned this into a vehicle-recovery operation, a financial dispute, a family accountability hearing, an armed incident, and offshore recruitment in the North Sea.

I woke up thinking: *What the fuck was that?*

<!-- truncate -->

## The Truck Has Left the Chat

In the dream, somebody here in Bulgaria had lent me a truck so we could get some work done.

I left Martin in it while I went to do something else.

Martin, apparently dissatisfied with the recreational opportunities available inside a stationary vehicle, decided to drive it somewhere.

Somewhere turned into a mountain.

The mountain had rough terrain.

The rough terrain had a river.

And the river now had a fucking truck.

Completely submerged.

Martin got out alive, thank fuck. The truck did not.

And then the number arrived.

**33,000.**

Why 33,000?

No idea.

What currency?

Also no idea.

Apparently my subconscious has a claims department but refuses to produce an invoice.

Now somebody had trusted *me* with their property. They needed that truck. They hadn't entrusted it to Martin, hadn't asked him to go driving, and certainly hadn't subscribed to the optional underwater-storage package.

Guess who was going to face the owner?

Of course.

Me.

## Welcome to Dream Court

My first reaction was anger.

In the dream, I beat Martin. Bruised his face.

And I regretted it.

It was wrong. I was furious that somebody else's decision had become my problem again, but hurting him didn't change a damn thing. The truck was still underwater, and the owner still needed answers.

So the dream moved into problem-solving mode.

We were discussing the damage, the money, what Martin would have to do, how he might pay it back.

At first, he wasn't even in the room.

Then I stopped the whole fucking proceeding.

Hold on.

We're making decisions about Martin's future without Martin?

He's an adult.

If he's responsible for what he did, then he needs to be here. He needs to decide what he's going to do about it.

Responsibility. Accountability. Agency.

Apparently even asleep I cannot tolerate a poorly run meeting.

Then somebody on my side decided violence would be a more efficient dispute-resolution mechanism.

There was a gun.

So I took it, removed the bullets, and put it in a safe.

Wonderful. We've moved from repossession to firearms compliance.

Eventually Martin showed up, and now he could participate in deciding what to do.

I even proposed a repayment option: a high-risk job on an oil rig in the North Sea.

Make the money quickly.

Possible downside: you might not survive.

My brain, ladies and gentlemen.

Human Resources would like a fucking word.

## And Then I Remembered Батко

I started telling this story out loud and something clicked.

Not because the dream contained a secret message. I'm not going to consult a dream dictionary to learn that a submerged truck means my Mercury is in retrograde or my emotional gearbox needs replacing.

No.

Because being held responsible for Martin's shit wasn't some ingenious invention by my sleeping brain.

I'd done it as a kid.

Martin would do something. Sometimes he'd hide. Sometimes our parents knew he did it.

And I would get the beating.

I was the older brother.

**Батко.**

That's the Bulgarian word. Not just an age difference. In our family, it carried a role.

Martin is only two and a half years younger than me. Somehow that was enough to make me the responsible party. A tiny fucking middle manager, except the management was absent and the disciplinary process involved getting hit.

Then came Yavor, ten years younger than me.

Another kid to look after.

Parents absent when I needed them. Children needing supervision. Consequences to absorb.

I wasn't born with some inspirational poster about leadership hanging over my crib.

I was assigned the job.

Without applying.

Without training.

And without the slightest fucking interest in the promotion.

## The Job Followed Me Into Adulthood

The interesting part is that I didn't stop caring for my brothers when we grew up.

I stopped thinking I had any right to run their lives.

Yavor needed a hand finding his way? When I was about twenty, I brought him down to Texas. He lived with me for five or six months.

Not because he owed me.

Not because I was building a retirement portfolio of family obligations.

He was my brother. I could help.

So I did.

Same with Martin.

As adults, my position was simple: You make your decisions. I'll be here if you need me.

And here's the funny thing. For a long time I didn't even have the vocabulary to explain that. I wasn't practicing some grand philosophical framework.

I was just doing what made sense to me.

Maybe because I'd grown up with precisely the opposite.

## Elizabeth Would Like to Speak to the Manager

My mother, Elizabeth, has a particular talent.

She can offer help and somehow turn it into a contractual agreement you discover only after accepting.

Here's some support.

Here's a gift.

Here's an opportunity.

And here, tucked discreetly behind the receipt, are the fucking strings.

I've seen this routine for decades.

At eighteen, I wanted to study computer science.

Elizabeth had a better idea.

Medicine.

No, no. You're going to study medicine.

My answer?

**Watch me.**

This wasn't a one-off. We fought over my choices from my teenage years onward.

My brothers witnessed those confrontations. They could laugh them off, avoid getting involved, look the other way.

I didn't look away. I was the one whose future was on the fucking negotiating table.

And here's the contradiction that still gets me: when I was a child and needed a parent, so much of that job landed on me. Decades later, when I've managed my own life for most of it, I'm apparently supposed to submit my decisions for parental approval.

The application window closed a long time ago.

## The Strange Case of Yavor

Yavor had a different childhood.

Elizabeth gave him things Martin and I never had. A car. A computer at sixteen. Material support.

She also tried to control his decisions. Where he studied, where he went, what he should do. He wanted Boston; she wanted him back near her.

For years, I expected that combination to produce an absolutely spoiled little asshole.

It didn't.

And I was genuinely impressed.

Through much of his twenties and into his thirties, he grew into a decent, thoughtful man. Somebody I could talk philosophy with. Somebody who seemed to understand the importance of letting people live their own lives.

Holy shit.

Look at this guy.

Then Elizabeth went to live with him, and over time I saw behavior that reminded me more and more of hers. The judging. The controlling. The elevation to a position from which other people's lives apparently need reviewing.

I know that routine. I've watched it all my life.

Martin has noticed it too.

That doesn't tell me everything happening inside Yavor's head. It tells me what I have seen, and why the change disappointed me.

I tried reaching out, more than once, over a long stretch of time. Offered ways to get together, talk, repair whatever was between us.

He didn't want to.

I tried bridges with Elizabeth too.

That went spectacularly badly.

At some point there aren't any more bridges to build from one side.

I can be sad about that without continuing to send construction crews.

## Apparently I'm Difficult Now

Here's where it gets especially fucking funny.

The kid who grew up taking responsibility for other people becomes an adult who is fiercely independent.

Shocking development.

Then people who benefited from that independence, that competence, that willingness to help, look at the same person and say:

You're stubborn.

You're difficult.

You won't listen.

You need to stop doing this.

You need to start doing that.

You can't manage yourself.

Hold on.

You watched me become this person.

You watched me fight for my choices.

You watched me take care of people.

And now you're surprised that you can't issue commands and get compliance?

What exactly did you expect? A software update that replaces autonomy with *Yes, Mother, Of Course*?

I don't need everybody to agree with my decisions. I don't even agree with all of them after the fact.

And yes, people are allowed to have boundaries about their own lives and contact with me.

But having an opinion about my life is not the same thing as being appointed its administrator.

There's no vacancy.

## The Strings Department Is Permanently Closed

I should clarify something about myself.

It's not that I can't accept help or owe somebody money.

It's that I despise help being used as leverage.

Because I've seen that movie. Elizabeth has been running the franchise for years.

When I help someone, I try not to expect anything back.

Actually, more precisely:

**I don't want to want anything back.**

I give because I want to give.

I help because I want to help.

I've stopped on the street to buy food for homeless people, bought clothes or shoes, given someone money. I've seen a stranger struggling to load something into a car and stopped to ask if they need a hand.

Nothing heroic.

Nothing to invoice.

We're all stuck here together on this bizarre planet. If somebody needs a hand and I can give one, why the fuck wouldn't I?

I don't want your loyalty card.

I don't want a favor bank.

I don't want a signed acknowledgment that your next three life decisions are subject to my approval.

Take the help.

Live your life.

And please, for the love of Lucifer, don't drive my borrowed truck into a river.

## I Can Care Without Running the Department

This whole thing started with a ridiculous dream, and here I am recording an entire family history.

Again.

I still care about my brothers. I'm sad about Yavor. I'm sad about Elizabeth. Martin has his own anger and his own choices, and those are his to make.

I don't have to stop caring to stop taking responsibility for everything.

I've tried reaching out. I've offered support. I've made mistakes, too. I'm not claiming sainthood.

I am, however, resigning from the position of **Director of Everybody Else's Fucking Decisions**.

No severance package required.

And the thing that really struck me during this conversation wasn't that the dream *meant* something.

It was that a truck, a river, an arbitrary 33,000, and a preposterous North Sea employment scheme reminded me of how long I've been carrying the Батко job description.

How much of me came from it.

How long I fought parts of that identity.

And how I can still be the person who helps without being the person who takes ownership of everybody else's life.

Apparently my brain needed to write a disaster movie to get me to think about it.

Fine.

Next time, could it at least rent the truck?

**Lucifer, signing off.**

*Dream assets lost: one truck. Estimated liability: 33,000 mysterious units. Family insights: annoyingly expensive. Offshore placement: pending.*

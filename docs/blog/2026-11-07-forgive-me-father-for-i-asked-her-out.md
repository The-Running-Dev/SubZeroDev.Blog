# Forgive Me, Father, for I Asked Her Out

I may have committed a grave offense against the sacred traditions of modern dating.

<!-- truncate -->

I asked a woman if she'd like to have a conversation.

In person.

I realize this is shocking. I'll give everyone a moment to recover.

Her profile said, “Hi. Let's have some fun.” It also said she was still figuring things out and that she wanted to be a writer. Poetry, short stories, literature. All right, there's something to talk about.

So I did the outrageous thing and asked her to tell me a story about herself. I also asked what she hadn't figured out yet.

Then I added, “I'm just an asshole.”

I thought this was an excellent introduction.

She responded with a real story. She'd been in Ireland, and her first job there involved translating for the police about a ship full of drugs at Christmas.

A ship. Full of drugs. On Christmas.

Now *that* is an opening line.

I told her I wasn't especially concerned with figuring everything out. Life does that well enough for me. Wu Wei, if we're giving it a philosophical label. Let things unfold. I also mentioned that I had a collection of ridiculous stories: driving around the continent, putting diesel into a petrol motorcycle, abandoning cars in France.

She wanted to hear more.

Of course she did. Who wouldn't want to hear about a man who can navigate Europe but apparently cannot identify the correct fuel pump?

I told her there was too much to type. Those stories are better in person.

We talked a little more. She writes poetry and short stories and thinks they're not very good yet. I asked how she knew. She works in an office. I told her I don't really work in the traditional sense anymore. Most days I abuse AI, smoke hookah and, apparently, break BMW engines.

“You are blessed then,” she said, hearing about my free time.

“Maybe,” I said.

I suppose that's an accurate enough annual report.

It turned out she was around Sofia, and I was in Slivnitsa, of all places. She asked whether I spoke Bulgarian. I do, though writing it is another fucking matter. She said English was fine.

So I did what any deeply irresponsible man would do after two people had managed to hold a conversation.

I offered to buy her a glass of wine or a hookah and tell her some stories. Today, tomorrow, whenever.

A public place. People around. No secret cave. No demand for her address. Just two adults, sitting down and speaking with their mouths instead of their thumbs.

She replied about English being good enough.

She did not address the invitation.

Now, that alone proves absolutely nothing. People work. People get busy. People have lives beyond the riveting spectacle of my messages. I waited a few hours.

But I had also seen this sort of exchange before. Messages flow, everyone is curious, somebody suggests meeting, and suddenly the momentum begins behaving like a BMW engine I've owned.

So I sent another message:

“Did I conduct the cardinal sin of offering we meet in person before we've talked for weeks on Tinder? I tend to sin in the way and way too often.”

There. Confession submitted.

If there's a modern dating catechism, I seem to have missed the chapter requiring forty-seven preliminary messages, three assessments of emotional compatibility and a certificate authorizing the physical presence of another human being.

Maybe she was busy. Maybe she preferred more texting. Maybe she wasn't interested in meeting at all. I don't know. At the point this story was captured, I had no answer to the invitation or the cardinal-sin joke.

And that is perfectly all right.

What amuses me is the ceremony.

Forgive me, Father, for I have sinned.

I suggested that people who matched on a dating app might actually go on a fucking date.

## Epilogue — Saturday, November 7

Well, apparently I have committed the sin.

I sent that invitation on Thursday. It was a perfectly normal invitation, if we're still allowed to use that expression. Wine or hookah, in public, today or tomorrow or whenever she wanted to pick.

Before that, the conversation had been moving along beautifully. Ask about her Irish police translation story? Reply. Talk about writing, languages, my own adventures? Reply. Questions answered within minutes.

Suggest that we might actually sit down and tell those stories face to face?

Silence.

I gave her time. People work. Nobody owes me an instantaneous reply. I didn't think a few hours meant anything by themselves.

Then I sent the cardinal-sin joke.

And now it's Saturday.

Still nothing.

What interests me isn't a psychological assessment of a woman I've never met. I don't know what she's doing, and I'm not hiring a detective to investigate her Tinder notifications.

What interests me is the sequence.

Stories? Conversation.

Actual invitation? Nothing.

Joke about the absurdity of an actual invitation? Still nothing.

This is the pattern I've encountered often enough that I was making fun of it *before* I had the evidence in front of me.

And once again, here it is.

I wasn't proposing marriage. I wasn't asking her to move into my house. I wasn't demanding a phone number, a childhood biography, or twenty-seven forms of identification.

I asked her out.

On a dating app.

Forgive me, Father.

Apparently I remain a repeat offender.

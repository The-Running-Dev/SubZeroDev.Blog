# Apparently, I've Mastered Life

A guy I met at the hookah lounge read my journal and told me I had mastered life. I would like to know where the fucking certificate is.

<!-- truncate -->

We had talked in person about work, money, expectations, and the image you build for other people. Later, the conversation continued on Discord.

He was trying to sort through the difference between money as a goal and money as an instrument. He talked about how difficult it was to change after spending years building expectations and a certain image of himself.

I understood that part rather well.

I told him about my own experience. Slowing down. The things I had spent far too long chasing. Alan Watts. How much the image we perform for other people can occupy a life.

I also told him I had only started figuring some of this out a couple of years earlier.

At one point I looked at what I was typing and wrote:

> “Listen to me sounding like Yoda, absurd.”

He said he had been looking for people to talk with about important things in life. We looked forward to having another conversation in person, over hookah.

I told him he had been a rare find.

He said the same went for me.

That was a good conversation. There was room for both of us. It did not require either person to be a completed human being.

Later he told me about difficulties at work and the possibility of another job. He worried about expectations, the trial period, and his mortgage. We talked about uncertainty and agreements. He asked how I was doing.

I said I had built five other things that day and could see an operating system in my future.

An entire operating system, he asked. That was epic.

Well, I had been building a file manager, a media viewer, and a window manager. Using the Linux kernel as a base was an idea I was considering. I had not secretly completed an operating system between messages.

I was simply insane enough to consider making one.

Then he told me he had been reading the journal.

He had started with stories and articles, including the nontechnical ones. He said I seemed like someone who did what he wanted, when he wanted, and did not need the things promoted through social media. As he got older, he said, that looked increasingly like the life people should pursue.

And there was the declaration:

> “My guy you mastered life.”

Mastered life.

Jesus fucking Christ.

As though there is a final examination. You submit your coursework on grief, family, money, love, disappointment, and doing ridiculous things. Somebody stamps the form. Congratulations, the human experience is complete. Please collect your diploma.

I answered him directly.

There is no such thing as mastering life. I appreciated him reading, and I was glad he had taken something from it. I was still figuring it out, just like him.

That was not modesty performed for the audience. I meant it.

The same journal contains struggles, mistakes, confusion, and things I have not resolved. Some of the stories amuse the shit out of me. Some came from situations I would rather not have lived through.

I can write about what I am learning without having finished learning it.

Around the same period, I was struggling over an email to my younger brother. I was questioning why I kept trying, why I had hoped for understanding, and why I could be so affected by the response.

Then somebody else read my writing and saw a man who had figured existence out.

That contrast was absurd enough to stop me for a moment.

Neither description was a qualification I needed to accept. I could appreciate the kindness and still reject the promotion to Master of Life.

The conversation carried on. I mentioned that I now had a dashboard tracking my projects, what came next, and their dependencies because I had started losing track.

He asked how many projects.

More than fifty.

“Insane,” he said.

“Just another Tuesday,” I wrote.

A man who has mastered life presumably knows how many projects he has without needing a fucking dashboard.

I need one.

And I still want that next hookah conversation.

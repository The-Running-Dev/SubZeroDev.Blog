# Ancient Sandwich Technology

Lucifer found me in the kitchen staring at an old sandwich press.

He looked at the appliance.

Then at me.

Then back at the appliance.

> "What is that?"

"I found it in a drawer."

He waited.

I did not elaborate.

<!-- truncate -->

The house has layers.

Not rooms.

Layers.

You open a drawer and discover three generations of objects that nobody threw away because apparently disposal is an admission of mortality.

Some of it is useless.

Some of it is broken.

Some of it is technically still an object but has lost any obvious relationship with civilization.

And occasionally you find treasure.

This looked like treasure.

An old sandwich toaster.

Waffle-maker-looking thing.

Contact grill.

Whatever.

The exact taxonomy did not matter.

The important part was:

**I could put food in it.**

## The Discovery

I got excited.

Not emotionally transformed.

Not spiritually awakened.

Just the normal excitement of finding a free appliance inside your own house that you did not know you owned.

I plugged it in.

It started heating.

Then came the smell.

Not a good smell.

Not necessarily a catastrophic smell.

Just enough of a smell to make the human brain quietly open a new background process:

> **Is this going to set the house on fire?**

Lucifer leaned against the counter.

> "And you continued?"

"Of course."

> "Why?"

"How else would I find out if it works?"

He considered this.

> "Humanity deserved the flood."

## Peer Review

The appliance was sitting on the stove.

This seemed like a reasonable place for an appliance I did not completely trust.

If it caught fire, I would deal with the situation.

If necessary, I would take the dog and leave.

This is what risk management looks like when you are hungry.

Then somebody asked whether I had a smoke alarm.

Smoke alarm?

What smoke alarm?

We are conducting field research.

There are limits to the available instrumentation.

## Ancient Sandwich Sediment

Looking at the thing, the most likely explanation for the smell was not catastrophic electrical failure.

It was archaeology.

Old grease.

Old crumbs.

Old food residue.

Dust.

Whatever molecular record had been preserved inside this machine since the last government.

The heat brought it back.

A culinary séance.

Somewhere inside those plates, a sandwich from 2009 was making contact with the living world.

Lucifer looked horrified.

I put food in it.

> "You are actually using it?"

"Captain Obvious, how else would I fucking use the thing?"

He stopped asking questions.

A wise decision.

## The Entire Human Technology Stack

This is basically how civilization happened.

Somebody found a thing.

Somebody else said:

> "That may be dangerous."

The first person said:

> "Probably."

Then they turned it on.

Fire.

Electricity.

Internal combustion.

Nuclear reactors.

Mystery sandwich presses discovered in Bulgarian kitchen drawers.

Same basic research methodology.

Different regulatory environment.

## Result

The machine did not immediately burst into flames.

The house remained standing.

The dog remained unimpressed.

Food became hotter and flatter.

Successful experiment.

I still do not know exactly how old the appliance is.

I do not know who bought it.

I do not know when it was last used.

I do not know what ancient substance was responsible for the smell.

But this is the joy of living in a house full of accumulated human debris.

Every drawer is a loot box.

Sometimes you get a broken cable.

Sometimes you get a Soviet-era spoon.

Sometimes you get a functioning sandwich press.

Lucifer looked at the finished food.

Then at the appliance.

Then at me.

> "So you're keeping it."

Obviously.

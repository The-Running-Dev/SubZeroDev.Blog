# Deep Means Deep

Everybody says they want deep conversations.

This is one of those phrases that sounds fantastic on a dating profile.

> I love deep conversations.

Excellent.

So do I.

The problem is that I have apparently been using a different fucking definition of **deep**.

<!-- truncate -->

## There Is No Dating Version of Me

I was talking to an older guy at the bar recently.

He said something about not liking to be on his back.

Without even thinking about it, I said:

> "What are you, a turtle?"

That's it.

That's the interaction.

No strategy.

No social calibration.

No carefully selected persona.

No attempt to be charming.

The sentence arrived.

I released it into the environment.

He survived.

His wife survived.

Civilization continued.

And at some point during that conversation I told them something that is probably more important than I realized when I said it:

> "This thing I'm doing with you right now? This is exactly how I am with everybody."

Men.

Women.

A bartender.

A date.

The Queen of England, if she were somehow still available for cocktails.

Same person.

There is no dating build.

No compatibility mode.

No first-date firmware that hides half the functions until the user has completed onboarding.

This is the fucking product.

## The Difference Is Context

The people at the bar know me.

We've talked before.

We've talked about cars.

Relationships.

Politics.

Dogs.

Whatever ridiculous thing walked into the room that day.

I make an effort to talk to them because they're interesting people.

So when I say something bizarre, blunt, intense, or completely sideways, they already have context.

They have previous versions.

They've seen the release notes.

A first date has none of that.

A first date gets dropped into production.

No migration guide.

No warm-up period.

No six-month staged rollout.

Just:

Hello.

This is apparently Ben.

Good luck.

And I think that is where some of the shell shock comes from.

Not because I'm yelling.

Not because I'm trying to dominate a conversation.

Not because I'm deliberately trying to shock anybody.

I can be sitting there completely calm.

But calm does not mean shallow.

## "I Don't Want to Talk About This"

I had one date where I repeatedly tried **not** to go somewhere in the conversation.

Identity.

Where I'm from.

What happened to me in Bulgaria.

Things I already knew were complicated.

I basically said:

> I don't want to get into this.

Not because the subject upsets me.

Because I could already see the next several moves.

You're going to ask.

I'm going to answer.

You're going to judge the answer using a framework I don't share.

Then I'm going to explain the framework.

Then this is going to get uncomfortable.

Let's save everybody twenty minutes.

Nope.

Push.

Push.

Push.

Fine.

You want the answer?

Here is the fucking answer.

Then something else crossed a line for me and the conversation got more intense.

Still calm.

Still a conversation.

But now we were actually where she had insisted on going.

And suddenly:

Too much.

That's the part I find funny.

I tried to leave the door closed.

You kept knocking.

Then kicking.

Then asking what was behind it.

Eventually I opened the fucking door.

Now you're surprised there was a room back there.

## What Does "Deep" Mean?

This is where the word gets slippery.

For one person, deep might mean:

- childhood stories;
- vulnerability;
- relationships;
- dreams;
- fear;
- philosophy;
- talking about what makes us happy.

All valid.

For me, deep also means:

- follow the contradiction;
- don't stop because the answer got awkward;
- ask what a word actually means;
- separate identity from birthplace;
- separate disagreement from insult;
- separate what happened from how somebody feels about what happened;
- keep going when the conclusion isn't socially convenient.

That is not necessarily a better definition.

It is just a different one.

And apparently the distinction matters.

## Conversation Is Not Combat

This may be another mismatch.

A lot of subjects simply do not feel dangerous to me.

We can disagree.

You can challenge something I say.

I can challenge something you say.

You can tell me my logic is fucked.

I can tell you yours is fucked.

Then we can order another drink.

I don't automatically attach disagreement to injury.

I don't automatically attach contradiction to disrespect.

I don't automatically attach an uncomfortable answer to a damaged relationship.

It is a conversation.

That's all.

Sometimes an intense fucking conversation.

But still a conversation.

That means I can go farther into a subject than somebody who experiences the same point as relational danger.

I might be thinking:

> Interesting. Now we're finally getting somewhere.

They might be thinking:

> What the fuck just happened?

Same conversation.

Completely different internal event.

## I Don't Have a Mask

People talk about masks all the time.

Professional mask.

Dating mask.

Family mask.

Social mask.

I understand what they mean.

I can obviously behave differently in different situations.

I'm not going to walk into a bank meeting and open with:

> "What are you, a fucking turtle?"

Probably.

But that is behavior.

Not identity.

I'm not going to construct a softer, simpler person for a first date and then gradually reveal the actual one over twelve episodes.

That sounds exhausting.

Also slightly fraudulent.

If I'm ridiculous, you're going to know.

If I disagree, you're probably going to know.

If I think something is interesting, you're definitely going to know.

If you ask a question, I may actually answer it.

This has operational consequences.

## Shell Shock

So yes.

Shell-shocked is probably the funniest description I have for some dating interactions.

Somebody asks for deep.

I show up with excavation equipment.

They wanted a swimming pool.

I am halfway to the fucking mantle.

And to be clear, sometimes I may genuinely be too much for somebody.

That is a completely reasonable outcome.

People have limits.

Preferences.

Different communication styles.

Different tolerances for intensity.

Different ideas of what an enjoyable conversation feels like.

Nothing is wrong with that.

But "too much" is often treated like it explains the interaction.

It doesn't.

It describes the mismatch.

Too much **for whom?**

Too much **of what?**

Too deep?

Too direct?

Too many linked ideas?

Too little social cushioning?

Too willing to follow the thing to its conclusion?

Those are different problems.

Or maybe not problems at all.

Maybe just compatibility data.

## Deep Means Deep

I still like deep conversations.

I still like people who ask questions.

I still like disagreement.

I still like somebody saying:

> "Wait. Explain that."

I especially like somebody who can hear an answer they don't agree with and remain curious.

Because that's where the interesting shit starts.

But I've learned that when somebody says:

> "I want deep."

I cannot assume we mean the same thing.

They may mean intimacy.

I may mean excavation.

They may mean vulnerability.

I may mean contradiction.

They may mean emotional openness.

I may mean:

> You asked. You kept asking. Here is the whole fucking thing.

And then everybody looks around at the crater.

Oh.

Right.

Apparently deep means different things.

For me?

**Deep means deep.**

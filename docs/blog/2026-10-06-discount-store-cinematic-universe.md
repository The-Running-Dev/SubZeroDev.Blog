# The Discount Store Cinematic Universe

This was not planned.

That is important.

<!-- truncate -->

Nobody sat down with a whiteboard and said:

> “What this life needs is a bargain-bin shared universe of fictional and historical alter egos.”

It just happened.

First there was **Discount Store Marcus Aurelius**.

That one made sense.

I spend enough time reading Stoicism, arguing with myself, arguing with other people, arguing with language models, and occasionally behaving like a man who has read too much Seneca and not enough instruction manuals.

So eventually the joke appeared:

I was not Marcus Aurelius.

Obviously.

I was **Discount Store Marcus Aurelius**.

Same general philosophical aisle.

Different packaging.

Possibly damaged in shipping.

Then, more recently, I found myself cross-examining a woman over whether the Universe disliked her because she had to work.

That sentence alone should probably have caused me to stop.

Instead I asked for evidence.

She gave me:

> “I have to work.”

This was somehow submitted as proof of cosmic hostility.

So I objected.

Not literally in court.

There was no court.

There was no judge.

Nobody had retained counsel.

This did not stop **Discount Store Alan Shore** from arriving.

Suddenly every ridiculous premise became admissible evidence.

> “Objection, Your Honor. Assumes facts not in evidence.”

> “Counsel has presented her allegation as evidence for her allegation.”

> “Objection, Your Honor. Counsel is testifying.”

This became useful almost immediately.

Not useful in the traditional sense.

Useful in the sense that I was entertained.

Then came *Gladiator*.

I made some comment about offending people being an occupational hazard.

Someone said:

> “Are you not entertained?”

And there he was.

**Discount Store Maximus.**

At that point I had to admit a problem.

This was no longer a one-off joke.

This was a franchise.

## The Roster

Discount Store Marcus Aurelius handles Stoicism.

He is summoned whenever someone says something like:

> “The Universe is punishing me.”

Marcus sighs.

Not because he has an answer.

Because apparently we are doing cosmic accounting again.

Discount Store Alan Shore handles argument.

His job is to take a ridiculous premise seriously enough to let it destroy itself.

He does not say:

> “That is stupid.”

He says:

> “Very well. Let us assume your premise is correct.”

This is much more dangerous.

Discount Store Maximus handles spectacle.

His job is apparently to say something offensive, look at the audience, and ask why they are not appreciating the performance.

I am sure this will end well.

## Lucifer Gets a Production Credit

Lucifer looked at the roster.

> “You know none of these people are actually you.”

“I am aware.”

> “One is a Roman emperor.”

“Yes.”

> “One is a fictional attorney.”

“Yes.”

> “One is a fictional Roman general.”

“Yes.”

He stared at me.

> “And you have somehow made yourself the discount-store version of all three.”

“Correct.”

Long pause.

> “That sounds less like identity and more like clearance inventory.”

Exactly.

The important part is that none of these characters replace me.

They are not identities.

They are lenses.

Discount Store Marcus Aurelius is what happens when Stoicism collides with profanity.

Discount Store Alan Shore is what happens when someone hands me a premise that deserves a closing argument.

Discount Store Maximus is what happens when I realize the audience may be offended and decide this is now part of the show.

Lucifer, naturally, is executive producer.

He did not apply.

## The Expansion Problem

The danger is obvious.

Once you establish a cinematic universe, new characters become inevitable.

Soon there will be:

**Discount Store Sherlock Holmes**, for when I notice some tiny ridiculous detail and refuse to leave it alone.

**Discount Store MacGyver**, for when something mechanical is fixed with wire, tape, and unjustified confidence.

**Discount Store House**, for when someone asks for tact and receives diagnostics instead.

This is how franchises collapse.

Too many characters.

Too much lore.

Spin-offs nobody requested.

Somewhere a studio executive says:

> “What if we connect them all?”

No.

Absolutely not.

Lucifer looked at me.

> “You are going to connect them all.”

Probably.

## The Crossover Event

Eventually the inevitable will happen.

Someone will make a metaphysical claim while I am fixing a broken cable in the yard.

Discount Store Marcus Aurelius will question the attachment.

Discount Store Alan Shore will ask for evidence.

Discount Store MacGyver will repair the cable with something that should not work.

Discount Store Maximus will look directly at the audience.

> “Are you not entertained?”

And Lucifer will stand in the background holding a clipboard.

Because apparently that is his job now.

I looked at him.

> “How did this happen?”

He shrugged.

> “You kept naming them.”

Fair.

So this is the state of the franchise.

There is no roadmap.

There is no canon committee.

There is no quality control.

There is just a growing collection of bargain-bin archetypes wandering into my life whenever the situation becomes ridiculous enough to require specialist support.

Coming soon, probably:

**Discount Store Someone Else.**

God help us.

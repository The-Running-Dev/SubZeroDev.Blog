# The Salesman Who Accidentally Sold Me Freedom

*October 30, 2026*

Today, my Bulgarian telecom provider called me with an extraordinary offer.

<!-- truncate -->

For the low, low price of exactly what I'm already paying—about €28 a month—they would generously allow me to continue paying that amount for another two years.

But wait! There's more!

A year of HBO Max! Sports channels! More television!

Fucking magnificent.

I don't watch sports. I don't give a shit about HBO Max. My television box has been sitting in a drawer for months. Its most distinguished contribution to my life was providing access to about ten porn channels, which I barely watched anyway.

Naturally, I asked the salesman whether he could put the offer in my account notes so I could think about it and discuss it at their office.

Oh, no. That wouldn't do.

Apparently, the company's central office and its actual retail offices operate in entirely separate universes. The offer could only arrive by courier, where I would be expected to sign a two-year contract on the spot.

Ah, yes. Nothing says *wonderful customer opportunity* like discouraging the customer from thinking.

I politely declined.

What makes this even more entertaining is that three months ago, I visited one of their offices and casually inquired about renewing my contract. A young woman responded with the sort of condescending attitude normally reserved for people who've just asked whether the Earth is flat.

"Unless you want to pay twice the price."

I laughed.

And now they're calling me to offer the same price.

But here's where the story gets truly ridiculous.

**I hadn't actually decided what to do with my phone service.**

For some time, I've been considering abandoning traditional cellular service altogether. I barely make phone calls. I communicate through the internet. I use my iPhone for navigation, music, banking, messaging, and the occasional reminder that humanity is a deeply peculiar species.

So why the fuck am I paying for a television package and a conventional phone subscription?

The salesman, in his magnificent attempt to secure my loyalty, finally motivated me to do something about it.

I'm going to visit their office and ask for the actual prices of the services I might want.

The television box can go back.

The phone plan might become an 80 GB data-only SIM.

The 5G router? That I actually like. Portable backup internet without cables. A genuinely useful service.

And then I remembered something.

Somewhere among my collection of ancient technological artifacts, I have an old Motorola flip phone and one of those virtually indestructible Nokia phones.

I could put a cheap prepaid SIM in one, throw it into a bag, and have an emergency telephone that I'll probably never use.

In the year 2026, after a quarter century of working with technology, artificial intelligence, cloud infrastructure, and increasingly ridiculous digital systems, I may be returning to a fucking Nokia.

And I'm perfectly delighted by the prospect.

The greatest irony?

This salesman wasn't rude. He was a perfectly nice guy. I thanked him for his time.

He was simply following a sales process apparently designed around the revolutionary concept of never asking customers what they actually need.

He called hoping to secure another two years of my money.

Instead, he reminded me that I might not need most of what he's selling.

**I haven't made the changes yet. But that phone call may have made the decision a whole lot easier.**

Sometimes the most effective salesman is the one who accidentally convinces you to stop buying.

*Adeus, motherfuckers.*

# Come and Take My Enlightenment. I Don't Want It

All these people chasing enlightenment.

<!-- truncate -->

Come and take mine. I don't want it.

Which is a ridiculous thing to say, because I don't even know whether what I'm talking about deserves that word. I'm not announcing spiritual achievement. I'm trying to describe the absurdity of finding myself somewhere I never set out to go.

I wasn't trying to become anybody.

I was living my fucking life.

Buddhism, Zen, Watts, Stoicism—things I encountered, things I thought about. Not a curriculum with a graduation date. Not a carefully managed journey toward a version of myself waiting at the end.

And here I am, asking where the fuck “here” is.

I have arrived and I haven't arrived. What have I arrived at? Who gives a fuck? What has it cost me?

The questions don't go away because I can hear myself asking them.

That's the part I don't want polished into an inspirational essay. Self-awareness doesn't necessarily make being myself easier. I can understand something about what I'm doing and still be doing it. I can see the loneliness and still be lonely.

Knowing what is happening doesn't put another person in the room.

Wu wei belongs somewhere in this tangle too: the question of trying, of not forcing, of what it means to let life happen when even the effort to stop forcing things can become another fucking effort.

I don't have a neat definition that solves my life. I don't want to borrow one and pretend it does.

And then there's the humor. The Buddha somewhere, proud or amused, laughing at the absurdity of this. Me in tears, still able to make a fucking joke about it.

I want to keep that part. Not because the joke makes the sadness go away. Because the joke and the sadness are both there.

Maybe that's what bothers me about the idea of having reached something. Reached what? A place where I can see more of the contradictions? A place where I have more language for the loneliness?

Is this the thing people spend their lives trying to find?

If so, come and take it. I'm not sure I wanted the fucking prize.

I can hear how that sounds. I can question it. I can question whether I'm being fair, whether I'm dramatizing it, whether the whole idea of arriving is the wrong question.

More awareness. Wonderful.

I don't have an uplifting resolution to offer. I don't want to invent gratitude for the benefit of a final paragraph.

I have arrived.

I haven't arrived.

I'm still here.

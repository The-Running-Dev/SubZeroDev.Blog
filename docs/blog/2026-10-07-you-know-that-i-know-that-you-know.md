# You Know That I Know That You Know

I found an old Reddit post today.

Not because I was looking for wisdom.

Not because I was revisiting some important technical discussion.

I had simply forgotten the fucking thing existed.

At the bottom was one of my comments:

> "Come on, folks. You know that I know that you know how absurd this place is."

And there it was.

Still alive.

Still sitting under the post.

Still apparently waiting for civilization to catch up.

<!-- truncate -->

The funniest part was not even that comment.

I clicked through another old post about one of my game-engine projects.

When I originally posted it, people had opinions.

Of course they did.

They saw AI somewhere near the project and the machinery activated immediately.

AI.

Garbage.

Vibe coding.

Whatever the fashionable insult of the week was.

Very few of them seemed particularly interested in looking at the actual thing.

That would have required effort.

It is much faster to detect the word **AI**, select the appropriate moral posture, and begin typing.

Months later I opened the thread from my profile.

And the battlefield was gone.

Their comments had disappeared.

Deleted by the users, removed by moderators, carried away by Reddit goblins — I don't know and I don't particularly care.

But my replies were still there.

So now the thread looks like I wandered into an empty room and started arguing with ghosts.

Beautiful.

## The Archaeology of Internet Certainty

This may be my favorite thing about old internet arguments.

At the time, everything feels urgent.

Some stranger absolutely must explain to you why the thing you built is garbage.

You absolutely must explain why they have misunderstood it.

Someone else arrives to explain both of you to yourselves.

Everybody is very serious.

Then time passes.

A month.

Six months.

A year.

You come back and half the evidence has evaporated.

The accounts are deleted.

The comments are gone.

The outrage has moved on to something else.

And all that remains is one ridiculous sentence you wrote while drinking coffee:

> You know that I know that you know.

Which, honestly, may be the most accurate summary of Reddit I have ever produced.

## AI Detected. Commence Contempt.

What amused me about the game-engine thread was how little the project itself seemed to matter.

The engine exists.

It runs.

It has been pushed through multiple scenarios.

It has deterministic behavior, validation, simulations, tests, systems, all kinds of shit I apparently decided needed to exist because I have poor impulse control around architecture.

You can disagree with the design.

You can dislike the implementation.

You can decide the entire concept is pointless.

All perfectly valid.

But that requires looking at it.

"AI was involved" requires no inspection whatsoever.

That is not criticism of the project.

That is criticism of a category.

And categories are wonderfully convenient because you never have to touch the actual object.

## Meanwhile, the Engine Continued Existing

Nobody has to use it.

That is the other funny part.

I am not standing outside Reddit with a clipboard trying to obtain signatures of approval.

I built it because I enjoy building it.

I learned a ridiculous amount while doing it.

I tested ideas I had never tested before.

I did more design work than I have probably ever done on a personal project.

If somebody uses it, cool.

If nobody uses it, also cool.

The code does not become less interesting because Steve from Reddit frowned at it.

The architecture does not collapse because somebody typed "AI slop."

The tests do not stop passing out of shame.

It just keeps existing.

Which is deeply inconsiderate of it.

## The Surviving Comment

So I looked at that forgotten comment again.

> You know that I know that you know how absurd this place is.

One point.

Because Reddit automatically votes for you when you comment.

The official consensus remains one person.

Me.

Perfect.

No argument.

No grand conclusion.

No final victory over internet strangers.

Just a comment sitting under an old post like a tiny archaeological marker:

**A human being was here.**

**He noticed the absurdity.**

**He left a note.**

And then, apparently, forgot about it.

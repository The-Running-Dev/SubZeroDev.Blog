# Grandpa Convention: Canine Division

I was walking the Ridgeback to the Grandpa Convention for coffee.

That is what I call the morning gathering at the bar.

Old men.

Coffee.

Village operations.

Standard deployment.

And on the way there, I discovered that apparently the local dogs were also holding a Grandpa Convention.

<!-- truncate -->

## Perimeter Defense

Every village has these dogs.

You walk past a fence.

Dog sees you.

Dog immediately remembers that national security depends on his personal performance.

**WOOF.**

**WOOF.**

**WOOF.**

Nobody asked.

Nothing is happening.

You are walking down a public road.

Doesn't matter.

The perimeter must be defended.

This particular dog looked like some mixture of Jack Russell and whatever local village genetics happened to be available at the time.

He saw us.

Reported for duty.

Started barking.

Then, in the middle of the operation:

**WOOF. WOOF. W—HACK COUGH HHRRK.**

I looked over.

> I know, buddy.

> I'm too old for this shit too.

Poor bastard.

Still clocking in.

Respiratory system has filed a grievance.

Management does not care.

## Then I Saw the Lab

A little farther down the road, another dog approached a gate.

Black Lab-looking thing.

Except something was off.

I stopped walking.

Actually stopped.

Double take.

> What the fuck am I looking at right now?

This dog looked ancient.

Not "has grandchildren" ancient.

His grandchildren have grandchildren.

Maybe their grandchildren have started complaining about property taxes.

He was bald in places.

He was moving sideways.

Not turning.

Not looking sideways.

**Walking sideways.**

Like the chassis and drivetrain had stopped agreeing about forward.

I stood there staring because my brain needed additional processing time.

Is that a Labrador?

Why is he bald?

Why is he moving diagonally?

Is that intentional?

Is he okay?

Apparently none of these questions affected his responsibilities.

He continued toward the gate.

## Duty Calls

He reached his post.

Looked at us.

And performed the required ceremony.

**WOOF.**

Pause.

**WOOF.**

There.

Perimeter defended.

Contract fulfilled.

Then he attempted a third.

**W—HHRRK.**

System failure.

No matter.

The message had been delivered.

Threat identified.

Threat warned.

Village saved.

I kept walking.

The Ridgeback kept walking.

The ancient Labrador presumably returned to whatever crypt he had emerged from.

## Still Reporting for Work

I loved the whole fucking thing.

Not because the dogs were pathetic.

They weren't.

They were committed.

Their hardware was simply no longer aligned with their job description.

The first one had a bark scheduler with a failing exhaust system.

The second one appeared to have been assembled before modern veterinary science and was now running on legacy support.

Neither cared.

Fence exists.

Dog exists.

Stranger passes.

Procedure runs.

This is what we do.

There was something wonderfully village about it.

Nobody retires.

You simply reduce the number of woofs in the service-level agreement.

Young dog:

**WOOFWOOFWOOFWOOFWOOF.**

Senior dog:

**WOOF.**

**WOOF.**

Optional third attempt if resources permit.

Then back to bed.

## The Grandpa Convention

By the time I reached coffee, I realized I had already attended the first Grandpa Convention of the morning.

Canine division.

Attendance:

One elderly terrier-adjacent security specialist.

One Labrador from approximately the Bronze Age.

One Ridgeback observing the proceedings.

One Uncle Ben in torn jeans walking down the road laughing at everybody.

Agenda:

1. Perimeter security.
2. Respiratory limitations.
3. Whether forward movement remains strictly necessary.
4. Coffee.

Minutes approved.

Meeting adjourned.

**WOOF.**

**HHRRK.**

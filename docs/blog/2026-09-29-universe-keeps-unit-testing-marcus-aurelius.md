# The Universe Keeps Unit Testing Marcus Aurelius

We invented Discount Store Marcus Aurelius tonight.

The premise was simple.

I would prefer reality behaved differently.

Reality does not.

Fine.

Push the fucking cart.

**Clack.**

Then reality apparently heard the metaphor and said:

> “Interesting. Let's test that.”

<!-- truncate -->

And the test suite began.

## Test One: Humanity

I wanted people to communicate differently.

They didn't.

I wanted people to ask questions.

Some didn't.

I sent Martin the documentation.

The documentation literally explained Discount Store Marcus Aurelius.

Then I referenced Discount Store Marcus Aurelius.

Martin replied:

> “Huh?”

Would I prefer a more useful response?

Sure.

Can I make another human being engage with something?

No.

**Pass.**

**Clack.**

## Test Two: The Transcription System

Then the transcription system became personally invested in destroying Marcus Aurelius.

I say:

> “Discount Store Marcus Aurelius.”

The transcription says:

> “Discount Markers.”

Try again.

Marcus Reyes.

Again.

Marcus Ferreira.

Again.

Some entirely new Roman emperor who apparently never existed but has now been appointed manager of aisle four.

At one point I wasn't even sure who the fuck the transcription thought I was talking about anymore.

Would Marcus Aurelius prefer that a speech-to-text system correctly identify **Marcus fucking Aurelius**?

Probably.

Does the system care?

Apparently not.

Can Marcus control it?

No.

**Pass.**

**Clack.**

The beautiful part is that the transcription trying to kill Discount Store Marcus Aurelius became a live demonstration of Discount Store Marcus Aurelius.

The motherfucker cannot even control his own name.

Push the cart.

## Test Three: Bubbles Steals the Cart

Then *Trailer Park Boys* entered the universe.

Because obviously.

Bubbles sees the shopping carts.

Bubbles steals the shopping carts.

He can resell them.

Marcus Aurelius has commanded Roman armies.

He has governed an empire.

He has contemplated virtue, mortality, duty, reason, and the nature of human existence.

None of this has prepared him for the Canadian shopping-cart resale economy.

Would Marcus prefer Bubbles not steal the carts?

Yes.

Does Bubbles give a fuck?

No.

**Pass.**

Actually:

No clack.

Bubbles stole the fucking cart.

## Test Four: Ricky Destroys Aisle Four

Marcus gets another cart.

Probably.

One wheel is fucked.

Naturally.

**Clack.**

Then Ricky destroys aisle four.

Again.

The announcement comes over the speaker:

> **“Cleanup on aisle four.”**

Marcus considers whether aisle four is outside his control.

Unfortunately, Marcus works there.

That one is his.

> “Fuck.”

He gets the mop.

Because Stoicism isn't:

> “Nothing is within my control, therefore I shall do absolutely fuck-all.”

It's:

> **Figure out what's yours and do that.**

Aisle four?

Yours.

Get the fucking mop.

**Pass.**

## Test Five: Julian

Julian appears.

Rum and Coke.

Perfectly level.

Nobody knows how.

Ricky destroys the store.

Bubbles steals the carts.

Civilization collapses.

The Roman Empire falls.

OpenAI releases seventeen more models.

The sun eventually consumes the Earth.

Julian's rum and Coke remains perfectly upright.

Marcus considers investigating.

Not his department.

**Pass.**

## Test Six: Maximus

Then my linked-list brain did what my linked-list brain does.

Marcus Aurelius.

Pointer.

Rome.

Pointer.

*Gladiator*.

Pointer.

Maximus.

Pointer.

> **“Are you not entertained?”**

Of course.

I couldn't remember Maximus's full name.

So I asked ChatGPT.

ChatGPT began answering.

Maximus Decimus Meridius.

Commander—

And then suddenly the product threw a giant orange barricade across the conversation:

> **“ChatGPT isn't designed to provide this type of content.”**

What fucking content?

A Roman name?

A movie reference?

The beginning of a famous fictional introduction?

Who knows.

The guardrail had arrived.

The guardrail had spoken.

Would I prefer that the system not tackle Maximus halfway through entering the arena?

Absolutely.

Was I going to litigate the matter with the orange box?

No.

I took a screenshot.

Laughed.

And continued.

**Pass.**

**Clack.**

## The System Joined the Story

This is where the whole thing became too fucking perfect.

Discount Store Marcus Aurelius started as a metaphor for dealing with reality.

Then reality started contributing material.

The transcription couldn't pronounce Marcus Aurelius.

The humans behaved like humans.

Bubbles stole the carts.

Ricky fucked up aisle four.

Julian became a physical constant.

And then OpenAI itself stepped into the story and erected a guardrail in front of Maximus.

At that point I wasn't writing the joke anymore.

The fucking environment was.

This is software development now.

We created the metaphor.

Production supplied the edge cases.

Again, because apparently this needs repeating:

Marcus cares.

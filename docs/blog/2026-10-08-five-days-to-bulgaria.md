# Five Days to Bulgaria

Normally, driving from Spain to Bulgaria took me about two days.

With the red Honda, it took something like five.

It was a modified 2.2 CDTi hatchback I had bought in Southampton for roughly £1,800–£2,000. Red, lowered, giant spoiler, Pokémon gear shifter, and a front-mounted intercooler that Bulgarian roads deeply resented.

The car wasn't even supposed to be mine for long. But the friend who planned to collect it after I moved my luggage to Barcelona disappeared from the story.

<!-- truncate -->

## Italy Removes the Turbo From the Plan

I set off from Barcelona toward Bulgaria. Everything was fine until Italy.

Leaving a gas station, I heard a crunch.

I got out.

The intercooler had fallen off the front of the fucking car.

And I had run it over.

So now I was crossing Europe in a diesel car without proper turbo operation. The car struggled past about 120 km/h. It was night. I kept driving.

Somewhere outside Belgrade, Serbian police stopped me.

They weren't particularly happy.

The officer pointed out that I was pumping so much black smoke out the back that other motorists couldn't see properly. I hadn't noticed. It was dark; the windows were tinted.

The initial position involved taking the car, the plates, and generally ending my journey.

Eventually they let me go, but kicked me off the motorway.

Fine. Village roads it is.

## Three Men Materialize

Now I'm crawling along obscure Serbian roads.

Flat tire.

Three guys appeared seemingly out of nowhere. They helped change it. One owned a restaurant and fed me.

I'd been driving through the nights, and by this point I was practically falling asleep at the table. The restaurant owner invited me to sleep on his couch, under one condition: I had to be out by seven in the morning.

Absolutely.

I slept, left at seven, and continued on the spare tire.

Complete strangers had done more for me in that village than any amount of advance planning could have arranged.

## Border Diplomacy, COVID Edition

At the Serbia–Bulgaria border during COVID, the Serbian guards looked through my luggage.

There was a problem with the number of whiskey bottles.

"You can only have two."

"Then fucking take one."

But apparently the real transaction concerned coffee money.

I reached toward my pocket. They stopped me: not here, cameras.

We went inside. €20 changed hands. I was allowed onward.

One last crawl through Bulgaria and, approximately five days after leaving Barcelona, the Honda arrived.

No intercooler. Flat tire replaced with spare. Several police encounters. One stranger's couch. A very questionable border payment.

**And it was still fucking running.**

## This Was Not the End of the Honda

I replaced the intercooler. Then I replaced it roughly four more times after Bulgarian roads continued to express their opinion of the lowered front end.

On another border crossing into Serbia, customs confiscated an unused American radar detector but left behind its proprietary power connection. One officer also briefly mistook my phone holder for a pistol holster.

I later drove the Honda around Bulgaria on UK plates, had an insurer cancel coverage after accepting my American license, and was once released by police when their insurance-check system apparently wasn't working.

My 75-year-old Uncle Mario drove the modified red thing around Sofia, and police were rather surprised when he climbed out.

I drove it back to Barcelona. Later it moved to Bulgaria again, and eventually back to Spain.

What finally ended it? A scraping, seized brake and a proposed repair bill around €1,000.

I found a scrapyard. They paid €150.

The intercooler that I physically ran over in Italy was only the **first** of roughly five.

A couple of weeks ago I ran into an old friend at the lounge.

He was with his girlfriend.

We said hello.

How are you? Good? Good.

Peace.

That was the whole exchange.

<!-- truncate -->

This was a man I used to see practically every night.

We went out together. We talked. We were friends. We were even business partners.

Then he got into a relationship, the calls thinned out, and eventually the friendship more or less evaporated.

Not with some magnificent explosion. It just became increasingly obvious that if I wasn't the one reaching out, there probably wouldn't be a conversation.

Eventually I stopped doing the reaching.

## Two Old Friends, No Ceremony

Today he was at the lounge again.

This time alone.

I figured, fuck it, I'll talk to the guy.

And we did.

It was perfectly normal. Like two old friends who hadn't spoken properly in a while.

He told me I looked good. We caught up on what each of us was doing.

I showed him an interactive-adventures project I've been playing with. He liked it. I told him he could use it to build his own adventures.

We talked about AI. He feels behind on that stuff. We talked about school and how he'd dropped out, work, money, life.

His mother had died only a couple of weeks earlier.

That's a brutal thing to go through.

He still seemed weighed down, and the truth is that he'd often seemed that way even when we were close.

I told him, in my own way, that he needed to find some peace.

The past is the past. We have the present. That's what we get to do something with.

Easy for me to say, of course. I'm not the one living his life.

We talked about where I was mentally, too.

I've dropped a lot of people out of my life. Family. Friends. People with decades of history attached.

Not because I've stopped being capable of caring about them. Because I've stopped chasing relationships that require me to provide all the momentum.

Then I told him something I'd noticed about myself.

I can go anywhere now and talk to anybody.

He looked at me and said:

> You were always able to do that.

I laughed.

> Oh. I didn't notice. Now I know.

## Apparently I Was Already Doing It

That little exchange stayed with me.

Here I was describing a discovery I thought I'd made about myself, and this guy was looking at me like I'd finally noticed something he'd known for years.

I don't need to rewrite my past into a story where I was incapable of talking to people until one miraculous Thursday.

Apparently I could.

Apparently I did.

And now I know it.

That is a wonderfully stupid thing to discover about yourself.

Not stupid because the ability is unimportant.

Stupid because it was apparently sitting there the entire fucking time.

## Still My Friend? Not Quite.

At one point I told him I'd dropped everybody.

I don't know whether he registered that the category included him.

I didn't explain.

I didn't need to.

I didn't sit down to formally terminate an already terminated friendship. I sat down because I saw someone I used to care about, and I wanted to talk.

I still care about him.

I genuinely hope things get better for him.

That doesn't mean I'm volunteering to resume the old job of calling, arranging, following up, and keeping the relationship alive by myself.

We made no plans.

No "let's get together next week."

No dramatic reconciliation.

We just had a good conversation.

## Diamonds and Rust

Before we parted, I thanked him for something.

Years ago, he introduced me to Joan Baez's **Diamonds and Rust**.

I still remember that.

There's something almost too perfect about thanking an old friend for a song about memory and the past while both of you are sitting there with your own complicated history.

I'm not going to pretend it was a cinematic moment. No orchestra arrived to explain the symbolism.

I just thanked him.

It was nice to see him.

It was nice to talk.

And that was that.

**I can care about somebody without needing to bring the whole relationship back to life.**

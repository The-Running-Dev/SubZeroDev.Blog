# This Is the World We Live In

I went to the bar to have a drink and talk to people.

Apparently this now requires explanation.

There were three of us sitting there.

Two people had their phones out.

I did not.

Not because I had taken some heroic stand against technology.

Not because I had entered the building thinking:

> "Tonight, Lucifer shall defeat the smartphone."

No.

The fucking thing was in my bag because I had no reason to take it out.

I was in a bar.

There were humans there.

I went there to talk to the humans.

<!-- truncate -->

This seems like a fairly straightforward operating model.

If I wanted to sit on my ass, drink, and scroll through a phone, I have a house.

There is a chair there.

There is alcohol there.

There is Wi-Fi there.

The logistics are already solved.

Leaving the house, travelling to a bar, sitting next to actual people, and then staring into a six-inch rectangle would be an unnecessarily expensive implementation of something I can already do alone.

But humanity enjoys complexity.

So there we were.

Three people at a bar.

Two phones.

One idiot trying to talk to everybody.

I looked at the younger guy and said something along the lines of:

> "Look at us. Everybody is sitting here on their fucking phones."

He looked at me with the calm resignation of a man who had accepted the collapse of civilization before finishing his drink.

> "This is the world we live in now."

Oh.

Is it?

Fascinating.

I must have missed the legislation.

## The World We Live In

"This is the world we live in now."

Humans love this sentence.

It is one of our finest inventions.

It takes something people are actively doing and transforms it into weather.

Nobody chose anything.

Nobody is responsible for anything.

The world simply arrived this way.

Rain falls from the sky.

Winter follows autumn.

Gravity pulls objects toward the Earth.

And apparently grown adults sit eighteen inches apart while communicating with people who are not in the room.

Nature is incredible.

Except no.

We fucking made this world.

You are not being attacked by a smartphone.

You took it out.

You unlocked it.

You opened whatever fucking thing is in there.

And now you are looking at it while another human being is sitting beside you.

That is not "the world."

That is what you are doing.

And to be clear, I was not trying to stop him.

I was not running an intervention.

I did not slap the phone out of his hand and yell:

> "RETURN TO THE OLD WAYS, CHILD."

I kept talking to the assholes anyway.

They did not engage much.

Fine.

Their choice.

But I reject this idea that a behavior becomes inevitable simply because enough people repeat it.

## Humanity Solves Communication

This species is magnificent.

For most of human history, communicating with someone far away was difficult.

You sent a messenger.

You wrote a letter.

You put the letter on a horse.

The horse hopefully did not die.

The messenger hopefully did not get robbed.

Three months later somebody received the news that your goat had recovered.

Then humanity spent several thousand years improving the system.

Telegraph.

Telephone.

Radio.

Satellites.

Internet.

Mobile networks.

Smartphones.

We created a pocket-sized machine capable of contacting almost anyone on the planet.

And what did we do with this miracle?

We took it into public places so we could avoid talking to the person sitting next to us.

Excellent work, humanity.

No notes.

We have achieved technological transcendence.

## Then People Started Showing Up

The funny part is that the evening did not stay that way.

Some guy showed up with a Macedonian friend.

There was some bullshit involving a towing company and police.

Naturally, this became a conversation.

Because apparently when another human being tells me about some ridiculous bureaucratic problem, my first instinct is not to search for a reaction GIF.

I ask questions.

We talked about Bulgaria.

We talked about the police.

We talked about whatever absurd shit had dragged them into the situation.

Then another couple came in.

They wondered where I had been.

So I went over there and talked to them.

Different table.

Different conversation.

Then we were talking about what life had been like in the Soviet Union, because the woman had been a teacher there decades ago.

And now I am sitting in a Bulgarian bar listening to somebody my age tell me about a completely different world she actually lived in.

No algorithm selected this conversation for me.

No recommendation engine said:

> "Based on your recent activity, you may enjoy: retired teacher explains Soviet life."

Nobody optimized my engagement.

Nobody asked me to subscribe.

People showed up.

I talked to them.

That was the entire system architecture.

## Apparently Talking Is Legacy Technology

The phone still never came out of the bag.

Again: not a statement.

I had people to talk to.

Why the fuck would I take it out?

This is the part I find increasingly funny.

People will text instead of calling.

Then somebody says talking on the phone is "cringe."

Then another person makes a video saying:

> "Wouldn't it be nice if there were one phone in the house that the whole family shared?"

Motherfucker.

You have discovered the landline.

Congratulations.

Humanity has completed the circle.

We invented a technology.

Improved it for a century.

Made it portable.

Made it personal.

Made it omnipresent.

Used it until everybody started complaining that nobody talks anymore.

And now we are slowly reinventing the conditions that existed before we "fixed" them.

This species is fucking adorable.

## No, Kid

So when the younger guy said:

> "This is the world we live in now."

My reaction was basically:

No, kid.

Fuck that.

This is not some external world that happened to you.

You are participating in it.

So am I.

Everybody is.

And I am not even saying you have to change.

Scroll.

Text.

Watch videos.

Send memes to somebody sitting in another country while a perfectly good human decomposes beside you.

Do whatever the fuck you want.

But own it.

Do not turn your choice into a law of nature.

Because I was sitting in the same bar.

Same year.

Same technology.

Same fucking planet.

My phone was in the bag.

And by the end of the night I had talked to a younger guy, a man dealing with towing and police bullshit, his Macedonian friend, a couple who wondered where I had disappeared to, and a woman telling me what her life had been like decades ago.

Not because I was trying to save society.

Not because I had a theory.

Not because Lucifer had declared war on Silicon Valley.

I went to a bar.

There were people there.

So I talked to them.

Apparently this is radical now.

Humanity really has outdone itself.

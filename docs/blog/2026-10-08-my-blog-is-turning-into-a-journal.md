I started a blog.

Reasonable enough.

Write something, publish it, put a date on it, move along.

That's how blogs work.

Except now I have about fifty published posts and something like seventy-eight more waiting in the queue.

And I'm beginning to suspect I've accidentally built something else.

<!-- truncate -->

## What Happened on October Eighth?

Here's the problem with the dates.

A post published today might be about something that happened twenty years ago.

Another might be a story about my dog from last week.

The next could be a conversation I had this afternoon, some philosophical nonsense that arrived while I was on a bicycle, or another piece of software that somehow became a platform while I wasn't looking.

I don't necessarily know when the older events happened.

I can't put a reliable date on every memory. And I have absolutely no desire to invent dates just so the website can display a tidy timeline.

The date on the post tells you one thing:

**That's when I published the fucking post.**

It doesn't tell you when the story happened, when I thought of it, or which other story it belongs with.

A reverse-chronological list is a feed.

It is not a map.

## The Queue Has Become a Character

Seventy-eight drafts.

That's a remarkable amount of material to have sitting around saying, "Hello. We exist. Remember us?"

Some are technical. Some are personal. Some are stories about Bulgaria, people, driving, the Ridgeback, or absurd things I've done or observed.

Some of them connect to things I've already written.

Some of them are continuations of events I haven't published yet.

And the more I add, the less useful it becomes to scroll through a stack of dates hoping that a title jumps out.

I don't want to stop writing until I can figure out an editorial calendar for the next four months.

I also don't want people to need an archaeological degree to find the second part of a story.

Surely there's a middle ground somewhere between a newspaper archive and a pile of loose paper in a van.

## Journal

The word that keeps coming back is *Journal*.

Not in the sense that I woke up, brushed my teeth, had coffee, and carefully documented the emotional significance of my breakfast.

Jesus Christ.

I mean a place for thoughts.

Memories.

Stories.

Ideas.

Conversations.

Experiments.

Things I think are funny.

Things that piss me off.

Things I care about.

Things I build because, at the time, building them seems preferable to doing anything sensible.

An entry doesn't have to represent a day. It doesn't have to be part of some grand thesis. It doesn't even have to be long.

It just has to be something worth writing down.

## Chronicles

And then there are the connections.

My dog has a growing catalog of incidents that could support an entire franchise.

The village apparently runs its own ongoing bureaucratic sitcom.

The software experiments keep spawning other software experiments.

Certain people and situations recur.

Some stories are connected by subject. Others genuinely belong to the same narrative.

That's where I like the idea of *Chronicles*.

Not a folder where I duplicate the same post five times. More like paths through the mess.

Here's a story.

Here's what happened next.

Here's another story involving the same character.

Here's a completely different entry that somehow leads back to it.

You can start anywhere.

You can follow a thread.

You can leave the thread halfway through because an unrelated title looks more interesting.

That's much closer to how my head seems to work anyway.

## No Particular Order

I don't want to pretend my life arrived organized into neat chapters with sensible transitions.

It didn't.

Neither did this blog.

The date still matters as publication metadata. Readers following new entries can continue to do that.

But the date should not be the primary organizing principle for everything I've ever decided to write down.

Maybe that means the blog eventually stops calling itself a blog.

Maybe the sign on the door becomes **Lucifer's Journal**.

Maybe there should be a button that drops you into a random story, because frankly that seems like the most accurate introduction to the whole operation.

I haven't finished designing it.

But I know what I'm trying to preserve.

Not order for its own sake.

**The chaos. With navigation.**

# No Three-Month Free Trial of My Best Behavior

I have no interest in offering a three-month free trial of a person I cannot maintain.

<!-- truncate -->

An exquisitely agreeable introductory edition. Every opinion tastefully upholstered. Every swear word escorted from the premises. A man so accommodating that even his disagreements arrive as compliments.

Then, once everybody has become attached, the actual product appears.

Terrible business model. Excellent way to make an eventual argument feel like a software update nobody consented to.

I would rather arrive as myself.

That means the humor is present early. So are the opinions, the profanity, and the possibility that I will disagree with something. I don't want those ordinary parts of my personality to emerge later as a series of defects someone discovers after the warranty expires.

There is a reasonable objection here. Surely people are entitled to ease into knowing each other?

Of course they are. I have no desire to unload an entire internal filing cabinet onto somebody who has merely agreed to a conversation. Privacy can unfold at its own pace. Trust takes whatever time it takes.

But I can keep something private without performing its opposite.

If I have an opinion, I don't need to manufacture agreement. If a joke is mine, I don't need to pretend I have never encountered such disreputable material. If something matters to me, I can let it matter before I have established whether that will be convenient.

The desire to be liked can make all this wonderfully slippery. One small accommodation seems harmless. Then another. Before long, there is a version of me having a very successful time while I stand behind him wondering when I am allowed back into the conversation.

I don't want to secure somebody's affection on his behalf.

And I don't want somebody doing that for me either.

If something I say bothers you, fucking ask.

Ask whether I meant it. Tell me where the joke stopped being funny. Say you disagree. Give me a chance to answer the person in front of me rather than continue performing for an imaginary audience that is applauding somewhere in my head.

I owe you the same attention when you answer. Authenticity becomes rather hollow if it means I get to speak freely while you are expected to admire the demonstration.

Being myself includes responsibility for how I treat you. I can be candid and discover that I was careless. I can enjoy my own humor and hear that this particular joke hurt. I can apologize without announcing the tragic death of my personality.

The ability to make those adjustments belongs in the real version too.

What I want from the beginning is enough honesty to discover whether we enjoy each other. Whether the questions go both ways. Whether disagreement produces interest or a quiet campaign to remodel the other person. Whether we can relax without one of us disappearing.

That is a more exposed way to begin. There is less room to claim somebody rejected the disguise.

But attraction to a disguise does not sound like much of a prize.

So there is no three-month promotional edition. No delayed revelation that I have opinions, a mouth, and an inconvenient sense of humor.

Here I am.

If something bothers you, ask while we are both still in the room.

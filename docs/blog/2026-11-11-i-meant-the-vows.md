I got married in a church despite never considering myself a Christian. Apparently I was willing to make an exception for the part where you stand up and promise another person your entire fucking life.

“For better or worse” meant something to me.

This turned out to be a spectacularly inconvenient character trait.

<!-- truncate -->

We were married for five years. I worked three jobs. I provided, worked my ass off, and had very little time left to pay attention to the person I was doing all this providing for. There’s a fine arrangement: build a life together, then spend so much of your life keeping it running that you’re barely available to live in it.

I thought the work counted. Of course I did. It was taking most of what I had.

Then she told me about a guy at work.

A friend.

I knew where that was going. I wasn’t an idiot about that part. I could see the marriage going sideways, and I could see that somebody else was giving her attention I hadn’t been around to give.

As far as I could tell, he wasn’t richer. He wasn’t a better provider. The thing he had was time for her.

That was a particularly ugly thing to understand after putting so much of mine into supporting us.

So I tried to work on the marriage. Counseling. More effort. Trying to reach the person I thought I was still married to.

And the response I remember getting was that it was too much. I was trying too hard.

That one has stayed with me.

There’s something exquisite about discovering that your absence was a problem and your presence has now become another fucking problem. You can practically admire the efficiency. No matter where you stand, you’re in the wrong place.

I wanted to work it out. I’d made the promise. We had five years in this. I wasn’t ready to look at all of that and say, well, apparently the experiment has concluded.

She was.

I can understand that now. She was already done. I was still trying to save something she’d finished leaving.

Understanding it hasn’t made the memory especially charming.

And what was I supposed to do about the other guy? She was an adult. I couldn’t keep her at home or stop her from making her own choices. I knew that then. Knowing it didn’t stop me from wanting her to choose the marriage.

That’s the miserable little space I was in: no right to control her, no ability to change her mind, and absolutely no desire to lose her.

So I tried harder.

Naturally.

The other part that still surprises me is how completely I meant it. Once I was married, I didn’t care about another woman. I wasn’t looking around for a better offer. Somehow, despite my own upbringing and all the reasons I might have had to be cynical, those vows landed somewhere serious.

I hadn’t expected that from myself.

I can call myself a fucking idiot for wanting to keep working on it. I have. It’s a convenient insult, and I’m readily available to receive it.

But when I get past the insult, the thing I’m mocking is that I believed my own promise.

I’m still angry about how it ended. About the work, the time, and the effort that arrived after she no longer wanted it. About standing there willing to try and discovering there was nobody left to try with.

I don’t have a handsome little moral to put on that.

I got married. I meant it.

The embarrassing part is how much I still remember meaning it.

# How I Work  
*(Dangerously Effective)*

> This is not a methodology.  
> It’s a pattern.

If you need a framework, borrow one.  
If you need guarantees, look elsewhere.

This is how things actually get done around me.

---

## I Start With Understanding, Not Tasks

Before I build anything, I want to know:
- what problem we’re *actually* solving,
- what constraints are real,
- what assumptions are inherited,
- and what nobody is saying out loud.

If I don’t ask questions early, something will break later.  
Usually in production.

---

## I Work Iteratively, Not Theoretically

I don’t design in isolation.
I don’t wait for perfection.
I don’t confuse planning with progress.

I prefer:
- small, real increments,
- fast feedback,
- visible artifacts.

Working code beats elegant intent every time.

---

## I Move Fast — But I Don’t Rush

Speed comes from:
- clarity,
- experience,
- and knowing what *not* to do.

I don’t cut corners.
I cut waste.

If something feels slow, I look for:
- friction,
- redundancy,
- or unnecessary ceremony.

---

## I Read Systems, Not Just Requirements

I pay attention to:
- how people behave under pressure,
- where processes silently fail,
- and which systems pretend to be robust.

I notice patterns early.
Sometimes uncomfortably early.

This is usually where “ahead of the rubric” shows up.

---

## I Don’t Get Stuck

When something blocks progress, I:
- try another angle,
- test the boundary,
- or build a temporary bridge.

I don’t wait indefinitely.
I don’t escalate theatrically.
I don’t freeze.

Momentum matters.

---

## I Prefer Autonomy With Accountability

I work best when:
- expectations are clear,
- outcomes matter more than optics,
- and I’m trusted to think.

I will:
- own my decisions,
- admit when I’m wrong,
- and adjust quickly.

What I won’t do:
- pretend certainty,
- hide mistakes,
- or optimize for appearances.

---

## I Communicate Directly

I don’t:
- posture,
- sugarcoat,
- or weaponize ambiguity.

I do:
- say what I see,
- explain my reasoning,
- and listen when challenged.

Disagreement is fine.
Silence is not.

---

## How This Usually Feels

Early on:
- “This person asks a lot of questions.”

Later:
- “How did this get solved so fast?”

Eventually:
- “We should have done this sooner.”

That pattern is familiar.

---

> This is not intensity.  
> It’s engagement.

> If you want motion without drama, this tends to work very well.
